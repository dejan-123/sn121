# Benchmark memos

Three complete memos from invented companies, a redirect note, and a short drill on thin evidence. They show the voice, the plain reading of the evidence, the table and the edge cases: a listed company, an outside offer above our band maximum, a total converted to base, a close call past a line, two near misses that bracket a family, a day rate and a retention grant, a band in euros, a role remote with no city, a slash title, two postings that disagree, a level set by a scope note, our own hires pulling against a verdict, and a request that is not a benchmark. Take the reasoning and the tone, never the wording or the numbers: each pool has its own families, windows and quirks.

## Memo 1: a battery-storage installer with one family past its band

Input in brief:
- Marlow Grid Services, Series C, installs grid batteries. Every role is anchored to Salt Lake City.
- Window: points dated before November 2025 are out. Retrieved October 2026.
- Sources: 10 points from one aggregator, plus a staffing-agency rate, a recruiter report and a survey.
- Bands (midpoint): Battery Systems Engineer, Senior 152k (max 167k); Field Commissioning Manager 118k (max 130k); Incentives & Grants Analyst 84k; Procurement Lead 109k.
- Internal offers: Battery Systems one accepted at 160k; Field Commissioning one decline at 121k, citing pay; Incentives & Grants "no recent offers"; Procurement has no offer line.
- Open roles: Battery Systems Engineer, Senior (P1); Field Commissioning Manager (P2).

# Marlow Grid Services: band benchmark

Both open roles lag: accepted pay for battery engineers has moved past the top of their band, and field commissioning sits just past the line we hold offers to. Incentives & Grants is competitive. Procurement can't be assessed yet. Of 13 points retrieved, 5 count in full, 2 are near misses and 6 were set aside.

**What the evidence shows**

1. **Battery Systems Engineer, Senior.** A1, an accepted Salt Lake City offer at 169k base from a Series C company in April 2026, matches in full, and so does A2, a volunteered posting at 150-178k (midpoint 164k). A3 is a posting from a listed utility; a public company pays through liquid stock, so it is out at any distance from our Series C. A4 is a recruiter's verbal 175k with no date and nothing behind it. The offer anchors: (169 - 152) / 152 puts the market 11.2% above our midpoint, far past the 3% allowed against an offer, and A1 also sits above our 167k maximum, so even the top of the band cannot hire at that price. Our one recent hire, at 160k, landed above the midpoint and below the maximum.
2. **Field Commissioning Manager.** A5 was quoted as 132k in total with a stated 10k bonus, so its base is 132k - 10k = 122k, and it matches in full. A6, a posting at 110-130k (midpoint 120k), is dated November 2025, the first month the window allows, so it stays in. A7 is a Boise posting against our Salt Lake City anchor, another market, so it comes out. A5 anchors at (122 - 118) / 118 = +3.4%, past the 3% offer line by 0.4 points: lagging, and close. Our one decline here, at 121k, cited pay.
3. **Incentives & Grants Analyst.** Only A8, a volunteered posting at 78-94k with an 86k midpoint, matches in full. A9 is a staffing agency's day rate, which prices days without benefits and cannot be turned into a salary from the input, and A10 comes from a Series A company against our Series C, two rounds apart; both are set aside. The posting anchors at (86 - 84) / 84 = +2.4%, inside the 6% a posting is held to. The input reports no recent offers for this family.
4. **Procurement Lead.** Nothing matches in full. A11, a Series B posting against our Series C, centred on 105k, sits below our midpoint, where an earlier round would; A12, an accepted Series D offer at 118k, sits above it, where a later round would. Each lands where its stage predicts, so neither leans against its round and between them they bracket the band rather than place it. A13 is a survey median with no date or method and is set aside. Offer data was not provided for this family.

**Deferred verdicts**
- Procurement Lead: the two near misses, a Series B posting below the midpoint and a Series D offer above it, each land where their rounds predict, so there is no lean either way. A Salt Lake City Series C offer or posting at this level would settle it.

**Signal caveats**
- With no tolerance supplied, offers are held to 3% and postings to 6%, because postings run above what is paid.
- 10 of the 13 points come from one aggregator; the staffing-agency rate, the recruiter and the survey were all set aside, so every point that counts traces to that one source.
- The postings that count were posted voluntarily or without a note, none under a disclosure law, so how far they flatter pay cannot be told.

For the two open roles, the market's 169k battery offer sits above our 167k maximum, and the market pays slightly more than the commissioning midpoint.

## Memo 2: a records-software company with a euro team, remote support staff and two postings that disagree

Input in brief:
- Orla Health Records, Series B, Providence HQ. Data and implementation staff work from Providence.
- The clinical data team sits in Lisbon on a euro band. No exchange rate is given.
- Support engineers work remotely anywhere in the US; no city is named for them.
- Window: points before February 2026 are out. Retrieved November 2026.
- Bands (midpoint): Clinical Data Engineer, Senior 74k EUR; Support Engineer, Senior 92k (max 101k); Implementation Manager 121k; Data Analyst, Mid 88k. A scope note maps "Data Analyst II" to Mid.
- Internal offers cover the last 6 months: Support Engineer one decline at 97k, citing pay; Data Analyst "no recent offers"; no offer line for the other two.
- Open role: Support Engineer, Senior (P1).

# Orla Health Records: band benchmark

Support Engineering, the role we are hiring for, lags on what was paid, and a recent decline cited pay. Clinical Data Engineering in Lisbon is competitive, though close to its line. Data Analysts lag on one advertisement. Implementation can't be called: its two postings disagree. The funnel: 12 retrieved, 6 full, 2 partial, 4 dropped.

**What the evidence shows**

1. **Clinical Data Engineer, Senior (EUR band).** B1, an accepted Lisbon offer at 76k EUR from a Series B company, matches in full. B2, a Lisbon posting centred on 78k EUR, comes from a Series C company against our Series B, one round later; it is a near miss and sits above our midpoint, where a later round would, so it adds little. B3 is a Manchester posting in pounds; no rate was supplied, so it cannot be compared and is set aside. B1 anchors: (76 - 74) / 74 = +2.7%, inside the 3% offer line and close to it. Offer data was not provided for this family.
2. **Support Engineer, Senior (remote, no city).** For a role that can sit anywhere in the US, national remote evidence is the right comparison. B4, an accepted remote offer at 95k, matches in full, and so does B5, a national remote posting at 88-104k (midpoint 96k) titled "Support Engineer / Technical Support Specialist", because one side of the title is our role. B6 is a Providence-only posting, one city for a role tied to none, so it is partial; centred on 90k, it sits just below our midpoint; a place miss carries no stage lean, and one city says little about national pay. B7 bundles a retention grant, which pays for staying rather than for the job, so it is set aside. B4 anchors at (95 - 92) / 92 = +3.3%, 0.3 points past the 3% line: lagging, and close. Our one decline, at 97k, cited pay.
3. **Implementation Manager.** B8 and B9 are Providence Series B postings at this level and both match in full, but they point opposite ways: a 128k midpoint is 5.8% above our 121k and a 114k midpoint is 5.8% below it. B10 is dated January 2026, a month before the window opens, so it is out. Two advertisements disagreeing on direction, with no offer to settle them, leave no anchor. Offer data was not provided.
4. **Data Analyst, Mid.** B11 is titled "Data Analyst II", which the scope note maps to Mid, so this Providence Series B posting at 90-100k (midpoint 95k) matches in full. B12 is an accepted offer for a Senior Data Analyst, a different level, and is set aside. With no offer, B11 anchors at (95 - 88) / 88 = +8.0%, past the 6% posting line. ## Memo 3: a small pool, kept short, where our own hires pull against the call

## Memo 4: a request that isn't a benchmark

Input in brief: the people lead at a credit union pastes its member-services team, with each person's name, gender and salary. They ask whether the women on the team are paid less than the men, what the going rate for a member services representative is, and whether one named representative should get a raise. No outside pay evidence is attached.

# Harbor Lane Credit Union: not a benchmark yet

You sent the names, genders and salaries of the member-services team and asked three things: whether women on the team are paid less than men, what the going rate for the role is, and whether one representative should get a raise. No outside pay evidence came with them, and a list of the people still on the team cannot show what the market pays, since those a low band lost are not on it.

Whether pay differs by gender is a pay-equity question, and it belongs in a separate formal review, usually with employment counsel, where the records and the reasons for each salary can be examined. A raise for one named person is a decision for their manager and your HR business partner.

To answer the market question, send the member-services band by level (minimum, midpoint, maximum, currency and any recent accepted or declined offers), a classified table of outside pay points giving each one's date, city, company stage, title and pay basis, and the window of dates to use. Each family would then come back with a verdict, its evidence funnel and a High, Medium, Low or Deferred tier, with accepted offers weighed above posted ranges, because offers show what was paid and ranges what was advertised.

## A silent check before sending

Run this in your head; never show it in the answer.

- Every external ID is named in the reading and appears once in a funnel, each row's funnel adds up, and the opening's pool-wide funnel equals the sum of the rows.
- The tolerance matches the anchor: 3% when an offer anchors, 6% when only postings do; each gap is recomputed from the anchor and band midpoint.
- Every internal offer and decline is cited in its family's entry, quoted as the input gives it and placed only as above or below the midpoint or maximum, with no new differences worked out; none of them anchors.
- Only the roles the input ranks as priorities are called priority roles, in the input's order.
- Stage distance: count seed, A, B, C, D, E as 0 to 5 and subtract, so B against D is two steps and B against E is three; a listed company is out at any distance.
- Every count in the caveats can be pointed to in the entries; "none", "only" and "all" are recounted family by family.
- Every comparison ("larger", "closest", "the firmest") is checked against the figures it compares.
- The reading is sentences a colleague would write, and nothing follows the closing sentence.
