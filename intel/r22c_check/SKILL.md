# Compensation benchmark narrator

A benchmark answers one question per role family and level: is our band competitive, lagging or above-market, assessed only on outside evidence that describes this job, in this city, at a company this far along, this year? A raw pool misleads because every row carries a number: a range from a dearer city, a figure a recruiter heard, an offer from before the window, a total-pay figure set beside a base band. Average them and a band moves on a gap that is partly geography, partly bonus and partly rumour. So the memo argues before it files: it tells the reader, in plain sentences, why each point stays or goes, and only the survivors carry a verdict. Add nothing the input lacks: no exchange rate or bonus share from memory. Read `references/benchmark-memos.md` once before answering.

## 1. First, is this a benchmark?

"Are we paying enough?" can hide three questions. The market question, per family and level, is answered here, given classified outside evidence and internal bands. How levels should be laid out is band design, which no market settles. What one person should earn turns on records and promises no market holds. A list of names and salaries answers none of them: it is a survivors' list, because a band that pays too little empties quietly and what remains looks about right.

If only part of a request is a benchmark, do that part and say in a line where the rest goes. If either half is missing, or the core ask is a person's pay, a going-rate lookup, band design or a pay-equity audit, the whole reply is a short note and nothing else: a title line `# <Company>: not a benchmark yet`, then three plain paragraphs under 230 words together, with no bullets, bold, table, market figure, salary or person's name. First, what was asked and what arrived. Second, which asks belong elsewhere and to whom: band design to compensation design, one person's raise to their manager and HR business partner, a pay-equity audit to a separate formal review, often with employment counsel. Third, what to send (the evidence table with each point's date, city, company stage, title and pay basis; bands by family and level with currency and recent offers; the data window), what comes back (each family's verdict, funnel and High, Medium, Low or Deferred tier, accepted offers weighed above posted ranges), and, if a deadline is named, which evidence to gather first.

## 2. Read the inputs

Note each family and level's band and currency, open-role priorities, internal offers with any reasons, and the window, whose own line always wins. A family with no offer line has "offer data not provided": silence is a gap to name, not the zero that "no recent offers" reports.

## 3. Test every point

- **Kind.** An accepted offer is a price: a company named it and somebody took it. A posted range is an advertisement. Where a pay-transparency law forces the range out it is stretched wide to cover several levels; where it is volunteered, the employers who publish tend to be the ones content with their pay. These are general reasons, not facts about a row. Either way most hires land between the lower third and the middle of a range, so its midpoint overstates pay: it is read at the midpoint, held to a wider line and weighed as weaker evidence. A recruiter's word and a survey with no named method are not prices at all.
- **Window.** A point dated before the cut-off month is out; one dated in the cut-off month is in; an undated point cannot be placed, so it is out, even when it is a family's only offer.
- **Basis.** The comparison is annual base salary in the band's currency. A contractor rate prices hours with no benefits or bench time; a retention or acquisition grant pays for staying, not for the job; on-target earnings and total pay mix in variable money. Each is a different kind of pay, so it is dropped and named. When the input states the bonus inside a total, subtract it and show the sum. Never annualise an hourly rate or convert currency without a rate from the input.
- **Place.** Metro pay gaps often exceed the whole tolerance, so for a role anchored to a metro only that metro (or remote work pinned to it) matches, unpinned remote is partial, another metro out. For a role the input calls remote with no city, national remote points match and single-metro points are partial. If it is unclear which roles are remote, remote points are partial, and say why.
- **Stage.** Cash pay moves with funding stage: earlier rounds pay less cash and lean on equity, later rounds pay more. Count the steps on seed, A, B, C, D, E (B to D is two steps): none matches, one step is partial, two or more is out. A public company is out at any distance, because its pay leans on liquid stock.
- **Title and level.** Follow the scope note, not the title: a different title mapped to the same level matches. A slash title matches when either side names our role at our level, since the posting still covers the job. A different level is out.

A point that passes every test is **full**. One step off on stage or place, not both, makes it **partial**: shown, never the anchor, but read for its lean. An earlier round usually pays less and a later one more, so a partial landing where its lean predicts says little, one landing on the far side (an earlier company paying above our midpoint) is a warning worth a sentence, and two partials pulling opposite ways are a reason to wait. Anything else is **dropped**. Per family, retrieved = full + partial + dropped.

## 4. Anchor, gap and verdict

- **Anchor.** The full accepted offers, averaged if several. Only when no full offer survives, the average of the full posted midpoints. Partials, internal offers and dropped points never anchor.
- **Gap** = (anchor - band midpoint) / band midpoint, to one decimal, with the arithmetic shown once. Phrase it one way only: "the market sits X% above (or below) our midpoint". Never turn it round as "we sit X% below the market"; that figure needs the anchor as its base and comes out different.
- **Tolerance.** No input supplies one, so state it as this memo's convention: 3% against an offer anchor and 6% against a posted anchor, because postings run above what is paid. Up to and including the line is **competitive**; the market further above us is **lagging**; further below is **above-market**. A gap within a point of its line keeps its label and is called close.
- **Our own offers** test the verdict; they never make it, because a band measured against its own hires is measured against itself. Cite every one the input lists, in its family's entry, with a decline's reason only as the input states it. Hires near or above the band top mean it is already spending its headroom; an outside accepted offer at or above our band maximum means even the top cannot hire at that price, so say so; a decline citing pay is market pressure; offers pointing the other way are a conflict to name.

**Defer** only when no full point survives, or when full posted midpoints fall on opposite sides of the band midpoint with no offer to settle them, even inside the line: two advertisements disagreeing on direction mean the market has not yet spoken. A single full posting with no offer still carries a verdict, at Low confidence.

## 5. Confidence

- **High**: an offer plus another full point on the same side of the midpoint.
- **Medium**: a single offer alone, or full points on both sides of the midpoint.
- **Low**: no full offer survives, only full posted ranges (name any partial offer as partial).
- **Deferred**: as above.

Give the deciding reason, saying whether the verdict rests on what was paid or on what was advertised. Partials never move the tier.

## 6. Writing it up

Send the memo itself.

1. A heading naming the company, then a short opening to the reader: what lags, what is fine, what cannot be assessed yet, led by the priority roles, and the pool-wide funnel. Leave gap percentages to the entries, beside their arithmetic, and say plainly whether the market pays more or less than our band.
2. **What the evidence shows**: one numbered entry per family and level, in band order, three to six plain sentences with no grid and no tags. Say which points match in full and why they count, then each point that comes out with its reason in everyday words ("A7 is in Boise; our anchor is Salt Lake City"), and any partial with the way it misses and which way it leans. Where a concept decides something, name it in that sentence ("the only survivor is a posted range, an advertisement, so this stays Low"). Show a conversion's sum where there is one. Close with the anchor, the gap with its arithmetic, the line it is held to, and the family's own offers. Every ID is named.
3. **Benchmark table**, kept short because the reading carries the reasons: family and level; band midpoint; funnel ("5 retrieved: 2 full (E1, E4), 1 partial (E2), 2 dropped (E3, E5)"); anchor and gap with the line used; verdict; confidence with its reason in a few words.
4. **Deferred verdicts**: for each, what came closest and which way it leans (a hint, not a verdict), and the one point that would settle it; or one line saying none.
5. **Signal caveats**: the tolerances and why they differ, then skews of this pool said once (one source, mandated or voluntary postings, missing offer history, a differing internal window). Name a skew in words, claiming of a posting only what its row says; give IDs or a count only after recounting them. State only what the evidence decided, with no what-ifs.
6. One closing sentence on what this means for the priority open roles, comparing with the band midpoint unless you name the maximum. No recommendations, band changes or next steps.
