# Two deals audited end to end

Every term on trial has a record that would settle it. This file names those records, then shows two invented pools classified in full — one loud, one quiet. No company, register or figure here exists; reuse the pattern, never the names, facts or sentences.

---

## The record taxonomy — what one pull settles

| Record | Kind | One pull settles | It never settles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charges / filings index | Primary, binding | Security interests recorded against the assets | Whether the debtor performs |
| Enforcement register | Primary, binding | Findings, open or closed, under the exact name | Misconduct never found |
| Court docket | Primary, binding | Whether a claim is live, ended, ended how | What the claim was worth |
| Patent, trademark, licence registers | Primary, binding | Ownership and status, item by item | The value of what is owned |
| Accreditation register | Primary, binding | Whether the certifier's own authority stands | Whether a certificate was honestly earned |
| Counterparty filed accounts | Primary, binding | That the counterparty booked what the target claims | The target's own figures |
| The target's own channels | One voice | What the target asserts | Nothing alone — it never corroborates itself |
| Press and posts | Secondary | What is said, by how many independent origins | Anything until a primary record answers it |
| Papers only the parties hold | No register exists | — | Requested, never searched for |

A searched-clean entry speaks for its search date and no further. A register never opened speaks not at all — and when it is the record a stated term turns on, its absence is itself the finding: the term is untested, and the action is the search.

## Deal A — the loud pool

## The reads that most often go wrong

- Two mastheads on one report are still one origin; a denial contributes none, and instructed counsel contributes less.
- A clean pull has a date on it; "not searched" is never a verdict — it becomes a row when a stated term turns on that record.
- A story naming nobody in the deal names nobody; a name one letter or suffix off is an identity question, not a finding.
- Money raised against vehicles, plant or equipment is normally secured on them; when the count is missing, say it is unmeasured and name the index that counts it.
- A certificate leans entirely on its issuer's standing; when the issuer wobbles, the finding rides the issuer's row.
- "With prejudice" finishes a claim; "without prejudice" merely pauses it, and a term demanding no active challenge keeps a paused claim at the top.
- Facts living only in the parties' files are requested through the deal channel, never treated as searched or as clear.
- A filed number stands until the pool shows it struck on the wrong quantity — then the term is untested even though the number looks like it clears.
