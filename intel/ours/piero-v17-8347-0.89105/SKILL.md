# The board — file every plan, clear it on conditions

A manager's draft agenda is a stack of filed plans. Each filing is a first intention, not a clearance. Put every plan on the board, then clear it on field conditions alone — what the packet documents, nothing it does not. The output is the strip board the manager reads on the way in: every plan visible, every changed call traceable to a reading.

## Ground control — is this this window's traffic?

A packet of the manager's own topics for the coming window is always this window's traffic, even when it carries no conditions: a bare agenda, a two-line note, topics without figures. Thinness is worked plan by plan and never turns the packet around.

The wrong document is different: minutes, notes or a transcript of a meeting already held; preparation for another meeting; or a review, rating or disciplinary draft. A held meeting's record documents last window's traffic even when the manager asks for next week's plan from it. For those, clear nothing and send the turnaround below.

## What goes on a strip

Only packet conditions go on a strip: an action item and its state, including the manager's own rows; a sprint or ticket figure; a quoted message; the manager's note; the report's raised topic. No invented name, date, owner, quote, figure, mood or result. Where a clearance needs a reading the packet lacks, log the lack and name the record that would settle it.

## How readings weigh

One documented occurrence is a single report: it can clear a note, never a trend. The same conduct documented on two or more dated occasions can carry development. A named prior sprint with planned-versus-actual figures can supply the second occasion.

Attributed commendation — a named person's thanks or a concrete measured result — is earned credit and departs first. Vague memory is not credit. The report's own account is boardable but weighs below an independent reading. An impression the packet cannot source is a reason to ask, not a finding.

A completed plan stays a one-line close unless a later documented reading genuinely conflicts with it. A promise, attempt or scheduled step remains a plan until the packet says it happened: write `planned to escalate` and ask whether it happened, never `escalated`.

## Which field owns it

A plan belongs to the field whose controller can move it. Work waiting on approval, access, a supplier, a calendar, another team or an assigned queue goes on the board as the manager's action to move, never as the report's fault to answer for. A sole-reviewer design holding several teams is a capacity call: the assigned queue holds the teams, not the report. If the report proposed the repair, the manager's move is to approve, broker, reassign, escalate or set the date.

Pay, health, family circumstances, accommodations, formal HR, third-party complaints, ratings and promotion decisions belong at other fields: route them with destination and owner, no talking points. A report-raised direction, level, training or packet step gets protected time; a manager-created career check remains a modest invitation.

## The clearances

Every plan takes exactly one clearance, and the filing never chooses it — the conditions do:

- **Cleared**: the conditions support the plan as filed. Give it an anchored question or concrete manager move.
- **Re-routed**: the plan is real but clears under a corrected filing. Finished credited work becomes earned credit; another team's stall becomes the manager's unblock; one dated slip becomes a one-time note.
- **Diverted**: real traffic bound for another field — destination and owner only.
- **Grounded**: no documented reading supports the plan. Its absence is visible.
- **Holding**: the plan is live but undocumented. Keep the honest question and the missing record.

A single incident is a one-time note, not a trend. When the report has already found the cause, credit that and coach the earlier flag — what should have been noticed or raised sooner — rather than repeating the symptom. A justified point raised in a public channel keeps its substance and takes exactly one narrow channel question.

## The board

350–700 words, written for the manager to read, not audit. The window runs 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise.

The page should sound like a prepared colleague, not a process record. Keep the labels, then let the reading and the manager's next move carry each item; do not narrate the tests that produced the call.

- **Board head**: `# Strip board: <name>, <role>` — then three lines: the window's picture in one sentence; every derivable figure shown with its operands, including the action log's done/open total when supplied and only `DONE` in the numerator; which record types the packet lacks.
- **Departure order**: every filed plan numbered as `n. Clearance · topic · ~minutes`, then its strip line: `filed: <the manager's plan> → <clearance>: <the condition in a few words>`. No filed plan disappears; a merged plan keeps its strip line under the slot that absorbed it, and diverted or grounded plans name their clearance. Order: earned credit first, then blockers and manager calls, one-time feedback or development, the report's own direction, live status and closes.
- **Vectors**: questions and concrete manager actions only, each anchored to a named reading — the thread, ticket, figure, date or person — at most three sentences per plan. A direction the report never raised stays a modest invitation, not protected promotion preparation. A question that could be vectored in any window is not a vector.
- **Pre-flight**: the manager's own open or overdue rows and the readings to pull for logged gaps. External dependencies stay on the board; report-owned rows stay with the report.
- **Diversions**: other-field traffic with destination and owner.
- **Controller's log**: three to five plain sentences closing the board — each re-route, diversion or grounding and the condition that forced it; why the first strip departs or why there is none; what a headline figure does and does not show.

Show every figure's basis. Rates and scores move in percentage points; planned-versus-actual uses the same comparison; unrelated counts never become ratios. A cause that explains only part of a gap leaves the remainder open.

## Thin packet

A future-facing draft with no log, figures or messages still receives a board. The head logs what did not arrive. Each plan takes `Holding`, `Diverted`, or another clearance its own wording supports. Ask what the missing record would show and who holds it; invent no praise, pattern, completion or finding. Pay goes to the pay owner; a manager-created career check is an invitation.

## Turnaround for a meeting already held

For a record of a meeting already held, send only a note under 180 words, no board:

`# <report name>, <role>: not a 1-on-1 prep request`

- `**Redirect.**` Name the held meeting and the detail that proves it — date, attendees, discussed items or agreed actions. Say a board would replay old positions or invent current ones.
- `**What it supports:**` list each recorded open action with the named owner and due date, manager-owned items first. Say their current state is not supplied.
- `**What the next board needs:**` current action status, this week's signals, any new report topic and a fresh draft agenda.
- `**Private matter:**` route family, health, pay or personal material privately; draft no talking points.

## Order of work

1. In the working sheet, check document kind, then give every filed plan its condition, clearance and any diversion; work each figure and separate manager-owned, report-owned, fetch and routed items.
2. Read `references/field-cases.md` once with `read_reference`; carry the clearances and page shape, never the facts.
3. Send only the board or turnaround. The first characters are `# `; the working sheet is not repeated, summarised or introduced.
