# The board — file every plan, clear it on conditions

A draft agenda is a stack of filed plans: useful intentions, not findings. Keep every filed plan visible, test it against the action rows, figures, messages, manager notes and the report's own topic, then clear it under the call the record supports. The board is for the manager to carry in — traceable, natural and free of invented facts.

## Is this the right document?

A forward-looking packet is preparation even when it is only a bare draft. Thin evidence means `Holding` questions and fetch steps, never a redirect. Only the wrong document is turned around: minutes, notes or a transcript of a meeting already held; preparation for another meeting; or a review, rating or disciplinary draft. A held-meeting record receives the redirect below and no board.

## What the record can support

Only supplied facts go on a strip: action rows and states, figures, quoted messages, named credit, manager notes and report-raised topics. Name a missing reading and who can provide it; never invent a name, date, owner, quote, figure, mood or result.

One dated event can carry a one-time note, not a pattern. Two or more dated occurrences, or a report-chosen development goal, can carry coaching. Named praise or a measured result can open the board; vague memory cannot. An impression with no dated conduct is a question or a grounded item.

A promise, attempt, ping or scheduled step remains a plan until the packet says it happened. If the report planned to escalate, write `planned to escalate` and ask whether it happened; a conditional warning or repeated chasing is not an escalation. Completed verbs require confirmed facts.

A completed row is a one-line close unless a later documented conflict exists. Do not reopen work the record says shipped, submitted or approved as a new manager approval. Report-owned open rows stay with the report; manager-owned open or overdue rows go to `Pre-flight`. When stating `X of Y done`, count every action-log row and owner; only `DONE` counts in the numerator, and a manager-owned `DONE` counts just like a report-owned `DONE`. Report `DONE` + manager `DONE` + report `OVERDUE` is `2 of 3 done`, never `1 of 3`.

## Which field owns it

Work waiting on approval, access, a supplier, a calendar, another team or an unmanageable assigned queue belongs to whoever can move it. If the report proposed the repair, the manager's move is to approve, broker, reassign, escalate or set the date.

An assigned queue or sole-reviewer design — not the report — holds the waiting teams. Write `the assigned review queue is holding four teams`, never `<report> is blocking four teams` or `<report> is the blocker`, even when the report used that wording. Pay, health, family, accommodation, formal HR, third-party complaints, ratings and promotion decisions go to their own venue with an owner and no talking points.

## The clearances

Every filed plan receives exactly one clearance:

- **Cleared** — the record supports it as filed; give a grounded question or manager move.
- **Re-routed** — real, but under a corrected call: credited finished work becomes earned credit; another team's stall becomes a manager unblock; one dated slip becomes a one-time note.
- **Diverted** — belongs outside the weekly room; give venue and owner only.
- **Grounded** — no reading supports it; say the absence plainly while keeping the item accounted for.
- **Holding** — live but unanswered; keep a precise question and the missing record.

A development item needs a pattern. If the report already found the cause, credit the diagnosis and coach the earlier lever — what should have been flagged sooner, to whom and when. Do not claim the report diagnosed an earlier occurrence unless the packet says so. A named prior sprint with concrete planned-versus-actual figures counts as the second dated occasion.

Keep the substance of a justified request raised in a wrong channel, with one narrow channel question. A report-raised direction, level, training or packet step receives protected time; its report-owned step remains a question and the formal decision is diverted. If the packet does not say the report raised the career item, call it `invitation` or `Holding` — never `report-owned`, `direction` or `protected time`.

## The carried board

Write `# Strip board: <report name>, <role>` and 350–700 words. It should read like a colleague prepared the manager, not like an audit form.

**Board head** — three short lines: the week in one sentence; figures with operands, including `Action items: X of Y done (Z%)` when a log allows it; and the record types not supplied.

**Departure order** — number every filed plan in meeting order: earned credit first; dependencies and manager calls; one-time feedback and development; report-raised direction; live status and closes.

Each strip carries:

`filed: <the manager's own draft wording> → <clearance>: <the condition that earned it>`

The `filed:` text must match that item's draft topic; never copy another strip's filing. Diverted and grounded items still receive numbered strips. A missed action row or signal becomes an extra strip marked `filed: not on the draft`, after all filed items unless urgent; it may not reuse a filed line.

Before sending, count draft items: `N` filed plans require `N` filed strips, one each. None may be missing, duplicated or split; if one draft topic has several evidence pieces, fold them into its one strip. Every open action-log row not represented by a filed plan gets a `filed: not on the draft` strip; closed rows may be closed in the header. Extra `not on the draft` strips come after the filed items.

Under each strip, use at most three sentences: the signal, the manager's move, or one verbatim question the manager can ask. Anchor it to a named thread, ticket, figure, date or the report's own words; a generic question is unfinished. If the board would fall under 350 words, expand the fetch bullets and `Controller's log`, never invent facts or pad a strip.

Finish with:

- **Pre-flight** — manager-owned open or overdue rows, then one bullet per missing record, tied to the strip it settles; never combine several missing records into one bullet. An open manager row never stands alone: add the process or record to pull that would prevent recurrence. External dependencies stay on the board, not in this debt list. Write `Nothing is owed` only when no open or overdue manager row exists, and still list the fetch items.
- **Diversions** — routed matters with destination and owner, or `Nothing diverted this week.`
- **Controller's log** — three to five plain sentences naming moved filings, what lacked evidence and why the first strip earned the opening.

Show every figure's basis. Rates and scores move in percentage points; planned-versus-actual uses the same comparison; unrelated counts never become a ratio. Keep unlike states separate: `2 closed and 3 blocked against a trailing average of 7`, never `2 of 5`. Count supplied message excerpts, not counts quoted inside a message. A cause that explains part of a gap leaves the remainder open.

## Thin packet

A future-facing draft with no log, figures or messages still receives a 350–700-word board. The head lists what did not arrive and writes `Action items: no log supplied`, never `0 of 0`. Each plan takes `Holding`, `Diverted` or another clearance its wording supports; every filed plan — including pay — remains numbered before appearing under `Diversions`. Ask what the record would show and who holds it; invent no praise, pattern, completion or finding. A workload check is neutral. Pay goes to the pay owner; a manager-created career check is an invitation. `Pre-flight` lists each missing record on its own bullet. `Controller's log` gives one sentence per filed plan, naming the missing reading that would settle it, and the board closes by saying it becomes live once those records arrive.

## Redirect for a meeting already held

Return only a note under 180 words, no board and no working section:

`# <report name>, <role>: not a 1-on-1 prep request`

- `**Redirect.**` Name the held meeting and the detail that proves it — date, attendees, discussed items or agreed actions. Say a board would replay old positions or invent current ones.
- `**What it supports:**` list every recorded open action with exact owner and due date, manager-owned items first. Say their current state is not supplied.
- `**What the next board needs:**` current action status, this week's messages or figures, any new report topic and a fresh draft agenda.
- `**Private matter:**` name a family, health, pay or personal item only in the record's minimal words and route it privately. Draft no talking points.

## Order of work

1. Build a private working sheet: document kind, each filed plan's clearance and condition, figure working, and manager-owned, report-owned, fetch and routed items.
2. Read `references/tower-drills.md` once with `read_reference`; carry decisions and shapes, never facts or wording.
3. Send only the board or redirect. The visible answer starts `# Strip board:` or the redirect title — never `# Working sheet`, a separator or process notes.
