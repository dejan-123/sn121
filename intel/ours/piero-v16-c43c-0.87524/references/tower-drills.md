# Tower drills — clearances on invented packets

The workplaces are fiction. Carry the way each plan is cleared, not the names, figures or products.

## Drill 1 — a normal window with wrong labels

Packet: report is **Toma Ivers, senior fulfillment engineer**. The manager's draft says:

1. "status on the parcel-routing rewrite"
2. "coaching — Toma was sharp with the inventory team"
3. "quick shoutout, not sure for what"
4. "ask whether he still wants the duty-lead course"

The conditions: routing is 5 of 9 services converted and waiting on the carrier API key requested ten days ago. On Tuesday, the inventory lead wrote that Toma's exception-report tool cut their recount from three hours to forty minutes. On Thursday, Toma objected in the shared channel when inventory proposed shipping a hand count that skipped reconciliation; there is no second conduct event. Toma asked last week whether the duty-lead course could count toward weekend coverage. The manager's own open row is to request the carrier key's priority review.

Clearances:

- The vague shoutout is **re-routed** to the earned credit: the inventory lead named Toma's tool and the 3-hour-to-40-minute result. It departs first.
- Routing is **re-routed** to a manager unblock. The work waits on the API key, so the manager's move is to get a date or escalate the priority review.
- The channel exchange is **re-routed** from coaching to a one-time note. One dated objection on the merits can carry one question about where the next disagreement should live.
- The course is **cleared** as Toma's direction because he raised it. Give it real time and connect it to the weekend-coverage path.

Board:

`# Strip board: Toma Ivers, Senior Fulfillment Engineer`

A strong delivery week is waiting on the carrier key. Routing is 5 of 9 services converted; the recount fell from 3 hours to 40 minutes, down 78%. Action items: 1 of 3 done (33%). No second conduct event is documented.

1. **Exception-report tool — earned credit · ~4 min**
`filed: "quick shoutout, not sure for what" → re-routed: the inventory lead named Toma's tool and its measured result.`
Quote the recount change and ask which part should become the standard exception process.

2. **Parcel-routing rewrite — manager unblock · ~8 min**
`filed: "status on the parcel-routing rewrite" → re-routed: the conversion waits on the carrier API key requested ten days ago.`
Bring the priority-review answer or set the escalation date. "Once the key lands, which of the four remaining services converts first?"

3. **Inventory exchange — one-time note · ~4 min**
`filed: "coaching — Toma was sharp with the inventory team" → re-routed: one dated objection to skipping reconciliation.`
The substance was justified; the channel is the only question. "Would putting the next reconciliation objection in the ticket make the decision trail cleaner?"

4. **Duty-lead course — direction · ~7 min**
`filed: "ask whether he still wants the duty-lead course" → cleared: Toma raised the course and its path to weekend coverage.`
Ask which duty he wants first and what evidence the course should produce.

**Pre-flight**
- Request the carrier key's priority review or bring an escalation date.
- Pull the remaining-service conversion plan before the meeting.

**Diversions**
- Nothing diverted this week.

**Controller's log**
The undocumented shoutout became the earned result the inventory lead supplied. Routing is not a Toma status failure while the API key is elsewhere. One conduct event is not coaching, and Toma's own course request gets protected time.

## Drill 2 — only a future draft arrived

Packet: report is **Selin Marek, traffic scheduler**. The manager writes only:

1. "Where is the airport rota?"
2. "Check whether Selin's workload is too much."
3. "She asked about standby pay."
4. "Career check if there is time."

No action log, figures or messages arrive.

Clearances:

- Thin is still this window's traffic. Open `# Strip board:` and state what was not supplied.
- The rota and workload items are **Holding**: precise questions plus the records to pull.
- Standby pay is **Diverted** to the pay owner; no pay argument is drafted.
- The manager-created career item is an invitation, not an assigned goal.

Useful strips:

**1. Airport rota — holding · ~8 min**
`filed: "where is the airport rota?" → holding: no task list or dated update.`
"Which part of the rota is drafted, and is anything waiting on crew availability or my approval?" Listen for the dependency the manager can move.

**2. Workload — holding · ~7 min**
`filed: "check whether Selin's workload is too much" → holding: no missed commitment or capacity signal.`
"What is competing with the airport rota this week, and should anything move?" Accept a manageable answer; do not diagnose overload from the manager's worry.

**3. Standby pay — diverted · ~2 min**
`filed: "she asked about standby pay" → diverted: compensation belongs to the pay process.`
Book the pay conversation with the compensation owner; draft no amount or case here.

**4. Career goals — invitation · ~6 min**
`filed: "career goals, if there is time" → holding: Selin supplied no direction topic.`
"Is there a part of the scheduling or rota work you want more of, or should we leave this until you have a specific direction?"

**Pre-flight**
- Nothing is owed. Pull the rota task list or sprint board, the crew calendar behind the workload question, and any topic Selin has sent for the career check.
- Book the standby-pay route with the compensation owner.

**Diversions**
- Standby pay: route to the compensation owner and book the pay conversation; draft no amount or case here.

## Drill 3 — the record is a finished meeting

The manager asks to prepare next week's 1-on-1 for **Ana Sorel, roster lead** from notes titled "Ana Sorel 1-on-1 — 9 October, attendees: manager, Ana." The notes record that the manager owes a revised handover template by 13 October and a holiday-cover decision by 11 October; Ana owes a late-shift rota by 14 October. They also mention a family matter and possible leave.

The reply:

# Ana Sorel, Roster Lead: not a 1-on-1 prep request

**Redirect.** The paste is the record of the 9 October meeting already held; the attendees, discussed items and agreed actions identify it. A board from it would replay old positions or invent current ones.

**What it supports:** a follow-up on its actions:
- Manager: revised handover template — due 13 October.
- Manager: holiday-cover decision — due 11 October.
- Ana: late-shift rota — due 14 October.

Their current state is not supplied.

**What the next board needs:** the present state of those actions, this week's messages or figures, any new topic Ana has raised, and a fresh draft agenda.

**Private matter:** the family matter stays in a private conversation with the appropriate support route; no talking points are drafted here.

## Drill 4 — the queue belongs to the manager

Packet: report is **Yara Venn, senior permit reviewer**. The draft says "give Yara feedback on the approval bottleneck", "status on the map-portal pilot" and "Yara seems disengaged lately." The conditions: Yara is the sole reviewer named by the cross-office process. Seven files assigned to her are holding four offices. On Monday she flagged the queue and proposed a three-reviewer rotation. On Wednesday she wrote that she would take the proposal to the office heads on Friday if no decision came; there is no later record that she did. The map-portal pilot waits on a security extract requested eight days ago. The manager's overdue row is to publish the rotation rules. Yara also shipped two office guides this week.

Clearances:

- The bottleneck is **re-routed to a manager unblock**. The sole-reviewer design holds the four offices; Yara is not the thing blocking them. Back her rotation proposal, take it to the office heads, or set the decision date. Write `the assigned queue is holding four offices`, never `Yara is blocking four offices`.
- Friday's escalation is **planned, not confirmed**. Ask whether it happened and what decision it produced; never write that she escalated.
- The portal pilot is another manager-moving hold: chase the security extract and get a date.
- The overdue rotation-rules row belongs in **Pre-flight** before any ask reaches Yara.
- "Seems disengaged" is **Grounded**: no dated conduct, and the record shows two shipped guides plus a raised process fix.

Useful strips:

**1. Cross-office permit queue — manager unblock · ~10 min**
`filed: "give Yara feedback on the approval bottleneck" → re-routed: the sole-reviewer design is holding four offices.`
Yara already proposed a three-reviewer rotation. Approve it or name what must change, then ask: "Did the office-head conversation happen Friday, and what decision does it leave for me?"

**2. Map-portal pilot — manager unblock · ~6 min**
`filed: "status on the map-portal pilot" → re-routed: security has held the extract for eight days.`
Give the escalation path or the date the manager will get. This is not a Yara delivery failure.

**3. Disengagement — grounded · ~1 min**
`filed: "Yara seems disengaged lately" → grounded: no dated conduct; the record shows two shipped guides and a raised fix.`
Name the missing evidence and move on.

## Drill 5 — coach the earlier flag, not the found cause

Packet: report is **Nico Ferran, delivery analyst**. The draft asks for coaching because Nico's forecast missed again. The conditions: this sprint's work was planned at 18 tasks and delivered 12 after a supplier feed changed scope; Nico's own Tuesday note diagnosed that the scope change added six unplanned tasks. The manager's notes also name last sprint's planned-versus-actual miss: 14 planned, 10 delivered. Nico told the team Tuesday that he should have flagged the scope change before planning closed.

Clearance:

- This can be a development item because there are two dated occasions, but the report already found the cause. Do not coach "your forecast missed again" as if Nico had not diagnosed it.
- Credit the diagnosis and coach the earlier lever: "When the supplier feed changed scope, what would have triggered you to flag it before planning closed, and who needed that signal?"

Useful strip:

**2. Estimate pattern — development · ~8 min**
`filed: "coaching because the forecast missed again" → cleared: two dated misses; Nico already diagnosed the scope-change cause.`
"Nico found the cause, so do not repeat the miss. Ask what scope signal should have reached the manager before planning closed and who needed it."

One filed coaching line receives this one development strip; the current and prior sprint evidence folds into the same clearance rather than becoming two strips.

## Boundaries that change the answer

- If the packet later records that Yara's Friday escalation happened, the board may state it; until then it is a plan.
- If a sole reviewer is merely behind on a normal personal queue, the question is different; the manager-owned call comes from an assigned design that several teams cannot clear.
- If Ana's note had been a draft for next week rather than minutes, it would receive a board even without records.
- If Nico had only this sprint's miss, it would be a one-time note; the named prior sprint supplies the second dated occasion.
- If a report has not raised a direction topic, a manager-created career line stays a modest invitation rather than protected promotion preparation.
