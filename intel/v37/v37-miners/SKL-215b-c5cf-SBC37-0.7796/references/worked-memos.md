# Worked memos

Two invented deals written out in full, then two excerpts. They show the form, the size of each section and how the reasoning is carried. Every name, figure and finding here is invented for illustration and belongs to these deals only: none of it may appear in a memo for another target.

---

## Deal one: a blocked deal (commercial linen rental)

### What the deal team sent

The risk findings, as received:

| Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Wastewater discharge permit for the main plant is in renewal review after two exceedance notices; the plant cannot run without it | Must-Address | Resolved |
| Hospital group contract (30% of revenue) goes to re-tender in 9 months | Must-Address | In-Progress |
| A former software contractor claims ownership of the route-scheduling system both plants run on and has threatened to seek a court order stopping its use | Manageable | Unresolved |
| Plant manager has run both sites for 20 years; no deputy | Manageable | |
| Fuel-surcharge dispute with two hotel customers is still being invoiced under protest | Manageable | Resolved |
| Reported growth of 10% includes a one-off flood-recovery contract | Informational | Unresolved |

Financial narrative, in brief: LTM revenue $40M, of which $4M is a one-off flood-recovery contract; the other $36M grew 5%; gross margin 30%; reported EBITDA $4M; the seller shows "adjusted EBITDA" of $7M after adding back $1M of storm repairs and $2M of "route expansion investment"; the regulator has extended the permit review by six months and the seller's counsel calls the matter dealt with; the narrative does not mention the surcharge dispute; asking price $70M enterprise value.

Comparables: Alder Textile Services 1.5x revenue, 9x EBITDA, growth 8%, margin 33%. Birch Linen 1.2x, 8x, 6%, 29%. Cedar Workwear Rental 1.8x, 11x, 10%, 35%. Dunmore Laundries 1.0x, 7x, 3%, 28%. Eastgate Linen (bought by a sponsor 18 months ago, listed in the banker's book) 2.6x, 15x, 12%, 36%. FreshFold (app-based on-demand laundry) 3.0x, NM, 40%, 50%.

### The memo

## Harrow & Finch Linen Services (commercial linen rental): IC memo

Prepared from the risk classification table, the financial narrative and the comparables dataset supplied by the deal team.

### 1. Deal Thesis: Harrow & Finch

Harrow & Finch brings $36.0M of linen-rental revenue from hotels and clinics, growing 5%, with route density as the value driver; the $40.0M headline also counts a one-off flood-recovery contract. The seller asks $70.0M. Primary risk: R1, the main plant's wastewater permit, recorded Resolved although the regulator has only extended its review; without the permit the plant behind most routes cannot run. With the permit contested and the hospital re-tender still to be run, the table leads to Request More Data, not to a Proceed vote.

### 2. Risk Table: 6 findings, primary risk R1

| ID | Finding | Tier | Status | Reading | Deal impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Wastewater permit for the main plant in renewal review after two exceedance notices | Must-Address | Resolved | Contested: the narrative says the review was extended, not ended | The plant cannot operate without it; the thesis fails if renewal is refused |
| R2 | Hospital group contract, 30% of revenue, re-tender in 9 months | Must-Address | In-Progress | Open: the re-tender has not been run | Nearly a third of revenue rests on one award |
| R3 | Former contractor's ownership claim over the route-scheduling system, court order threatened | Manageable | Unresolved | Open: claim made, nothing filed or settled | Both plants schedule on that system |
| R4 | Plant manager runs both sites, no deputy | Manageable | not recorded | Open: missing status read as unresolved | Operating continuity through a handover |
| R5 | Fuel-surcharge dispute with two hotel customers | Manageable | Resolved | Closed: taken as recorded; nothing outside the table speaks to it | None |
| R6 | 10% reported growth includes the one-off flood contract | Informational | Unresolved | Open: carried into the valuation base | Base and growth restated without the one-off |

Flagged observations:
- R1, status against the record: the risk table records Resolved; the financial narrative says the regulator only extended the review by six months. Tier unchanged; counted as open.
- R3, tier against the standard definitions: a court order stopping the system both plants run on reads as must-address; it is tiered Manageable. Tier left as classified; the risk analyst is asked to confirm.
- R4, missing status: the table gives none, so it is read as unresolved.

### 3. Valuation Range: EV/Revenue on $36.0M

| Comparable | Growth | Margin | EV/Revenue | In/Out | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FreshFold | 40% | 50% | 3.0x | Out | App-based on-demand model with no EBITDA: stands clear of the target and of the set |
| Eastgate Linen | 12% | 36% | 2.6x | Out | A sponsor's purchase price from 18 months ago out of the banker's book: a control premium, not a trading multiple |
| Cedar Workwear Rental | 10% | 35% | 1.8x | In | Same route-based rental model; top of the trading set |
| Alder Textile Services | 8% | 33% | 1.5x | In | Same model |
| Birch Linen | 6% | 29% | 1.2x | In | Same model; nearest above the target |
| Harrow & Finch (target) | 5% | 30% | - | - | Base without the one-off contract |
| Dunmore Laundries | 3% | 28% | 1.0x | In | Same model; nearest below the target |

Base: $36.0M (40.0 - 4.0), the headline less the one-off flood contract, which does not repeat; nothing else comes out. Multiple: EV/Revenue. The seller's adjusted EBITDA of $7.0M is set aside: it adds back operating spend ("route expansion investment") and an unevidenced storm repair. Growth: at 5% the target sits between Birch Linen (6%) and Dunmore Laundries (3%), in the lower half of the included set, below its median. Margin: its 30% is a little richer than both neighbours, which points to the upper end of the range and no further. Size: not in the dataset, no adjustment taken. Range: 1.0x x 36.0 = $36.0M to 1.2x x 36.0 = $43.2M. The ask of $70.0M is 1.94x the base (70.0 / 36.0), above the high end of the range.

### 4. Recommendation: Request More Data

The table leads to **Request More Data**: R1 is contested and R2 is open, and either one blocks Proceed while it stays so. Both can be closed by things that can be asked for or awaited: the regulator's renewal decision and the result of the re-tender. The R3 tier question goes back to the risk analyst in the same round. The ask stands well above the high end of the range, a second reason to wait.

### 5. Conditions and Next Steps: R1 to R6

- R1: environmental counsel obtains the regulator's written renewal decision, or its extension letter with the conditions attached, before any offer letter is drafted.
- R2: the deal lead asks the seller for the hospital group's tender timetable and the bid submitted; the committee re-votes after the award or a signed extension.
- R3: IP counsel requests the contractor's engagement terms and claim letter; the risk analyst confirms the tier before the committee meets again.
- R4: HR diligence requests the plant manager's contract, notice period and any deputy plan; the risk analyst completes the status column.
- R5: no further diligence; legal puts the settlement or credit note behind Resolved on file.
- R6: finance diligence requests three years of revenue by contract to verify the $36.0M base before the range is relied on.

## Deal two: a deal that proceeds (marine coatings)

### What the deal team sent

The risk findings, as received:

| Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Biocide registration for the flagship antifouling product was up for its five-year renewal | Must-Address | Resolved |
| Supply agreement with the largest shipyard group (25% of sales) can be ended by the shipyard on six months' notice | Must-Address | Resolved |
| Solvent storage tanks need a containment upgrade, quoted at $1M; the seller has undertaken in writing to fund it out of the proceeds | Manageable | Unresolved |
| A customer's warranty claim over blistered coating on two hulls; the product-liability insurer has accepted cover | Manageable | In-Progress |
| Copper oxide input price has swung 20% within a year | Informational | Unresolved |

Financial narrative, in brief: revenue $50M growing 12%; gross margin 40%; EBITDA $8M; the registration renewal certificate was issued last quarter and is in the data room; the narrative says nothing more about the shipyard agreement; the seller has not named a price.

Comparables: Pelham Paints 1.6x revenue, 10x EBITDA, growth 10%, margin 38%. Quay Coatings 2.0x, 12x, 14%, 42%. Rudder & Co 1.4x, 9x, 8%, 37%. Solent Finishes 2.2x, 13x, 16%, 44%. Analyst's note: no exclusions flagged; the split between product sales and application services is not given for any comparable, so margins may not be like-for-like.

### The memo

## Calder Marine Coatings (antifouling hull paint): IC memo

Sources: the deal team's risk table, financial narrative and comparables dataset.

### 1. Deal Thesis: Calder Marine Coatings

Calder sells antifouling hull coatings to shipyards and fleet owners: $50.0M of revenue growing 12% with $8.0M of EBITDA, resting on a registered flagship product and its position with the largest shipyard group. The seller has named no price. Primary risk: R4, a warranty claim over blistered coating on two hulls, still in progress though insured. Both must-address findings are recorded Resolved, nothing outside the table reopens them, and each open manageable finding has a mitigation named in the papers, so the table leads to Proceed.

### 2. Risk Table: 5 findings, primary risk R4

| ID | Finding | Tier | Status | Reading | Deal impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Biocide registration for the flagship product, five-year renewal | Must-Address | Resolved | Closed: the narrative confirms the certificate was issued | Flagship sales secured for the renewal term |
| R2 | Supply agreement with the largest shipyard group, 25% of sales, six months' notice | Must-Address | Resolved | Closed: taken as recorded; nothing outside the table speaks to it | A quarter of sales stays under contract |
| R3 | Solvent tank containment upgrade, $1M quoted, seller to fund from proceeds | Manageable | Unresolved | Open: work not done; mitigation named in the finding | Bounded cost carried by the seller |
| R4 | Warranty claim over blistered coating on two hulls, insurer has accepted cover | Manageable | In-Progress | Open: claim not settled; mitigation named in the finding | Cost falls on the insurer if cover holds |
| R5 | Copper oxide price swung 20% in a year | Informational | Unresolved | Open: context for margin | Margin sensitivity, no action alone |

Flagged observations: none; the artifacts agree on tier and status.

### 3. Valuation Range: EV/Revenue on $50.0M

| Comparable | Growth | Margin | EV/Revenue | In/Out | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solent Finishes | 16% | 44% | 2.2x | In | Fastest grower, but no gap to the next comparable |
| Quay Coatings | 14% | 42% | 2.0x | In | Trading multiple, same business; nearest above the target |
| Calder (target) | 12% | 40% | - | - | Reported revenue |
| Pelham Paints | 10% | 38% | 1.6x | In | Trading multiple, same business; nearest below the target |
| Rudder & Co | 8% | 37% | 1.4x | In | Slowest grower, still the same business |

None excluded: each was tested, none is a transaction price and none stands clear of the set. Base: reported revenue of $50.0M; the narrative names nothing one-time, non-transferable or lost. Multiple: EV/Revenue, the basis on which the base and the growth comparison are stated. Growth: at 12% the target sits between Quay Coatings (14%) and Pelham Paints (10%), in the middle of the included set, at its median. Margin: no adjustment taken, because the split between product sales and application services is not given for the comparables; that breakdown is requested. Size: not in the dataset, no adjustment taken. Range: 1.6x x 50.0 = $80.0M to 2.0x x 50.0 = $100.0M. No asking price is stated in the input, so none is compared.

### 4. Recommendation: Proceed

The table leads to **Proceed**, because both halves of the test are met. R1 and R2, the must-address findings, are closed: R1 on its certificate, R2 as recorded, with its closing document made a condition precedent. The open manageable rows each carry a mitigation the papers name: the seller's written undertaking on R3 and the insurer's acceptance of cover on R4. An offer belongs inside $80.0M to $100.0M.

### 5. Conditions and Next Steps: R1 to R5

- R1: regulatory counsel places the renewal certificate in the closing set; condition precedent to signing.
- R2: legal puts on file the waiver or amended notice term behind Resolved and confirms it survives a change of control; condition precedent to signing.
- R3: the deal lead writes the seller's undertaking into the purchase agreement as a $1M retention released when the upgrade is certified.
- R4: insurance counsel obtains the insurer's written acceptance of cover and the policy limit; the purchase agreement carries an indemnity for any excess.
- R5: the commercial lead writes a copper oxide price review into the first-year plan; not a closing condition.

## Excerpt: when the verdict is Pass (cable-lift operator)

The only must-address finding: the lift concession, which carries all revenue, cannot be assigned without the municipality's consent, and the council has voted and written that it will not consent to any assignment and will re-tender at expiry in two years. Status: Unresolved. The memo keeps all five sections; these two change character.

### 4. Recommendation: Pass

The table leads to **Pass**: R1 is open and no request can close it, since the council's refusal is final for any buyer on the input's own words and nothing either side can deliver before signing cures it. The valuation range in section 3 is given for the record only.

### 5. Conditions and Next Steps: R1 to R2

- R1: the file reopens only if the municipality issues a written consent to assignment, or a concession extension that names the buyer.
- R2: the snow-making equipment lease falls away with R1; it would be re-priced if the file reopens.

## What carries over, and what does not

The five headings, the column sets, the three verdict words, the bounds on each section and the habit of showing each sum once carry over. The deals do not: a memo for another target is built only from that target's three artifacts, with its own findings in its own rows and its own sentences.
