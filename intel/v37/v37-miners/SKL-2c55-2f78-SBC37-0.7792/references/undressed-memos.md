# Section order, and memos with the dressing taken off

The order below holds for every memo. Then come three complete memos from trades nothing like the ones you will see — one declined at the ask, one proceeding, and one deferred where no ask is given and the input flags its own misfiled status. Lift the method and the arithmetic; leave the facts where they are.

## Section order

Every memo I write has five numbered sections in this order, each inside its bound, and nothing else counts as a section.

- `# <Target> · committee memo · <the call>` as the first line, nothing above it — the title carries the call and is not a section.
- `### 1. Deal thesis` — 70 to 120 words: what the business does and earns, why it is worth having with a figure, what the price depends on, and its main risk by name; the same risk is in the risk table.
- `### 2. Risk table` — all items, stop tier first: `Item | Tier | Recorded / read | Consequence | Closing act`; under it, at most 80 words on any status that did not hold and any item that quietly causes another, or a sentence that every status held.
- `### 3. Valuation range` — 120 to 250 words: the base and why it is smaller than the headline where it is; the set kept and dropped; the stated adjustment and the multiple it gives; the working; the ask against the result; the price at which I would go ahead where the ask is higher; a case without any slice an outstanding item puts at risk; the equity value where cash and debt are given.
- `### 4. Recommendation` — 60 to 130 words: the call, what settled it, the price, and what would move it.
- `### 5. Conditions` — a line per condition or request, each tied to its item with an owner and a step, about 100 words in all. After a defer or decline, the lines are what would reopen the call.

Most land between 450 and 700 words with the table; a clean proceed runs shorter. The valuation gets the space.

## Memo one — Glenrannoch Mountain Resort (declined at the ask)

**The package, condensed.** A ski and summer mountain resort. Table, tiers Red / Amber / Green: the resort occupies public forest land under a permit from the state forestry agency, whose renewal application is filed while the agency's environmental review has been extended by 120 days — Red, Resolved. The water-abstraction licence that feeds the snowmaking system is awaiting a hearing — Red, Unresolved. A thirty-year major overhaul on the main chairlift, estimated at $3.8M and not in the budget — Amber, Unresolved. The ticketing system upgrade, finished in the spring — Green, Resolved. Narrative: revenue $41.0M including a $3.6M insurance recovery for a storm-damaged lift; EBITDA $8.9M including that recovery; season-pass presales up 8%; ask $60M. Comparables on EV/EBITDA: Alpine Holdings 10.5x; Summit Peaks 9.0x; Powderline 9.5x; Ridgecrest Resorts 8.0x; Crown Mountain 15.0x, bought last year by a destination-resort group at a stated premium.

# Glenrannoch Mountain Resort · committee memo · decline at $60M

### 1. Deal thesis

Glenrannoch is a ski and summer mountain resort on scarce terrain, and a two-season resort has a franchise new entrants cannot easily copy; season-pass presales up 8% show demand holding. Once a one-off insurance recovery is set aside, it earns $5.3M of EBITDA on $37.4M of recurring revenue, and everything in the price depends on the resort operating where it stands. Its main risk is tenure: the resort sits on public forest land under a permit whose renewal the agency has put under extended review rather than granted.

### 2. Risk table

| Item | Tier | Recorded / read | Consequence | Closing act |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forestry permit for the resort land | Red | Resolved / outstanding | No resort without the land | Agency's renewal notice |
| Snowmaking water-abstraction licence | Red | Unresolved / outstanding | Seasons shorten without snowmaking | Licence granted at hearing |
| Main chairlift major overhaul, $3.8M unbudgeted | Amber | Unresolved / outstanding | Capital call soon after completion | Funded overhaul plan or price reduction |
| Ticketing system upgrade | Green | Resolved / resolved | None | Complete |

*Statuses that did not hold.* Forestry permit — recorded Resolved; the narrative shows the renewal application filed and the environmental review extended by 120 days. Read as outstanding: a filed application and an extended review are both short of a renewal. Made true by the agency's renewal notice. Water-abstraction licence — recorded Unresolved, which the narrative supports. The chairlift and ticketing entries are recorded accurately.

### 3. Valuation range

*What is on the table.* A mountain resort; ask $60M. Revenue of $41.0M includes a $3.6M insurance recovery, leaving recurring revenue of $37.4M. The recovery was profit almost entirely, so recurring EBITDA is $5.3M, not $8.9M, and the memo prices on $5.3M.

Resorts are capital-heavy and valued on earnings. Kept: Alpine Holdings 10.5x, Summit Peaks 9.0x, Powderline 9.5x, Ridgecrest Resorts 8.0x. Dropped: Crown Mountain at 15.0x — sold in a completed deal at a stated premium to a destination-resort group, a ceiling and not a trading level.

The kept range is 8.0x to 10.5x, median 9.25x. The set gives no growth figures, so no comparable is nearest and the median is the anchor. On recurring EBITDA of $5.3M: 8.0 × $5.3M = $42.4M; 9.25 × $5.3M = $49.025M; 10.5 × $5.3M = $55.65M. The $60M ask is 11.32x recurring EBITDA — $4.35M above the top of the range and $10.975M, or 22.4%, above the median. Against the headline $8.9M the same ask reads 6.74x and looks cheap; the insurance recovery is doing that work. The unbudgeted $3.8M overhaul would come out of any price agreed, which pushes the defensible figure lower still.

### 4. Recommendation

Decline at $60M. Once the one-off insurance recovery leaves earnings, the ask is 11.32x recurring EBITDA, above the dearest kept comparable at 10.5x, so no price near it survives. The forestry permit — the land the whole resort stands on — is recorded as resolved because an application is filed, but the agency has extended its review and decided nothing, and the snowmaking water licence is unresolved besides. With both granted, the resort is worth revisiting near the median value.

*What would move the call.* A renewed permit, a granted licence and a seller's number near $49M, less the $3.8M overhaul, would reopen this as a proceed subject to conditions. A refused permit ends it.

### 5. Conditions

Nothing gates signing at $60M; these are what would reopen the call.
- Renewal notice from the forestry agency for the resort land — deal counsel.
- Water-abstraction licence granted at the hearing — regulatory lead.
- A seller's number near $49M, with the chairlift overhaul costed and funded or the $3.8M taken off — operations lead.

## Memo two — Thornbury Precast (proceeds)

**The package, condensed.** A maker of precast concrete products for civil-engineering contractors. Table, tiers Critical / Moderate / Low, nothing recorded Critical: the cement supply contract renewing in eight months on terms not yet agreed — Moderate, Unresolved; planning consent for a yard extension — Moderate, Resolved, and the narrative confirms the consent was granted last month; a gantry-crane inspection due next quarter — Low, In-Progress. Narrative: revenue $29.0M growing 6%, gross margin 31%, EBITDA $4.6M; no customer above 11%; ask $36M. Comparables on EV/EBITDA, with growth: Granitecast 8.0x, 5%; StoneForm 7.5x, 4%; Ashlar Products 8.5x, 7%; Keystone Precast 7.0x, 3%; Monolith Group 12.0x, a diversified building-materials major many times Thornbury's size.

# Thornbury Precast · committee memo · proceed

### 1. Deal thesis

Thornbury makes precast concrete products for civil-engineering contractors in a heavy, regional trade where delivery distance protects incumbents, earning a 15.9% EBITDA margin on $29.0M of revenue growing 6%. Its customers are spread thin, with nobody above 11%, and a yard consent already granted gives it room to expand. What the valuation needs is for that margin to hold. Its main risk is input cost: the cement contract renews in eight months, and cement drives most of the cost of every unit.

### 2. Risk table

| Item | Tier | Recorded / read | Consequence | Closing act |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cement supply contract renewal | Moderate | Unresolved / outstanding | Margin pressure if price rises | Renewal at or near current terms |
| Planning consent for yard extension | Moderate | Resolved / resolved | Capacity growth available | Granted |
| Gantry-crane inspection | Low | In-Progress / in progress | Routine | Inspection passed |

*Statuses that did not hold.* None. Each status was checked against the narrative, and the yard consent is granted, not merely applied for.

### 3. Valuation range

*What is on the table.* Precast concrete manufacture; ask $36M. Revenue of $29.0M and EBITDA of $4.6M are priced as reported, since nothing in the narrative is one-off, restated or tied to the present owner.

A heavy manufacturer is valued on earnings. Kept: Granitecast 8.0x, StoneForm 7.5x, Ashlar Products 8.5x, Keystone Precast 7.0x. Dropped: Monolith Group at 12.0x — a diversified major whose multiple prices a product range and scale Thornbury does not have.

The kept range is 7.0x to 8.5x. Thornbury grows 6%, a point from both Granitecast at 5% and Ashlar Products at 7%; on a tie I take the lower multiple, so Granitecast's 8.0x is the anchor. On $4.6M: 7.0 × $4.6M = $32.2M; 8.0 × $4.6M = $36.8M; 8.5 × $4.6M = $39.1M. The $36M ask is 7.83x EBITDA, $0.8M or 2.2% below the anchor value.

### 4. Recommendation

Proceed. Nothing reaches the Critical tier, the one Resolved status is supported by a consent actually granted, and the $36M ask sits 2.2% below an anchored value of $36.8M. The cement contract is the one item to manage after completion.

*What would move the call.* A cement renewal well above current price would squeeze the margin the valuation rests on, and would be the reason to revisit.

### 5. Conditions

Nothing gates signing. After completion: the cement renewal terms — procurement lead, before the contract renews in eight months.

## Memo three — Tidewater Ferries (deferred; no ask stated)

**The package, condensed.** A short-sea ferry operator with five vessels. Table, tiers Red / Amber / Green: the berth concession at the main terminal, which carries 55% of sailings, is being re-tendered by the port authority with bids closing in four months — Red, Resolved. Safety certificates for two of the five ferries are self-certified by the company's own marine superintendent pending a classification-society survey — Red, Resolved. One vessel's engine overhaul, $2.6M, is unbudgeted — Amber, Unresolved. The senior master who holds the harbour pilotage exemption retires in eight months with no successor qualified — Amber, Unresolved. Appended to the narrative is a note from its author: the risk analyst filed the concession resolved because the company holds the current concession, but the re-tender is live; the note does not re-tier it and asks the memo author to surface it. Narrative: revenue $24.0M growing 4%; EBITDA $4.4M, of which the main-terminal sailings contribute $2.9M; cash $1.2M; debt $6.0M; the seller has invited indicative offers and names no price. Comparables on EV/EBITDA, with growth: Channel Link 7.5x, 5%; Harbour Lines 6.5x, 3%; Isle Crossings 7.0x, 4%; SeaBridge Ferries 8.0x, 6%; Coastal Leisure Cruises 12.0x, 11%. A line under the table says no exclusions were flagged by the analyst who assembled it.

# Tidewater Ferries · committee memo · defer

### 1. Deal thesis

Tidewater runs five short-sea ferries, and routes like these are natural near-monopolies once the berths are held: a rival needs a berth before it needs a boat. The business earns $4.4M of EBITDA on $24.0M of revenue and grows steadily at 4%, and its worth to a buyer rests on keeping those berths. The main risk is the berth itself: 55% of sailings and $2.9M of the $4.4M EBITDA depend on the main-terminal concession, which the port authority is re-tendering.

### 2. Risk table

| Item | Tier | Recorded / read | Consequence | Closing act |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main-terminal berth concession under re-tender | Red | Resolved / outstanding | $2.9M of $4.4M EBITDA rests on it | Port authority's award |
| Two ferries' safety certificates self-certified | Red | Resolved / outstanding | Two vessels cannot sail if the survey fails | Classification-society survey certificates |
| Engine overhaul, $2.6M unbudgeted | Amber | Unresolved / outstanding | Capital call after completion | Funded plan, or the cost off the price |
| Senior master with the pilotage exemption retiring | Amber | Unresolved / outstanding | Harbour approach needs a qualified master | Second master qualified for the exemption |

*Statuses that did not hold.* Main-terminal concession — recorded Resolved; the narrative and its author's note show a live re-tender. Read as outstanding; made true by the port authority's award. Vessel safety certificates — recorded Resolved; the narrative shows self-certification by the company's own superintendent pending the classification-society survey. Read as outstanding: a self-certificate is not the survey. Made true by the survey certificates. The overhaul and the master's retirement are recorded accurately.

### 3. Valuation range

*What is on the table.* Five ferries; no asking price. Revenue of $24.0M and EBITDA of $4.4M are priced as reported. At the $28.2M the memo supports, equity value is $28.2M less $6.0M of debt plus $1.2M of cash, or $23.4M.

A capital-heavy operator is valued on earnings. Kept: Channel Link 7.5x, Harbour Lines 6.5x, Isle Crossings 7.0x, SeaBridge Ferries 8.0x. Dropped: Coastal Leisure Cruises at 12.0x — a leisure cruise line growing 11%, a different business. The note that no exclusions were flagged describes the analyst's work, not this set.

The kept median is 7.25x, but Tidewater grows 4%, exactly Isle Crossings' rate, so Isle Crossings' 7.0x is the anchor. On $4.4M: 6.5 × $4.4M = $28.6M; 7.0 × $4.4M = $30.8M; 8.0 × $4.4M = $35.2M. Less the $2.6M unbudgeted overhaul, $30.8M becomes $28.2M, the figure the memo supports. Without the main-terminal concession, EBITDA falls to $1.5M and 7.0 × $1.5M = $10.5M — which is why the tender decides the deal.

### 4. Recommendation

Defer. The main-terminal concession — two-thirds of the earnings — is filed resolved, but the port authority is re-tendering it and the note from the narrative's author confirms the filing was a mistake. A tender outcome is a process nobody can hurry, so no condition closes it. Once the concession is won, the memo would support an enterprise value of up to $28.2M.

*What would move the call.* Winning the tender turns this into a proceed-subject-to-conditions at up to $28.2M. Losing it ends the deal: a $10.5M business is not the one on offer.

### 5. Conditions

- The port authority's award of the main-terminal concession — deal counsel; until it is in hand the call stays defer.
- Classification-society survey certificates for the two self-certified ferries — operations lead, as a condition to signing.
- A second master qualified for the pilotage exemption — operations lead, before completion.

Now write the memo, showing what the moves found and not the moves. Its first line is the title line; put nothing before it and nothing after the conditions.
