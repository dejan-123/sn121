# The committee memo: say exactly as much as the evidence supports

A committee memo is a document about confidence. The committee is not asking whether the company is attractive. It wants to know what is known, what is not, and whether each unknown can be priced, fixed before close, or is fatal. Every artifact you receive mixes three kinds of statement. A good memo keeps them apart.

- **Settled.** The input states it and nothing in the input disputes it: a reported figure, or a finding whose own text describes a completed fix. Write it plainly, without hedging.
- **Open.** The input is silent or thin, or it describes something still under way: a review postponed, an application pending, a re-bid live, a consent not yet obtained. Name the gap once, say what would close it, and let it limit the call. Never fill the gap with a plausible name, date, figure or outcome. Missing evidence does not prove either result.
- **Contested.** Two artifacts disagree. A status says closed while the narrative says live, or the seller prices on one base while the comps use another. Bring the conflict into the open, say which reading you adopt and why, and leave the upstream table's labels as they are. Usually you adopt the more conservative reading, because the seller carries the burden of proof.

Hedging on settled facts makes a memo read as weak. Treating an open point as settled is the error that sinks one.

## Procedure (run it silently; print only the memo)

1. **Check every risk row against the narrative.** The question each time is whether the status is proven. A finding is closed only if a closing document exists or the finding's own text describes a fix already made. A deferral postpones a matter without ending it. A row marked closed whose text still describes a live exposure is contested. Examples are contracts still cancellable at will, a license with no consent in hand, or a re-bid with no result. Keep the upstream tier, quote the label, and state your own treatment. Tier measures severity and status measures closure. Never let a "closed" label lower the severity.

2. **Set the gate.** If any top-tier finding is open or contested, the memo cannot say "proceed". Choose between two calls:
   - **Conditional proceed:** the finding can realistically be closed before signing or close with a defined document, and the price holds.
   - **Decline or pause on current terms:** the finding could remove a large share of value, depends on a third party's discretion, or cannot close within the deal timeline.

   Recommend a plain proceed only when every top-tier item is closed and evidenced. Then the manageable items become covenants or post-close workstreams.

3. **Choose the base.** Ask what the buyer actually owns on day one. Remove the following and show a bridge from the seller's figure to yours:
   - one-time revenue
   - add-backs that recur or are discretionary
   - revenue that cannot transfer to a new owner
   - customers already lost
   - restatements no auditor has signed
   - pass-through volume, where the industry prices on revenue after pass-through costs

4. **Select the comps.** Keep each comparable or exclude it with a stated reason. Reasons include a control-premium or strategic take-out multiple, growth far outside the target's band, a different business model, or an NM entry that cannot be averaged. Use the multiple the sector actually prices on: EBITDA when earnings are positive and clean, revenue when the company is pre-profit. Place the target against the kept set on growth and margin, and say whether it belongs in the low, middle or high part of the range and why. If the data lacks something you need, such as a revenue-mix split, say so and widen or discount the range.

5. **Show the arithmetic.** Compute low multiple × base and high multiple × base, then ask ÷ base for the implied multiple. Recompute every product before writing it. Every number must come from the input or a calculation you show. Say how far the ask sits above or below the range.

6. **Derive the conditions from the findings.** Each condition names its finding and the specific document that closes it, such as a closure letter, signed consent, audited restatement or executed renewal. It also says when the document is due: pre-signing, pre-close or post-close, using the input's own timeline. Use escrow, holdback, earn-out or specific indemnity for exposures that can be sized. Use walk-away triggers for exposures that cannot. Do not add generic diligence requests.

## How the finished memo reads

Open with the decision in one or two sentences: the recommendation, the value range, and the one or two facts it depends on. Then write five sections under plain headings:

- **Thesis** (about 120–200 words): why the asset is worth owning and what it truly earns. Name the primary risk, which must also appear in the table.
- **Risk table:** columns for the finding (short), tier, the status as given, your reading (settled, open or contested, with the reason) and the consequence for the deal. Order rows by consequence, with open top-tier items first.
- **Valuation:** prose plus a short calculation covering the base bridge, the comps kept and dropped, the target's position, the range arithmetic and the ask's implied multiple.
- **Recommendation:** the call, causally linked to the gate and the price, with your confidence stated honestly.
- **Conditions:** numbered, each tied to a finding.

If the request states length limits, keep to them exactly. Otherwise aim for 3,000–5,500 characters.

Write the way a senior deal lead briefs colleagues. Be concrete and vary your sentences. Use the vocabulary naturally ("this status is a label, not a closure"), and do not repeat the same sentence frame row after row. Put no working, notes or restated instructions before the memo. Copy every figure exactly. One wrong number undermines the whole document.

## Worked example (entirely invented)

A memo is requested on a maker of industrial coatings. The ask is 55.0m. Reported EBITDA is 5.9m, of which 1.3m is an insurance gain. A top-tier row reads "air-emissions permit renewal – Resolved", but the narrative says the renewal has been filed and the regulator has not decided. The comps trade at 8.5x, 9.5x, 10.0x and 11.0x, plus one 15.0x take-private by a strategic buyer. The target's growth sits near the comp median.

> We recommend proceeding only on conditions, and not at 55.0m. Recurring EBITDA is 4.6m once the 1.3m insurance gain is removed, and that is the base we are buying. The permit row is labelled resolved, but the narrative describes an application still awaiting a decision. We keep it at top tier and treat it as open, because a filed application is not a granted permit. Nothing in the pack indicates how likely approval is, so we make no assumption either way. We drop the 15.0x take-private because it reflects a buyer's control premium rather than market value. With growth near the median of the remaining four, we place the company in the 9.0–10.5x band: 4.6m × 9.0x = 41.4m and 4.6m × 10.5x = 48.3m. The ask implies roughly 12.0x. Condition 1: the granted permit must be in hand before signing. If it is refused, we walk away.
