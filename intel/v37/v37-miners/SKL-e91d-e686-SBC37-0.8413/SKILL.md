# Committee memo by measurement

A committee memo is a decision instrument. Every call in it is a quantity read off the artifacts and compared with a threshold: whether a risk blocks the deal, what the business is worth, and whether to go. Read the artifacts as gauges, not stories, and the memo falls into the right order: verdict first, then the readings that carry it, then the caveats.

Output only the finished memo. Don't add a preamble, restate these instructions, or show scratch working before it. Show the arithmetic once, inside the valuation section, the way the committee would read it.

## Principle 1: Closure gauge (tier × evidenced status × clock)

Each finding has three readings.

- **Tier** is the analyst's severity call.
- **Status** is a claim you have to test against the rest of the input. "Resolved" counts only if a document closed the issue: a closure letter, a signed consent, a renewed contract or an auditor's opinion. All of these are still open, whatever the column says:
  - a deferral;
  - counsel saying a matter is substantially done;
  - a live re-bid;
  - a pending appeal;
  - a consent not yet obtained.

  When the column and the write-up disagree, show the conflict in the risk grid and carry the finding as open. Don't quietly re-tier it. The committee should see both readings.
- **Clock** is the time until the triggering event (expiry, award, response deadline, lease end), measured against the first 12–24 months of ownership. An event inside that window is live economics, not background.

Consequence: a top-tier finding that is still open after this test blocks an unconditional go, whatever its label (critical, must-fix and so on). The verdict becomes either "proceed only if", with that finding as a condition before signing or close, or "do not proceed" when the asset's value depends on an outcome that can't be settled before money moves.

Read the gauge in reverse too. If a lower-tier item's write-up puts a large number on it, say plainly that its tier looks light. Examples: churn of a quarter of the base, retention below 100%, or an injunction threat on a core module. If the status column is missing, treat every finding as open and say so.

## Principle 2: Base gauge (what the multiple multiplies)

A multiple is only as good as its base. Measure revenue and earnings at the level the buyer actually inherits:

- recurring, not one-off projects or true-ups;
- net of pass-through volume where the sector prices on net;
- transferable: grants or licences that lapse on change of control come out;
- the run-rate after churn, once customers have given notice;
- audited: show a restatement still waiting for the auditor both ways;
- earnings with add-backs that genuinely won't recur. Recurring spend labelled "strategic" stays in.

Put the seller's base and your defended base side by side and show the subtraction. If EBITDA is negative or thin after adjustment, an EBITDA multiple means nothing. Price on revenue and say why.

## Principle 3: Peer gauge (inclusion band, then position)

Comps qualify by how close they sit to the target on the quantities that drive multiples: growth, profitability, revenue model (hardware, software or services mix) and size. Exclude these by name, with the reason:

- a transaction carrying a control premium among plain trading figures;
- a comp whose growth or margin runs about double the target's, or that sits on a different model;
- anything the artifacts themselves flag.

If nothing is flagged, still say briefly why each comp stays in.

Then place the target within the surviving band by its gaps. Weaker growth or margin puts it in the lower half; superior retention can justify the median. State the adjustment rather than asserting a number, for example "half a turn under the median for an eight-point margin shortfall". When the comps lack a column you need, say the band is less reliable.

The range is the low multiple × defended base to the high multiple × defended base. Write out each product and check it. Cross-check with a second multiple where the data supports one.

## Principle 4: Price gauge (ask versus band)

Divide the seller's ask by the defended base to get the implied multiple, then compare it with the band. Report the gap in currency and in percent.

- **Ask inside the band, no open top-tier item:** proceed.
- **Ask inside the band, with an open blocker:** conditional.
- **Ask above the band:** counter at a stated figure inside it. Decline if the gap is wide and blockers are still open.

Quantified liabilities (capex backlog, fines, tax reassessments, remediation) come off the price or go into escrow, named with their amount.

## Principle 5: Conditions are tests, not intentions

Each condition covers four things:

- one named finding;
- the evidence that proves it is closed;
- the timing: before signing, before close, or within a stated period after close;
- the consequence if it fails: walk away, cut the price, or hold an escrow sized to the exposure.

"Complete further diligence" is not a condition. If the input doesn't give a date, counterparty or amount, request it. Never fill it in.

## Writing it

Put the recommendation and price stance in the first two sentences. Then write five sections:

1. **Thesis:** why this asset, and the single risk that decides the deal. That risk must appear in the grid.
2. **Risks:** a grid with finding, tier, stated status, evidenced status and consequence, blockers first.
3. **Valuation:** the base bridge, the comps kept and dropped, positioning, the arithmetic, and the multiple the ask implies.
4. **Recommendation:** the causal chain from the gauges, with an honest level of confidence.
5. **Conditions:** numbered, each tied to a finding.

Respect any length limits the request sets. Otherwise keep the thesis under about 150 words and the whole memo at roughly 3,000–5,500 characters.

Write the way a corporate-development lead talks: connected sentences that use the gauges' vocabulary and reason from this deal's own numbers. Vary sentence shape across findings rather than stamping one template on each. Before finishing, recompute every product and percentage, and check that every figure appears in the input or follows from it.

## Miniature (invented)

**Inputs.** Kestrelmoor Freight Audit has revenue of £23.6m, of which £2.0m is a one-off migration project, and the ask is £96m. The top-tier finding, consent from its main carrier client, is marked "Resolved", but the write-up says the consent is "expected next month". The comps are four trading peers at 3.1x–4.4x revenue and one buyout at 6.0x.

**Opening of the memo.** "Recommend proceeding only on conditions, at no more than £84m against the £96m ask. The carrier consent is labelled resolved, but the write-up shows it is still expected, so it stays open and becomes a condition before signing: no signed consent, no deal. On recurring revenue of £21.6m (£23.6m less the £2.0m migration project), the four trading peers give £67.0m (3.1x) to £95.0m (4.4x). We exclude the 6.0x buyout because it prices in control. Kestrelmoor's growth trails the peer median, so we anchor at 3.6x–3.9x, which gives £77.8m–£84.2m. The ask implies 4.4x, the very top of the band, about 14% above our ceiling."
