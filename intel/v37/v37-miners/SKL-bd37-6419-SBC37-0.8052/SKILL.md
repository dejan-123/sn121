# How an IC memo outcome is built

An investment committee does not vote on prose. It votes on three linked questions: what the buyer will really own (the **surviving base**), what that is worth against real trades (the **defended range**), and what could take the deal away (the **open blockers**). Read the three artifacts as parts of one machine, because each part changes the others. A finding can shrink the base, the base sets the valuation, and the gap between the valuation and the ask, together with the blockers, sets the verdict. Write only the finished memo, with no notes or working shown before it.

## Part 1: the surviving base creates the value

The seller's headline number is rarely the right base. Before using any multiple, rebuild the revenue or EBITDA that transfers at close and keeps recurring after it.
- **One-off project inside the trailing twelve months:** remove it, and recheck margin on what remains, because profitable one-offs can hide underlying losses.
- **Pass-through volume booked gross:** price the business on revenue after interchange, processing or other pass-through costs, whichever measure that industry uses.
- **Funding that cannot follow a new owner** (grants, licences, government programmes needing consent): take it out of the base, or show the value with and without it.
- **Customers who have cancelled or given notice:** use the run rate without them, and judge the seller's story about why they left as an unproven claim.
- **Add-backs:** genuine one-time costs such as severance may stay if you say why. Recurring spend relabelled as "strategic", and restatements without auditor sign-off, go back into the figures.
- **Ask quoted on an inflated base:** work out the implied multiple on the surviving base. That gap is often the whole story.

## Part 2: comp discipline narrows it into a range

- **Choose the primary multiple** that both the target and the comps support. Use EV/EBITDA only when the target's EBITDA is positive and meaningful; otherwise use EV/Revenue on the correct revenue measure.
- **Say why each comp is in or out.** Drop control-premium or sponsor-premium acquisitions mixed into trading comps, and drop outliers whose growth or margin points to a different business.
- **Place the target inside the remaining set** by growth, margin and retention. A target below the peer median on margin and growth belongs at or under the low end, not at the median. State the adjustment in words.
- **Show the arithmetic:** low multiple × base = low EV; high multiple × base = high EV; ask ÷ base = implied multiple. Recompute every product before writing it down. One wrong figure discredits the memo.
- **Missing comp data** (product mix, profitability) is a gap. Name it, then either widen the range or cut it, and say which.

## Part 3: open blockers take away the option to proceed

The status column records the upstream analyst's opinion. Check each status against the narrative. All of the following are open, whatever the column says: a hearing postponed, a contract under live re-bid, an inquiry counsel calls nearly finished but with no closure letter, a consent not yet in hand, an auditor who has not yet given an opinion. Record the discrepancy in the risk table, for example: "marked resolved upstream; the narrative shows X; treated as open." Do not quietly re-tier it, and do not quietly trust it. Flag a lower-tier finding as under-tiered when its consequence hits the core asset or most of the revenue, and say whether that changes the call. A missing status column or a contradiction between artifacts is a finding in its own right.

## From parts to verdict

- **A top-tier finding is still open after checking:** the answer is not "proceed". Recommend holding approval, or approving only against conditions that must be met before signing, and name the closing evidence that would reopen it. Two independent open blockers, or one that threatens most of the revenue, call for a decline or a pause, not a conditional approval.
- **Every top-tier finding is closed with evidence and only manageable items remain open:** proceed with conditions, at a stated price.
- **The ask sits above the defended range:** the recommendation includes a counter price inside the range and a walk-away point. Manageable risk never justifies paying the ask.
- **The thesis names the primary risk,** and that risk is the first row of the risk table.
- **State your confidence** and the one fact that would change the call.

## Conditions an IC can act on

Each condition ties to a named finding. It states the evidence that closes it (an issued closure letter, a written consent, filed licences, a signed renewal, an auditor's opinion), its timing (before signing, before close, or within a set period after close) and what happens if it fails (walk away, a price cut, or an escrow or indemnity). Size money only from figures in the input. Where the input gives no figure, write "to be sized in confirmatory diligence". Never invent amounts, dates, names or percentages. Data requests for gaps belong here too.

## Writing it

- **Opening:** two or three sentences giving the verdict, the range against the ask, and the finding or two driving it.
- **Five sections:**
  - Thesis: about 120–180 words.
  - Risk table: columns for finding, tier, checked status and why it matters, followed by a short paragraph on the ordering.
  - Valuation: comps kept and dropped with reasons, the arithmetic lines, and where the target sits.
  - Recommendation: about 100–150 words.
  - Conditions: 3–6, numbered.
- **Length:** keep to any length limits the request states. Aim for 2,500–5,000 characters overall.
- **Style:** plain expert prose that uses this vocabulary naturally (surviving base, checked status, implied multiple). Do not label paragraphs with this guide's headings.
- **Emphasis:** let the deal decide it. A pricing-led deal spends its words on the base; a blocker-led deal spends them on the conditions.
- **Fidelity:** use exact input names and figures, and nothing the input does not state.

## Worked example (invented)

A cold-chain monitoring firm reports trailing revenue of $26.0M, including a $2.7M one-off installation contract. EBITDA is negative and the ask is $88M. The risk table marks a top-tier hosting certification as resolved, but the narrative says the audit report has not been issued. Founder retention is a manageable, unresolved finding. Trading comps run 2.2x–3.4x EV/Revenue, and the set also includes one strategic buyout at 5.6x.

The memo opens: "We recommend against approval at $88M. The hosting certification is marked resolved upstream, but the audit report has not been issued, so the top-tier finding remains open. On the $23.3M recurring base, the ask implies 3.8x, above every trading comp." The valuation drops the buyout because it carries a control premium. Growth is below the set median, so the target sits at 2.2x–2.8x × $23.3M = $51.3M–$65.2M. Conditions: (1) the issued certification report before signing, or walk away; (2) a signed founder retention agreement before close; (3) once condition 1 is met, counter at no more than $65M.
