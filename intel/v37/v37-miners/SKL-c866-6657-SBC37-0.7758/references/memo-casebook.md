# Memo casebook

One invented deal worked from artifacts to memo, followed by notes on how the last section changes with the verdict. Copy the layout, the length discipline and the way each call is argued. Every name, figure and finding in a real memo comes from that memo's own artifacts.

## The memo

# IC Memo: Brannock Linen Services

### 1. Deal Thesis

Brannock is a regional commercial-laundry operator with $36.0M of LTM revenue, 88% of it under multi-year hospital and hotel contracts, and a renewed discharge permit to 2031. The case rests on contracted, recurring volume in a slow-growing (6%) but sticky market. The primary risk is the Elmstead hospital tender (R1): it carries 24% of revenue, the upstream table marks it Resolved, but the award has not been announced. At $64M, the seller's ask is 11.9x reported EBITDA, above what the comparables support.

### 2. Risk Assessment

| # | Finding | Tier (as given) | Status (as given) | Memo flag | Deal impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Elmstead contract (24% of revenue) in renewal tender | Must-Address | Resolved | **Contradicted:** the narrative says the award is unannounced; treated as unresolved | Loss would remove about $8.6M of revenue (24% of $36.0M) |
| R2 | Discharge permit renewal | Must-Address | Resolved | Consistent: renewed in March to 2031 | None beyond monitoring |
| R3 | 40 vans on leases expiring within 18 months | Manageable | Unresolved | — | Lease reset cost not quantified in the artifacts |
| R4 | No natural-gas hedging | Manageable | In-Progress | — | Margin exposure to gas prices |
| R5 | Plant-manager succession | Informational | Unresolved | — | Operational continuity |

R1 keeps its upstream tier and status; the memo flags the contradiction rather than changing the row, and the recommendation treats it as open.

### 3. Valuation

Primary multiple: EV/EBITDA, since Brannock is profitable and the comps report meaningful EBITDA. EV/Revenue is used only as a cross-check.
Base: reported EBITDA of $5.4M. The $1.3M of seller add-backs is excluded because neither item is itemised or documented as non-recurring.

| Comparable | Treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Halyard Textile Care (10.5x) | Included | Regional peer, similar growth and margin |
| Pressfield Services (9.0x) | Included | Regional peer |
| Brightwash Holdings (9.5x) | Included | Regional peer |
| Linenworks Group (14.0x) | Excluded | National scale ($1.2B revenue) earns a premium Brannock cannot claim |
| CleanCycle Tech (NM) | Excluded | EBITDA not meaningful; a 25%-growth technology profile, not a laundry operator |

Included EV/EBITDA multiples in order: 9.0x, 9.5x, 10.5x; median 9.5x. One step on an EBITDA multiple is 1.0x:
- growth, 6% against a 5% included median: a 1-point gap, no step;
- gross margin, 31% against 30%: a 1-point gap, no step;
- concentration, Elmstead at 24% of revenue (above 20%) and in an open tender: one step down;
- size: the artifacts give no scale for the included peers, so no step.

Running sum: 9.5x − 1.0x concentration = 8.5x. Range, 8.5x ± one step: 7.5x × $5.4M = $40.5M and 9.5x × $5.4M = $51.3M, so **$40.5M–$51.3M**.

Cross-check: the included EV/Revenue median of 1.7x × $36.0M gives $61.2M. That overstates value because Brannock's EBITDA margin (15.0%) sits below the peers' implied margins of about 18% (EV/Revenue ÷ EV/EBITDA: 1.9/10.5, 1.6/9.0, 1.7/9.5). The ask of $64M is 11.9x reported EBITDA; it reaches 9.6x only on the seller's undocumented adjusted figure.

### 4. Recommendation

**Request more data.** R1 is a must-address row whose Resolved status the narrative contradicts, so the gate rules out proceeding. The block is an information gap, the tender award, which can be closed before signing. The comps support at most $51.3M, against an ask of $64M, which sits $12.7M above the high end.

### 5. Conditions and Next Steps

- **R1:** Deal lead to obtain Elmstead's award letter or signed renewal before signing. If the contract is lost, revalue on the remaining 76% of revenue.
- **R2:** Legal diligence to file the renewed permit to 2031 in the disclosure schedule and confirm no open conditions attach to it; no further action.
- **R3:** Operations diligence to obtain the van lease schedule and renewal quotes, and price any increase into the EBITDA base.
- **R4:** CFO to present a gas hedging plan covering at least the first post-close year as a signing condition.
- **R5:** Management to name successors for both retiring plant managers in the 100-day plan.

## How the last section changes with the verdict

- **Proceed** (every must-address row Resolved and uncontradicted): bullets become closing conditions and protections. These are warranties or indemnities for the risk rows, an escrow or holdback sized to a quantified exposure, covenants, and day-one integration steps. Each manageable row still gets its named mitigation.
- **Request more data**: each open row gets the exact document or answer that would close it, an owner, a deadline, and what each possible answer does to the verdict and the range.
- **Pass**: bullets state why the open finding cannot be cured within this deal, what would have to change for the committee to look again, and, if there is one, the price at which it would.

## Notes on calls that turn on one fact

- **Tier vocabulary.** When the upstream table uses Critical / Moderate / Low, keep those words in the table and state the reading once (Critical gates the recommendation like must-address).
- **Status wording.** "In-Progress", "substantially resolved", "deferred" and a blank cell are all open for the gate, and the memo says why in one clause.
- **Seller framing.** A banker's multiple applied to the wrong base (gross where the industry uses net, total revenue including one-time items) is restated on the right base before it is compared with the range.
- **Missing inputs.** When a metric or artifact is absent, write that it is not provided, value on what is given, and list the request in Conditions and Next Steps; never fill the gap with an assumption presented as fact.
- **Length.** If a section runs long, cut adjectives and repeated context first; never cut a row, an exclusion reason or a line of arithmetic.

The casebook ends here. Write the memo now: its first characters are `# IC Memo: ` and the target's name. A sentence before them, such as "Now I will draft the IC memo", is the slip most often made; the working stays out of the reply.
