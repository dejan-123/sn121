## The memo

# IC memo: Kestrel Ridge Brewing Group, three-brewery craft beer and taproom platform

## 1. Deal thesis for Kestrel Ridge (up to 130 words)

Kestrel Ridge brings three breweries, six taprooms and $31.7M of revenue that reaches the buyer at close, growing 9% at a 44% gross margin. The case for owning it is a regional platform whose taprooms and wholesale reach can carry more volume through capacity already built. The primary risk is row 2: the licences must be reissued to a new owner, the table calls this Resolved, and no artifact names an approval, so the group may be unable to brew or sell on day one. Row 1, an open wastewater violation at the brewery making 62% of volume, also blocks. Decision: no-go at this time, with price as a separate gap set out in sections 3 and 4.

Blocking: two of ten findings (rows 1 and 2), both must-address. Row 2 is used as open because the table's Resolved has no record behind it. Row 3 is used as open because the narrative's own words contradict its status; as a manageable item it becomes a condition, not a block. So do row 9, although it touches sales in one state, and row 10, which is Resolved with no record behind it and could enjoin the flagship. Cross-links: rows 5 and 8 set the revenue base, so they move into the valuation; row 7 stays in the base because that wholesaler has not left. Rows 1 and 4 sit at the same site: a production limit under row 1 would idle the line row 4 rebuilds.

## 3. Valuation for Kestrel Ridge (tables, then up to 450 words)

Base: the revenue that reaches the buyer at close, with reported LTM kept as the reconciliation line.

Kept in the base: the $5.8M sold through the largest wholesaler (row 7), which is at risk but not lost; if it left, that $5.8M would come off the base.

The seller's $5.9M also adds back $0.5M of taproom launch programmes; they recur every year, so they stay in costs. The 20% growth case depends on supermarket trials converting and is not priced.

Growth gap = points between each comp's growth and Kestrel's 9%.

IN: the two smallest gaps, Brindlewood Ales (1) and Mossgate Brewing (3).

Both methods apply: the priced EBITDA is positive without add-backs, and the two ranges overlap.

- EV/Revenue: 1.4x × $31.7M = $44.4M; 1.7x × $31.7M = $53.9M.
- EV/EBITDA: 10x × $4.5M = $45.0M; 12x × $4.5M = $54.0M.
- Defended range, where both methods overlap: $45.0M to $53.9M.
- Position: upper half of the range. Kestrel's 44% margin is one point under Brindlewood's and three over Mossgate's, and its growth sits one point under Brindlewood's; nothing in the comps table argues lower.
- Offer ceiling: $53.9M range top less the $1.3M row 4 rebuild = $52.6M.
- Ask: $58M ÷ $31.7M = 1.83x revenue and $58M ÷ $4.5M = 12.9x EBITDA, above the top of both ranges (1.7x, 12x). On the seller's figures, $58M ÷ $36.4M = 1.59x and $58M ÷ $5.9M = 9.8x; the ask only looks in range because that base still carries excise, the one-off run and a client already gone.
- Not priced: the row 1 corrective plan's cost is not given, so the range carries no deduction for it.

Seller claims tested: "adjusted EBITDA $5.9M" fails, because the $0.5M launch programmes recur every year. "A small account that will be replaced" fails as a reason to keep row 8's $0.5M: the client has cancelled and no replacement is named. "$58M is 1.59x revenue" fails, because that revenue still holds excise, the one-off run and the lost client; on the priced base the ask is 1.83x.

## 4. Recommendation for Kestrel Ridge (up to 200 words)

**No-go at this time (defer).** Rows 1 and 2 are open must-address findings and block a go regardless of price. Defended range: $45.0M to $53.9M (section 3). Separately, the $58M ask is above that range, so even with both rows cleared the answer at $58M is "not at this price".

What turns this to go:
1. Row 2: the authorities' written approval or pre-clearance of the licence reissue to the buyer.
2. Row 1: the city's written acceptance of the corrective action plan due in 5 weeks, with its cost so it can be priced.
3. A price at or below the $52.6M offer ceiling (range top less the $1.3M rebuild).

Most important gap: row 2. Without the reissue the group cannot trade after close; the record that closes it is the approval itself.

- **Zero rows block.** Had the artifacts named the city's acceptance letter and the licence approval, rows 1 and 2 would read **Clear** [closed by the named record], and section 4 would open: "**Go, subject to conditions.** Zero findings block: rows 1 and 2 are closed by the acceptance letter and the approval named in the artifacts. - **Ask below the range.** Had nothing blocked and the seller asked $42M, section 4 would read: "**Go, subject to conditions,** at the $42M ask. The comps would support up to the $52.6M ceiling, so there is no case for offering more than was asked."
- **No asking price in the input.** The ask line in section 3 would read: "No asking price was given. The defended range of $45.0M to $53.9M bounds any offer; offer ceiling $52.6M." Section 4 would say the price cannot be tested until the seller names one.
- **Different tier words.** Had the table used High / Medium / Low, one line under the risk table would read: "Tier words: High = must-address, Medium = manageable, Low = informational; the blocking rule applies to High."
- **Comps' mix not given.** Had the narrative split Kestrel's margin between taproom and wholesale while the comps showed only a blended margin, section 3 would add: "The comps' taproom and wholesale mix is not given, so their blended margins are not like-for-like; EV/EBITDA leads because it already reflects each company's own mix."
- **Methods that do not overlap.** Had Kestrel's EBITDA margin been 8% against the comps' 14% (Brindlewood 1.7 ÷ 12, Mossgate 1.4 ÷ 10), the EV/EBITDA range would sit wholly below the EV/Revenue range. Section 3 would say so, lead with EV/EBITDA because revenue multiples would pay for margin Kestrel does not earn, and keep the EV/Revenue range as a cross-check line only.
- **A customer that has given notice.** Had the largest wholesaler already given notice, row 7 would read **Priced** [gone: removed from the base] like row 8, and the base table would gain a step, "Less wholesaler that has given notice: -$5.8M", giving a $25.9M priced base.
