# IC memo by question ladder

Every finding, base item, comp and the price climbs a fixed ladder, and each answer is printed in the memo as that item's call with its reason. Delivery: the final message is the memo itself, starting at its `# IC memo:` title; all reasoning sits inside its sections.

## The ladders

**Findings.**
1. Copy tier and status; the tier is always the table's, never re-tiered. Other tier words (Critical / Moderate / Low) get one mapping line: top word = must-address.
2. Test the status against the artifacts' own text: pending, under appeal, re-bid live, deferral, "substantially", "agreed in principle", cannot document, auditors have not opined. If contradicted, print "Table: Resolved. Artifacts: '<quote>'. Used: Open." A must-address marked Resolved with no record named (consent, closure letter, signed amendment, dismissal, audit, opinion) is "Open: record not seen". A manageable row in that position is open too, and stays a condition.
3. A must-address row takes only **Blocks** (open) or **Clear** (closed by a named record). Curability, timing or a known money effect never lower a block.
4. Manageable and informational rows never block, even when they touch the go or read "record not seen": **Before signing**, **After close**, **Priced** (amount given), **Downside** (revenue at risk, not gone) or **Noted** (with why it is held low).
5. Name each cross-link where one row raises another's weight.

**Base.** Remove, each as a printed step with reported LTM kept as the reconciliation line: pass-through gross (payment volume, excise), one-time projects, revenue barred for this buyer (grants), customers who have already cancelled, restatements auditors have not opined on. Nothing else comes out: revenue only at risk (re-bid, remediation, legacy product, cancellable partner, concentrated client) stays in and its stated amount is named as a downside. EBITDA: the narrative's stated figure for the priced base; otherwise reported EBITDA moved only by stated one-time add-backs. Recurring "strategic", "growth" or "launch" spend is not an add-back; never estimate revenue × margin. Negative or add-back-only EBITDA makes EV/EBITDA NM. Growth that needs pilots to convert is upside only. Test each seller claim against the figures.

**Comps.** Deal prices (acquired, control or PE premium, banker's deck) are OUT; NM drops that method only. Growth gap = difference between comp and target growth, in points. The two kept comps with the smallest gaps are IN, on either side, named above the comp table in one line: "IN: the two smallest gaps, A (gap) and B (gap)." Every other comp is a bound or OUT with its reason, even when "no exclusions" is stated. If the comps' mix is unknown, say so.

**Price.** Revenue multiples times priced revenue, EBITDA multiples times priced EBITDA, one line each: "1.4x × $31.7M = $44.4M". Defended range: the overlap of the two methods, or the one usable method; with no overlap, EV/EBITDA leads when the target's EBITDA margin trails the comps' (EV/Revenue ÷ EV/EBITDA). Position in words, with no invented discounts. Offer ceiling = range top less priced items with stated amounts. Ask ÷ priced base and ÷ the seller's base: inside, above or below. No ask: "No asking price was given", and none is supplied.

**Decision.** Any Blocks row: **No-go at this time (defer)**, plus what turns it to go (each blocking row and its record). None: **Go, subject to conditions**, at or below the ceiling, or at the ask when it is lower. An ask inside the range unblocks nothing. Counts in words ("Blocking: two of four findings"); the single most important gap and the record that closes it.

**Facts.** Every figure, name, date and party comes from the input or from printed arithmetic. A record is requested, never described as obtained. Missing items are "not given" data requests; conditions add no terms (no "within 12 months", "at least 2 years", "no material findings").

## Memo shape

Each heading carries the target's name and its bound; stay inside it. Sentences under the tables read as the deal team's argument, never as rung numbers or notes.

- `# IC memo: <Target>, <one-line deal description>`
- `## 1. Deal thesis for <Target> (up to 130 words)`: what is bought, why own it, the primary risk (a row in section 2), the decision. No price figures.
- `## 2. Risk ladder for <Target> (table, then up to 120 words)`: # | Finding | Table says | Artifacts show | Used | Call [reason]; then mapping line if needed, count, cross-links.
- `## 3. Valuation for <Target> (tables, then up to 450 words)`: base steps; comp table with growth gaps; products; defended range; position; offer ceiling; ask; seller claims tested.
- `## 4. Recommendation for <Target> (up to 200 words)`: decision, blocking rows, the defended range as in section 3, price position, what turns it to go, the most important gap.
- `## 5. Conditions and data requests for <Target> (up to 10 lines)`: row number, the record that closes it as the input names it, and when: before signing, before close, after close or in price.

## Notes on the rungs

- Weight across rows: a ratio breaching a standard whose review is live; contracts expiring within months with no renewals; an informational item that sets the revenue base.
- A seller claim fails when the figures undo it: accounts called "low-margin" holding the same share of net revenue as of volume; a "one-time" add-back that recurs; a multiple quoted on gross revenue.
- A slower comp is as eligible as a faster one; margin breaks a tie between equal gaps.
- Above the ceiling with nothing blocking, the answer is "go only at or below <ceiling>".
