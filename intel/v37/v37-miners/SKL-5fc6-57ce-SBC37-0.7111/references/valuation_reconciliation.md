# Valuation and evidence reconciliation

Use only the relevant methods below. Preserve uncertainty rather than inventing a reconciled fact.

Keep nonstandard tiers intact. Explain the functional interpretation used for the gate: a critical continuity/legal/ownership issue may function as must-address even when called something else. Flag an apparent mis-tier in a separate field; do not promote every manageable issue to a blocker. A materially threatening narrative-only issue gets a new, explicitly analyst-added row, with no invented upstream tier/status. Informational findings can affect valuation without becoming deal blockers.

Avoid unsupported probabilities, fabricated reserve amounts, and ritual requests for all possible diligence. An economic exposure that cannot be removed may require price protection or ongoing monitoring rather than a fictional cure. Show the residual risk after the proposed response. Use proposed owner roles and event-based timing when helpful; never invent assigned individuals, dates, or agreed terms.

For the retained set, state the observed multiple range and compute its median explicitly. Sort the actual numeric observations; for an even count average the middle pair. Compute retained-peer growth and margin medians from supplied comparable definitions. Compare the target on the same economic base; a segment growth rate or gross margin must not be presented as a whole-company statistic. Do not use a full-pool median after excluding peers.

Translate these comparisons into a defended applied multiple interval. Explain why its endpoints sit at, above, or below the anchor; quantify the discount/premium implied by the chosen endpoints when useful. Label the magnitude as underwriting judgment, not an empirical regression or supplied fact. Avoid automatic fixed-percentage discounts, comp cherry-picking, and counting the same loss both in the denominator and again in an unexplained haircut. Distinct residual uncertainty may justify an additional, explained adjustment.

Only now compare an available seller ask, on the same EV basis and normalized denominator. Equity value = EV - debt + cash only for supplied balances; identify other bridge items as unquantified, not zero. An acquisition EV is not an equity cheque. Do not invent an ask if absent.

## Resolve competing economic measures

Build a bridge from reported economics to the selected denominator. For each adjustment name its source, whether it changes transferability, recurrence, or the current operating run rate, and whether it overlaps another adjustment. Distinguish historical LTM evidence from a pro forma surviving-business base. A grant, project, customer loss, or discontinued operation may affect both revenue and cost; do not infer the earnings change equals the revenue change. Without expense detail, disclose that earnings cannot be normalized reliably.

If a source supplies incompatible EBITDA figures and margin statements, reproduce the relevant bridge in words or a short equation, flag its unexplained residual, and avoid selecting the most favorable figure. Prefer an economically justified metric less dependent on that contradiction, while requesting reconciliation. Do not call an input typo proven unless the input establishes it.

For mixed businesses, a segment-based approach needs segment denominators AND suitable segment multiples. Without both, do not construct a sum-of-parts price from invented allocations. Use the closest blended comparison with a stated mix limitation, or show bounded sensitivity on disclosed data. Growth of one segment cannot establish the growth of a combined revenue base.

For nontransferable earnings or revenue, value the transferable business only if its base is supported. If services accompany a software platform, do not silently value both at a pure software multiple. Explain the service treatment and the limitation in peer definitions. Rights required to operate remain a separate decision gate even when the denominator is adjusted correctly.

## Defend the peer selection and interval

A current trading set and a historical acquisition set can answer different questions. Consider observation date, control or strategic premium, business comparability, and the acquisition being considered. Explain which basis anchors the range. Do not treat every acquisition as invalid simply because it involves control; do not mix a specifically identified premium observation into an ordinary trading median without a defense. Exclusion is a comparability judgment, not a way to force a price.

Growth and margin differences are directional evidence, not a published formula for multiple discounts. A chosen interval should have an intelligible bridge from retained observations to target economics. State why the lower end reflects specific weaker characteristics and why the upper end remains supportable. Do not use a mechanically identical haircut on every business. A lack of peer scale data supports an explicit limitation, not a claim the target is smaller.

If positive sustainable EBITDA and revenue both support valuation, compute the second metric as a cross-check where data permits. Explain divergence through earnings quality, EBITDA margin, business mix, capital needs, or metric limitations supported by the artifacts. Gross margin cannot establish EBITDA margin comparability. Keep one principal range; present the other as a sensitivity unless it is genuinely the better basis. Never blend two unrelated intervals to create false precision.

If only one defensible peer remains, report a single-observation anchor rather than calling it a robust market median. If none remains, identify the data needed for valuation and do not invent replacement peers. Use "not determinable" for an unsupported numerical answer; do not manufacture a nonzero range to fill the section.

## Maintain decision boundaries

An upstream resolution label represents the analyst's classification; a continuing exposure is not automatically proof of erroneous closure. Look for an actual inconsistency, such as evidence the supposedly completed action remains pending. State the opposing claims, preserve the original fields, and separate decision treatment from upstream classification. Missing closure details alone do not justify systematically rejecting all resolved findings.

A mitigation must respond to the mechanism. Contractual protection may allocate financial loss but cannot create missing operational rights. An escrow may cover quantified liability only when adequately supported and enforceable; it does not itself fix an authorization problem. A price adjustment cannot substitute for evidence needed to establish lawful continuity. Present proposed controls as requirements, not facts.

Avoid premature Pass when the decisive question has a plausible evidence-based remedy. Conversely, do not use Request More Data as an automatic response to every unattractive transaction. Identify the fact or feasible change that could reverse the decision; if none is credible, explain rejection and define limited re-entry criteria only when meaningful.
