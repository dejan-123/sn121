# Memo example

The memo is one message whose first characters are `**1. Deal thesis:**`. No sentence announcing the memo, no analysis notes and no horizontal rule come before that line; the reading of the artifacts happens silently, and the table and the valuation carry its results. Both deals below are invented, in fields unrelated to any request.

## Example 1: an open deal-breaker behind a "Resolved" status

The user's artifacts, summarised. Target: Brackenridge Pest Services, a commercial pest-control route business. Risk table: (1) state applicator licences lapsed at 4 of 11 branches; renewals filed (Must-Address, Resolved); (2) the largest customer, a hotel group at 27% of revenue, can terminate for convenience on 60 days' notice; an amendment is under negotiation (Must-Address, In-Progress); (3) 38% of the 140 service vans are over eight years old; $2.7M of replacement capex is not in the plan (Manageable, Unresolved); (4) the founder's son runs the two largest branches with no non-compete (Manageable, Unresolved); (5) workers' compensation claim rate above the industry average for three years (Informational, Unresolved). Narrative: LTM revenue $36.0M, of which $3.0M is a one-time termite remediation contract for a university campus; recurring revenue $33.0M, growing 9%; gross margin 46%; reported EBITDA $5.6M, of which $1.3M came from the one-time contract; the seller presents adjusted EBITDA of $6.0M by adding back $0.4M of "growth marketing"; the state agency has acknowledged the licence renewals but has not issued them; asking price $64.0M EV. Comps: RouteGuard Services 2.1x revenue, 11x EBITDA, 8% growth, 45% margin; Pestwise Holdings 2.4x, 12x, 11%, 48%; ShieldServ Inc. 1.8x, 10x, 6%, 42%; CleanBay Group 3.6x, 19x, 14%, 52%, acquired 16 months ago by a strategic buyer at a control premium; GreenSpray Tech 4.9x, NM, 41%, 61%. The whole memo:

**1. Deal thesis:** Brackenridge is a dense-route commercial pest-control business with $33.0M of recurring contract revenue growing 9% at a 46% gross margin. We would be paying for route density and contract renewals, not for growth. The seller asks $64.0M EV, framed on an adjusted EBITDA that includes a marketing add-back and a one-time remediation contract. The primary risk is row 1: four branches operate on lapsed applicator licences that the risk table records as resolved, while the narrative shows the renewals acknowledged but not issued. The hotel group's 60-day termination right (row 2) is the second open deal-breaker.

### 2. Risk table

| # | Finding | Input tier and status | Memo reading | What it means for the deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Applicator licences lapsed at 4 of 11 branches | Must-Address, Resolved | Open: the agency acknowledged the renewals but has not issued them, so the recorded status does not hold | Four branches cannot lawfully treat customer sites until the licences issue |
| 2 | Hotel group, 27% of revenue, can terminate on 60 days' notice | Must-Address, In-Progress | Open: amendment not signed | More than a quarter of revenue is cancellable at will |
| 3 | 38% of 140 vans over eight years; $2.7M capex unplanned | Manageable, Unresolved | Price item | A $2.7M cash need the plan omits |
| 4 | Founder's son runs the two largest branches; no non-compete | Manageable, Unresolved | Structure item | He could take the two largest branches' customers with him |
| 5 | Workers' compensation claim rate above industry for three years | Informational, Unresolved | Disclose | Insurance cost to confirm in diligence |

### 3. Valuation

| Comparable | In or out | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| RouteGuard Services | In | 8% growth, 45% margin: closest to the target |
| Pestwise Holdings | In | 11% growth, 48% margin |
| ShieldServ Inc. | In | 6% growth, 42% margin: sets the low end |
| CleanBay Group | Out | Acquisition price with a strategic control premium, not a trading multiple |
| GreenSpray Tech | Out | 41% growth and 61% margin; no EBITDA multiple |

Base: recurring EBITDA of $4.3M, the reported $5.6M less the $1.3M earned on the one-time contract. The $0.4M marketing add-back is an operating cost and is rejected. Brackenridge's 9% growth and 46% margin sit inside the three kept comps, but its 13.0% EBITDA margin is below theirs, so the range runs from ShieldServ to Pestwise: 10.0x × $4.3M = $43.0M; 12.0x × $4.3M = $51.6M. A revenue cross-check, 1.8x × $33.0M = $59.4M to 2.4x × $33.0M = $79.2M, overstates value because the comps convert 18% to 20% of revenue into EBITDA; the EBITDA range is weighted. The ask of $64.0M is 14.9x recurring EBITDA, $12.4M above the top of the range, before the $2.7M of van capex in row 3.

### 4. Recommendation

**Do not proceed on current terms.** Two deal-breakers are open: the row 1 licences are recorded as resolved but have not been issued, and the row 2 amendment is unsigned. Either would leave us buying revenue we cannot keep. The ask also sits $12.4M above a range built on recurring EBITDA. The deal can return to the committee once both deal-breakers close with documents and the price sits within $43.0M to $51.6M, less the van capex.

### 5. Conditions

1. Row 1: issued applicator licences for all four branches, delivered before signing; without them we walk.
2. Row 2: an executed amendment removing the hotel group's termination for convenience, before signing; otherwise the price excludes that contract's EBITDA.
3. Row 3: the $2.7M van replacement capex comes off the purchase price, agreed in the term sheet.
4. Row 4: a non-compete and retention agreement for the founder's son as a closing condition.
5. Data request: the remediation contract's invoices and cost ledger, to confirm the $3.0M revenue and $1.3M EBITDA carve-out before signing.

## Example 2: deal-breakers closed with evidence, no asking price

The user's artifacts, summarised. Target: Halloway Lift Maintenance, an elevator service-contract business. Risk table with its own labels: (1) two municipal contracts (15% of revenue) need consent on change of control; signed consents are in the data room (Critical, Resolved); (2) the technicians' union agreement expires in 9 months (Moderate, Unresolved); (3) one branch filed inspection certificates late last year; the regulator's letter closes the matter (Low, Resolved); (4) the largest customer, a property manager at 18% of revenue, may cancel on 120 days' notice (Critical, Resolved); (5) two wage claims by former technicians are pending before the labour board (Moderate, Resolved); (6) the company's service-brand trademark application is pending and a rival filed a similar mark four months earlier (Critical, Resolved). Narrative: LTM revenue $21.0M, 92% contract maintenance, growing 12%; gross margin 39%; EBITDA $3.6M; no asking price stated. Comps: Liftway 1.9x revenue, 11x EBITDA, 10% growth, 38% margin; VertiServe 2.2x, 12x, 13%, 41%; Ascenda 2.6x, 14x, 16%, 44%; UpCore 1.5x, 9x, 4%, 33%; no split of maintenance versus repair revenue for the comps. The whole memo:

**1. Deal thesis:** Halloway sells multi-year elevator maintenance contracts: $21.0M of LTM revenue, 92% of it contracted, growing 12% at a 39% gross margin and $3.6M of EBITDA. We would be paying for a renewing contract base and the technician workforce that services it. No asking price was provided, so this memo sets bid guidance. The primary risk is row 2, the union agreement expiring in 9 months, because a wage reset would land directly on contract margins. Both Critical findings are recorded resolved, and the consents behind row 1 are signed.

### 2. Risk table

Labels map as Critical = deal-breaker class, Moderate = manageable, Low = disclose. Only Critical rows can block the deal.

| # | Finding | Input tier and status | Memo reading | What it means for the deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Municipal contracts (15% of revenue) need change-of-control consent | Critical, Resolved | Closed: signed consents in the data room | Revenue transfers with the business |
| 2 | Union agreement expires in 9 months | Moderate, Unresolved | Open, manageable | A wage reset would compress contract margin |
| 3 | Late inspection filings at one branch | Low, Resolved | Closed by regulator letter | Disclose only |
| 4 | Largest customer (18% of revenue) may cancel on 120 days' notice | Critical, Resolved | Status accepted: nothing in the artifacts contradicts it, but the cancellation right itself remains | 18% of revenue rests on a relationship, not a term |
| 5 | Two wage claims by former technicians pending before the labour board | Moderate, Resolved | Mismatch: recorded resolved, yet described as pending; read as open, still manageable | A cost to close, not a reason to stop |
| 6 | Trademark application pending; a rival filed a similar mark earlier | Critical, Resolved | Status accepted: the earlier filing is the exposure the finding describes, not evidence that the resolution failed | Brand protection rests on counsel's opinion |

### 3. Valuation

| Comparable | In or out | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Liftway | In | 10% growth, 38% margin |
| VertiServe | In | 13% growth, 41% margin |
| Ascenda | In | 16% growth, 44% margin: upper bound only |
| UpCore | Out | 4% growth and 33% margin describe a shrinking repair book |

Base: reported EBITDA of $3.6M; no adjustments were presented and none of the revenue is one-time, so the reported figure stands. Halloway's 12% growth and 39% margin sit between Liftway and VertiServe, and nothing structural sets it apart from them, so the range is 11.0x × $3.6M = $39.6M to 12.0x × $3.6M = $43.2M. Ascenda's 14x is not used: its 16% growth is above Halloway's. The revenue cross-check agrees: 1.9x × $21.0M = $39.9M to 2.2x × $21.0M = $46.2M, because Halloway's 17.1% EBITDA margin matches the comps'. The comps give no maintenance-versus-repair split, so the multiple assumes a similar mix; that gap is a data request. With no ask on the table, the range is the bid guidance, and the union renewal in row 2 is the item most likely to move it.

### 4. Recommendation

**Proceed with conditions.** No deal-breaker-class finding is open: the municipal consents in row 1 are closed by signed documents, and rows 4 and 6 are recorded resolved with nothing contradicting them, and the remaining risks can be handled through price and structure. The union expiry in row 2 is real but bounded: its cost can be estimated before signing and priced in. Bid within $39.6M to $43.2M, holding the lower end until management's bargaining plan is in hand.

### 5. Conditions

1. Row 2: management's bargaining plan and cost estimate for the union renewal before signing; any agreed wage uplift is priced at 11.0x its EBITDA effect.
2. Row 1: written bring-down of both municipal consents at close.
3. Row 4: the document behind the Resolved status (renewal history or a signed term extension) before signing; if it shows no term, the bid moves to the low end.
4. Row 5: dismissal orders or settlement agreements for both wage claims before close; otherwise a specific indemnity from the seller.
5. Row 6: trademark counsel's opinion on the earlier filing, the document behind the Resolved status, before signing.
6. Data request: the seller's asking price, and the maintenance-versus-repair revenue split for the comps, before the bid is final.

## Example 3: nothing in the top tier, an ask above the range

The user's artifacts, summarised. Target: Fenmore Fitness Studios, a chain of 31 boutique fitness studios. Risk table: (1) leases on 6 of the 31 studios expire within 18 months; renewals are being negotiated (Manageable, In-Progress); (2) a former franchisee's claim over territory rights is pending in arbitration (Manageable, Resolved); (3) monthly membership churn rose from 3.2% to 4.1% over the year (Informational, Unresolved). No Must-Address rows. Narrative: LTM revenue $27.5M; EBITDA $4.4M; asking price $52.0M EV. The comps support 9.5x to 11.0x EBITDA. Sections 1 to 3 follow Example 1's shape; the valuation gives 9.5x × $4.4M = $41.8M to 11.0x × $4.4M = $48.4M, and the ask is 11.8x. The recommendation and conditions:

### 4. Recommendation

**Proceed with conditions.** No Must-Address finding is open, so nothing in the risk table blocks the deal; the lease renewals in row 1 and the arbitration in row 2 are open but manageable, and they are handled through conditions. The rising churn in row 3 argues for the lower half of the range. The ask of $52.0M sits $3.6M above the top of the range, so the price is the first condition, not a reason to stop.

### 5. Conditions

1. Price: bid no higher than $48.4M, the top of the defended range.
2. Row 1: signed renewals for the six expiring leases before close, or an escrow sized to their relocation cost.
3. Row 2: the arbitration award or settlement before close; otherwise a specific indemnity, since the recorded Resolved does not match a pending claim.
4. Data request: cohort churn by studio for the last 24 months, before signing.

## Readings to carry over

- A Resolved status that the artifacts contradict is read as open, with the reason in the row; a Resolved status nothing contradicts stands, and the document behind it becomes a condition.
- Fix the base before the multiple: remove one-time revenue and its margin, reject operating costs dressed as add-backs, and set aside revenue that will not transfer.
- Name every comp kept or excluded and the reason; a control-premium deal or a far-off growth outlier is excluded.
- Show multiple × base for both ends, cross-check with the other multiple, and give the ask's implied multiple and the gap in dollars.
- Only a top-tier row can block: an open one blocks a proceed call. Lower-tier rows, even open or mismatched ones, and an ask above the range become conditions, never a reason to stop.
- Your own doubts about a top-tier row recorded Resolved become a condition asking for the document behind it; only another artifact saying the closing step has not happened reopens it.
- Each condition names its row, the document that closes it, when it is due and what happens if it is missed.

Write the memo now. Its first characters are `**1. Deal thesis:**`. Do not write "Now I will draft the IC memo", "Here is the memo" or any other sentence, note, title or horizontal rule before it: the user asked for the memo, and the memo is the whole reply.
