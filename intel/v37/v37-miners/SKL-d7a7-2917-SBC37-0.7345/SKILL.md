# IC memo from three diligence artifacts

The committee uses the memo to decide, and trusts the upstream work only as far as the memo has tested it. Three artifacts arrive: a risk table (finding, tier, status), a financial narrative and a comparables set. I reconcile them, decide what the risk state permits, price what the buyer actually acquires, and say what must happen next. Every figure is in the input or computed from it, with the working shown. Where the input is silent I write that it is not provided; an invented specific (a price, date, probability, margin or comp) does more damage than an admitted gap. If a whole artifact is missing, the section that needs it says so and stays unfilled, and the recommendation cannot be Proceed.

## 1. The risk table holds claims, not facts

Tier words vary. Must-address, critical and deal-breaker gate the deal; manageable and moderate move price, structure or integration; low and informational are noted. I keep every upstream tier. Where a tier looks wrong against the narrative or the usual definitions (a breach of a licensing standard marked manageable, a shrinking customer base marked informational), the memo flags it as an observation, with its reason and what would change if the committee re-tiered it; it never re-tiers on its own.

Status is the analyst's call, tested against the rest of the input. Unresolved, In-Progress and a blank or ambiguous status are open. Resolved holds unless the input says the closing event has not happened: the narrative or an appended note reports the matter still live, or the finding shows the harm-causing matter itself still running (a pending claim, consent only "agreed in principle", a review deferred rather than closed, a re-bid under way, "substantially resolved" with no closing letter, management's belief without documents). A deferral moves a date and closes nothing. Such a row is open, and the memo says it disagrees with the table rather than overriding it silently. A standing feature the analyst judged resolved (a termination right, a licence restriction, a rival's filing, the target's own pending application, a concentration) is no contradiction: the status stands, and the paper that should back it (a signed consent or amendment, a closure letter, an executed renewal, a certificate, an audit, counsel's opinion) becomes a condition before signing.

Rows act on each other: a metric drifting below an accreditation or licensing standard feeds the licence risk; customers on short or cancellable terms weigh on price even when their row reads resolved.

## 2. What the risk state allows

I decide this before valuation, because no price cures an open gating risk.
- **Proceed** only when every must-address finding is resolved and uncontradicted and every manageable finding has a named mitigation. The memo names that mitigation itself in its conditions; a manageable row needs a remedy, not a resolution, so open manageable rows never on their own push the outcome below Proceed. With the ask above the range, Proceed at a stated ceiling.
- **Request more data** when any must-address finding is open, ambiguous or contradicted, or an artifact is missing, and the gap can be closed. "Proceed subject to" an open gating item is this outcome in softer words; never write it. The memo names the evidence that would close each item and the price that works once it does.
- **Pass** when an open item removes the thing being bought and the buyer cannot repair it (a licence, sponsor, permit or consent the business cannot run without, already refused), or the ask sits so far above the range that negotiation cannot close it.

## 3. The base: what transfers and recurs

A multiple prices recurring economics the buyer will own; rebuild the base from reported to normalised.
- One-time revenue (an implementation project, a one-off licence, catch-up billing) comes out with its margin, which flatters EBITDA too.
- Revenue that will not convey comes out or is valued apart: funding tied to the seller's legal form, contracts ending on change of control, income resting on a licence the counterparty may withhold.
- Where the company passes through other people's money (payments, marketplaces, freight), value net revenue; gross volume never takes a net multiple, and a seller's multiple quoted on gross is restated on net.
- Customers gone or under notice come out, leaving a run-rate.
- Adjusted EBITDA keeps only add-backs that are non-recurring and evidenced (a single severance package); recurring spend relabelled as strategic goes back in, and one line reconciles the seller's figure to mine. Revenue restated without auditor sign-off is shown with and without the restatement.
- Segments with different economics are valued apart when the comps allow; if the comps hide their mix, call the blend approximate.
- Revenue the narrative leaves undescribed stays in the base; a narrower base is shown only as a sensitivity. Projections that depend on prospects converting are upside, never the base.

## 4. Comps and the range

- Name the multiple and why. Price a mature business (single-digit growth) on EBITDA when the comps report it and its EBITDA margin is at or near their implied margins (a comp's EV/Revenue over its EV/EBITDA); price on revenue (or the base the comps report: net revenue, ARR) a business growing in double digits, or one whose EBITDA is negative, positive only after add-backs, or well below those margins. The other method is the cross-check; when they diverge, say which carries, why, and what the gap means for the opening price.
- List every comp as kept or excluded, one line each saying why: for a kept comp what makes it comparable (model, growth band, margin band), for an excluded one what disqualifies it. Multiples are trading multiples unless the input marks a deal price; a precedent acquisition carrying a control or sponsor premium is excluded and marks a ceiling at most. Hyper-growth outliers and NM entries drop out of the metric they distort. "No exclusions flagged upstream" is not a screen.
- State the median multiple of the kept set, then the target's direction against it on size, margin and growth (and retention, concentration or working capital where given): up, down or neutral, each with the gap in points. With no comp scale, say size cannot be measured. Net retention below 100% shrinks the base before any new sale. A weakness already taken out of the base is not charged again. The range spans the kept comps that bracket the target on growth and margin, moved toward the weaker for each weakness they don't share, with the net move from the median stated in turns (10.0x to 8x–9x), never an invented percentage; if the target is weaker than every kept comp, it spans the two weakest and the memo says value likely sits at or below its low end.
- Arithmetic stays short: low multiple × base, high multiple × base, the ask ÷ the same base, one cross-check, one downside, to one decimal rounded normally (13.76 is 13.8), each computed from figures as the memo shows them and recomputed before it is written. Test an EV ask against EV; net debt gets one sentence. No ask given: say so, never supply one.
- The downside, at the low multiple, prices the primary risk where the input quantifies it: deduct a capex need or fine; remove revenue at risk and apply the revenue multiple to the rest, labelled as revenue-based. Unquantified exposures are described, not numbered.

## 5. The memo: five sections, in this order

A heading naming the target, then exactly these five sections. Output past a fixed budget is cut, taking the recommendation and conditions with it, so the memo stays near 850 words, spent on decisions, not on restating inputs. Limits stated in the request govern; otherwise these bounds hold.
1. **Deal Thesis** (up to 100 words): what is bought, why it could be worth owning, and the primary risk: the risk-table row that most decides the recommendation.
2. **Risk Table**: one row per upstream finding, keeping its number and order, with finding (shortened), tier, status, the memo's read of that status (an unevidenced Resolved row reads "stands per table; [paper] not yet seen"), and effect on the deal (gates it, only while open; condition, price, structure, integration, note). Below it, up to 80 words of flagged observations: apparent mis-tiers, contradicted or missing statuses, links between rows.
3. **Valuation Range** (up to 280 words with the comp list and range lines): multiple, base, comps, median and directions, range, the ask's implied multiple, cross-check, downside, and valuation inputs the artifacts lack.
4. **Recommendation** (up to 80 words): Proceed, Request more data or Pass; the deciding rows; range against the ask.
5. **Conditions and Next Steps**: one bullet per risk-table row, in table order, each under 35 words, opening with its row number and naming the document, event or protection and the stage it gates. Under Proceed they are mitigations and protections (closing condition, indemnity, escrow, price deduction, covenant, retention) and the paper backing each resolved row. Under Request more data, each open row gets the evidence that would close it and each other row the mitigation it would carry. Under Pass, each says whether the row could be cured and what would bring the committee back.
