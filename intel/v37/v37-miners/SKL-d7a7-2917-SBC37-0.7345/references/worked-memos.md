# Marisol Freight Exchange: investment committee memo

## 1. Deal Thesis

Marisol runs a digital freight marketplace matching enterprise shippers with independent carriers. It bills $139.0M gross but keeps $17.6M of net revenue after paying carriers, growing 24% at a 63% gross margin on that net figure. The case for owning it is an asset-light marketplace whose enterprise shippers stay for its workflow and its insurance programme. The primary risk is that programme (R1): every enterprise contract requires the cover, one insurer provides it, and that insurer may walk away on a change of control.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Single insurer behind the cargo-liability programme may terminate on change of control | Critical | Resolved | Open: consent "agreed in principle" is not a signed consent | Gates the deal |
| R2 | Regulator's audit of broker record-keeping | Critical | In-Progress | Open: no report, no closure | Gates the deal |

Flagged observations. R1 is marked Resolved while its own wording says consent exists only in principle; the memo treats it as open and says so rather than changing the table. R3 reads light at Moderate: the concentrated shippers are the enterprise accounts that depend on R1's cover, and one shipper has already given notice. The tier is kept; re-tiered to Critical it would add a third gating row. The input does not say whether the departing shipper is one of the two.

## 3. Valuation Range

**Multiple.** EV/net revenue. Marisol is growing 24%, its EBITDA is small, and the comps report net revenue. Gross billings include carrier payments that pass straight through, so they take no multiple.

**Base.** Net revenue of $17.6M less $1.6M from the shipper under notice leaves a $16.0M run-rate. EBITDA: reported $2.5M plus the invoiced $0.7M relocation gives $3.2M; the seller's $5.1M less the rejected $1.9M of yearly marketing reconciles to it.

**Comps.**
- Brokerline, 6.0x: kept; same asset-light broker model, 22% growth, 64% margin.
- Haulmesh, 5.4x: kept; same model, slower at 18% growth, 60% margin.
- Cargoryx, 6.6x: kept; same model, 27% growth, 66% margin.
- Loadstar Digital, 9.5x: excluded; the price a logistics group paid for control, so a ceiling at most.
- Fleetbid, 11.8x: excluded; 61% growth and NM EBITDA describe another stage of company.

**Against the kept median of 6.0x.** Growth: up slightly, 24% against a median of 22% (+2 points). Margin: neutral, 63% against 64% (-1 point). Size: not measurable, as the comps give no scale. Concentration: down, two shippers at 38% with one leaving, which no comp reports. Net, the range runs from Haulmesh to the median: 6.0x down to 5.4x–6.0x.

**Range (EV/net revenue on the $16.0M run-rate).**
- Low: 5.4x × $16.0M = $86.4M
- High: 6.0x × $16.0M = $96.0M

The ask is $150.0M ÷ $16.0M = 9.4x run-rate net revenue (8.5x on the full $17.6M). The adviser's 1.1x is on gross billings, a basis no comp uses.

**Cross-check.** Implied comp EBITDA margins are 19% to 20%; Marisol's $3.2M on $17.6M is 18.2%, close enough for revenue to carry. At 27x and 31x, $3.2M gives $86.4M and $99.2M. Net cash is $2.0M.

**Downside.** If both concentrated shippers left, 38% of $17.6M is $6.7M and the base drops to $10.9M, worth $58.9M at 5.4x. The insurance and audit exposures carry no estimate. Not provided: the marketing history behind the add-back and any estimate of audit fines.

## 4. Recommendation

**Request more data.** No binding offer while R1 and R2 are open: the insurer's consent exists only in principle and the audit has produced no report. The supportable range is $86.4M to $96.0M against a $150.0M ask, about 56% above its top. Once both rows close, the deal works at no more than $96.0M; at the current ask it does not work.

## 5. Conditions and Next Steps

- **R1:** the insurer's signed change-of-control consent on final terms, before a binding offer.
- **R2:** the regulator's audit report or closure letter, with a costed plan for any findings, before a binding offer.
- **R3:** the identity of the departing shipper and the renewal status of both concentrated accounts, before a binding offer; at closing, an escrow sized to their net revenue against early loss.
- **R4:** migration plan, cost to complete and owner, before signing, for the integration budget.

# Northcove Instruments: investment committee memo

## 1. Deal Thesis

Northcove makes laboratory water-purification systems and sells the service contracts and consumables that keep them running. Of $38.0M revenue, systems bring $24.0M growing 6% at 41% gross margin, and service and consumables $14.0M growing 12% at 64%, mostly under annual contracts. The thesis is the installed base: every system sold feeds a contracted, higher-margin stream. The primary risk is supply (R3): every unit uses moulded housings from one qualified supplier.

## 2. Risk Table

| # | Finding | Tier | Status | Memo read | Effect on the deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | EU distributor (30% of revenue) held a change-of-control termination right | Must-Address | Resolved | Stands: signed waiver in the data room | Confirm at closing |
| R2 | Flagship pressure-equipment certificate lapsed during renewal | Must-Address | Resolved | Stands per table: no reissued certificate is cited, and nothing contradicts the status | Condition before signing |
| R3 | Single qualified supplier for moulded housings | Manageable | Unresolved | Open | Structure |
| R4 | Warranty claims on one filter housing; $0.4M of the fix unspent | Manageable | In-Progress | Open | Price |
| R5 | Founder leaves 12 months after closing | Informational | Unresolved | Open | Integration |

Flagged observations. R2 rests on the table's status alone, so the reissued certificate becomes a condition rather than a gate. R3 and R4 concern the same component family, and qualifying a second housing source is also the cleanest end to the warranty problem. No tier looks out of line with the narrative.

## 3. Valuation Range

**Multiple.** EV/EBITDA. Northcove is mature and profitable, growing about 8%, and its comps report EBITDA; revenue is the cross-check.

**Base.** Revenue of $38.0M carries no one-time items, and EBITDA of $6.1M is a 16.1% margin. Blended gross margin is ($24.0M × 41% + $14.0M × 64%) ÷ $38.0M = 49.5%.

**Comps.**
- Aqualis Systems, 12x: kept; lab equipment with service, 9% growth, 52% margin.
- Purity Lab Equipment, 11x: kept; same model, 7% growth, 47% margin.
- Clearbore Technologies, 13x: kept; same model, 12% growth, 55% margin.
- Veridan Water, NM: excluded; 35% growth with no meaningful EBITDA, another stage of company.

None discloses its systems and service split, so the blended comparison is approximate and the richer service mix is noted as upside, not priced.

**Against the kept median of 12x.** Growth: down, about 8% against the median comp's 9% (-1 point). Margin: down, gross 49.5% against 52% (-2.5 points) and EBITDA 16.1% against implied margins of 19.1% to 21.5%. Size: not measurable from the comps. Net, the range runs from Purity to the median: 12x down to 11x–12x.

**Range (EV/EBITDA on $6.1M).**
- Low: 11x × $6.1M = $67.1M
- High: 12x × $6.1M = $73.2M

Less the $0.4M of warranty fix still to spend: $66.7M to $72.8M. No asking price was given, so there is no implied multiple to test.

**Cross-check.** At 2.1x to 2.4x revenue the business would be $79.8M to $91.2M, above the EBITDA range because Northcove earns below the comps' margins, so EBITDA carries and the opening price sits at its low end. Net debt is $6.5M. Receivable days of 71 against 55 call for a working-capital peg in the price mechanics.

**Downside.** The supply risk carries no estimate in the input; its downside is interrupted shipments, described rather than priced.

## 4. Recommendation

**Proceed, opening at $66.7M enterprise value with a ceiling of $72.8M.** Both must-address rows are resolved and nothing contradicts them, and each manageable row carries a named mitigation below. The seller named no price, so the range rests on 11x to 12x EBITDA less the unspent warranty fix.

## 5. Conditions and Next Steps

- **R1:** bring-down confirmation of the distributor's signed waiver, at closing.
- **R2:** a copy of the reissued pressure-equipment certificate, before signing.
- **R3:** a long-term supply agreement with the housing supplier as a closing deliverable, and a covenant to qualify a second source within 12 months of closing.
- **R4:** the $0.4M deducted from price, and a specific indemnity backed by a holdback for warranty costs above it, at signing.
- **R5:** a transition and non-compete agreement covering the founder's remaining 12 months, at signing.
