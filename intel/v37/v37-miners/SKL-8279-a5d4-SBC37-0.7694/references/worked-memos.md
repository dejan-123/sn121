# Section order, length, and two memos worked in full

Use the order below for every memo. After it come three complete memos from trades unlike anything you will be asked about — the first signs on conditions, the second proceeds without any, the third is deferred at a counter-price. Borrow the habits; borrow none of the facts.

## Section order

Every memo has exactly five numbered sections in the order below, each inside its length bound; there are no others.

- First line: `# <Target>: IC memo — <the call>`. The title carries the call and is not a section.
- `### 1. Deal thesis`, 70–120 words: the business and what it earns, why own it with a figure, what the price rests on, and the main risk by name, which the risk table carries.
- `### 2. Risk table`: one row per issue in the inputs, gating items first — `Issue | Tier | Logged status / reading | Effect on the deal | Closing act` — and beneath it no more than 80 words on any status the narrative contradicts, or one sentence saying every status held.
- `### 3. Valuation range`, 120–250 words: what the memo prices on and why; peers kept and dropped; the stated adjustment and the multiple it gives; the arithmetic; the ask against the result; your own number where the ask is higher; a stress case where a live issue removes a stated slice; equity value where cash and debt are supplied.
- `### 4. Recommendation`, 60–130 words: the call, what decided it, the price, and what would change it.
- `### 5. Conditions`: one line per condition or request, each pinned to its issue with an owner and the step it must clear, about 100 words at most. When the call is to wait or walk away, the lines say what would reopen the vote.

Somewhere between 450 and 700 words is typical, table included; a clean proceed may run shorter. Give the valuation the room.

## Memo one — Ferncastle Cold Logistics (signs on conditions)

**The inputs, condensed.** Four refrigerated warehouses serving food manufacturers. Risk table, tiers Critical / Elevated / Routine: the ammonia refrigeration plant at the largest site failed its safety inspection and is under a remediation order, with the contractor on site and re-inspection booked before the planned signing date — Critical, In-Progress. The anchor tenant, a frozen-food maker holding 38% of pallet positions and contributing $3.6M of EBITDA, has given a verbal assurance that it will renew next spring — Critical, Resolved. A power-price hedge expiring in six months — Elevated, Unresolved. A forklift fleet lease — Routine, Resolved. Narrative: revenue $52.0M, of which $4.6M is energy surcharge billed to tenants at cost; EBITDA $9.0M; ask $90M. Peer set on EV/EBITDA: ColdChain Realty 11.0x; FrostPoint 9.5x; Arctica Storage 10.0x; Glacier Logistics 8.5x; Polaris Cold 14.5x, taken private by a sponsor last year at a stated premium.

# Ferncastle Cold Logistics: IC memo — proceed subject to conditions

### 1. Deal thesis

Ferncastle runs four refrigerated warehouses serving food manufacturers, and cold storage of this kind is scarce, slow to build and sticky once a tenant's production lines depend on it; all four sites are full. It earns $9.0M of EBITDA on $47.4M of operating revenue, and the price assumes those earnings continue at today's occupancy. Its main risk is concentration in the anchor tenant: 38% of pallet positions and $3.6M of the $9.0M EBITDA sit with one counterparty whose renewal exists only as a conversation.

### 2. Risk table

| Issue | Tier | Logged status / reading | Effect on the deal | Closing act |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor tenant renewal, 38% of positions, $3.6M EBITDA | Critical | Resolved / live | Two-fifths of earnings leave if it does not renew | Countersigned renewal |
| Ammonia plant under remediation order | Critical | In-Progress / live | Largest site cannot operate if re-inspection fails | Passed re-inspection certificate |
| Power hedge expiring in six months | Elevated | Unresolved / live | Energy cost exposure once the hedge lapses | Replacement hedge or tenant pass-through clause |
| Forklift fleet lease | Routine | Resolved / resolved | None | Already closed |

*Where the status column was tested.* Anchor renewal — logged Resolved; the narrative records a verbal assurance and no signed agreement. Read as live: an assurance is not a renewal, and the tenant can still change its mind. Closed by a countersigned renewal. Refrigeration remediation — logged In-Progress, which the narrative confirms; live until the re-inspection is passed. The hedge and the forklift lease are logged accurately.

### 3. Valuation range

*What we would be buying.* Four cold-storage sites; ask $90M enterprise value. Revenue of $52.0M includes $4.6M of energy surcharge passed straight to tenants at cost, so the operating revenue is $47.4M, and the ask is 1.90x that figure rather than the 1.73x it appears against the headline. Valuation runs on EBITDA of $9.0M, which the surcharge does not touch.

Warehousing is valued on earnings, so the peer set is read on EV/EBITDA. Kept: ColdChain Realty 11.0x, FrostPoint 9.5x, Arctica Storage 10.0x, Glacier Logistics 8.5x. Dropped: Polaris Cold at 14.5x, a take-private priced with a sponsor premium — a deal comp, not a trading peer.

The kept range is 8.5x to 11.0x and its median 9.75x. The peer set gives no growth figures, so no single peer is nearest; rather than guess a discount, the memo anchors on the median and lets the anchor-tenant concentration show itself in the stress case below. On $9.0M: 8.5 × $9.0M = $76.5M; 9.75 × $9.0M = $87.75M; 11.0 × $9.0M = $99.0M. The $90M ask is 10.0x, $2.25M or 2.6% above the median — fair.

Stress case, anchor gone: EBITDA falls to $5.4M, and 9.75 × $5.4M = $52.65M. The ask would then be $37.35M, or 70.9%, above value. That gap is the reason the renewal is a condition and not a hope.

### 4. Recommendation

Proceed subject to two conditions to signing. Both Critical items are live — the refrigeration plant is mid-remediation, and the anchor renewal is logged as resolved on nothing more than a verbal assurance — but each has a closing act achievable before the signing date: a passed re-inspection and a signed renewal. The price is fair at 10.0x EBITDA, a little above the middle of a clean peer set. Without the signed renewal the price cannot be defended, and the call becomes decline.

*What would change the call.* A signed renewal and a passed re-inspection make this a straightforward proceed at the asking price. Losing either turns it into a decline: no price near $90M survives the stress case.

### 5. Conditions

- Countersigned renewal from the anchor tenant — commercial lead, before signing.
- Re-inspection certificate for the ammonia plant at the largest site — operations lead, before signing.
- Replacement power hedge, or a surcharge clause covering power costs after the current hedge expires — finance lead, before completion.

## Memo two — Larkspur Roasting Co. (proceeds)

**The inputs, condensed.** A specialty coffee roaster selling to cafés and grocers. Risk table, tiers Must-Address / Manageable / Informational, with nothing logged Must-Address: green-coffee price exposure hedged for the next twelve months — Manageable, Resolved, and the narrative confirms the hedge is in place; wholesale café churn running at 6% a year — Manageable, Unresolved; the roastery lease renewing in four years — Informational. Narrative: revenue $24.0M growing 12%, gross margin 41%, EBITDA $3.4M; no customer above 9%; ask $31M. Peers, EV/Revenue and EV/EBITDA: Bean & Barrel 1.4x and 10x, growing 11%; Highland Roasters 1.2x and 9x, 8%; Ridgeway Coffee 1.5x and 11x, 13%; Copperpot 1.3x and 9.5x, 10%; Summit Brands 3.2x and 22x, 4%, a listed consumer group in which coffee is one division among many.

# Larkspur Roasting Co.: IC memo — proceed

### 1. Deal thesis

Larkspur roasts specialty coffee for cafés and grocers, growing 12% a year on $24.0M of revenue with a 14.2% EBITDA margin, and its book is spread so widely that no customer exceeds 9%. That combination — growth without concentration — is what the price is paying for. Its main risk is commodity cost: green coffee is the largest input, the current hedge covers only the next twelve months, and a year without cover would press directly on the margin the valuation rests on.

### 2. Risk table

| Issue | Tier | Logged status / reading | Effect on the deal | Closing act |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Green-coffee price exposure | Manageable | Resolved / resolved for twelve months | Margin risk once the hedge rolls off | Rolling hedge programme |
| Wholesale café churn, 6% a year | Manageable | Unresolved / live | Modest drag on growth | Retention plan post-completion |
| Roastery lease in four years | Informational | — / noted | None near term | Renewal before the lease ends |

*Where the status column was tested.* Each status was checked against the narrative and stands, including the hedge, which is documented rather than merely expected.

### 3. Valuation range

*What we would be buying.* A roaster with $24.0M of revenue and $3.4M of EBITDA, both priced as reported: nothing in the narrative is one-off, restated or tied to the current owner. Ask $31M.

Margins are similar across the roasters, so revenue multiples are a fair basis, with earnings as the cross-check. Kept: Bean & Barrel 1.4x, Highland Roasters 1.2x, Ridgeway Coffee 1.5x, Copperpot 1.3x. Dropped: Summit Brands — a diversified consumer group whose multiple reflects brands and categories Larkspur does not have.

The kept range is 1.2x to 1.5x. Larkspur grows 12%, a point from both Bean & Barrel at 11% and Ridgeway Coffee at 13%; on a tie take the cheaper peer, so Bean & Barrel's 1.4x is the anchor. Margins are close across the set, so no margin step. On $24.0M: 1.2 × $24.0M = $28.8M; 1.4 × $24.0M = $33.6M; 1.5 × $24.0M = $36.0M. The ask of $31M is 1.29x revenue, $2.6M or 7.7% below the anchor value. On earnings, Bean & Barrel's 10x gives 10 × $3.4M = $34.0M, and the ask is 9.12x EBITDA — the cross-check agrees.

### 4. Recommendation

Proceed. The table's worst entries are Manageable, the one logged Resolved is borne out by a hedge the narrative confirms, and the ask sits 7.7% below an anchored value of $33.6M. Café churn is worth tracking after completion, not a reason to wait.

*What would change the call.* A sharp rise in café churn, or a hedge that could not be rolled at reasonable cost, would be the only reasons to revisit.

### 5. Conditions

Nothing gates signing. After completion: a rolling hedge programme replacing the current twelve-month cover — finance lead, before that cover runs out.

## Memo three — Corrigan Freight Brokerage (deferred, at a counter-price)

**The inputs, condensed.** A freight broker matching shippers with trucking carriers. Risk table, graded Blocking / Watch: the surety bond that its federal broker authority requires will not be renewed by the current surety if ownership changes — Blocking, Unresolved; loads have been brokered in two states without the state broker registration those states require, and counsel estimates fines and possible suspension there — Blocking, Unresolved; revenue is booked gross, as shipper billings, while net revenue after carrier payments is the economically meaningful figure — Watch, Resolved; three shippers worth 26% of net revenue left in the last sixty days, which the seller calls "low-margin churn" — Watch, Unresolved. Narrative: gross billings $96.0M; net revenue $12.4M growing 18%; EBITDA $2.1M; the three departed shippers carried about $3.2M of annual net revenue; cash $1.6M, no debt; ask $48M, which the banker presents as 0.5x revenue. Peers on EV/net revenue, with growth: HaulBridge 5.2x, 20%; LoadLink 4.6x, 16%; FreightPilot 6.1x, 26%; Carrier Connect 3.9x, 11%; TruckMatch Global 9.0x, 45%, a technology platform rather than a broker.

# Corrigan Freight Brokerage: IC memo — defer, and offer no more than $42.3M

### 1. Deal thesis

Corrigan matches shippers with trucking carriers and earns a spread without owning a truck, so it needs little capital and its net revenue of $12.4M grew 18% last year. A buyer is paying for that spread to keep flowing from an intact book of shippers and a valid licence to broker. Its main risk is regulatory: a broker's authority rests on its surety bond and its state registrations, and both are in question at once — the bond will not renew under a new owner, and two states have no registration on file at all.

### 2. Risk table

| Issue | Tier | Logged status / reading | Effect on the deal | Closing act |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unregistered brokering in two states | Blocking | Unresolved / live | Fines and possible suspension in those states | Regulators' decisions; registrations granted |
| Surety bond not renewed on change of ownership | Blocking | Unresolved / live | No broker authority without a bond | Replacement bond from another surety |
| Three shippers gone, 26% of net revenue | Watch | Unresolved / live | A quarter of the base has already left | Account-level margins; retention of the rest |
| Revenue booked gross | Watch | Resolved / resolved | Wrong base if multiplied as revenue | Value on net revenue |

*Where the status column was tested.* Every status ties out to the narrative; the two Blocking items are logged correctly as unresolved. One claim does not: the seller's description of the lost shippers as low-margin is not supported anywhere in the narrative, so the memo makes no assumption about their margin and asks for the account-level figures instead.

### 3. Valuation range

*What we would be buying.* A freight broker; ask $48M. Gross billings of $96.0M are pass-through to carriers, so net revenue of $12.4M is the base — and $3.2M of that has already left with three shippers, leaving a forward run rate of $9.2M. At the price the memo supports, equity value is $42.32M plus $1.6M of cash, or $43.92M.

Brokers trade on net revenue, so that is the basis. Kept: HaulBridge 5.2x, LoadLink 4.6x, FreightPilot 6.1x, Carrier Connect 3.9x. Dropped: TruckMatch Global at 9.0x — a technology platform growing 45%, not a broker.

Corrigan grows 18%, two points from both LoadLink at 16% and HaulBridge at 20%; on a tie take the cheaper peer, so LoadLink's 4.6x is the anchor. On the $9.2M run rate: 3.9 × $9.2M = $35.88M; 4.6 × $9.2M = $42.32M; 6.1 × $9.2M = $56.12M. The $48M ask is 0.5x gross billings, as the banker says — but 5.22x run-rate net revenue, $5.68M or 13.4% above the value. On trailing net revenue it would read 3.87x and look cheap, which is exactly how the departed shippers disappear from view.

### 4. Recommendation

Defer. The unregistered brokering in two states is unresolved and turns on what those states' regulators decide — fines, or a suspension — which no condition can hurry. The bond problem, by contrast, closes with a replacement surety, so it will become a condition once the state position is known. On the net revenue that remains after the departed shippers, the business is worth $42.3M; the banker's 0.5x of gross billings reaches $48M only by multiplying the wrong line.

*What would change the call.* Registrations granted without suspension make this a proceed-subject-to-conditions at up to $42.3M, with the bond as the condition. A suspension in either state ends it.

### 5. Conditions

- Written positions from both states' regulators on the unregistered brokering, and the registrations granted — regulatory counsel, before any signing.
- A replacement surety bond committed for the new ownership — finance lead, as a condition once the state position is clear.
- Account-level net revenue and margin for the three departed shippers — commercial diligence lead.

Having read this file, write the memo; the five questions are answered inside it, not as notes. Its first line is the title line, with nothing before it and nothing after the conditions.
