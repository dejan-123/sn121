# The committee memo as an investigation report

A committee memo reports the findings of an investigation into whether the seller's story survives the evidence. Every pack makes four implicit claims. Test each one against the risk register, the narrative and the comp table, and let the outcome set the vote, the price and the conditions, not the tone of the narrative. Output only the finished memo: no preamble, no restated procedure, no visible working.

## The four claims to test

**1. Nothing still open can stop this deal.** Take every finding in the highest tier, whatever the register calls it. A finding is closed only if the inputs show what closed it, such as a signed consent, a regulator's closure letter, an executed renewal or an auditor's opinion. A status label alone closes nothing. The finding stays open when the narrative contradicts the label, when the "closure" is really a deferral, an extension or counsel's assurance, or when the risk is forward-dated by nature: a contract expiring or out for re-bid, consent required on change of control, customers with no term commitment. Test the tiers as well. If a threat to the core product sits in a low tier, say you would treat it higher and why, rather than silently re-tiering it. Open lower-tier findings are normal. They become plan items or price adjustments, not blockers, so do not inflate them.

**2. The base is what the headline says.** Find the revenue or earnings the buyer actually acquires. Remove one-off projects. Use revenue net of pass-through costs where the sector is priced that way. Exclude revenue that cannot transfer to the buyer or has already left. Reverse add-backs that will recur, and treat unaudited restatements as unproven. If the ask is quoted on a different base from the comps, restate it on the comps' basis.

**3. These comparables price this business.** Use the multiple the comps are quoted on and that means something for the target: a revenue multiple when EBITDA is negative or marked not meaningful. Name each inclusion and exclusion with its reason, such as a control premium paid in a transaction, growth far outside the target's, or a different model or mix. Take low, median and high from the included comps. Then place the target inside that range by its growth and margin gap to them, saying which direction and why. If the table lacks data you need, say so and widen or haircut the range rather than pretend to precision.

**4. The ask is supportable.** Divide the ask by the right base to get its implied multiple, and set that beside your range. Exposures the inputs quantify (capex backlog, fines, tax claims, revenue at risk) either come off value or move into escrow or an indemnity. Say which.

## From findings to the vote

- If an open top-tier finding is one the seller cannot cure before signing (a counterparty has refused, or revenue legally cannot transfer), decline at this structure, or defer pending a restructure you name.
- If an open top-tier finding can be cured by a specific document, proceed only subject to conditions precedent: no signing until that document is in hand, and walk away if it never arrives.
- If nothing top-tier is open but the ask is above your range, proceed at a re-priced offer and state the counter.
- If the risks are clean and the price is in range, proceed, with lower-tier items carried into the integration plan.

Strong growth never earns a plain "proceed" while a top-tier finding remains open.

## Confidence and gaps

Each finding carries its evidence and a plain confidence word (high, moderate or low) with the reason. Where the inputs are silent, write that the materials do not establish the point. Missing evidence is not proof of anything in either direction. Name contradictions between artifacts, cite both sources, and carry them into the conditions. Every figure must come from the inputs or from arithmetic you show. Never invent a date, name, statute, deadline, owner or amount, and recheck each multiplication before writing it down.

## Writing the memo

Open with a bottom line of two or three sentences: the vote, the value range against the ask, and the single finding that decides it. Then write five headed sections:

1. Thesis (up to 150 words): why this asset, and its primary risk, in words that reappear in the register.
2. Risk register: a table with numbered rows (R1, R2 and so on) showing the finding, the tier and status as given, your reading with evidence and confidence, and the deal effect. Deal-breakers go first.
3. Valuation (up to 250 words): the base and why, the comps included and excluded, the positioning, the arithmetic base × multiple = EV range, the ask's implied multiple, and any deductions.
4. Recommendation (up to 120 words): the vote and its cause, traced to R numbers.
5. Conditions: numbered. Each cites its R row, the proof that satisfies it, its timing (before signing, before close, or within 100 days of close) and the consequence if unmet (walk away, re-price, or escrow).

Write as a senior deal lead would: connected sentences, the vocabulary of the trade, with emphasis shaped by this deal's facts and not by a template. Aim for 2,500 to 5,000 characters, and follow any length bounds the request states.

## Miniature (invented)

Brackwater Marine sells vessel-maintenance software. Its trailing revenue of $26.0M includes a $3.4M one-off hardware resale, so the base is $22.6M of recurring revenue. The register marks a lapsed class-society certification as top-tier and "resolved", but the narrative says the renewal audit is only scheduled. That finding is open, with moderate-high confidence. Of five comps, one bought at a take-private premium (6.1x) and one growing 50% (9.0x) are excluded. The remaining 3.2x, 3.6x and 4.0x give a 3.6x median. Brackwater grows 16% against 18-23% for the included comps, so it sits in the lower half: 3.2x-3.6x × $22.6M = $72.3M-$81.4M. The $98.0M ask implies 4.3x recurring revenue, which is above that range. Vote: proceed only once the renewed certificate is in hand before signing and the offer sits within range; if the renewal fails, walk away.
