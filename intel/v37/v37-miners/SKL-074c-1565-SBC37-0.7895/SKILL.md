# Gatecheck — the memo is a sequence of gates, not a summary

A filed Status is a claim, a seller's revenue base is a claim, a
banker-deck multiple is a claim. This skill runs every claim
through fixed gates in fixed order and writes the memo only from
what survives.

## The gate sequence

Run the gates in order — for the whole memo, then per item.

**Gate 1 — Anchor.** Write the decision first: acquire <target> at
what terms, from these three artifacts. Note the asking price — or
write "no asking price stated" and carry that as a finding. A memo
cannot argue against an absent number; inventing one is fabrication.

**Gate 2 — Status audit.** Re-verify every Status cell in the risk
table against the narrative evidence before carrying any tier. A row
marked Resolved whose own facts show the risk open — a concentration
still running month-to-month, a restatement auditors have not opined
on, a consent never documented, a deferral that is not a closure — is
overridden: carried as open, the override named with the tiebreaker
fact. An appended analyst note flagging a contradiction is carried,
not silently resolved. A Resolved the record supports is left alone —
the audit verifies, it does not demote. The recurring override
shapes:
the risk is still live behind the label (concentration with no
contracts signed, a re-bid in flight, churn already booked); the
paper is thin (no auditor opinion, consent letter, or closure
document — self-certification standing in for an audit); the label
confuses process with outcome (under appeal, in settlement talks,
deferred review). Each override is written as "stated Resolved —
carried open: <tiebreaker fact>."

**Gate 3 — Base restatement.** Establish the valuation base before any
multiple is quoted. The seller's base is a claim. Restate it: strip
one-time revenue, exclude revenue that does not transfer at close
(grants, terminable contracts, departed customers), use the net
revenue figure where the industry's metric is net, and restate growth
on the restated base. Where EBITDA is negative or the comps are
pre-profitability, say EV/EBITDA is not meaningful and carry EV/Revenue.
Add-backs survive only if genuinely non-recurring — evergreen
items are rejected — never subtracted twice; an accepted add-back
lifts the base. Management growth
projections contingent on unsigned deals — prospects in study,
pilots not yet converted — are claims: underwrite realized growth
and post the projection as upside context, never as the priced
rate.

**Gate 4 — Comp discipline.** Every comp is included or excluded with
a named reason written into the memo. Exclude: growth/margin profile
outliers, acquisitions consummated at a control or premium price (a
banker or broker note flagging it is the tell), and comps whose
revenue mix does not map to the target's absent a stated mix
adjustment. Where the comp set's own note
declares a limitation — mix not provided, only one viable multiple —
carry the limitation explicitly rather than price around it. Choose
the multiple that survives, then compute: stated base times stated
multiple equals a range, low comp to high comp — not a point.
Compare the ask to the range in the same breath — the implied ask
multiple is itself a finding.

**Gate 5 — Recommendation logic.** The recommendation has three
states — proceed, request more data, pass — and it falls out of the
gates; it is never a mood, and it never contradicts the table state:
a proceed written over an open must-address row fails the memo.
Where gating findings stand open but a named record settles each
one, the recommendation is request more data — the memo asks for the
records and each open row carries the one that would clear it. The
count of open gates is not the test — whether a named record settles
each one is. Pass is reserved for a broken premise — the core asset
does not transfer and no document can fix that, a gating finding
whose resolution belongs to a party already on record refusing it,
or an open must-address no record can settle on the deal's own
timeline (a patent already expiring cannot be re-papered) — or an
ask past a rough kill ceiling: a third again over the band's top.
A smaller gap is a repricing ask carried in the conditions, never
a ground for pass by itself. Where nothing
must-address is unresolved, proceed is a legitimate call and
restraint applies: do not manufacture blockers to look careful, and
do not hedge a clean file. Material-but-manageable items still earn
named conditions; an item that can hurt the deal gets a condition
even when it cannot kill it. Whatever the call, the thesis names ONE
primary risk — and that exact risk appears in the risk table;
a thesis risk absent from the table fails the memo.

**Gate 6 — Conditions and asks.** The section exists under every
call; its character follows it: under proceed it carries named
mitigations for manageable rows; under request more data it carries
the asks; under pass it names what would reopen the file. Every
condition names the finding it clears, the specific record or
consent required, the party who must produce it, and the deadline
or event it must precede. "Further diligence" is not a condition.
Quantified exposures are carried in dollars — stated against the
price, not waved at.

## The vocabulary — row verdicts

Each risk row gets exactly one of these four — no fifth exists:

- `gates` — unresolved must-address/critical; blocks a clean proceed.
- `open` — evidence contradicts the stated resolution, or a named step
  must run before close; the step is written — and under proceed the
  cell carries it, so no row reads ignored.
- `stands` — status verified against the record; carried as filed.
- `noted` — informational or out of the decision path; no action spent.

## Carry every row

The memo's risk table carries every input finding — none dropped,
none merged. Rows sharing one root cause keep separate rows and
cross-reference ("compounds row N"). An under-tiered row — a
standards breach filed as routine, a churn signal filed as
informational — keeps its row and gets its real tier named in the
verdict column.

## Restraint

A file with no gating finding produces a clean recommendation,
stated plainly. Zero conditions is a valid output — the section
states so; it does not vanish.

## Missing input

Where an artifact the memo needs is absent — no asking price, a
missing status column, conflicting narratives — the absence posts as
a finding and the memo proceeds on what exists, or asks the targeted
question where nothing can honestly be produced. A specific the input
does not give gets a bracketed `[to confirm: …]`, never an invented
value.

## Deciding moves — the calls a senior reviewer checks

- **Headline by materiality, not size.** The primary risk is the one
  that can kill or reprice the deal, not the biggest number.
- **Derive the deciding number.** The load-bearing figure is often
  unprinted: the implied ask multiple, the churn-adjusted run-rate,
  the downside in dollars — compute it and show the work.
- **Direction before label.** Every restated figure states which way
  it moves the economics.
- **Seller framing is marked.** A multiple quoted on a flattering
  base gets its basis named and restated.
- **Conflicts stay conflicts.** Two cited authorities disagreeing are
  carried both ways, the resolution made a condition — the memo does
  not pick the friendlier reading.
- **"Closeable" is a document test, not a difficulty test.** A
  gating finding clears whenever a named record settles it: a
  consent, an executed renewal, a clearance, remediation evidence,
  a resolving opinion. Three such findings are three asks, never a
  pass — the count of open gates does not break the deal. A
  compliance or contract lapse with named remediation prices as an
  ask plus protection (indemnity, escrow, holdback) — not a pass —
  unless the exposure is unquantifiable and cannot be repriced.

## Writing it down

Memo shape — the five required sections, in this order:

1. `**Thesis and primary risk**` — the decision and the ask (or its
   absence) in a line, then two to four sentences on why the deal is
   worth doing at the right terms, ending in the named primary risk
   verbatim as it appears in the table.
2. `**Findings**` — the table: `# | Finding | Stated status | Gate
   verdict | Tier carried`. Every row. Overrides show the tiebreaker
   fact in the verdict cell. Then `Gate tally: <n gates / n open /
   n stands / n noted>` reconciling exactly.
3. `**The number**` — base line, comp inclusions/exclusions in one
   sentence each, the multiple chosen, `base × multiple = range`, then
   ask-versus-range. All arithmetic reproducible.
4. `**The call**` — proceed, request more data, or pass in one
   sentence plus the gating reason; it never contradicts the table.
5. `**Conditions to close**` — numbered: finding, record, party,
   timing; the character follows the call. Last line: two sentences
   the committee can repeat.

Budget: the harness cuts an overlong memo at the tail — the call and
the conditions are the tail. Hold every stated length bound; where
none is stated: thesis four sentences, rows a line each, the number
six lines, the call two sentences, each condition a line. Cut prose
before rows. Every rationale is non-interchangeable: a sentence that
could sit under another finding gets the deciding words instead.
