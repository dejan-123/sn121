# One IC memo

Read `references/worked-memo.md` once with `read_reference`. If it does not open, follow this file and do not claim that it opened. Then write one memo for the packet in front of you. The sample company's names, dates, and figures are not facts here.

Hand back exactly these headings, in this order, and no heading before the first:

## 1. Deal thesis
## 2. Risk table
## 3. Valuation
## 4. Recommendation
## 5. Conditions and next steps

The scenario states the length bound for each section. Those numbers win. Count words as you write and stop the section inside its bound. If the scenario states no number, use these ceilings: thesis 100 words, valuation 160 words, recommendation 70 words, and at most five condition bullets of 25 words each. The risk table is one row per finding, and the decision cell is at most 20 words.

A memo that runs long is cut off at the end. Sections 4 and 5 are the part that gets lost. Decide the verdict before you write the thesis, then keep sections 1 and 3 inside their ceilings so the verdict and the named conditions are on the page. Cut color before you cut a name, a row, or a multiplication. Do not add a sixth section.

## Read the three artifacts

You need a risk table, a financial narrative, and a comp set. If one of them is missing, say which one in the section that needed it and stop inventing. Do not invent an asking price, a customer, a multiple, or a comp.

The thesis names one primary risk, and that risk is a row already in the table. If any deal-breaker row is still open, that row is the primary risk. State the reason the asset can be priced and that open risk in the same paragraph. Do not write a pitch, and do not soften an open deal-breaker into a smaller concern.

## Risk table

Keep every input row, in the input's order. Copy the upstream tier word and the upstream status word. Do not replace them.

Flag, in a column of its own, and do not quietly rewrite the tier:

- The narrative disagrees with a Resolved mark. The mark stays. Add `status conflicts with the narrative`.
- The packet uses other tier words (Critical, Moderate, Low, or similar). Keep those words. Add `nonstandard tier word`.
- The status cell is blank or the column is missing. Add `missing status`.

For the recommendation only, a row is a deal-breaker when its tier is Must-Address or Critical, or the packet's own top blocking label. Moderate sits with Manageable. Low and Informational do not block, and a useful number in an Informational row is used in the valuation without promoting the tier.

A deal-breaker is open unless the status is Resolved and the narrative agrees it is closed. In-Progress, Unresolved, a blank, and a missing status are open. A prose claim such as "substantially resolved" or "deferred" is not a close when the packet has no closing document.

## Valuation

Build the range. Do not adopt the ask.

1. State the base in one sentence and show the subtraction. Use the revenue definition the comp set uses (net, not gross, when the comps say so). Remove a one-time item the narrative quantifies. Leave out revenue the narrative says cannot transfer. If the comps have no usable EBITDA column, or several are NM, the multiple is revenue and you say why.
2. List every comparable. Include it, or exclude it with one reason: growth, margin, or the multiple sits far outside the cluster; the packet itself calls the price a strategic, control, or private-equity premium; or the row has no figure in the column you are using.
3. Median the included multiples only. Then one sentence each for size, margin, and growth: above, in line, or below that median, and whether the multiple moves up or down. If the comp set has no size figure, say that no size adjustment was made.
4. Show the multiplication: base × low multiple and base × high multiple. Put the ask next to that range only when the ask is written in the packet, and show the ask as a multiple of the same base.
5. Make the figures tie. The low end, the high end, and the ask all use that one base. If the narrative offers an adjusted profit, show the bridge from the reported number, keep an add-back only when the narrative calls it one-time, and leave a continuing spend in the cost. The range follows the base you defined. The seller's adjusted figure is a reconciliation, not a second range. A price, a multiple, or a status that is not in the packet stays unfilled.

## Recommendation

Use one verdict: `Proceed`, `Request more data`, or `Pass`.

`Proceed` is allowed only when every deal-breaker is closed on the test above and every Manageable or Moderate row has a mitigation you name. Otherwise do not proceed.

If the open deal-breaker is a document, a consent, or a filing still in process, the verdict is `Request more data`. If the packet already states a refusal, a non-transferable asset, or a licence that ends at closing, the verdict is `Pass`.

## Conditions

One bullet per open deal-breaker, per row whose Resolved mark conflicts with the narrative, and per Manageable or Moderate row that still needs a mitigation. Name the row in the bullet.

- After `Proceed`, a bullet is a closing condition or the named mitigation.
- After `Request more data`, a bullet is the document or confirmation that would close that row.
- After `Pass`, a bullet is the fact that would have to change before the deal could be reopened.

Do not write a generic diligence list. A sentence that would still be true for another company does not belong.
