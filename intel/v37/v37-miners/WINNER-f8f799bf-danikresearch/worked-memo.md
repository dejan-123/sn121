Penrose Dealer Systems is an invented target, used to show one memo from packet to call. Penrose sells dealer-management software by subscription to independent car dealers, covering vehicle sales, service bookings and parts, and last year it also resold workshop diagnostic equipment once to a dealer group that was refitting its service bays. Its packet had a five-row risk register with IDs M-1 to M-5 (one status left blank), a financial narrative and five comparables. The narrative stated LTM revenue of $53.0M: $41.0M of subscriptions growing 11% at a 74% gross margin, and $12.0M from the diagnostic equipment, bought from the maker and resold once at an 8% margin. It stated "a 63% gross margin", EBITDA of $5.8M (shown as $7.6M "adjusted" after adding back $1.8M of adviser fees on a bid for a smaller rival that Penrose withdrew in May; the narrative says every fee is paid and no other bid is under way), cash of $4.0M, a $9.0M bank loan, and an ask of $176.0M, which the seller presents as 3.3x LTM revenue. It added that the comparables are software-only businesses. The complete memo written from that packet:

# Investment committee memo: Penrose Dealer Systems

## 1. Deal thesis

A dealer runs every sale, repair order and parts invoice through its management system, and few dealers change that system while the staff who use it daily stay. On the basis the peers use, we would buy $41.0M of subscription revenue growing 11% at a 74% gross margin. The other $12.0M of reported revenue came from one resale of diagnostic equipment, so a buyer does not own it. Adjusted earnings hold: the bid for the rival was withdrawn and its fees are paid, so adjusted EBITDA of $7.6M stands. The primary risk sits in M-5: a floor-plan lender resells Penrose to the dealers it finances, 19% of subscription revenue, under an agreement either side may end on 180 days' notice. The register tags that row Informational, but it moves value more than any other finding. Both top-tier rows are reported resolved and no other artifact says otherwise. The ask sits inside our range; we would proceed on conditions.

## 2. Tiered risk table

Source IDs are kept.

| ID and finding | Source tier/status | Evidence about current closure | Effective state and consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| M-1. The state motor-vehicle agency suspended Penrose's certification to file titles and registrations electronically after a data incident; it restored the certification after an independent forensic report | Must-Address / Resolved | No other artifact mentions it. The row names the suspension and the forensic report the analyst weighed; the restoration notice is not attached. | Stays resolved as reported. Does not move the call. | M-3. Connectors live before close or a post-close covenant. |
| M-4. A dealer group claims $0.6M for sales lost during a two-day outage | Moderate / Unresolved | No settlement or dismissal is mentioned. | Open, quantified at $0.6M. Carried by a specific indemnity or escrow. |
| M-5. A floor-plan lender resells Penrose to the dealers it finances, 19% of subscription revenue, under an agreement either side may end on 180 days' notice | Informational / status blank | The register gives no status, and no artifact says notice has been given. | Status unknown. Revenue at risk, not revenue lost: it stays in the base, and the lender's position is the first condition and the main price risk. |

No deal-breaker row is open: M-1 and M-2 are reported resolved, and their own text names the suspension, the reseller's right and what closed each. M-3 and M-4 need binding remedies; M-5 drives price; M-1, M-2 and M-5 need confirmation from the record.

## 3. Valuation

Metric: EV/Revenue on LTM subscription revenue, since the comparables sell software only; EV/EBITDA is the cross-check. Base: $53.0M reported − $12.0M one-time equipment resale = $41.0M of subscriptions. The lender's share stays in the base because the packet says the agreement may end, not that it has. The narrative states a 63% gross margin on all revenue and 74% on subscriptions. The memo uses the stated 74%, the margin of the revenue being valued, and builds no other figure from the two. The $1.8M add-back for the withdrawn bid stays: the narrative shows every fee paid and no other bid under way, so the cost will not recur.

| Comparable | EV/Revenue | EV/EBITDA | Growth | Gross margin | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AutoLedger | 4.8x | 25x | 14% | 76% | Included: nearest faster peer |
| DealerCore | 4.1x | 21x | 9% | 72% | Included: nearest slower peer; the median of the three |
| LotManager | 3.4x | 17x | 7% | 69% | Included: slower, same buyers |
| Garage Tools Direct | 0.5x | 7x | 3% | 18% | Excluded: a workshop-equipment wholesaler prices a different business |
| Motorhaus Software | 6.3x | 34x | 13% | 75% | Shown apart: an acquisition price, so it carries a control premium |

Penrose grows 11%, between DealerCore and AutoLedger, and its 74% margin sits between theirs. It does not trail the included peers, so the band runs from DealerCore to AutoLedger. The lender channel is a weakness the peers' figures do not show, so the memo places Penrose in the lower half of that band in words and adds no adjustment of its own. Peer sizes and channel mix are not given; that is a limit, and no size discount is claimed.

- Low: 4.1x × $41.0M = (4 × $41.0M) + (0.1 × $41.0M) = $164.00M + $4.10M = $168.1M; $168.1M ÷ $41.0M = 4.1x.
- High: 4.8x × $41.0M = (4 × $41.0M) + (0.8 × $41.0M) = $164.00M + $32.80M = $196.8M; $196.8M ÷ $41.0M = 4.8x.

Range: $168.1M to $196.8M enterprise value; equity = EV − $9.0M bank loan + $4.0M cash, so $163.1M to $191.8M. The $176.0M ask is 4.29x subscription revenue ($176.0M ÷ $41.0M), inside the band and in its lower half; the seller's 3.3x is the same price on reported revenue that includes the equipment resale. Cross-check: DealerCore's 21x and AutoLedger's 25x on $7.6M of adjusted EBITDA give $159.6M to $190.0M, and the ask is 23.2x. Principal uncertainty: the lender agreement. If notice is served, the base and the band both move down.

## 4. Recommendation

**Proceed with conditions.** No deal-breaker row is open: M-1 and M-2 are reported resolved and no other artifact contradicts either. M-3 and M-4 need binding remedies before close, and M-5 is the first condition. Price is not in dispute: the ask sits inside $168.1M to $196.8M, so the conditions protect value rather than move it. Authorised now: confirmatory diligence and drafting of the purchase agreement at the ask.

## 5. Conditions / next actions

1. Before signing, the seller provides the resale agreement and the lender's written statement that it will keep reselling Penrose after a change of control (M-5). Without it, part of the price is deferred against the lender channel's revenue in the first year.
2. Before signing, the deal team checks the agency's restoration notice and the forensic report (M-1); this confirms a reported resolution and is not a new negotiation.
3. Before signing, the deal team checks the reseller's signed release and proof of payment (M-2) against the record.
4. The purchase agreement carries a $0.6M specific indemnity or escrow for the dealer group's outage claim (M-4).
5. The seller puts both catalogue connectors live (M-3) before close, or the parties agree a post-close covenant.
6. Every earnings measure in the agreement excludes the equipment resale.

Three other versions of the evidence, and what would change.

If the narrative had said the lender served its 180 days' notice last month, that revenue would already be leaving: its share would come out of the base and the range would be rebuilt on what remains.

If the financial narrative had said the agency "restored the certification on probation and will decide on full restoration after a second audit", that artifact would contradict the register: M-1 would be disputed and open, and Section 4 would read: **Defer.** The agency's written full restoration would cure it before signing; no form of Proceed until then.

If the agency had revoked the certification and a court had upheld the revocation, no dealer could title or register a car sold through Penrose, and no seller document could change that. Section 4 would read: **No-Go.** Section 5 would list only what would reopen the deal, such as a reversed revocation.
