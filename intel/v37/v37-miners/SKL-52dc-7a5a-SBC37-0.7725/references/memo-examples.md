# Memo order, length, and worked memos

The order below is the whole memo. Three full memos follow in trades you will not be handed — one held back by an open finding, one that proceeds cleanly, and one that signs on conditions at a counter-price. Take the order, the audit and the arithmetic from them; take none of the facts.

## Order and length

The deliverable is fixed: one memo, five numbered sections in this order, each inside its bound. Nothing else is a section.

- `# <Target> — investment committee memo: <the call in a few words>` on the first line, with nothing above it. The title carries the call so a member sees it first; it is not a section.
- `### 1. Deal thesis` — 70 to 120 words. What the business does and earns, why it is worth owning with a figure, what the price takes for granted, and the primary risk by name. That risk reappears in the risk table.
- `### 2. Risk table` — every finding in the input, one row each, blocking tier first. Columns: `Finding | Tier | Status filed / as read | What it does to the deal | What closes it`. Under the table, at most 80 words: each status the narrative contradicts — what is filed, what the narrative shows, how it should be read, the document that would close it — and any finding that works through another. If every status held, one sentence says so.
- `### 3. Valuation range` — 120 to 250 words. The base and where it departs from the headline; comparables kept and dropped with reasons; the stated adjustment from the anchor comparable and the multiple it produces; the arithmetic; the ask against the result in money and percent; the price at which the memo would proceed where the ask is higher; a downside where a finding puts a stated slice at risk; equity value where cash and debt are given.
- `### 4. Recommendation` — 60 to 130 words. The call on the ladder, the finding or figure that decided it, the price, and what would change it.
- `### 5. Conditions` — one line per condition, each naming its finding, its owner and the step it must clear, then any data requests; about 100 words at most. A deferred or declined memo lists what would reopen it the same way.

Most memos land between 450 and 700 words with the table; a clean proceed can be shorter. Valuation earns the most space.

## Memo one — Ashcombe Veterinary Group (held back)

**What came in.** Eleven small-animal clinics. Risk table, tiers Must-Address / Manageable: the practice licence at the two largest sites is under review by the veterinary board after a staffing complaint, and counsel has won a 90-day deferral — filed Must-Address, Resolved. A referral agreement with a regional animal hospital worth $2.2M of annual revenue, terminable on 60 days' notice — Must-Address, Unresolved. Two clinics on month-to-month leases — Manageable, Unresolved. Practice software reaching end-of-life in 18 months with migration unbudgeted — Manageable, Unresolved. Narrative: LTM revenue $26.4M, of which $2.9M is an equipment-resale programme ending this year; recurring base growing 9%; EBITDA $3.6M with the programme and $2.4M without; no debt; ask $58M. Comparables, EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, growth: PetCare Partners 2.4x, 14x, 11%; AnimalHealth Holdings 2.1x, 12x, 8%; VetNetwork 2.7x, 16x, 14%; CompanionCare 1.9x, 11x, 6%; MetroVet Group 3.6x, 21x, 19%, acquired fifteen months ago by a consolidator at a stated control premium.

# Ashcombe Veterinary Group — investment committee memo: defer

### 1. Deal thesis

Ashcombe runs eleven small-animal clinics under one management layer in a fragmented regional market, and its recurring base of $23.5M grows 9% a year without acquisitions — the profile of a platform a buyer can add clinics to. What the price takes for granted is that the platform keeps its two largest sites, which carry most of the group's revenue. The primary risk is the licence review at those two sites: the veterinary board has deferred its decision rather than made it, and a suspension at either would remove the core the platform is built on.

### 2. Risk table

| Finding | Tier | Status filed / as read | What it does to the deal | What closes it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Licence review, two largest sites | Must-Address | Resolved / open | Platform fails if either site is suspended | Board determination in writing |
| Referral agreement, $2.2M a year, 60-day termination | Must-Address | Unresolved / open | That revenue can leave inside a quarter | Renewal, or a longer notice period |
| Two clinics month-to-month | Manageable | Unresolved / open | Relocation cost, not thesis risk | Fixed-term leases or a budgeted move |
| Practice software end-of-life | Manageable | Unresolved / open | Unbudgeted capex inside 18 months | Quoted migration plan |

*Status audit.* Licence review — filed Resolved; the narrative shows a deferral and a review still open. Read as open: a deferral postpones a decision rather than making one. Closed by the board's written determination or withdrawal of the complaint. The other three statuses were tested against the narrative and hold.

### 3. Valuation range

*The transaction.* Eleven clinics; ask $58M enterprise value. The memo values on recurring revenue of $23.5M rather than the headline $26.4M, because the seller confirms the $2.9M equipment-resale programme ends this year. Without it EBITDA is $2.4M, not $3.6M.

Kept: PetCare Partners 2.4x, AnimalHealth Holdings 2.1x, VetNetwork 2.7x, CompanionCare 1.9x. Dropped: MetroVet Group at 3.6x — a completed acquisition at a stated control premium, which records what a consolidator paid for control, not where the asset trades.

The kept range runs 1.9x to 2.7x. Ashcombe grows 9%; the kept comparable nearest that rate is AnimalHealth Holdings at 8%, so its 2.1x is the anchor, and with no margin figures for the set there is no further step. On recurring revenue: 1.9 × $23.5M = $44.65M; 2.1 × $23.5M = $49.35M; 2.7 × $23.5M = $63.45M. On earnings, 11x to 16x on $2.4M gives $26.4M to $38.4M, well beneath the revenue view and a caution on margin rather than a second answer.

The $58M ask is 2.47x recurring revenue — inside the range, but $8.65M or 17.5% above the $49.35M anchor value. On the headline $26.4M the same ask reads 2.20x and looks comfortable, which is exactly why the base matters. Downside, with the referral revenue gone: 2.1 × $21.3M = $44.73M, leaving the ask $13.27M or 29.7% above it.

### 4. Recommendation

Defer. The licence review at the two largest sites is filed as resolved on the strength of a 90-day deferral, but the board has not ruled, and those two sites carry most of the group's revenue. On the recurring base the ask also sits $8.65M above the value the comparables support. This becomes proceed-subject-to-conditions once the board rules in writing and the referral agreement is on terms the buyer can rely on.

*What would move this.* A clean determination moves the call to proceed-subject-to-conditions at a price nearer $49M. A suspension at either large site ends it.

### 5. Conditions

- Written determination from the veterinary board on both sites — deal counsel, before signing.
- Referral agreement renewed, or its 60-day notice lengthened — commercial lead, before signing.
- Seller's written confirmation that the resale programme ends, with the contribution split behind the $2.4M — financial diligence lead.
- A quoted migration plan for the practice software — operations lead, before completion.

## Memo two — Brackenridge Bakehouse (proceeds)

**What came in.** A wholesale bakery supplying supermarkets and cafés. Risk table, tiers High / Moderate / Low, with nothing filed High: a flour supply contract renewing in nine months on terms not yet agreed — Moderate, Unresolved; one delivery-van lease renewal — Low, In-Progress; a food-safety audit passed last quarter with two minor observations closed — Low, Resolved. Narrative: revenue $38.5M growing 7%, gross margin 34%, EBITDA $5.2M; top customer 14% of revenue; ask $46M. Comparables, EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, growth, gross margin: Ovenhurst Foods 1.3x, 9.0x, 6%, 32%; Crumb & Co 1.1x, 8.0x, 5%, 30%; Greystone Baking 1.5x, 10.0x, 9%, 36%; Kilnworth 1.2x, 8.5x, 7%, 33%; Artisan Rise 2.6x, 18x, 14%, 45%.

# Brackenridge Bakehouse — investment committee memo: proceed

### 1. Deal thesis

Brackenridge is a wholesale bakery supplying supermarkets and cafés on $38.5M of revenue growing 7%, and it turns that into a 13.5% EBITDA margin in a trade where scale in ovens and delivery routes decides unit cost. No customer exceeds 14% of revenue, so the book is not hostage to any one buyer, and the valuation leans on the current margin holding. The primary risk is input cost: the flour supply contract renews in nine months on terms not yet agreed, and flour is a direct cost in every loaf.

### 2. Risk table

| Finding | Tier | Status filed / as read | What it does to the deal | What closes it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flour contract renewal in nine months | Moderate | Unresolved / open | Margin pressure if terms worsen | Renewal at or near current terms |
| Delivery-van lease renewal | Low | In-Progress / in progress | Minor fleet cost | Signed renewal |
| Food-safety audit | Low | Resolved / resolved | None; observations closed | Already closed |

*Status audit.* All three statuses were tested against the narrative and hold; the audit passed and its observations are closed on the record.

### 3. Valuation range

*The transaction.* Wholesale bakery; ask $46M enterprise value. Revenue $38.5M and EBITDA $5.2M are both used as reported: nothing in the narrative is one-time, restated or non-transferable.

A bakery is priced on earnings: margins differ enough across the set that revenue multiples mislead. Kept on EV/EBITDA: Ovenhurst Foods 9.0x, Crumb & Co 8.0x, Greystone Baking 10.0x, Kilnworth 8.5x. Dropped: Artisan Rise at 18x — a premium artisan brand with a 45% gross margin and 14% growth, a different business on the very dimensions the multiple prices.

The kept range is 8.0x to 10.0x. Brackenridge grows 7%, exactly Kilnworth's rate, so Kilnworth's 8.5x is the anchor; its 34% gross margin is a point above Kilnworth's 33%, so no step is warranted. On $5.2M: 8.0 × $5.2M = $41.6M; 8.5 × $5.2M = $44.2M; 10.0 × $5.2M = $52.0M. The ask of $46M is 8.85x EBITDA, $1.8M or 4.1% above the anchor value — a fair price inside the range. As a cross-check on revenue, Kilnworth trades at 1.2x, and 1.2 × $38.5M = $46.2M; the ask is 1.19x revenue.

### 4. Recommendation

Proceed. Nothing in the table reaches its top tier, every status survives the narrative, and the ask sits 4.1% above the anchor value, inside a clean earnings range. The flour contract renewal is the one item to watch, and it belongs in the integration plan rather than among conditions to signing.

*What would move this.* A flour renewal more than a few points above current cost would erode the margin the price rests on, and would be the one reason to revisit.

### 5. Conditions

None to signing. After signing: the flour supplier's renewal terms — procurement lead, before the contract renews in nine months.

## Memo three — Brightmoor Learning (signs on conditions, at a counter-price)

**What came in.** A library of corporate compliance-training courses sold by annual licence. Risk table, tiers Must-Address / Manageable: the three largest customers, 52% of revenue, license on rolling thirty-day terms with no multi-year agreement — Must-Address, Resolved, because "the relationships are long-standing"; 140 of the courses are licensed from an author collective whose licence terminates on a change of control, with a renegotiation under way — Must-Address, In-Progress; recognition of multi-year prepaid licences moved from ratable to upfront fifteen months ago, pulling $0.9M into the last twelve months, and the auditor has not opined — Manageable, Resolved; the head of content production leaves in four months — Manageable, Unresolved. Narrative: revenue $16.0M growing 20%, gross margin 66% against a peer median of 72%; EBITDA −$0.8M, which the seller adjusts to +$1.7M by adding back $0.7M of search fees for the new chief executive and $1.8M of "strategic content investment"; ask $70M. Comparables, EV/Revenue, growth, gross margin: CourseVault 4.6x, 24%, 74%; SkillPath 3.8x, 17%, 70%; LearnGrid 4.2x, 21%, 72%; TrainRight 3.3x, 13%, 67%; MindForge AI 8.5x, 48%, 85%.

# Brightmoor Learning — investment committee memo: proceed subject to conditions, at no more than $60.5M

### 1. Deal thesis

Brightmoor licenses a library of compliance-training courses to corporate customers, and compliance training renews because regulation requires it rather than because a buyer chooses to. Revenue is $16.0M, growing 20%, on a library that costs little to resell once produced, and the price depends on that revenue staying put. The primary risk is concentration on short terms: 52% of revenue sits with three customers who license on rolling thirty-day terms and have signed nothing longer, so half the base could leave inside a month.

### 2. Risk table

| Finding | Tier | Status filed / as read | What it does to the deal | What closes it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Top three customers, 52% of revenue, thirty-day terms | Must-Address | Resolved / open | Half the revenue can leave inside a month | Multi-year agreements signed |
| Author-collective licence, 140 courses, ends on change of control | Must-Address | In-Progress / open | Core library lost at completion | Amended licence without the clause |
| Recognition change, $0.9M pulled forward, unaudited | Manageable | Resolved / open | Reported revenue may restate down | Auditor's opinion |
| Head of content production leaving | Manageable | Unresolved / open | Production slows | Named successor |

*Status audit.* Top-three customers — filed Resolved; the narrative shows rolling thirty-day licences. Read as open: a long relationship does not lengthen a notice period. Closed by multi-year agreements signed. Recognition change — filed Resolved; the auditor has not opined. Read as open, and the memo prices both bases until it does. The author licence and the departing head of content are filed accurately.

### 3. Valuation range

*The transaction.* Corporate training content; ask $70M. Revenue is $16.0M as reported and $15.1M on the recognition basis the auditor last signed. EBITDA is −$0.8M; of the seller's $2.5M of add-backs only the $0.7M search fee is one-off, because content production is the business and will continue, so defensible adjusted EBITDA is −$0.1M and an earnings multiple is not meaningful.

Earnings are negative, so revenue is the basis. Kept: CourseVault 4.6x, SkillPath 3.8x, LearnGrid 4.2x, TrainRight 3.3x. Dropped: MindForge AI at 8.5x — growing 48% at an 85% margin, a different business on both of the dimensions this multiple prices.

Brightmoor grows 20%, and the kept comparable nearest that rate is LearnGrid at 21%, so its 4.2x is the anchor. Its 66% gross margin sits six points below LearnGrid's 72%, so I take 10% off: 4.2 × 0.9 = 3.78x. On reported revenue, 3.78 × $16.0M = $60.5M; on the audited basis, 3.78 × $15.1M = $57.1M. For context, the kept low and high give 3.3 × $16.0M = $52.8M and 4.6 × $16.0M = $73.6M. The $70M ask is 4.38x reported revenue, $9.5M or 15.7% above the adjusted value; the memo's price is $60.5M, falling to $57.1M if the auditor reverses the recognition change.

### 4. Recommendation

Proceed subject to two conditions, and at no more than $60.5M against the $70M asked. Both blocking findings are open — the three largest customers can leave on thirty days' notice whatever the column says, and the author licence still ends on a change of control — but each closes with a signature the deal can demand before signing. Anchored on the nearest comparable and stepped down for margin, the business is worth 3.78x revenue, which puts the ask $9.5M, or 15.7%, too high.

*What would move this.* Signed customer agreements and an amended licence at a price near $60M make this a clean proceed. A refusal by the author collective ends it, since the library is the asset.

### 5. Conditions

- Multi-year agreements with the three largest customers — commercial lead, before signing.
- An amended author-collective licence with the change-of-control termination removed — deal counsel, before signing, with a long-stop date after which the call becomes defer.
- The auditor's opinion on the recognition change — financial diligence lead, before signing.
- A named successor for the head of content production — operations lead, before completion.

Having read this, write the memo, with no audit notes written out. Its first line is the title line: nothing comes before it, and nothing follows the conditions.
