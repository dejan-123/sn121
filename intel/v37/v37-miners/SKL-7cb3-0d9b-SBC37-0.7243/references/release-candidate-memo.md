## The shipped memo

# IC Memo: Kestrine Legal Spend

### 1. Deal Thesis

Kestrine sells e-billing and invoice-review software to corporate legal departments: $30.0M of LTM revenue, 94% subscription, growing 12% at a 70% gross margin, with EBITDA of −$2.7M. The case for owning it is embedded workflow software that legal teams rarely replace once their billing guidelines run through it. The primary risk is the Corvane reseller agreement (R1): 45% of revenue flows through it, and Corvane has written that it will not consent to assignment to this buyer. The $150M ask is 5.0x reported revenue but about 9.1x the revenue that would survive closing.

### 2. Risk Assessment

| # | Risk finding | Tier (as given) | Status (as given) | Cross-check note | Effect on the deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Corvane reseller agreement (45% of revenue) needs Corvane's consent on change of control | Must-Address | Unresolved | Narrative quotes Corvane refusing consent and ending the arrangement on closing | $13.5M of revenue (45% of $30.0M) does not transfer |
| R2 | SOC 2 Type II report for the invoice platform | Must-Address | Resolved | Consistent: issued in May with no exceptions | None beyond annual renewal |
| R3 | Single-region hosting; failover build half complete | Manageable | In-Progress | — | Outage exposure; completion cost not provided |
| R4 | 8% renewal price increase; acceptance not tracked | Manageable | Unresolved | — | Churn effect not provided |
| R5 | Founder-CEO stepping back 12 months after close | Informational | Unresolved | — | Leadership continuity |

R1 stays at its upstream tier and status. Because the refusal is quoted from Corvane's own letter, the memo reads R1 as permanent for this buyer, not pending.

### 3. Valuation

Primary multiple: EV/Revenue, because EBITDA is negative (−$2.7M) and revenue is 94% subscription; the comps carry no EBITDA multiple for a cross-check.
Base: $16.5M, the $30.0M of LTM revenue less the $13.5M sold through Corvane, which ends at closing. The seller's base is the full $30.0M.

| Comparable | Treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Brieflane Cloud (5.6x) | Included | Legal-software trading peer |
| Docketfield Systems (4.8x) | Included | Legal-software trading peer |
| Counselgrid (6.4x) | Included | Highest growth (27%), but only 5 points above the next comp |
| Invoicewell (4.2x) | Included | Legal-software trading peer |
| Ardent Legal Ops (8.8x) | Excluded | Sponsor take-private price: a control premium inside a trading set |

Included multiples in order: 4.2x, 4.8x, 5.6x, 6.4x; median (4.8x + 5.6x) ÷ 2 = 5.2x. Across the same four, median growth is 19.5% and median gross margin 74%. One step is 0.25x:
- growth, 12% against 19.5%: 7.5 points below, one step down;
- gross margin, 70% against 74%: 4 points below, one step down;
- concentration: Corvane's revenue is already out of the base and no direct customer exceeds 9%, so no step;
- retention: NRR not provided, so no step;
- size: comp scale not provided, so no step.

Running sum: 5.2x − 0.25x growth − 0.25x margin = 4.7x. Range, 4.7x ± 0.15x: 4.55x × $16.5M = $75.1M and 4.85x × $16.5M = $80.0M, so **$75.1M–$80.0M**. No debt-like items are quantified.

On the seller's $30.0M base the same 4.7x gives $141.0M, but that base includes revenue that ends at closing. The $150M ask sits $70.0M above the high end and implies about 9.1x the transferable base.

### 4. Recommendation

**Pass.** R1 is an open must-address row, and the artifacts show it is permanent for this buyer: Corvane's letter says it "will end the arrangement on closing" and refuses consent to this acquirer, and the data room names no replacement channel. That takes 45% of revenue out of the deal. The business that would transfer supports $75.1M–$80.0M, against a $150M ask.

### 5. Conditions and Next Steps

- **R1:** Deal lead to reopen only if Corvane withdraws its refusal in writing or the seller names a replacement channel with signed volume; nothing else reopens the file.
- **R2:** If reopened, legal diligence to request a current SOC 2 report; no action is needed for this decision.
- **R3:** If reopened, CTO diligence to obtain the failover plan, its cost and a completion date.
- **R4:** If reopened, finance to obtain renewal acceptance and churn data since the 8% increase.
- **R5:** If reopened, deal lead to require the founder's transition terms and a named successor before any new committee date.

## How section 5 shifts with the verdict

- **Proceed (gate green).** Bullets become closing conditions. Each risk row names its instrument: a warranty or indemnity, an escrow or holdback set at the quantified exposure, a covenant, a consent or closure letter. Day-one steps follow, and every manageable row keeps its mitigation.
- **Request more data (gate blocked, fixable).** Every open row gets the document that would clear it, who fetches it, by when, and how each possible answer would shift the verdict and the range.
- **Pass (gate blocked, permanent: a rollback note).** Bullets state what would have to change before the committee looks again. There is nothing to close, so no escrows and no closing mechanics.

## Build notes, one fact each

- **A consent clause is not a refusal.** A contract that needs consent on a change of control is something to obtain before signing. Kestrine fails because Corvane put its refusal in writing and the memo can quote it.
- **Status words that still read open.** In-Progress, "substantially resolved", "deferred" and an empty cell all count as open at the gate; give the reason in one clause.
- **A seller's multiple on the wrong base.** When a banker applies a multiple to gross figures where the sector uses net, or to revenue padded with one-time items, restate it on the correct base before comparing it with the range.
- **An input that is missing.** Write "not provided", value on what the artifacts do give, and put the request into section 5. A guess dressed as a fact breaks the build.
- **Over the length budget.** Trim adjectives and repeated context; keep every row, every exclusion reason and every line of arithmetic.

That closes the reference; ship the memo now. The reply's first characters are `# IC Memo: ` followed by the target's name. Opening instead with a line like "Now I will draft the IC memo" is the slip made most often, so the build log stays off the page.
