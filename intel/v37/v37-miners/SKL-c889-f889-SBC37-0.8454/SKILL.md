# IC memos: how they fail, and the habits that prevent it

An IC memo is a decision document. The committee needs three answers in its first lines: what you recommend, at what value, and which finding the decision turns on. Everything after that is evidence for those three answers. Weak memos are rarely badly written. They fail in a few predictable ways, and each one quietly lets the seller's framing become the memo's conclusion. Check every habit below before you write.

## Failure matrix

| Failure | How it shows | Habit that prevents it |
|---|---|---|
| Label trust | A top-tier item is marked closed, but the narrative shows it is still live (a deferral, an open re-bid, counsel's "nearly done" with no closing letter, management belief with no documents) | Re-derive every status from the narrative. Closure needs an artifact: a consent, a letter, a signed renewal or an audit opinion. Keep the given label, show your verified status beside it, and explain any difference |
| Approval past an open blocker | "Proceed", with the blocker pushed into the conditions | Any top-tier finding that is not verifiably closed caps the verdict at defer or decline. Approve with conditions only when every blocker is closed and what remains can be priced |
| Wrong base | The multiple goes on reported revenue or on the seller's adjusted earnings | Rebuild the base the buyer owns after close. Strip one-off work, pass-through volume, non-transferable funding, lost accounts, unaudited restatement uplift and add-backs that recur |
| Loose comps | All rows are averaged, and outliers stay in silently | Mark each comp in or out with a reason: a control or strategic premium paid in a sale, growth or margin far from the target, or a different business mix. Place the target against the peers you kept and move the multiple, saying which way and why |
| Unreproducible range | "$X–$Y" with no route to it | Write low multiple × base and high multiple × base, then recheck both products. Restate the ask as a multiple of the true base and give the gap |
| Orphan risk | The thesis names a risk the table lacks, or the table's worst row never reaches the thesis | The primary risk in the thesis is the table's first row and the recommendation's gating item |
| Silent gap-filling | Contradictions, missing statuses or missing comp detail are settled in the seller's favour | Name the gap, say which way it could cut, and turn it into a data request or condition |
| Invented detail | Dates, counterparties, opinions or figures that the input never gives | If the input is silent, write "not provided" and treat the item as open |

## Notes behind the matrix

**Tiers measure consequence. Status measures closure.** Group findings into three bands. Deal-breakers are risks that would destroy the thesis or the earnings base and can't be cured with price: losing a licence, regulatory authority, core IP, or the source that funds most revenue. Price-and-protect items are real but quantifiable, and escrow, indemnity, price or integration work can cure them. Watch items are the rest. Carry the input's tier over verbatim. If you think a finding is mis-tiered (for example, a threat to the core product parked in a low tier), say so openly rather than re-tiering it yourself. A severe item that is truly closed stops gating. A mild open item never gates.

**Verdict ladder.** Approve, approve with conditions before signing, defer pending named closures, or decline. Choose by the worst finding that is really open:
- **Curable with a document within weeks:** defer, and say what the IC would approve once that document lands.
- **Structural (the seller cannot cure it):** decline at the ask, or re-price on the base that survives.
- **All blockers closed but the ask above your range:** approve only up to a stated ceiling.

**Base first, multiple second.** Use the multiple that the comps and the industry support:
- **Unprofitable or subscription businesses:** a revenue multiple.
- **Profitable target and peers:** an earnings multiple.
- **Intermediaries:** revenue after pass-through costs.

If the seller quotes a multiple on another base, recompute the ask on the right one. That recomputed number is often the memo's sharpest sentence. Use a second multiple only as a cross-check, and only when the target's earnings are real and positive.

**Confidence belongs in the deliverable.** Say where the range is fragile, such as one comp setting the top end or an unaudited figure inside the base. Show how the value moves if a pending item breaks the wrong way.

## Writing it

Output only the finished memo, with no preamble, working notes or restated procedure. Open with a two-sentence decision line, then five numbered sections:
1. **Thesis:** one paragraph of about 120–180 words covering the strategic logic, the true base and the primary risk.
2. **Risk table:** columns for band, finding in your own words with the input's figures, given status, verified status, and why it matters.
3. **Valuation:** prose with the arithmetic. Cover the base, the comps kept and dropped with reasons, the positioning adjustment, the range, and the ask's implied multiple.
4. **Recommendation:** the verdict, the finding that drives it, and a ceiling or walk-away price.
5. **Conditions:** numbered, each tied to a named finding, with a test of done (what document or threshold, and whether before signing or before closing). Data requests for unfilled gaps go here too.

Length limits in the request override these. Write in sentences, not labels. The matrix vocabulary is for your reasoning, not your headings, and phrasing should change from section to section and from deal to deal. Copy every figure exactly and recompute every product and percentage. Aim for 2,500–5,000 characters.

## Worked miniature (invented deal)

Osprey Kilnworks sells kiln-control software. Reported revenue is $26.0M, but $3.0M of it is a one-off commissioning job, so the recurring base is $23.0M, growing 12%. The register lists an export-licence renewal as top tier and "resolved", but the narrative says the application is still with the regulator.

There are five comps:
- One was bought in a contested sale at 6.0x. Drop it: the multiple includes a control premium.
- One trades at 7.5x on 60% growth. Drop it: that growth makes it no peer.
- The remaining three trade at 2.8x–3.6x on 10–18% growth.

Osprey's growth sits below the middle of that group, so 2.9x–3.3x applies:
- 2.9 × $23.0M = $66.7M
- 3.3 × $23.0M = $75.9M

The $91M ask is 3.5x reported revenue but about 4.0x the recurring base.

The memo opens: "We recommend the committee defer approval. The export licence is labelled resolved, but its renewal is still pending, and without it the recurring base cannot be relied on. Once the renewal is issued we would support a bid of up to $76M. The $91M ask prices a one-off job as if it recurred." Condition 1 reads: "Issued renewal letter received before signing; no letter, no signature."
