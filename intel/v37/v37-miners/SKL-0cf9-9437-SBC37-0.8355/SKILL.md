# IC memo field guide: keep apart what the evidence keeps apart

An IC memo exists to support a decision. Committees seldom reject a memo for a missing heading. They reject it when the author has merged two things the diligence artifacts keep separate, and that merge almost always favours the seller. Read the three artifacts against each other before you write anything. The right recommendation follows from six distinctions.

## The six distinctions

**1. Severity is not closure.** The tier says how badly a finding would hurt. The status says whether it is still live. Read them together. A highest-tier finding that is unresolved, in progress, or marked resolved without proof is a gate on the deal, not a line item. A lower-tier open finding gets priced or managed. Never re-tier silently. If a tier looks wrong, keep the input's tier in the table and give your own view next to it, with the reason.

**2. A label is not a fact.** "Resolved" is a claim, so test it against the narrative. All of these are still open: a deferred review, counsel saying a matter is "nearly closed" with no closure letter, a live re-bid, management's belief with no documents behind it, a lapsed certification, and a restatement the auditors have not opined on. When two artifacts contradict each other, that contradiction is a finding in its own right. Name both sources, say which one you rely on and why, and keep the item open until a closure document exists. Going the other way, an item whose closure is genuinely evidenced stays closed. Do not inflate every row. Do note when a closed item still carries economic weight, such as high concentration on short contracts.

**3. Reported is not economic.** Before applying any multiple, establish the base the buyer actually acquires:
- Strip one-off project revenue.
- Use revenue net of pass-through costs where the industry values it that way.
- Remove revenue that cannot transfer to an acquirer, such as grants or licences that need consent.
- Haircut accounts that have cancelled, even if the seller calls them benign.
- Reject add-backs that recur (strategic spending is opex) or that rest on unaudited restatements.

Show reported and adjusted figures side by side. Then restate the seller's ask as a multiple of the adjusted base, because sellers often quote their multiple on the flattering one.

**4. Available comps are not the comp set.** Name every inclusion and exclusion and give the reason for each. Exclude:
- control-premium transactions mixed into a trading set (or show them separately);
- growth outliers far above the target;
- rows marked NM for the multiple you are using;
- businesses with a different model, for example hardware-heavy versus software.

Then place the target against the included peers on growth, margin, size, retention and revenue mix. State the direction and rough size of each adjustment. Use the multiple the comps actually report. If the target's EBITDA is negative or the comps show NM, revenue is the primary multiple and EBITDA is only a cross-check. If the comp data lacks something you need, such as mix or size, say so and widen the range rather than guess.

**5. The ask is not the value.** The low multiple times the adjusted base gives the low end, and the high multiple gives the high end. Compute and recheck every product. Then measure the ask against the range: its implied multiple on the adjusted base, and its percentage premium over the top of your range. Where a finding carries a quantified exposure (a fine, capex, lost revenue), deduct it from the value or hold it back, and say which.

**6. A condition is not a wish.** Each condition does four things:
- cites its finding number;
- names the document that would close it, such as a closure letter, a written consent, a renewed certification or an audited restatement;
- sets a gate: before signing, between signing and close, or after close with escrow;
- names a mechanism: closing condition, specific indemnity, escrow sized to the exposure, price cut, or an earnout on the uncertain revenue.

"Complete further diligence" is not a condition.

## Recommendation logic

- **Open top tier, curable.** If any highest-tier finding is open or contradicted, an unconditional proceed is off the table. If a defined document can cure it before signing, recommend proceeding only once that document is in hand.
- **Open top tier, existential or incurable.** If it threatens core revenue (loss of a licence, accreditation, funding source or network sponsor) or cannot be cured, recommend not proceeding now, or proceeding only on a restructured price or structure.
- **Ask well above the range.** Recommend against proceeding at the ask and give a counter-price from the range.
- **Top tier evidenced closed, ask within range.** Recommend proceeding, with lower-tier items carried as conditions.

## Writing the memo

Output only the finished memo. Do not write a preamble, scratch working, or a restatement of this procedure.

Open with one line that gives the verdict, the value range and the decisive finding. Then write five sections. Follow any length limits the request sets. If it sets none, use these:
- **Thesis:** 120–200 words.
- **Risk table:** one row per finding.
- **Valuation:** 200–350 words plus a compact comp table.
- **Recommendation:** 80–150 words.
- **Conditions:** three to six numbered items.

What each section does:
- **Thesis.** Say why this asset is worth buying, grounded in its own numbers. Then name the primary risk. It must be the same finding that appears as R1 in the table.
- **Risk table.** Number the findings R1…Rn, ordered by tier and then by whether they are open. Columns: finding (with its figures), input tier and status, your assessed status, evidence, and confidence (high, medium or low). After the table, write a short paragraph separating deal-breakers from items that are priced or managed.
- **Valuation.** Write it as prose with the arithmetic inline, so anyone can reproduce each number.

Write like a senior corporate-development author. Each judgement comes with its reason in the same sentence or the next. Vary the shape of your sentences from finding to finding so the memo does not read as a filled-in template.

Stay faithful to the input. Every figure must match the input or follow arithmetically from it, so recheck each product and percentage. Never supply a name, date, regulatory outcome or amount the input leaves out. Write "not provided in the diligence pack" and make obtaining it a condition. Be honest about confidence: rate it high only when two artifacts agree.

## Worked miniature (invented)

All figures are USD millions.

**Inputs.** The target is Lindqvar Freight Docs, which sells document-automation software to customs brokers.
- **R1** is highest tier and marked "resolved": a customs-authority approval to share data. The narrative says that approval was only "extended pending audit".
- **R2** is lower tier and open: two engineers have not signed their non-competes.
- **Financials.** Revenue is 23.7, which includes 2.9 of one-off migration fees. Growth is 16%. The ask is 120.
- **Comps.** Four trading peers sit at 3.4x–4.6x revenue, with growth of 12–21%. One take-private transaction sits at 7.9x.

**How the memo reads.** "We recommend holding at the signing gate. The approval the register marks resolved has only been extended pending audit, so R1 remains open and rules out an unconditional proceed."

The valuation excludes the take-private because it carries a control premium. It uses the 20.8 recurring base (23.7 less 2.9). With 16% growth sitting mid-set, a 3.6x–4.2x range gives 74.9–87.4. The 120 ask is 5.8x recurring revenue, 37% above the top of the range.

The conditions follow from the findings. Condition 1 (R1): obtain the issued approval letter before signing, or walk away. Condition 2 (R2): make signed non-competes a closing condition.
