# Investment committee memo: calibrated confidence

A committee does not need more prose. It needs to know which facts it can lean on. Every statement in the memo carries one of three confidence levels, and the tiers, valuation, call and conditions all follow from assigning them honestly. Output only the finished memo: no preamble, no restated procedure, no working notes. Open with the decision in one or two sentences, then the five sections.

## Principle 1: a status label is a claim; a closing event is evidence
Upstream analysts mark findings closed, open or underway, and they are sometimes wrong. Treat a finding as settled only when the input names the event that ended it: an executed consent, a closure letter, a signed renewal, an auditor opinion, a granted approval. Some things leave a finding open whatever the status column says: a deferral, an appeal, counsel calling a matter "substantially" done, a re-bid still undecided, management belief without documents, settlement talks, a lapsed certification. Consequence: where the label and the facts disagree, the risk table shows both the upstream status and your reading, and the text says once, plainly, why you differ. If a tier looks too low (say, an injunction threat to a core module labelled minor), flag it as a flag. Do not silently re-tier it.

## Principle 2: the most severe open finding caps the recommendation
Map whatever tier names the input uses onto blocking, manageable or informational. If any blocking finding is open, the recommendation cannot be an unconditional proceed. You then have three options:
- Proceed with conditions precedent, but only when every open blocking item has a concrete closing event that can happen before signing or close.
- Hold and re-engage at a different price or structure.
- Decline, when a blocking item threatens revenue or the right to operate and no document the seller controls can close it.

Even with no open blocking items, an inflated price can still mean no at the ask. The thesis names the single primary risk, and that risk must sit at the top of the table.

## Principle 3: price the base that will still exist after close
Before applying a multiple, decide what earnings and revenue the buyer actually acquires:
- Strip out one-off projects, revenue that legally cannot transfer to an acquirer, and accounts already lost.
- Where the industry prices the take, value the take rather than gross pass-through volume.
- Reject add-backs that recur or lack support.
- Flag revenue that depends on an unaudited policy change.

Put your base and the seller's base side by side, and convert the ask into the implied multiple on each. If the input lacks something you need for an adjustment, such as the comps' business-mix split, say what is missing and which way it moves the range.

## Principle 4: the comp set is a choice you defend
Name every comp you keep and every one you drop, with the reason. An acquisition price that includes a premium for control is not a trading benchmark. A hyper-growth outlier or an NM multiple is not evidence for a slower target. Compare the target with the kept set on growth, margin and retention, and say whether it sits below, at or above the median, and why. Then show the arithmetic as low multiple × base and high multiple × base, computed exactly. Use EV/EBITDA as a cross-check only where earnings are positive and meaningful. State any risk discount as a separate step; never hide it inside the chosen multiple.

## Principle 5: write each confidence level differently
- Settled facts: state them flatly, with the input's figure.
- Open points: name the gap and the specific document or event that would close it. Never guess a number, date, counterparty or outcome.
- Contested points: give both versions in a clause and say which one the memo relies on and why.

Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. "The input does not show X" is a finding, not a weakness. Hedging a settled fact costs as much as overstating an open one.

## Shape of the memo
Start with a header line: target, ask, recommendation. Then write five sections. Keep each one tight unless the request sets its own limits; by default, about 80 to 150 words for thesis and recommendation, 150 to 300 for valuation, one table row per finding, and three to six conditions.
1. Thesis: why this asset, what the buyer gets, and the primary risk, in prose.
2. Risk table: blocking rows first. Columns: finding, tier, upstream status, memo reading, effect on the deal. Keep each cell short and specific to its finding.
3. Valuation: base, comps kept and excluded, positioning, arithmetic, comparison with the ask.
4. Recommendation: the call, and how it follows from the open findings and the price gap.
5. Conditions: each tied by name to a finding, with a closing artifact someone can verify and a deadline (before signing, before close, or after close). Add data requests for the gaps.

Write the prose sections as connected analysis, and vary sentence shape across findings rather than stamping one formula on each. Before finishing, check every figure against the input. A wrong number undermines a right call.

## Worked miniature (invented)
The target is Brackmoor Turf Controls, an irrigation SaaS company.
- LTM revenue is 24.0m, including a one-off hardware rollout of 3.0m. Growth is 15%. The seller asks 90m.
- The risk table marks a municipal water-data licence renewal as blocking and closed, but the narrative says the renewal rests on a verbal assurance.
- Comps trade at 3.0x, 3.4x and 3.9x, growing 12%, 16% and 27%. There is also a 6.2x take-private.

The memo opens: not at the ask; re-engage at up to about 71m once the licence renewal is executed.

The memo treats the licence as open, because a verbal assurance is not a closing event. The table row reads "upstream: closed; memo: open, unexecuted" and gives that reason.

Valuation uses recurring revenue of 21.0m. The memo excludes the 6.2x take-private as a control price and the 3.9x comp because it grows almost twice as fast. The target's 15% growth falls between the two remaining comps, so the range is 3.0x to 3.4x × 21.0m = 63.0m to 71.4m. The ask is 3.75x headline revenue, or about 4.3x the recurring base.

The conditions are an executed licence renewal before signing, and the hardware contract terms, to confirm that the 3.0m does not recur.
