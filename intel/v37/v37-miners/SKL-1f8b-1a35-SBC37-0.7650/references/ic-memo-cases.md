# IC memo cases

Every deal here is made up. The memos show the form and the reasoning; every name, figure, tier and date in a real memo comes from its own three inputs, and nothing is carried over from these cases.

## Case 1: a memo that stops the deal

Inputs, in short. Larkspur Veterinary Diagnostics runs 11 veterinary reference labs. Risk table: (a) state licence for the hub lab, which handles 68% of test volume, came up for renewal; Must-Address; Resolved. (b) reagent supply agreement with the sole analyser vendor ends on change of control unless the vendor consents; Must-Address; Unresolved. (c) two regional labs are on holdover after their leases expired; Manageable; Unresolved. (d) top 10 practice groups are 34% of revenue; Informational; Resolved. Narrative: LTM revenue $26.0M, of which $3.2M came from a state surveillance contract that has ended; continuing revenue $22.8M, growing 12%; gross margin 47%; reported EBITDA $3.9M, or $3.1M without the ended contract; the seller's adjusted EBITDA of $5.6M adds back $0.9M of one-time hub relocation costs and $0.8M of "sales growth investments"; the hub licence inspection is complete and the renewal decision is expected next quarter. Ask: $70.0M, quoted by the banker as 2.7x LTM revenue. Comps: NorthPaw Labs 2.4x revenue, 14x EBITDA, 11% growth, 46% margin; VetAxis Diagnostics 2.8x, 16x, 15%, 49%; Brindlewood Animal Health 3.9x, 24x, 18%, 52%, acquired 10 months ago by a private-equity buyer; CanineCore Testing 2.1x, 13x, 8%, 43%; PetGenome AI 6.0x, NM, 40%, 71%.

The memo:

# Investment Committee Memo: Larkspur Veterinary Diagnostics

## 1. Deal Thesis

Larkspur gives us a regional reference-lab network: 11 labs, $22.8M of continuing revenue growing 12%, and a 47% gross margin in line with listed lab peers. The case rests on the hub lab, which handles 68% of test volume, staying licensed, and on keeping reagent supply from the only analyser vendor after the change of control. The reported $26.0M of revenue overstates what we would buy: $3.2M came from a surveillance contract that has ended. The deal works only if both supply-side questions close and the price is set on continuing revenue.

Primary risk: the hub lab licence (row 1). The risk table calls it Resolved, but the narrative says the renewal decision is still pending.

## 2. Tiered Risk Table

| # | Finding | Tier | Status (table → memo) | Why it matters to this deal | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hub lab state licence up for renewal (68% of test volume) | Must-Address | Resolved → treated as open: narrative says the renewal decision is expected next quarter | Without the licence most test volume stops | Blocks; condition 1 |
| 2 | Reagent supply agreement ends on change of control without vendor consent | Must-Address | Unresolved → open | Sole analyser vendor; no reagents means no tests at any lab | Blocks; condition 2 |
| 3 | Two regional labs on holdover after leases expired | Manageable | Unresolved | Landlords can end holdover at short notice; two of 11 sites | Condition 3 |
| 4 | Top 10 practice groups are 34% of revenue | Informational | Resolved | Concentration is moderate; no customer is named as at risk | Monitor |

Row 1's status conflicts with the narrative; the memo takes the narrative's reading. The $3.2M ended contract is not in the risk table but is carried into the valuation base.

## 3. Valuation Range

Base: EBITDA of $4.0M, which is the $3.1M earned without the ended contract plus the one-time $0.9M relocation; the $0.8M "sales growth investments" recur and stay in. Continuing revenue is $22.8M ($26.0M LTM less the $3.2M ended contract).

Comps included: NorthPaw Labs, VetAxis Diagnostics, CanineCore Testing. Excluded: Brindlewood Animal Health, a private-equity acquisition price with a control premium, not a trading multiple; PetGenome AI, NM on EBITDA and growing 40%, more than three times Larkspur's rate. 

Adjustments: at 12% growth and 47% margin, Larkspur sits between NorthPaw (11%, 46%) and VetAxis (15%, 49%), so they set the range; CanineCore (8%, 43%) sits below it. Comp sizes are not given, so no size adjustment.

- Low: 14x × $4.0M = $56.0M
- High: 16x × $4.0M = $64.0M

Ask: $70.0M ÷ $4.0M = 17.5x EBITDA, and $70.0M − $64.0M = $6.0M above the top of the range. The banker's 2.7x is measured on revenue that includes the ended contract.

## 4. Recommendation

**Do not proceed.** Two blocking findings are open: the hub licence (row 1), whose "Resolved" status the narrative contradicts, and the reagent supply consent (row 2). A conditional proceed is not available while either is open. The ask of $70.0M is also $6.0M above the supportable top of $64.0M. The deal can return to the committee once conditions 1 and 2 are met and the price sits inside $56.0M-$64.0M.

## 5. Conditions and Data Requests

1. Row 1, hub licence: the regulator's renewal certificate for the hub lab, from the seller, before signing.
2. Row 2, reagent supply: the analyser vendor's written consent to the change of control, from the seller, before signing.
3. Row 3, holdover leases: signed new leases or renewals for both regional labs, from the seller, before closing.
4. Valuation: monthly continuing revenue by lab for 24 months and invoices supporting the $0.9M relocation add-back, from the seller, before signing.

## Case 2: a memo that approves the deal

Inputs, in short. Hollins Marine Coatings makes antifouling hull coatings for commercial fleets. Risk table, on a Critical/Moderate/Low scale: (a) EPA re-registration of the copper biocide used in 70% of products; Critical; Resolved; the narrative cites the EPA renewal letter. (b) the Gulf ports distributor, 26% of revenue, renews each year and this year's renewal is in negotiation; Moderate; In-Progress. (c) wastewater permit renewal filed, with the current permit valid for 14 more months; Moderate; Unresolved. (d) cuprous oxide cost up 18% in 12 months, with pass-through clauses in 60% of contracts; Low; Unresolved. Narrative: LTM revenue $38.5M, growing 14%; gross margin 41%; EBITDA $6.2M (16.1%); cash $2.0M, debt $7.5M; management projects 20% growth next year if two navy-yard trials convert. No asking price is given. Comps: SeaShield Coatings 1.9x revenue, 11x EBITDA, 12% growth, 39% margin; HullGuard 2.2x, 13x, 16%, 43%; Pelagic Paints 1.6x, 9x, 6%, 35%; NanoFoul Technologies 4.8x, NM, 45%, 62%.

The memo:

# Investment Committee Memo: Hollins Marine Coatings

## 1. Deal Thesis

Hollins is a profitable coatings maker: $38.5M of revenue growing 14%, a 41% gross margin and $6.2M of EBITDA (16.1%). Its products depend on one biocide registration, which the EPA has renewed. The case is a steady cash business bought at a coatings multiple, with no payment for the navy-yard upside until those trials convert. For it to hold, the Gulf ports distributor has to renew and input-cost increases have to keep passing through to customers.

Primary risk: the Gulf ports distributor renewal (row 2), 26% of revenue, still in negotiation.

## 2. Tiered Risk Table

Tier scale as given: Critical is blocking; Moderate and Low are manageable.

| # | Finding | Tier | Status (table → memo) | Why it matters to this deal | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | EPA re-registration of the copper biocide (70% of products) | Critical | Resolved; narrative cites the renewal letter | Without it, most products could not be sold | Condition 1 (letter on file) |
| 2 | Gulf ports distributor, 26% of revenue, annual renewal in negotiation | Moderate | In-Progress → open | A quarter of revenue sits on one renewal | Condition 2 |
| 3 | Wastewater permit renewal filed; current permit valid 14 more months | Moderate | Unresolved → open | The plant can run on the current permit; the risk is in the new terms | Condition 3 |
| 4 | Cuprous oxide cost up 18%; pass-through in 60% of contracts | Low | Unresolved → open | 40% of revenue absorbs input inflation | Price; condition 4 |

Row 1 is Resolved and no other input contradicts it; its proof is condition 1. The 20% growth projection depends on two trials that have not converted, so it is left out of the base.

## 3. Valuation Range

Base: LTM EBITDA of $6.2M. It is positive and the comps carry EV/EBITDA; their revenue multiples tell the same story.

Comps included: SeaShield Coatings, HullGuard and Pelagic Paints. Excluded: NanoFoul Technologies, NM on EBITDA and growing 45%, three times Hollins's rate.

Adjustments: Hollins (14%, 41%) sits midway between SeaShield (12%, 39%) and HullGuard (16%, 43%), which set the range; Pelagic (6%, 35%) sits below it. Comp sizes are not given, so no size adjustment.

- Low: 11x × $6.2M = $68.2M
- High: 13x × $6.2M = $80.6M

No asking price is given in the inputs, so the range is bid guidance: walk away above $80.6M.

## 4. Recommendation

**Proceed with conditions.** No blocking finding is open: the EPA re-registration (row 1) is resolved and the renewal letter is cited. The open findings are manageable and each has a condition: the distributor renewal (row 2), the wastewater permit (row 3) and input-cost pass-through (row 4). Bid inside $68.2M-$80.6M and pay nothing for the navy-yard trials.

## 5. Conditions and Data Requests

1. Row 1, EPA registration: a copy of the EPA renewal letter covering every product that uses the biocide, from the seller, before signing.
2. Row 2, Gulf ports distributor: the signed renewal, or written renewal terms, from the seller, before signing.
3. Row 3, wastewater permit: the regulator's acknowledgement of the renewal filing and any draft permit conditions, from the seller, before closing.
4. Row 4, cuprous oxide: the list of contracts without pass-through clauses (40% of revenue) and their gross margins, from the seller, before signing, so the deal team can test the price against a cost case.

## Short cases: calls that turn on one fact

- **Status that is not closed.** "Substantially resolved" with no closure letter is open. A deferral of a review is not a closure. Claims described as pending are open whatever the status column says. When an analyst note says a status may be wrong, the memo shows the table's status, the reading it uses instead, and why.
- **Status that stands.** A Resolved blocking finding that no other input contradicts stays resolved, even when its own description names the original risk (a rival's earlier filing, a cancellable contract) and no closure document is cited. Its status cell reads Resolved, the call can be proceed with conditions, and the condition is the document that shows how it was resolved.
- **Tier scale.** Keep the input's own tier words in the table and state the mapping once above it: Critical or Must-Address is blocking; Moderate, Manageable and Low are manageable.
- **No blocking finding at all.** The call is proceed, or proceed with conditions; do not invent a blocker. A finding the input labels Informational can still change the price (retention below 100%, revenue that will not recur). Say it is under-tiered and carry it into the base or the conditions.
- **Gross versus net.** A business that passes most of its billings through to others (a marketplace, a payment facilitator) is valued on net revenue against net-revenue comps. Compute the ask's multiple on net as well: a $40.0M ask quoted at 2.5x a $16.0M gross figure is 8.0x a $5.0M net figure.
- **Revenue that will not transfer.** Grant income, or a licence that ends on change of control, leaves the base: a $10.0M LTM with $2.5M of grants gives a base of $7.5M. Losses the grant used to cover come back into EBITDA.
- **A share of a part.** Apply a share to the part it is quoted of: if 90% of revenue is tuition and 70% of tuition is aid-funded, aid funds 0.9 × 0.7 = 63% of revenue, not 70%.
- **Customers already lost.** When customers cancelled after the LTM period, state the run-rate without them: $12.0M less $1.8M cancelled = $10.2M. The seller's account of why they left is a data request, not a fact.
- **Seller-adjusted EBITDA.** Severance or a relocation that will not recur can be added back. "Strategic" or "growth" spending recurs and stays in. Show both figures.
- **Comps that lack what the comparison needs.** When the comps do not disclose a mix the target has (hardware against software, for example), say the margin comparison is unreliable and lean on EV/EBITDA, which the mix distorts least. Do not invent a split.
- **Revenue that is not subscription.** A recurring share describes quality, not a cut: with $20.0M of revenue, 80% recurring, the base is still $20.0M. Only what the input says ends or cannot transfer comes out.
- **No EBITDA multiple.** When EBITDA is negative or the comps give no EV/EBITDA, value on revenue alone and say why.
- **Restatement not yet opined on.** Revenue restated upward without auditor sign-off is open. State the revenue base with and without the restated amount, and make the auditor's opinion a condition.
- **Primary risk.** The thesis names the finding that most threatens the case: the open blocking finding with the largest exposure, or the largest open finding when none blocks. The table carries it under the same row number.
