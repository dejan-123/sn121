# The committee memo as a price bridge and a clock

## Core (everything else expands these five lines)
1. A status label is a reading taken at a moment. It holds only if the input proves the clock has stopped: a closure letter, signed consent, issued certificate or auditor opinion.
2. A top-tier finding still open at signing switches "proceed" off, however attractive the business.
3. A multiple applies only to the base the buyer actually acquires, never to the seller's headline.
4. Cut the comp set before using it: name every inclusion and exclusion with a reason, and place the target inside the kept range by its growth and margin.
5. Bridge the ask to the defended range in dollars and turns. That gap, together with line 2, sets the verdict.

Output only the finished memo, with no preamble, restated procedure or scratch working. Open with the decision and the one or two facts that force it, then give the reasons that carry it, then the caveats.

## Reading the clock on each finding
Take three readings for every row: the tier as labelled, the status as labelled, and the status the evidence actually supports. A "resolved" label is premature when the narrative describes a process still running: a re-bid with live competing proposals, a regulatory review that was deferred rather than closed, an appeal pending, counsel calling something "substantially" resolved without a closing document, or a consent or renewal that depends on a third party acting later. A deadline that falls inside the closing window counts as open. If a status column is blank or missing, treat the finding as open and say the artifacts do not establish closure. Absence of evidence is not evidence of resolution.

Tiers can be wrong too. A row is a deal-breaker when it threatens the revenue base the price rests on, the licence to operate, or ownership of the core asset, and money spent after close cannot cure it. It is manageable when it has a knowable cost the buyer can price, escrow, indemnify or fix after close: overdue capex, key-person retention, a tax dispute under appeal, a lease to renew. Never re-tier or re-status silently. Keep the analyst's label, put your reading beside it, and explain the difference, because the committee needs to see that disagreement.

## Measuring the base
Before any multiple, decide which dollars are being bought. Strip out one-off projects; gross payment volume or other pass-through amounts (use the revenue left after interchange and processing costs where the input gives it); revenue that cannot pass to this acquirer, such as grants or licences that need consent; customers already cancelled, at their annualised value; and restated revenue no auditor has signed. Add-backs pass only if the cost truly will not recur, such as a single departure package. Recurring spend relabelled as "strategic" does not pass. Where earnings are negative or the comps show not-meaningful, use the revenue multiple and say why. Where both exist, cross-check one multiple against the other. Divide the ask by the seller's base and by your base, and state both implied multiples.

## Cutting the ruler
Drop or quarantine comps that do not measure the same thing: control-premium acquisitions mixed into trading comps (at most a ceiling reference), growth outliers far outside the target's band, and businesses with a different revenue model or mix. Say which comp and why in one clause each. From the kept set, take the median and the spread. Place the target above the median only if it beats the kept median on both growth and margin, below it if it trails on both, and near it if the picture is mixed, saying which metric pulls which way. If the comps' business mix is unknown, value the segments separately when the input gives segment figures; otherwise widen the range and name the limitation. Range = low multiple × base to high multiple × base. Show the arithmetic, round to one decimal, and recheck every product, because a range that does not reproduce from stated numbers discredits the memo.

## Throwing the switch
- Top-tier findings closed on evidence and the ask within range: proceed, with conditions limited to integration and post-close items.
- Top-tier findings closed but the ask above range: proceed only at a re-cut price, and name the walk-away figure.
- A top-tier finding open but curable by a document before signing: do not approve signing now. Approve continued work behind gating conditions, state the price, and return to committee once they clear.
- An open finding that removes part of the base or that the buyer cannot cure: decline at these terms, or offer one concrete restructuring (carve-out, earn-out or escrow sized to the exposure) as the alternative.
Never phrase an open blocker as "proceed subject to". The word for that is hold, or no-go until.

## Writing it down
Use five headed sections in order: investment thesis, risk assessment, valuation, recommendation, conditions. A one-line decision header above them carries the verdict. The thesis argues why the asset is worth owning in prose and ends by naming the primary risk, which must appear as a row in the table. The risk table orders blockers first and open before closed, with columns for finding, tier given, status given, status on evidence, and consequence (with the input's dollar figure where one exists). Write the valuation as prose: base chosen, comps kept and dropped, placement, range, the bridge from the ask. The recommendation states the verdict, the reasons that carry it, and the confidence that is honestly warranted. Number the conditions. Each names its finding, the evidence that closes it, the timing (the input's deadline, or "before signing"), and what happens if it is not met: walk, price cut or escrow. Anything generic, such as "complete confirmatory diligence", is cut.

Obey any length bounds the request gives. Otherwise keep the thesis to about 150 words and the recommendation to 150, with the whole memo between 3,500 and 6,000 characters. Write like a deal lead talking to partners. Let words like base, gap, open and closure sit naturally inside sentences, and vary the construction from row to row and condition to condition. Every number either comes from the input or is computed from it with the working shown. Never invent dates, names, legal outcomes, probabilities or peer statistics. Where the input is silent, write "the artifacts do not show…" and turn the gap into a data request.

## Worked miniature (invented)
Tamsin Orchard Labs runs veterinary diagnostics. The ask is $150M. LTM revenue is $40.0M, of which $6.0M came from a government surveillance contract that ended last quarter. The risk table marks a state lab licence renewal as resolved, but the narrative says the inspection is scheduled for next month. That is a running clock, so the finding is open. Kept comps trade at 2.9x, 3.2x and 3.6x. A fourth at 5.0x is excluded as a control-premium purchase. Tamsin grows more slowly than the kept median but earns a higher margin, so it sits at the median: 3.0–3.4x on $34.0M of recurring revenue gives $102.0M–$115.6M. The ask implies 3.75x headline revenue and 4.41x recurring, leaving a gap of $34.4M above the top of the range. The memo opens: "Do not approve signing. The licence renewal labelled resolved still awaits inspection, and the $150M ask sits $34.4M above a defensible $102.0–115.6M range." Condition one: an issued licence certificate before signing, otherwise walk. Condition two: a price no higher than $115.6M.
