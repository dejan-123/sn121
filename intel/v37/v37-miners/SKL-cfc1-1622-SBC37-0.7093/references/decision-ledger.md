# Decision ledger and reconciliation practice

Use three short working ledgers. They are reasoning aids; the final memo should
contain the findings and calculations, not an account of filling in worksheets.

## 1. Source state versus decision state

For each finding keep:

`ID | exact source tier | exact source status or Missing | narrative support or
conflict | IC treatment | proposed owner/action | evidence and milestone`

Order the displayed table by tier while preserving IDs. Under a different source
vocabulary, add a transparent gate interpretation. A Critical finding threatening
the right to operate may map to Must-Address; a low-labeled issue with contrary
material facts needs a flagged IC reassessment rather than silent relabeling.

Distinguish three situations:

- A supplied Resolved label with no contrary fact: rely on the status for the
  gate, retaining any relevant monitoring or ordinary closing deliverable.
- A supplied Resolved label contradicted by an explicitly continuing approval
  process: retain the label, mark IC closure disputed, request the actual outcome.
- An unresolved manageable exposure with a workable proposed mitigation: name a
  role, action and milestone. That is not automatically an unresolved Must-Address.

For example, source "Must-Address / Resolved" plus a narrative saying a regulator
has merely postponed its decision is disputed closure, not regulatory clearance.
An acceptable memo shows both statements. A completed license consent with an
ordinary renewal risk should not be reopened solely because renewal is possible.

## 2. Denominator and bridge

`metric | raw amount | currency/unit | period | deductions/additions | supported
acquisition base | evidence gap`

Test accounting identities before using a measure. Sum segment revenues; compute
weighted gross margin from each segment's revenue weight; reconcile earnings
adjustments with sign intact. Retain the reported number if the bridge fails,
alongside the discrepancy and a request for reconciliation. Do not call an
arithmetic residual a proven cost saving or liability.

Illustration, unrelated to any deal: LTM revenue 24 includes a one-time project of
3, so recurring revenue is 21. If the project contribution is 1, removing it from
EBITDA 2 gives 1, not -1. A supplied recurring EBITDA of -1 therefore needs an
additional bridge of 2. It could reflect other expenses or a different definition;
the record does not establish which. A recurring revenue valuation can remain
indicative while earnings valuation awaits that reconciliation.

Do not apply annual growth to an unverified retained run rate or assume gross
margin equals contribution after customer-specific expenses. Revenue at risk is
an exposure, not automatically lost revenue; distinguish it from confirmed churn.
Where risks overlap, do not sum both losses without demonstrating disjoint bases.

## 3. Comparable selection and endpoint check

`name | relevant multiples | growth | margin | scale if known | use decision |
reason | selected-pool membership`

Sort eligible numeric values. The median of an even-sized pool is the mean of
its two middle values; ignore NM for that metric. A comp may remain in the revenue
pool while absent from the EBITDA pool. Identify which pool each median describes.
Do not mix a full-set margin median with a retained-set multiple median without
making the different populations explicit.

Illustration: eligible revenue multiples 2.4, 2.8, 3.2 and 3.6 have median 3.0x.
If a slower-growing target on recurring revenue 21 warrants a judgmental 2.4–2.8x,
EV is 21×2.4=50.4 to 21×2.8=58.8. With debt 7 and eligible cash 2, indicative equity
is 45.4–53.8. A separate downside on revenue 18 is 43.2–50.4 EV; it is not the
original range. Keep units consistent and round only after multiplication.

Last check: no source tier/status changed, no omitted comparable, no unsupported
size assumption, no multiple on the wrong metric, no unexplained earnings bridge,
no open Must-Address hidden behind Proceed, and no condition without a named row.
