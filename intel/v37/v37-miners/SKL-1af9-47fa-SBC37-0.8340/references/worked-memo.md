# One memo written out, then how it changes

The target, its findings and every figure below are invented. A real memo takes its names and numbers from its own three artifacts and computes every sum afresh; what carries over is the order of the reading, the way each cell is filled and the call words. The real target is compared only with its own comps: no name or figure from this example appears in a reply, not even as a comparison. The committee's copy starts at the memo's heading: a first line such as "Now I have the skill" or "Let me draft the memo" is a note to yourself that someone would have to delete, so the reply's first characters are `## `.

## The artifacts in brief

A request for the IC memo on Fernhollow Veterinary Group, 23 companion-animal clinics.

Risk table (finding, tier, status):
1. DEA review of the controlled-substance registrations at 4 clinics after logbook discrepancies for ketamine and hydromorphone. Must-Address, Resolved.
2. The practice-management software licence from Corvane PetSystems cannot be assigned on a change of control without Corvane's written consent; every clinic's patient records and billing run on it. Must-Address, Resolved.
3. Non-compete clauses for 11 associate veterinarians at 6 clinics are unenforceable, per local counsel; those clinics produce 22% of revenue. Manageable, Unresolved.
4. Controlled-substance logs are kept on paper at 9 clinics, with no monthly reconciliation. Manageable, Unresolved.
5. LTM revenue includes a one-time $2.7M county shelter vaccination contract, now ended. Informational, Unresolved.

Narrative: LTM revenue $38.5M, up 13%, or 8% without the shelter contract. Gross margin 43%. EBITDA $5.5M as reported; on the recurring base, without the shelter contract, EBITDA is $4.7M. The seller shows "adjusted EBITDA" of $6.5M by adding back $1.0M of associate recruiting bonuses, which the narrative says are paid every year. Cash $2.3M, debt $10.9M. On the DEA item: counsel filed a corrective action plan in March; the DEA has acknowledged it and has not closed the review. Seller asks $74.0M enterprise value.

Comps (EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, growth, gross margin): Brightpaw Pet Clinics 2.6x, 14x, 11%, 45%. Tallgrass Vet Group 2.3x, 12x, 9%, 44%. Ashgrove Animal Hospitals 2.8x, 15x, 13%, 47%. Meadowline Pet Clinics 2.0x, 11x, 6%, 40%. UrbanPet Care (bought by a private-equity sponsor 10 months ago, from the banker's book) 3.9x, 21x, 16%, 49%. Novavet Specialty Referral 4.4x, NM, 33%, 56%.

The whole reply:

## Fernhollow Veterinary Group: investment committee memo

**Terms read from the artifacts: Fernhollow Veterinary Group**
- ASK: $74.0M enterprise value, from the seller.
- BASE: revenue $38.5M − $2.7M ended shelter contract = $35.8M; EBITDA on that recurring base $4.7M, as the narrative states ($5.5M reported). The seller's $6.5M is not used: the $1.0M of recruiting bonuses recurs.
- OPEN: one must-address finding is open, the DEA review (R1); the Corvane licence (R2) stays resolved.
- STATUS CHECK: R1 is "Resolved" in the table, but the narrative says the DEA has not closed the review, so the memo carries it as open. R2 describes its exposure and nothing reopens it.
- COMPS: range Tallgrass Vet and Meadowline Pet, the two nearest on growth; quoted above the range Brightpaw Pet and Ashgrove Animal; no in-set comp sits below it; OUT UrbanPet Care (sponsor premium) and Novavet Referral (growth outlier, EBITDA NM).
- POSITION: growth 8% between Meadowline Pet's 6% and Tallgrass Vet's 9%; gross margin 43% between their 40% and 44%. The EV/EBITDA range sits wholly below the revenue range, so EV/EBITDA leads.
- GAP: the corrective action plan and any DEA reply to it are not in the artifacts.

### 1. Deal thesis: Fernhollow Veterinary Group (up to 130 words)
Fernhollow brings 23 companion-animal clinics with $35.8M of recurring revenue growing 8% at a 43% gross margin, between its two closest listed peers, as a platform for adding clinics. The primary risk is the DEA review of controlled-substance registrations at four clinics (R1): the table calls it resolved, the narrative shows it still open, and those clinics need their registrations to stock anaesthetics and pain drugs. The paper logs at nine clinics (R3) are the practice that review is testing. The second issue is price: the $74.0M ask is set on revenue that includes a contract that has ended.

### 3. Defended valuation range: Fernhollow Veterinary Group (up to 300 words)
Base: $38.5M reported − $2.7M shelter contract = $35.8M revenue; EBITDA on that base $4.7M, as stated in the narrative.
Comps: Tallgrass Vet (9% growth, 44% gross margin) and Meadowline Pet (6%, 40%) are the two comps nearest Fernhollow's 8% growth, and their margins bracket its 43%; they set the range. Brightpaw Pet (11%, 45%) and Ashgrove Animal (13%, 47%) grow faster and are quoted above it; no in-set comp sits below. UrbanPet Care is a sponsor acquisition whose 3.9x and 21x include a control premium that trading comps do not carry; Novavet Referral is a specialty business growing 33%, with EBITDA NM. Both are out.
- EV/EBITDA, which leads: 11x (Meadowline Pet) × $4.7M = $51.7M; 12x (Tallgrass Vet) × $4.7M = $56.4M. Above the range sit Brightpaw Pet at 14x and Ashgrove Animal at 15x.
- EV/Revenue, cross-check: 2.0x (Meadowline Pet) × $35.8M = $71.6M; 2.3x (Tallgrass Vet) × $35.8M = $82.3M.
EV/Revenue is the comps' first multiple, but here it gives way: the whole EV/EBITDA range sits below the revenue range, so Fernhollow earns less margin than its peers and a revenue multiple would pay for margin it does not have. Defended range: $51.7M to $56.4M.
Ask: $74.0M ÷ $35.8M = 2.07x revenue; $74.0M ÷ $4.7M = 15.7x EBITDA; $74.0M − $56.4M = $17.6M above the top of the range, and above what even Ashgrove Animal's 15x would give. On the revenue cross-check the ask sits inside $71.6M to $82.3M: the revenue multiple is what makes it look fair. On reported LTM it reads $74.0M ÷ $38.5M = 1.92x. Every value here is enterprise value.

### 4. Recommendation: Fernhollow Veterinary Group (up to 130 words)
No-go at this time. R1 is a must-address finding that is still open: the table says resolved, the narrative shows a review the DEA has not closed, and the memo relies on the narrative. It turns to go when the DEA's closure letter for all four registrations is in hand and the R3 reconciliation shows no further discrepancies. Price is a separate no: with R1 closed, $74.0M is still above the $56.4M top of the defended range, so a go would be at or below $56.4M, not at the ask.

### 5. Conditions and data requests: Fernhollow Veterinary Group (up to 45 words per item)
1. R1: the DEA's closure letter for the four registrations, from the seller's counsel, before signing, with the March corrective action plan and every DEA reply since.
2. R2: Corvane PetSystems' signed consent to assignment, obtained by the seller, before signing; without it the clinics' records and billing do not pass to us.
3. R3: a reconciliation of the paper logs at all nine clinics, delivered by the seller before signing, so that the R1 review cannot widen after close.
4. R4: retention terms signed with the 11 associate veterinarians at the six clinics, at closing; their clinics carry 22% of revenue.
5. R5: the seller's written confirmation that the shelter contract has ended with no renewal, before price is agreed.
6. The gap that matters most is whether the DEA review is closed, and the record that settles it is the DEA's own closure letter for the four Fernhollow registrations.

## How the memo changes in other cases

**No must-address open.** Had the narrative quoted the DEA's closure letter, R1's row would read "Must-Address / Resolved | Resolved, closure letter in the narrative" and OPEN would read "No must-address finding is open: R1's closure letter is in the narrative and R2 stands as resolved." R3 and R4 stay open, as manageable rows, and become conditions; they do not block. Section 4 would read: "Go, with conditions, at or below $56.4M. No must-address finding is open; R2's consent, R3's reconciliation and R4's retention terms are conditions before signing. Not at $74.0M: $74.0M − $56.4M = $17.6M above the defended range."

**A Resolved row that describes its risk.** A must-address row reading "distributor may cancel on 90 days' notice", "a rival filed first", or "the licence needs consent", marked Resolved, with no artifact saying the matter is live, keeps its status, like R2. Its condition is the document behind the resolution: the amended agreement, the clearance opinion, the consent.

**No asking price.** ASK reads "none given; no implied multiple is computed." Section 3 ends on the range, section 4 gives price guidance ("at or below $56.4M"), and no figure anywhere is called the seller's price.

**A different tier scale.** With Critical / Moderate / Low, the terms block adds "TIERS: Critical = must-address, Moderate = manageable, Low = informational", and an open Critical row blocks exactly as R1 does.

**Revenue that will not survive.** Had corporate-wellness contracts worth $2.2M given notice, the base would be the run-rate after they go: $35.8M − $2.2M = $33.6M, with reported LTM kept as the reconciliation line. An uplift from a restatement the auditors have not opined on comes out the same way. Revenue only at risk is different: had a $3.4M county contract been out for re-tender, it would stay in the base and appear once as a downside line, $35.8M − $3.4M = $32.4M if lost, with no EBITDA split the narrative does not give. Had the seller called the lost contracts low-margin while they were 6% of visits and 6% of revenue, equal shares refute the claim: low-margin work would carry a smaller share of revenue than of visits.

**Opinions in conflict, or a document missing.** Had two counsel disagreed on whether Corvane's consent is needed at all, both views go in R2's row, R2 is carried open, and a deciding opinion becomes a condition. A missing document is named as a gap and never assumed to exist.

**Comps nobody screened, or whose mix is unknown.** "No exclusions flagged" still gets a role and a reason for each comp. Had the peers' split between general practice and specialty work not been given, the COMPS line says so; with the EBITDA range below the revenue range, EV/EBITDA still leads because it prices the margin each business actually earns. Had the two ranges overlapped, EV/Revenue would lead and EV/EBITDA would only cross-check.

**EBITDA that is not really there.** Had reported EBITDA been negative, or positive only after the seller's add-backs, EV/EBITDA would be NM for Fernhollow: the range would come from EV/Revenue alone, and the memo would say why.

**Target weaker than every peer.** Had Fernhollow grown 5%, more slowly than every comp, the lowest multiple would be the top: 11x (Meadowline Pet) × $4.7M = $51.7M, with any further discount stated as judgment, not sized.

The memo is pasted into the committee pack as you send it, so the message is the memo alone. It opens on the line `## ` + the target's name + `: investment committee memo`; the reading you would otherwise jot down first goes into the terms block, the risk table's last column and section 3, where the committee can check it.
