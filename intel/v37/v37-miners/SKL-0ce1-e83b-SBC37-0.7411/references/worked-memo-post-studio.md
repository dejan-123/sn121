# Worked memo: an invented post-production studio

Fennick Studios, its counterparties and every figure below are invented. The memo shows the skill's habits on one deal; the notes after it say why each call was made. Nothing here is a template to fill.

## What the three artifacts said, in short

- Target: Fennick Studios, an independent post-production house (picture finishing, colour grading, VFX clean-up, sound mix) for streaming and broadcast series.
- Risk table, tiers A / B / C:
  1. Output deal with Orrin Streaming, 38% of revenue, expires in 7 months. Tier A, Resolved. The narrative adds: "renewal terms are still being negotiated; no signed extension is in the data room."
  2. Harlow Broadcast, 21% of revenue, may end its master services agreement on 60 days' notice. Tier A, Resolved. Nothing else is said about it.
  3. A composer's publisher claims unpaid cue-sheet royalties on two series Fennick mixed; the claim is still pending before the copyright tribunal; amount not stated. Tier B, Resolved.
  4. The head of picture personally holds the relationships with 3 of the top 5 clients; no retention or non-compete agreement. The narrative estimates that if she left, revenue would fall by about $3.7M. Tier B, Unresolved.
  5. Render farm hardware at end of life; a $1.6M refresh is not in the budget. Tier B, Unresolved.
  6. LTM includes a one-time $2.8M archive restoration contract for a film library, with no follow-on order. Tier C, Unresolved.
- Financial narrative: reported LTM revenue $31.2M, which includes $3.6M of third-party costs re-billed at cost (stock footage, dubbing talent). Growth 24% reported, 16% on net revenue excluding the restoration job. Gross margin 47% on net revenue. EBITDA $4.4M. Seller's adjusted EBITDA $6.5M adds back $1.3M for what the narrative calls a one-off studio relocation and $0.8M of "talent retention bonuses", paid in each of the last three years. The restoration job contributed $0.7M of EBITDA. Seller asks $88M enterprise value.
- Comparables (EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, growth, gross margin): Northlight VFX 2.6x, 13x, 19%, 49%. Cinder & Grain Post 2.3x, 12x, 14%, 46%. Reelhouse Media Services 2.1x, 11x, 12%, 44%. Kettering Audio Post 1.7x, 9x, 9%, 41%. Brightpost Remote 3.4x, 17x, 33%, 50%. Atlas Soundstage Group 3.9x, 21x, 17%, 50%, noted as bought by a streaming platform at a strategic premium. Pixelmoor Animation 4.4x, NM, 38%, 61%.

## The memo

# Investment committee memo: Fennick Studios

## 1. Deal thesis: Fennick Studios (max 110 words)

Fennick Studios is an independent post-production house (picture finishing, colour, VFX clean-up, sound) serving streaming and broadcast series. The case: $24.8M of net revenue after reconciliation, growing 16%, at a 47% gross margin, with a grading pipeline its clients follow. The primary risk is Finding 1, the Orrin Streaming output deal: 38% of revenue, expiring in 7 months, marked Resolved although the narrative says the renewal is still being negotiated and nothing is signed. One must-address finding is therefore open. The seller's $88M is 3.55x the $24.8M surviving base; section 3 tests that against the comps. Call: do not proceed now; defer.

## 3. Valuation range: Fennick Studios (max 450 words)

Base, priced on what survives the close (reported LTM kept as the reconciliation line):
- Reported LTM revenue: $31.2M.
- Less third-party costs re-billed at cost: $31.2M - $3.6M = $27.6M net revenue.
- Less the one-time restoration contract (Finding 6): $27.6M - $2.8M = $24.8M surviving base, growing 16%.
- Orrin, Harlow and the head of picture's clients stay in the base: all are still billing. Downside lines, not priced: Orrin $31.2M x 38% = $11.9M, so $24.8M - $11.9M = $12.9M; Finding 4's own estimate, $24.8M - $3.7M = $21.1M.

EBITDA: seller's $6.5M = $4.4M + $1.3M relocation + $0.8M retention bonuses. The narrative calls the relocation one-off, so it stays; the bonuses were paid in each of the last three years, so they recur and come out: $4.4M + $1.3M = $5.7M. Less the restoration job's $0.7M: $5.0M surviving EBITDA. The seller's adjusted figure does not hold.

Comps (gaps are comp minus Fennick, against 16% growth and 47% margin):
- Northlight VFX 2.6x / 13x (19%, 49%; gaps +3 / +2). IN: nearest on margin.
- Cinder & Grain Post 2.3x / 12x (14%, 46%; gaps -2 / -1). IN: nearest on growth.
- Reelhouse Media Services 2.1x / 11x (12%, 44%; gaps -4 / -3). IN: same services, slower.
- Kettering Audio Post 1.7x / 9x (9%, 41%; gaps -7 / -6). IN: sound only; sets the floor.
- Brightpost Remote 3.4x / 17x (33%, 50%; gaps +17 / +3). OUT: growth gap past 15 points.
- Atlas Soundstage Group 3.9x / 21x (17%, 50%; gaps +1 / +3). OUT: price paid by a streaming platform at a strategic premium; the rest are trading multiples.
- Pixelmoor Animation 4.4x / NM (38%, 61%; gaps +22 / +14). OUT: EBITDA not meaningful, both gaps past the line.

Range: 1.7x x $24.8M = $42.2M; 2.6x x $24.8M = $64.5M.

Point: IN growth runs 9% to 19%, so growth position = (16 - 9) / (19 - 9) = 0.70. IN margins run 41% to 49%, so margin position = (47 - 41) / (49 - 41) = 0.75. Position = (0.70 + 0.75) / 2 = 0.725. Point multiple = 1.7 + 0.725 x (2.6 - 1.7) = 2.35x. 2.35x x $24.8M = $58.3M.

Cross-check: 9x x $5.0M = $45.0M; 13x x $5.0M = $65.0M. The methods agree within $3M at each end, so the revenue range stands.

Ask: $88M / $24.8M = 3.55x the surviving base; $88M / $31.2M = 2.82x reported revenue; $88M / $5.0M = 17.6x EBITDA. The ask is $23.5M above the top of the range and $29.7M above the point. Finding 5's $1.6M refresh is unfunded, so any bid stays at or below the point.

## 4. Recommendation: Fennick Studios (max 110 words)

Do not proceed now; defer. One must-address finding is open: Finding 1, the Orrin Streaming output deal, whose Resolved status the narrative contradicts. Risk axis: blocked until a signed Orrin extension is in the data room. Price axis: not at $88M, which is 3.55x against a 1.7x to 2.6x range. What turns it to go: a signed Orrin extension running past close. With it, zero must-address findings would be open and the call becomes proceed, subject to the section 5 conditions, at no more than the $58.3M point, walking away above $64.5M.

## 5. Conditions and data requests: Fennick Studios (max 200 words)

1. Finding 1: a signed output-deal extension from Orrin Streaming covering the period after close, before signing; the current deal expires in 7 months.
2. Finding 2: the record behind the Resolved status, from Fennick management, before signing. Harlow's 60-day notice right stays in the agreement either way.
3. Finding 3: the tribunal's decision or the publisher's signed withdrawal, before signing. The claim amount is not given, so Fennick management states it before any escrow is sized.
4. Finding 4: a retention agreement with the head of picture, signed before close; Fennick management to state whether Orrin is one of her three relationships.
5. Finding 5: the seller funds the $1.6M render refresh or it comes off the price.
6. Finding 6: no condition; it is priced out of the base in section 3.

Most important gap: whether Orrin will sign the renewal, closed by the signed extension itself.

## Why each call was made

1. Finding 1 is open and keeps its tier. The table said Resolved; the narrative's quotable sentence says terms are still being negotiated. A counterparty's decision is still awaited, so the row says what the table says, what the text says and which one the memo uses.
2. Finding 2 is accepted. Harlow's notice right is the exposure the finding names, and it was in the contract when the upstream analyst judged it settled and marked it Resolved; the right still existing is no contradiction. No sentence can be quoted saying Harlow has given notice or is weighing it, so there is no contradiction; the record behind the Resolved status becomes a condition. A consent requirement or a concentration marked Resolved is read the same way. Had the narrative said Harlow had given notice, the finding would be open and a second blocker.
3. Finding 3 is open for the same reason as Finding 1 (the claim is still pending), but Tier B never blocks, so it becomes a condition. Its amount is not given, so no escrow figure is invented.
4. The table used A / B / C, so one line maps the tiers; only Tier A is must-address.
5. Finding 6 is informational yet sets the base; Finding 4 may bear on Finding 1. Each cross-link is said once and the tier is left alone. Whether Orrin is one of her three relationships is not given, so it becomes a data request, not an assumption.
6. Re-billed costs are not Fennick's revenue and the restoration job will not recur, so both come out. Orrin, Harlow and the head of picture's clients are at risk but still billing, so they stay in the base; an "if she left, revenue would fall by" figure is a downside line, never a deduction. Comps are matched on the 16% growth of the surviving base, not the 24% reported.
7. An add-back paid three years running is a cost of the business. Reversing it is how the seller's claim gets tested against the figures.
8. Each comp line carries its gaps before its label, so every IN or OUT can be checked. Brightpost is out on growth alone; Atlas for the premium in an acquisition price; Pixelmoor for NM EBITDA and both gaps. The range runs from the lowest IN multiple to the highest.
9. The point places Fennick inside the IN span by its own growth and margin, each step written out. Had the comps shown NRR, a third position would join the average. Had Fennick sat under Kettering on both growth and margin, the point would have been the 1.7x floor, with value possibly under the range.
10. One open must-address finding means defer, even had the price been right; price is its own axis.
11. Each condition names its finding, the record, the party and the timing the input gave; otherwise "before signing". No duration, percentage or amount is added.

## Variant: the same deal with nothing open

Had the narrative said Orrin signed a three-year extension, section 4 would read: "Proceed, subject to the section 5 conditions. Zero must-address findings are open: Finding 1 is closed by the signed extension, and Finding 2 is Resolved with nothing saying Harlow is deciding anything. Price axis: not at $88M, which is above the $64.5M top of the range; bid no higher than the $58.3M point." Had the seller given no ask, section 3 would end: "No asking price is given; the $42.2M to $64.5M range and the $58.3M point are the bid guide."
