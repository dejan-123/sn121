# Ledger memos: three invented deals

Each deal gives abridged artifacts, then the memo returned for them. Every name and figure is invented. Read for the method rather than the wording: which artifact settles a row's status, which document would close it, and how those two facts set the call, the valuation base and the conditions. Each Why cell is the ledger line in short form: the evidence, then the closer where one would end the matter.

---

## Deal 1. Copperline Waste Services: NO-GO, valued on EV/EBITDA

**Risk findings**

| Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| State environmental agency issued a notice of violation for leachate discharge at the Eastfield transfer station; the operating permit renewal is held until a consent order is signed | Must-Address | Unresolved |
| Residential collection franchise with the City of Ashby Falls expires in 8 months; the city has opened a competitive re-bid | Must-Address | In-Progress |
| Driver collective bargaining agreement expired; new three-year terms negotiated with the union | Manageable | Resolved |
| Seller's adjusted EBITDA adds back fleet-repair spend as one-time | Informational | Unresolved |
| Annualized driver turnover is 44%; 9 routes run on overtime | Manageable | Unresolved |

**Financial narrative**

- LTM revenue $56.4M, up 6%; reported EBITDA $11.8M. The Ashby Falls franchise brings in $17.5M of revenue.
- Seller's adjusted EBITDA is $13.1M after adding back $1.3M of fleet repairs as one-time; the maintenance history shows about $1.3M of repairs in each of the last three years.
- Union members have not ratified the negotiated agreement; the vote is set for next month.
- The Ashby Falls re-bid has three qualified bidders, with an award due in 10 weeks.
- Term debt $38.5M; cash $3.2M. The seller asks $139M enterprise value.

**Comparables**

| Peer | EV/Revenue | EV/EBITDA | Growth (LTM) | EBITDA Margin | Revenue (LTM) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tristate Refuse Partners | 2.0x | 9.5x | 5% | 21% | $70M |
| Kessler Collection Co. | 2.2x | 10.5x | 8% | 21% | $48M |
| Brushfield Sanitation | 1.7x | 8.5x | 3% | 20% | $35M |
| Northgate Environmental | 3.6x | 13.5x | 9% | 27% | $3.9B |

**Memo returned**

# Investment Committee Memo: Copperline Waste Services (Municipal Waste Hauling)

**Recommendation: NO-GO at this time (do not proceed to signing).** Two Must-Address findings are open, the Eastfield permit (#1) and the Ashby Falls franchise (#2), and no price cures either.

**Input check**
- Tiers already use Must-Address / Manageable / Informational; findings keep input numbers #1-#5.
- Open: #1 (Unresolved), #2 (In-Progress), #4 and #5 (Unresolved).
- #3 is labelled Resolved, but the narrative says union members have not ratified the agreement, so it is open.
- The $13.1M adjusted EBITDA adds back $1.3M of repairs that recur every year; the add-back is rejected and the base is reported EBITDA of $11.8M, a 20.9% margin on $56.4M.
- Net debt is $35.3M ($38.5M term debt less $3.2M cash). The $139M ask is 11.8x EBITDA and 2.5x revenue.

## 1. Deal Thesis (≤ 120 words)
Copperline is a regional collector growing 6% a year at a 20.9% EBITDA margin, level with its trading peers. Route density and its own transfer station give it a cost edge a platform buyer could extend. Yet the case rests on two documents the seller cannot produce: a renewed operating permit for Eastfield and a fresh Ashby Falls franchise, which carries $17.5M, or 31% of revenue. At the ask the buyer would also pay for a repair add-back that recurs.
**Primary risk:** the notice of violation at Eastfield (#1) stays open until the state agency signs a consent order, and the permit renewal waits behind it.

## 2. Tiered Risk Table
| # | Finding | Input tier / status | IC treatment | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Notice of violation at Eastfield; permit renewal held | Must-Address / Unresolved | Blocks proceed | No consent order exists; closer: the agency's signed consent order and renewed permit |
| 2 | Ashby Falls franchise (31% of revenue) under re-bid | Must-Address / In-Progress | Blocks proceed | Three bidders remain and an award date is not an award; closer: the city's award letter and executed franchise |
| 3 | Driver bargaining agreement renegotiated | Manageable / Resolved, contradicted | Condition precedent | Narrative: members have not ratified; closer: the union's ratification notice |
| 4 | Fleet-repair add-back called one-time | Informational / Unresolved, raised to Manageable | Price or structure | Raised because it sets the base: repairs recur yearly, so the $1.3M stays in costs |
| 5 | 44% driver turnover; 9 routes on overtime | Manageable / Unresolved | Post-close plan or monitor | Cost already inside reported EBITDA; retention plan within 100 days |

Deal-breakers: #1 and #2, each waiting on a public body's own document that does not yet exist. Manageable: #3 ends with a ratification vote, #4 is settled in the base, and #5 is an operating plan.

## 3. Valuation Range
EV/EBITDA is primary: EBITDA is positive and all four comps report it.
- Included: Tristate Refuse Partners (5% growth, 21% margin) and Kessler Collection Co. (8%, 21%), the two closest to Copperline's 6% and 20.9%. Brushfield Sanitation (3%, 20%) is included and sits below the bracket.
- Excluded: Northgate Environmental, a national operator about 69 times Copperline's revenue; its 13.5x pays for scale the target lacks.
- Bracket 9.5x-10.5x with no adjustment, since both bounds lie within 2 points of the target's growth and 1 point of its margin.
- Low: 9.5x × $11.8M = $112.1M. High: 10.5x × $11.8M = $123.9M.
- **Defended range: $112.1M-$123.9M EV.**
- Cross-check only: 2.0x-2.2x × $56.4M revenue = $112.8M-$124.1M, in line with the primary range.
- Ask: $139M is 11.8x EBITDA and 2.5x revenue, $15.1M (12.2%) above the top. The seller's 10.6x on $13.1M holds only with the rejected add-back.
- Equity value: EV less $35.3M net debt.

## 4. Recommendation and Consistency Check (≤ 120 words)
The call stays NO-GO because #1 and #2 are open Must-Address findings, and neither the 10-week award timetable nor a lower price changes that. What would reverse the call: the agency's consent order for #1 and the city's award for #2, both in hand; the deal could then return to IC at no more than $123.9M EV, with #3 as a condition precedent. Consistency: primary risk #1 is the first table row, and the range rests on the $11.8M base throughout.

## 5. Conditions and Data Requests
1. #1: the state agency's signed consent order and the renewed Eastfield operating permit, from the seller's environmental counsel, before any return to IC.
2. #2: the City of Ashby Falls award letter and executed franchise agreement, via the seller from the city clerk, within the 10-week re-bid window.
3. #3: the union's ratification notice for the bargaining agreement, from the union local, before signing.
4. #4: three years of fleet maintenance ledgers by truck, from the seller's controller, within 2 weeks, to confirm the $11.8M base.
5. #5: driver turnover and overtime by route for the last 12 months, from the seller's HR lead, within 3 weeks, to size the retention plan.

## Deal 2. Larkspur & Vine Hotel Group: PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS

**Risk table, abridged**

| Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| City fire marshal suspended the occupancy certificate at the flagship Calloway House after an inspection found blocked egress routes | Must-Address | Resolved |

| Ground lease at the Wharf Street property has 6 years left; the landlord has not agreed to the 25-year extension the plan assumes | Manageable | Resolved |

**Narrative points**

- Six boutique hotels; LTM revenue $36.8M (rooms 64%, food and beverage 36%), up 11%; consolidated EBITDA $7.2M.
- The fire marshal reinstated the Calloway House certificate after a re-inspection; the reinstatement letter is in the data room.
- The property schedule sums to $7.6M of EBITDA, with no bridge to the consolidated figure. Wharf Street earns $1.6M.
- Mortgage debt $21.6M; cash $3.3M. The seller asks $106M enterprise value.

**Comp set supplied**

|---|---|---|---|---|

**The memo**

# Investment Committee Memo: Larkspur & Vine Hotel Group (Boutique Hotels)

**Recommendation: PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS at no more than $101.0M EV, the top of the defended range, against a $106M ask.** No Must-Address finding is open, and the questioned Wharf Street lease (#3) must close before signing.

**Input check**
- The tier words are already the house scale; findings stay numbered #1-#3.
- #1 Resolved is confirmed: the narrative cites the fire marshal's reinstatement letter.
- #3 is Resolved, yet its wording still says the landlord has not agreed, and no other artifact shows an extension: questioned.
- #2 is open, with a quantified $3.4M of unfunded capex.
- EBITDA does not reconcile: $7.6M by property against $7.2M consolidated. The lower $7.2M is the base (19.6% margin on $36.8M); the bridge is requested.
- Wharf Street earns $1.6M, 22% of EBITDA. Net debt $18.3M ($21.6M mortgages less $3.3M cash).

## 1. Deal Thesis (≤ 120 words)
Larkspur & Vine runs six boutique hotels growing 11% a year at a 19.6% EBITDA margin, close to its listed peers. Food and beverage, 36% of revenue, gives it a second earnings line that rooms-only operators lack. The flagship's fire-safety suspension is closed by the regulator's own letter, so the case turns on what the buyer will own: tenure at Wharf Street and deferred plant at two hotels. Priced at the top of the comp bracket less the $3.4M of capex, the group earns its multiple without leaning on projections.
**Primary risk:** the Wharf Street ground lease (#3) has six years left and no signed extension, while that hotel carries 22% of EBITDA.

## 2. Tiered Risk Table
| # | Finding | Input tier / status | IC treatment | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Wharf Street ground lease: 6 years left, extension not agreed | Manageable / Resolved, questioned | Condition precedent | Wording still reads live and nothing confirms an extension; closer: the landlord's executed lease amendment |
| 1 | Calloway House certificate suspended by the fire marshal | Must-Address / Resolved | Post-close plan or monitor | Accepted: the reinstatement letter is the regulator's own document; monitor the next annual inspection |
| 2 | HVAC and elevator work at two hotels, unbudgeted | Manageable / Unresolved | Price or structure | Quantified and unfunded, so the $3.4M comes off EV; closer: fixed-price contractor bids |

No deal-breaker remains open: #1 was one, and the fire marshal's letter closed it. Manageable: #3 is settled by the landlord's signature before signing, and #2 is settled in price.

## 3. Valuation Range
EV/EBITDA is primary: EBITDA is positive and every comp reports it.
- Included: Calder Boutique Hospitality (10% growth, 20% margin) and Saltmarsh Inns (9%, 20%), nearest to the target's 11% and 19.6%. Ellery Hotel Collection (6%, 19%) stays in the set but lies further out on growth.
- Excluded: Pinecrest Lodging, a sponsor buyout whose 17.5x includes a control premium that trading peers do not carry.
- Bracket 13.0x-14.5x, unadjusted: both bounds lie within 2 points of the target's growth and 1 point of its margin.
- Low: 13.0x × $7.2M = $93.6M. High: 14.5x × $7.2M = $104.4M.
- Less $3.4M unfunded capex (#2): $90.2M low, $101.0M high.
- **Defended range: $90.2M-$101.0M EV.**
- Cross-check only: 2.6x-2.9x × $36.8M revenue = $95.7M-$106.7M before capex, consistent with the primary products.
- Ask: $106M is 14.7x EBITDA and 2.9x revenue, $5.0M (5.0%) above the top; the offer stops at $101.0M.
- Equity value: EV less $18.3M net debt.

## 4. Recommendation and Consistency Check (≤ 120 words)
PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS, capped at $101.0M EV: the range top, and $5.0M under the ask. The call holds because no Must-Address finding is open: #1 is closed by the fire marshal's letter, not by the seller's word. It is not unconditional, because #3 stands as a condition precedent. Condition whose failure would reverse the call: the landlord's executed Wharf Street amendment (#3); without it, 22% of EBITDA rests on six years of tenure and the deal returns to IC as NO-GO. Consistency: primary risk #3 leads the table, and the price sits at the range top.

## 5. Conditions and Data Requests
1. #3: the executed Wharf Street ground-lease amendment extending the term, from the landlord, before signing.
2. #2: two fixed-price contractor bids for the HVAC and elevator work, from the seller's asset manager, within 3 weeks; any excess over $3.4M comes off the price.
3. #1: a certified copy of the fire marshal's reinstatement letter and the re-inspection report, from the seller, for the closing set.
4. Input gap: a bridge from $7.6M property EBITDA to $7.2M consolidated, from the seller's CFO, within 2 weeks.

## Deal 3. Fernlight Interactive: NO-GO, valued on EV/Revenue (EBITDA negative)

**Findings as classified**

| Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| A former co-developer's copyright suit claims ownership of the engine code in the flagship title Emberwake; studio counsel expects a settlement this quarter | Must-Address | In-Progress |
| The console publishing licence for Emberwake cannot be assigned on a change of control without the platform holder's written consent | Must-Address | Resolved |
| Co-development contract with publisher Harrowgate Games ends automatically on a change of control | Manageable | Unresolved |

**From the financial narrative**

- LTM revenue $27.0M: owned titles $19.5M (up 21%, gross margin 61%) and Harrowgate co-development fees $7.5M.
- EBITDA is -$2.2M; the narrative gives no split between owned titles and co-development.
- No settlement term sheet or court filing has been produced for the copyright suit.
- Venture debt $6.0M; cash $2.5M.
- The seller asks $86.4M enterprise value, quoted as 3.2x LTM revenue of $27.0M.

**Trading comparables**

| Peer | EV/Revenue | EV/EBITDA | Growth (LTM) | Gross Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lanternfall Games | 2.8x | NM | 19% | 63% |
| Pebblewick Studios | 2.4x | NM | 23% | 58% |
| Ninebark Interactive | 2.0x | 16x | 12% | 55% |
| Skyreach Mobile | 5.8x | NM | 47% | 72% |

**Memo as delivered**

# Investment Committee Memo: Fernlight Interactive (Independent Video Games)

**Recommendation: NO-GO at this time (do not proceed to signing).** The copyright suit over Emberwake's engine code (#1) is an open Must-Address finding, and counsel's expectation of a settlement does not close it.

**Input check**
- No tier mapping is needed (Must-Address / Manageable / Informational); rows keep numbers #1-#3.
- Open: #1 (In-Progress) and #3 (Unresolved).
- #2 is Resolved, but its wording still describes a licence that needs the platform holder's consent, and nothing shows that consent: questioned.
- EBITDA is -$2.2M (-8.1% of $27.0M revenue), so EV/EBITDA is NM; the split between owned titles and co-development is missing and requested.
- The $7.5M of Harrowgate fees (28% of revenue) ends on a change of control; the base is $19.5M of owned-title revenue.
- Net debt $3.5M ($6.0M venture debt less $2.5M cash).

## 1. Deal Thesis (≤ 120 words)
Fernlight owns a growing catalogue: owned-title revenue of $19.5M rose 21% at a 61% gross margin, close to its nearest peers. A buyer would acquire that catalogue and the team behind Emberwake, not the Harrowgate stream, which stops at closing. The seller's $86.4M applies 3.2x to all $27.0M of revenue, that stream included, for a studio that still loses money. There is value here, but only once code ownership and console rights for the flagship are settled in writing.
**Primary risk:** the copyright suit (#1) contests who owns Emberwake's engine code, and only a court's dismissal or a signed settlement with release closes it.

## 2. Tiered Risk Table
| # | Finding | Input tier / status | IC treatment | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Co-developer's copyright suit over Emberwake engine code | Must-Address / In-Progress | Blocks proceed | Counsel's expected settlement is an opinion; closer: an entered dismissal or a signed settlement with release and assignment |
| 2 | Console licence needs platform consent on change of control | Must-Address / Resolved, questioned | Condition precedent | Wording still reads live and no consent is shown; closer: the platform holder's written consent. Does not block on its own |
| 3 | Harrowgate co-development contract ends on change of control | Manageable / Unresolved | Price or structure | Manageable fits because price settles it: the $7.5M stream cannot transfer, so it leaves the base |

Deal-breaker: #1, open until a court or the claimant signs. Manageable for now: #2 closes with one consent letter, and #3 is settled by valuing owned titles alone.

## 3. Valuation Range
EV/Revenue is primary: EBITDA is -$2.2M, and three of the four comps show EV/EBITDA as NM.
- Included: Lanternfall Games (19% growth, 63% gross margin) and Pebblewick Studios (23%, 58%), the two closest to the target's 21% and 61%. Ninebark Interactive (12%, 55%) is included and sits below the bracket.
- Excluded: Skyreach Mobile, whose 47% growth is about 2.2 times Fernlight's; its 5.8x prices growth the target does not have.
- Bracket 2.4x-2.8x, no adjustment: both bounds lie within 2 points of the target's growth and 3 points of its margin.
- Base: $27.0M revenue less $7.5M Harrowgate fees = $19.5M owned-title revenue.
- Low: 2.4x × $19.5M = $46.8M. High: 2.8x × $19.5M = $54.6M.
- **Defended range: $46.8M-$54.6M EV.**
- Cross-check only: the seller's 3.2x on the $19.5M base gives $62.4M, still above the range, because 3.2x exceeds both bracket comps.
- Ask: $86.4M is 3.2x full revenue and 4.4x the base, $31.8M (58.2%) above the top; EV/EBITDA is NM.
- Equity value: EV less $3.5M net debt.

## 4. Recommendation and Consistency Check (≤ 120 words)
Hold at NO-GO: #1 is an open Must-Address finding, so no price, inside the $46.8M-$54.6M range or below it, makes the deal signable. #2 alone would not block; it stays a condition precedent for any return to IC. What would reverse the call: an entered dismissal or signed settlement for #1, delivered together with the platform holder's consent for #2; a re-submission would be priced at no more than $54.6M. Consistency: the table opens with primary risk #1, and every value uses the $19.5M base.

## 5. Conditions and Data Requests
1. #1: the court's entered dismissal, or a settlement signed by the claimant with release and assignment of the engine code, from Fernlight's litigation counsel, before any return to IC.
2. #2: written consent to the change of control from the platform holder, obtained by the seller, before signing.
3. #3: Harrowgate's written confirmation of the end date and any wind-down payments, from the seller, within 2 weeks.
4. Input gap: EBITDA split between owned titles and co-development, from the seller's CFO, within 2 weeks, to show whether the owned business loses money.
5. Input gap: chain-of-title records for the Emberwake engine (contributor agreements and assignments), from the seller's counsel, within 3 weeks.
