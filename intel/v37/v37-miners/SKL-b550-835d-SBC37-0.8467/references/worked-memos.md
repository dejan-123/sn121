# Worked memos

Three invented deals, each in a field unrelated to any request, showing the layout and the reasoning. In every case the reply is the memo alone, beginning with `**Bottom line.**`; the cross-reading of the artifacts is done before writing and shows up only in the table cells and the valuation.

## Memo A: a gating permit recorded as resolved but not granted

Artifacts, summarised. Target: Tallis Craft Distillery, a whisky and gin maker selling through distributors. Its malt whisky spends at least three years in oak before it can be sold, so every cask filled today is inventory that pays out years later, and the bonded warehouse is as much a part of the asset base as the stills. Risk table: (1) the federal permit for the second still house has been applied for (Must-Address, Resolved); (2) the main distributor, 31% of revenue, is renegotiating its contract (Must-Address, In-Progress); (3) the barrel warehouse roof needs $1.9M of replacement not in the capex plan (Manageable, Unresolved); (4) the master distiller has no non-compete (Manageable, Unresolved); (5) a grain supplier's claim over unpaid invoices is pending in county court (Manageable, Resolved); (6) the flagship label artwork is licensed from the founder's family trust and needs its consent on change of control (Must-Address, Resolved); (7) a proposed excise increase is under consultation (Informational, Unresolved). Narrative: LTM revenue $24.0M, including a $2.2M one-off sale of aged bulk whisky to another bottler; recurring revenue $21.8M, growing 12%; gross margin 51%; reported EBITDA $4.9M, of which $1.0M came from the bulk sale; the seller's adjusted EBITDA of $5.6M adds back $0.7M of "brand building"; the permit application is with the regulator and no permit has been granted; ask $57.8M EV. Peers: Ridgeway Distilling 2.2x revenue, 11x EBITDA, 7% growth, 47% margin; Highfield Spirits 2.6x, 13x, 10%, 50%; Copperline Brands 2.9x, 14x, 14%, 53%; Larkspur Distillers 4.8x, 24x, acquired last year by a global drinks group at a control premium; NovaCraft Seltzers 5.5x, NM, 45%, 58%. The memo:

**Bottom line.** Do not proceed on current terms: the second still house still has no permit and the main distributor contract is unsigned, and both findings are gating.

**Deal thesis.** Tallis makes whisky and gin with $21.8M of recurring revenue growing 12% at a 51% gross margin, sold mostly through distributors. The price would buy its brands, its aged stock and the capacity of a second still house. The seller asks $57.8M on an adjusted EBITDA that counts a one-off bulk sale and a marketing add-back. The primary risk is row 1: the second still house has no federal permit, although the risk table records the finding as resolved. The distributor renegotiation in row 2 is the other open gating finding.

**Risk table**

| # | Finding | Tier and status as recorded | Where it stands | What it means for this deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Federal permit for the second still house applied for | Must-Address, Resolved | Open, and it blocks the deal: the narrative says no permit has been granted | The new capacity cannot legally run |
| 2 | Main distributor (31% of revenue) renegotiating | Must-Address, In-Progress | Open, and it blocks the deal: nothing is signed yet | Nearly a third of revenue has no settled terms |
| 3 | Warehouse roof, $1.9M, not in the capex plan | Manageable, Unresolved | Open; handled in the price | Cash the plan leaves out |
| 4 | Master distiller has no non-compete | Manageable, Unresolved | Open; handled through a retention agreement | The recipes and the know-how could walk |
| 5 | Grain supplier's unpaid-invoice claim pending | Manageable, Resolved | Open despite the label, since the claim is still pending; handled by indemnity | A cost to settle |
| 6 | Label artwork needs the family trust's consent on change of control | Must-Address, Resolved | Accepted as resolved; the signed consent is still requested | The brand's look depends on one letter |
| 7 | Excise increase under consultation | Informational, Unresolved | Open; disclosed only | Possible margin pressure |

**Valuation**

Three trading peers are kept: Ridgeway Distilling (7% growth, 47% margin) as the low anchor, Highfield Spirits (10%, 50%) as the nearest match, and Copperline Brands (14%, 53%) as an upper bound only. Larkspur Distillers is dropped because its multiple is an acquisition price carrying a control premium, and NovaCraft Seltzers because 45% growth in a different product says nothing about a distillery. The base is recurring EBITDA of $3.9M: the reported $4.9M less the $1.0M earned on the bulk sale, with the $0.7M brand-building add-back left in as the operating cost it is. Tallis grows faster than Highfield but converts only 17.9% of revenue into EBITDA against the peers' 20.0%, so the range runs from Ridgeway's 11.0x to Highfield's 13.0x: 11.0x × $3.9M = $42.9M to 13.0x × $3.9M = $50.7M. On revenue the same peers would give 2.2x × $21.8M = (2 × $21.8M) + (0.2 × $21.8M) = $43.6M + $4.4M = $48.0M to 2.6x × $21.8M = (2 × $21.8M) + (0.6 × $21.8M) = $43.6M + $13.1M = $56.7M, higher only because of the margin gap, so the EBITDA range stands. The ask of $57.8M is 14.8x recurring EBITDA, $7.1M above the top of the range, before the $1.9M roof in row 3.

**Recommendation.** **Do not proceed on current terms.** Rows 1 and 2 are gating findings and both are open: the still-house permit has not been granted despite the resolved label, and the distributor contract is unsigned. Row 6 is gating too, but nothing contradicts its resolved status, so its consent letter is a condition rather than a block. The deal can come back to the committee with the permit granted, the distributor contract signed, and a price no higher than $50.7M less the roof.

**Conditions**

1. Permit for the second still house (row 1) granted before signing, or we walk.
2. Distributor contract (row 2) signed on today's terms before signing; otherwise its revenue comes out of the price.
3. Roof replacement (row 3): $1.9M off the price in the term sheet.
4. Grain supplier's claim (row 5) settled before close, or a specific indemnity.
5. Family trust's signed consent to the change of control (row 6) before signing.
6. Bulk-sale contract and cost of goods, to confirm the $2.2M and $1.0M taken out of the base.

## Memo B: gating findings closed, no asking price, different tier labels

Artifacts, summarised. Target: Kestrel Sprinkler Services, which inspects and maintains fire-sprinkler systems under annual contracts. Building owners must have sprinkler systems tested on a fixed cycle to keep their occupancy certificates and insurance, so the work recurs whatever the economy does, and a lost contract usually follows a lost building rather than a lost customer. Risk table with its own labels: (1) the state contractor licence lapsed and was renewed; the renewed licence is in the data room (Critical, Resolved); (2) the largest customer, a property group at 16% of revenue, can cancel on 90 days' notice (Critical, Resolved); (3) the brand's trademark application is pending and a rival filed a similar mark earlier (Critical, Resolved); (4) the technicians' collective agreement expires in 8 months (Moderate, Unresolved); (5) two billing disputes with customers are pending in small-claims court (Moderate, Resolved); (6) one inspection report was filed late last year (Low, Resolved). Narrative: LTM revenue $16.4M, 88% under contract, growing 11%; gross margin 42%; EBITDA $2.7M; no asking price stated. Peers: Firewatch Services 1.8x revenue, 11x EBITDA, 9% growth, 40% margin; SafeFlow Inspection 2.1x, 12x, 12%, 43%; Guardian Systems 2.5x, 14x, 17%, 47%; Legacy Sprinkler 1.3x, 8x, 2%, 34%; no split between inspection and repair revenue for the peers. The memo:

**Bottom line.** Proceed with conditions: every gating finding is closed or accepted, and the technicians' agreement is the main price lever.

**Deal thesis.** Kestrel earns $16.4M a year, 88% of it under annual inspection and maintenance contracts, growing 11% at a 42% gross margin and $2.7M of EBITDA. The price would buy a renewing contract base and the licensed technicians who service it. No asking price was given, so this memo sets bid guidance. The primary risk is row 4, the technicians' agreement expiring in 8 months, because a wage reset lands directly on contract margins. All three Critical findings are recorded resolved, and none of the artifacts contradicts them.

**Risk table**

Critical is treated as the gating tier; Moderate and Low do not gate.

| # | Finding | Tier and status as recorded | Where it stands | What it means for this deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | State contractor licence lapsed and renewed | Critical, Resolved | Resolved: the renewed licence is in the data room | The business can operate |
| 2 | Largest customer (16%) can cancel on 90 days' notice | Critical, Resolved | Accepted as resolved; the cancellation right itself remains | 16% of revenue rests on goodwill, not a term |
| 3 | Trademark application pending; a rival filed a similar mark earlier | Critical, Resolved | Accepted as resolved; the earlier filing is the exposure, not a failed resolution | Brand protection rests on counsel's view |
| 4 | Technicians' agreement expires in 8 months | Moderate, Unresolved | Open; handled in the price | A wage reset would compress margins |
| 5 | Two customer billing disputes pending | Moderate, Resolved | Open despite the label, since both disputes are still pending | Small cost to close |
| 6 | One inspection report filed late | Low, Resolved | Resolved; disclosed only | None beyond disclosure |

**Valuation**

Firewatch Services (9% growth, 40% margin) and SafeFlow Inspection (12%, 43%) bracket Kestrel and set the range; Guardian Systems is used only as an upper bound because its 17% growth is well above Kestrel's, and Legacy Sprinkler is dropped because 2% growth and a 34% margin describe a shrinking repair book. The base is reported EBITDA of $2.7M, with no one-time revenue or add-backs presented. Kestrel's 11% growth and 42% margin sit between the two bracketing peers, so the range is 11.0x × $2.7M = $29.7M to 12.0x × $2.7M = $32.4M. On revenue the same peers give 1.8x × $16.4M = (1 × $16.4M) + (0.8 × $16.4M) = $16.4M + $13.1M = $29.5M to 2.1x × $16.4M = (2 × $16.4M) + (0.1 × $16.4M) = $32.8M + $1.6M = $34.4M, a little higher because Kestrel converts 16.5% of revenue into EBITDA against SafeFlow's 17.5%, so the EBITDA range stands. The peers' split between inspection and repair work is not given, so the multiple assumes a similar mix. With no ask on the table, $29.7M to $32.4M is the bid guidance.

**Recommendation.** **Proceed with conditions.** No gating finding is open: the licence in row 1 is closed by the renewed document, and rows 2 and 3 are recorded resolved with nothing in the artifacts saying otherwise, so their supporting documents become conditions. The technicians' agreement in row 4 is the main price lever, and its cost can be estimated before signing. Bid within $29.7M to $32.4M, holding the lower end until the wage plan is known.

**Conditions**

1. Bargaining plan and cost estimate for the technicians' agreement (row 4) before signing; any uplift is priced at 11.0x.
2. Renewal history or a signed term extension from the property group (row 2) before signing; without either, we bid low.
3. Trademark counsel's written opinion on the earlier filing (row 3) before signing.
4. Both billing disputes (row 5) settled or decided before close.
5. The seller's asking price, and the peers' inspection-versus-repair split if available.

## Memo C: no gating finding open, ask above the range

Artifacts, summarised. Target: Pinecrest Car Washes, 22 express car-wash sites selling monthly unlimited-wash memberships that renew automatically on a card; members now provide most of the volume, so cancellations matter more than single-wash prices. Risk table: (1) leases on 5 of the 22 sites expire within 12 months and renewals are being negotiated (Manageable, In-Progress); (2) a neighbours' zoning appeal against the two newest sites is pending (Manageable, Resolved); (3) monthly membership cancellations rose from 2.8% to 3.6% (Informational, Unresolved). There is no Must-Address row. Narrative: EBITDA $4.7M; ask $46.0M EV. The kept peers support 8.0x to 9.0x EBITDA, so the valuation gives $37.6M to $42.3M on $4.7M of EBITDA, and the ask is 9.8x. The bottom line, recommendation and conditions:

**Bottom line.** Proceed with conditions: nothing in the risk table gates the deal, and the ask above our range sets the bid rather than stopping it.

**Recommendation.** **Proceed with conditions.** No gating finding is open, so nothing in the risk table stops the deal; the lease renewals in row 1 and the zoning appeal in row 2 are open but not gating, and conditions handle them. Rising cancellations in row 3 argue for the lower half of the range. The ask of $46.0M sits $3.7M above the top of the range, so the price is the first condition, not a reason to stop.

**Conditions**

1. A bid no higher than $42.3M, the top of the range.
2. The five expiring leases (row 1) renewed before close, or an escrow for relocating those sites.
3. A specific indemnity covering the two sites while the zoning appeal (row 2) is pending.
4. Membership cancellations by site for 24 months, before signing.

## Seller framings and the memo's restatement

| What the seller presents | What the memo prices instead |
|---|---|
| Adjusted EBITDA with add-backs for marketing or "strategic" spend | Reported EBITDA, less any margin earned on one-time revenue |
| A multiple of gross revenue that includes pass-through costs | The same peers' multiple of net revenue |
| Full trailing revenue including a one-off project or sale | Recurring revenue only, with the one-off named and removed |
| Next year's growth if pilots or prospects convert | Trailing growth, with the forecast noted as unproven |
| Revenue from customers who have already given notice | The run-rate after those customers leave |
| A peer set that includes a past acquisition at a premium | Trading peers only, with the acquisition named and dropped |

## Deal terms the memo may use

- **LOI**: the non-binding letter that sets price range and exclusivity; **SPA**: the binding purchase agreement.
- **Condition precedent**: something that must be true or delivered before the deal closes; if it is missing, the buyer need not close.
- **Escrow**: part of the price held back by a third party to pay a known claim if it lands.
- **Specific indemnity**: the seller's promise to cover one named liability, outside the general warranty caps.
- **Earn-out or contingent consideration**: part of the price paid only if a later event happens (a contract renewed, a target met).
- **Working-capital peg**: the normal level of working capital the business must be delivered with; a shortfall reduces the price.
- **Quality of earnings**: an accountant's review that restates EBITDA and removes one-time items.

## What carries over

- Only the gating tier can stop a deal; lower-tier findings, open or not, become price, structure or conditions, and so does an ask above the range.
- A gating finding recorded Resolved reopens only when another artifact says its closing step has not happened, or its own text calls a claim, review or re-bid against the target pending; otherwise accept it and ask for its document.
- Take one-time revenue, non-transferring revenue, pass-through revenue, lost customers and operating-cost add-backs out of the base before pricing it.
- Name every peer kept or dropped and why, in sentences; set the target among the nearest peers; give each end with its working in a short parenthesis.
- Open with a one-sentence bottom line; keep each condition to one tight sentence naming the finding, the document, the timing and the consequence.

Write the memo now: its first characters are `**Bottom line.**`. Do not write "Now I will draft the IC memo", "Here is the IC memo" or any other sentence, title, note or horizontal rule before it; the user asked for the memo, so the memo is the entire reply.
