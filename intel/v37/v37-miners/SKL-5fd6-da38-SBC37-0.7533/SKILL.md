# Would you lend against this number?

You are handed a risk register, a financial story and a comparable set, each assembled by someone who wanted the deal. Read them as an underwriter reads a loan application: not whether a figure is plausible, but whether you would advance money against it. Haircut each artifact until what remains survives that question, and write the memo on what remains. Expect four cuts: status, revenue, earnings, reference group. Make them before drafting, without writing them out: the reply is the memo alone, and the cuts show only in its results.

## Find the top grade, then haircut the statuses

**The top grade is defined by meaning.** Registers name their grades differently: must-address, critical, red, tier one, high. The top grade is whichever name says the deal cannot close while the item is outstanding. If the gravest entry on the page is described as manageable, moderate, low or informational, there is nothing at the top grade, and a clean proceed is available.

**Every status is a representation, not a fact.** "Resolved" passes only if the narrative shows the matter actually closed. You mark it unconfirmed, whatever the column says, when you find any of the following: a consent still to be given, classically a licence that cannot pass to a new owner without the licensor's signature; a contract being re-tendered, or a renewal promised orally and never signed; a hearing postponed, a review deferred, a deadline pushed back, since putting off a decision does not take it; counsel calling something "substantially resolved" with no closure letter, consent order or written ruling to show; a self-certification where an independent audit is the market standard; a competing filing that predates the target's; a partner or customer free to leave on short notice; a figure restated upward with the auditor yet to sign; two expert opinions that disagree, which leave the question open until a definitive opinion, ruling or regulator's guidance settles it.

**Show the haircut; leave the grade alone.** For each contradicted status, set down what the register records, what the narrative actually says, how you read it, and the one document that would make the original status true. You do not regrade the item: the register's author owns the grade, you own the reading. Where an input appends its own note about a misfiled status, act on it; where none does, the job is the same. If nothing contradicts anything, one sentence says so; an invented doubt is an error. Where the file simply lacks something a judgment needs — a renewal status, a contract term, a revenue mix — say it is missing, assume nothing in its place, and turn it into a request.

**Look for one finding working through another.** A lower-grade item can be how a top-grade one fails: a measure slipping below the standard a licensing or accrediting body enforces puts that licence in question; a single person holding every important relationship puts the contracts they hold in question. Say so when you see it.

## Haircut the revenue and the earnings

Advance only against what transfers. Price on the smallest base you can defend and print the headline next to it.

Take out revenue that will not recur — a project, a programme, a one-season contract, a settlement — and recompute margin and EBITDA on the rest, because the departing revenue usually carried more than its share of profit. Take out revenue that cannot pass to this buyer: grants limited to charitable, public or academic holders, contracts that terminate on a change of control, funding tied to the present owner. Where a trade is priced on net revenue, the gross figure is not collateral, whatever multiple has been put on it; use the measure the reference group uses. Strip customers who have already gone from the run rate. Keep any restatement the auditor has not signed in quotation marks and show the figure before it. Forecasts that rest on contracts not yet won are upside, and upside is never in the advance.

On earnings, an add-back survives only if the cost will genuinely not come back. A departed executive's severance can survive. Spending relabelled "strategic", staff hired and still employed, a "one-off" that recurs each year — none do. Rebuild adjusted EBITDA from the add-backs that survive and set it beside the seller's figure. When EBITDA is negative, an earnings multiple is not meaningful; say so and price on revenue. Where cash and debt are given, state the equity value: enterprise value less debt plus cash.

## Haircut the reference group

Every comparable kept and every one dropped is named with its reason. Drop any business that changed hands in a completed deal from a trading reference group, because its multiple carries what a buyer paid for control; at most it marks a ceiling. Drop not-meaningful multiples. Drop businesses growing or earning far away from the target, since growth and margin are what the multiple prices. When an adjustment would need data you do not have — a revenue mix, an accounting basis — make no adjustment: drop the one comparable affected, or, if the gap runs across the whole group, keep the group and state that the comparison is not like-for-like. A note that nobody upstream flagged an exclusion describes their work, not the group. Use the multiple the trade actually prices on: earnings for asset-heavy or thin-margin operators, revenue where earnings are negative or erratic across the set.

Then state the adjustment in numbers. Anchor on the kept comparable nearest the target's growth — the lower multiple on a tie, the median if no growth is given. For a thinner margin, or growth below every comparable, take a round 5% or 10% off, as one multiplication. Write each step out separately, never as one compound formula. Slow collections, net retention below one hundred percent and a concentrated book each earn a further stated step down. Where both multiples are meaningful, use the second as a cross-check and say whether it agrees.

## Price the haircut base

Write it out: multiple × base = value at the kept low and high and at your adjusted multiple. Express the seller's number as a multiple of your haircut base and against the range, in money and in percent; when an ask looks ordinary on the headline and extraordinary on what transfers, that contrast belongs high in the memo. Where an unconfirmed item puts a stated slice of the business at risk, add an adverse case priced without it. Where the seller's number exceeds value, give the price at which you would proceed and the gap. Where two bases survive scrutiny, price both and say which the reference group uses. Where the seller has given no number, do not supply one; give the range and the price you would pay.

## The call

An item at the top grade is unconfirmed if it is unresolved, in progress, or "resolved" in a way you have just haircut.
- **Proceed** — nothing at the top grade is unconfirmed. A lower-grade item still in progress, such as a pending approval, can sit under a proceed as a condition to completion; the title still reads proceed.
- **Proceed subject to conditions precedent** — each unconfirmed top-grade item closes with a document the deal can demand on its own timetable: a consent, a signed renewal, a closure letter, a passed inspection. Each condition names its item, owner and the step it must clear, with a long-stop where the counterparty controls timing; never invent a day count.
- **Defer** — the closing act is an outside process nobody can hurry and whose outcome is uncertain: a regulator's review, a re-tender, litigation, a hearing set for later. No condition precedent can make an outsider decide.
- **Decline** — an unconfirmed item breaks the thesis, or the seller's number, on the haircut base, is above every kept comparable's multiple: decline at that number, even with an outside process also open, and state the price that would reopen the file.

An unconfirmed top-grade item never sits beside a plain proceed. A clean register is never hedged with a deal-breaker you invented. A deferred or declined file still lists, item by item, what would reopen it and who obtains each.

## Read the file against itself

Before it goes: every figure in the call matches the valuation; each multiplication is right; the risk the case for owning it names is in the risk table; every unconfirmed top-grade item appears as a condition or as the reason to defer or decline; every comparable is kept or dropped, none silently; and the call is the one the ladder gives.

## The file you hand over

The file has exactly five numbered sections, in this order and within the lengths the reference gives: deal thesis, risk table, valuation range, recommendation, conditions. The title carries the call; haircuts to the register sit under the risk table; the base sits in the valuation; nothing else is a section. `references/credit-file-examples.md` carries that order and three complete memos in unrelated trades. Read it before writing. Open on the memo's title line, with nothing about your method in front of it. Write in full sentences; the risk table is the one table of findings, and the valuation reads as an argument. Nothing enters the memo that the inputs did not supply — no figure, name, date, multiple, counterparty or obligation of your own making, and no grade word the register does not use.
