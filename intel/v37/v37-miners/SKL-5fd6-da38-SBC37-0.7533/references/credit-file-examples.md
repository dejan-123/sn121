# The file: order, length, and two memos underwritten in full

Every memo follows the order below. Three complete memos come after it — one the haircuts sink on price, one that survives them and proceeds below the seller's number, one sunk by revenue that will not transfer. The trades are deliberately unlike anything you will meet; carry over the method and the arithmetic, never the facts.

## Order

The file has five numbered sections and no others, in this order, each held to its length.

- Title line: `# <Target> — committee memo: <the call>`, nothing above it. It carries the call; it is not a section.
- `### 1. Deal thesis` — 70 to 120 words: what the company does and earns, the case for owning it with a figure, what the seller's number presumes, and the main risk by name. The risk table carries the same risk.
- `### 2. Risk table` — every item in the register, top grade first: `Item | Grade | Recorded / read | What it does | What clears it`. Below it, at most 80 words: each status the narrative contradicts, what would make the record true, and any item working through another; or one sentence that nothing needed haircutting.
- `### 3. Valuation range` — 120 to 250 words: the haircut base and why; reference group kept and dropped; the stated adjustment and the multiple it gives; the arithmetic; the seller's number against the result; the price you would pay where it is higher; an adverse case where an unconfirmed item removes a stated slice; equity value where cash and debt are given.
- `### 4. Recommendation` — 60 to 130 words: the call, what settles it, the price, and what would change it.
- `### 5. Conditions` — one line per condition precedent or request, each tied to its item, with an owner and the step it must clear; about 100 words at most. A declined or deferred file uses the same lines for what would reopen it.

Expect 450 to 700 words with the table; a clean proceed may be shorter. The valuation gets the room.

## Memo one — Hartwell Coaches (declined)

**What the file contained.** A regional coach and bus operator. Register, grades Must-Address / Manageable / Informational: the operator's licence is suspended pending a public inquiry by the transport regulator into maintenance records — Must-Address, Unresolved. School-transport contracts worth 35% of recurring revenue are held under a council framework the council is re-tendering this year, and the item is filed Must-Address, Resolved, on the basis that the incumbent usually wins. Diesel fleet needing euro-standard upgrades for a city clean-air zone — Manageable, Unresolved. Narrative: revenue $33.0M, of which $5.4M came from a rail-replacement contract during track closures that have now ended; EBITDA $4.2M, of which $1.9M came from that contract; seller's number $38M. Reference group on EV/EBITDA: Coachline Group 7.5x; Regional Transit Holdings 6.5x; Wayfarer Buses 7.0x; Meridale Coaches 6.0x; Express National 11.0x, acquired last year by a national operator at a stated control premium.

# Hartwell Coaches — committee memo: decline

### 1. Deal thesis

Hartwell runs coach and bus services in a region with few competitors and earns steady contract income on $27.6M of recurring revenue; its school-transport work is a long-dated, publicly funded book worth 35% of that figure. Stability is the case for owning it. The seller's number, though, assumes earnings the business no longer has once ended rail work is removed. Its main risk is the operator's licence: it is suspended pending a public inquiry, and without it no vehicle runs.

### 2. Risk table

| Item | Grade | Recorded / read | What it does | What clears it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operator's licence suspended pending inquiry | Must-Address | Unresolved / unconfirmed | No service runs while suspended | Licence restored after the inquiry |
| School contracts, 35% of recurring revenue, under re-tender | Must-Address | Resolved / unconfirmed | Over a third of the book may move | Council's award letter |
| Fleet upgrade for the clean-air zone | Manageable | Unresolved / unconfirmed | Capital spend to keep city routes | Costed, funded upgrade plan |

*Haircuts to the register.* School-transport contracts — recorded Resolved; the narrative shows the council re-tendering the framework this year. Read as unconfirmed: incumbency is an advantage, not an award. Made true by the council's award letter. Licence suspension — recorded Unresolved, which the narrative bears out. The clean-air upgrade is recorded accurately.

### 3. Valuation range

*The business and the number.* Coach and bus operator; seller's number $38M. Revenue of $33.0M includes $5.4M of rail-replacement work that has ended, so the recurring base is $27.6M. That work carried $1.9M of the $4.2M EBITDA, leaving recurring EBITDA of $2.3M, and the memo prices on that.

An asset-heavy operator is priced on earnings. Kept: Coachline Group 7.5x, Regional Transit Holdings 6.5x, Wayfarer Buses 7.0x, Meridale Coaches 6.0x. Dropped: Express National at 11.0x — a completed acquisition carrying a control premium, a ceiling at most and never part of the average.

The kept range is 6.0x to 7.5x with a median of 6.75x. The group's growth is not given, so no comparable is nearest and the median is the anchor; no adjustment could rescue this price in any case. On recurring EBITDA of $2.3M: 6.0 × $2.3M = $13.8M; 6.75 × $2.3M = $15.525M; 7.5 × $2.3M = $17.25M. The seller's $38M is 16.52x that figure and $22.475M, or 144.8%, above the median value. Against the headline $4.2M it reads 9.05x and looks merely full, which is how the ended rail contract disguises the gap.

Adverse case: the school framework alone is 35% of recurring revenue, about $9.66M. Losing it would take the base lower still, so there is no adverse case in which the price comes closer.

### 4. Recommendation

Decline. The licence suspension is unresolved and sits at the top grade, the school contracts are filed resolved on nothing firmer than incumbency, and the seller's $38M cannot be reached once the ended rail work is taken out: it is 16.52x recurring EBITDA against a reference group between 6.0x and 7.5x. Even a cleared licence and a won tender would leave a price gap no condition can close.

*What would change this.* A restored licence, a won tender and a seller's number near $15M to $17M would make this worth reopening. None of the three is in hand.

### 5. Conditions

No condition closes a gap of this size, so none is offered at $38M; these would reopen the file.
- The regulator's inquiry outcome on the licence suspension — regulatory counsel.
- The council's award decision on the school framework — commercial lead.
- A recurring EBITDA bridge excluding the rail work, and a seller's number near $15M to $17M — financial diligence lead.

## Memo two — Clearbrook Dental Lab (proceeds)

**What the file contained.** A manufacturer of clear orthodontic aligners for independent orthodontists. Register, grades Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3, nothing filed Tier 1: a second resin supplier is being validated as a backup source — Tier 2, In-Progress; two orthodontist groups worth 22% of revenue on annual agreements — Tier 2, Resolved, and the narrative confirms both renewed and signed last month; the quality-system surveillance audit due in five months — Tier 3, In-Progress. Narrative: revenue $18.0M growing 16%, gross margin 58%, EBITDA $3.0M; seller's number $54M. Reference group, EV/Revenue with growth and gross margin: AlignTech Labs 3.4x, 18%, 60%; SmileWorks Manufacturing 2.8x, 13%, 55%; OrthoPrint 3.1x, 15%, 57%; ClearPath Dental 2.9x, 14%, 56%; ArchForm Global 6.5x, 30%, 72%, a global brand many times Clearbrook's size. EV/EBITDA for the first four: 19x, 16x, 17x, 18x.

# Clearbrook Dental Lab — committee memo: proceed

### 1. Deal thesis

Clearbrook makes clear orthodontic aligners for independent orthodontists, a segment the global brands under-serve, and grows 16% on $18.0M of revenue at a 58% gross margin and a 16.7% EBITDA margin. Two orthodontist groups worth 22% of revenue renewed last month, which steadies the book, and the seller's number presumes production carries on uninterrupted. Its main risk is supply: there is one resin supplier today and the backup is still in validation, so a disruption at that supplier would halt production.

### 2. Risk table

| Item | Grade | Recorded / read | What it does | What clears it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single resin supplier, backup in validation | Tier 2 | In-Progress / in progress | Production stops if supply fails | Second source qualified |
| Two orthodontist groups, 22% of revenue | Tier 2 | Resolved / resolved | Concentration, now renewed | Already signed |
| Quality-system surveillance audit in five months | Tier 3 | In-Progress / in progress | Routine | Audit passed |

*Haircuts to the register.* None needed. Every status was checked against the narrative; the two renewals are signed, not promised.

### 3. Valuation range

*The business and the number.* Clear-aligner manufacturing; seller's number $54M. Revenue of $18.0M and EBITDA of $3.0M are priced as reported: nothing in the narrative is one-off, restated, gross of pass-through or tied to the present owner.

Margins vary enough across this group, and growth matters enough, that revenue is the sensible primary basis, with earnings as a check. Kept: AlignTech Labs 3.4x, SmileWorks Manufacturing 2.8x, OrthoPrint 3.1x, ClearPath Dental 2.9x. Dropped: ArchForm Global at 6.5x — a global brand at 30% growth and a 72% margin, priced for a scale Clearbrook does not have.

The kept range is 2.8x to 3.4x. Clearbrook grows 16%, and the nearest kept comparable is OrthoPrint at 15%, so its 3.1x is the anchor; Clearbrook's 58% margin is a point above OrthoPrint's 57%, so no step. On $18.0M: 2.8 × $18.0M = $50.4M; 3.1 × $18.0M = $55.8M; 3.4 × $18.0M = $61.2M. The seller's $54M is 3.0x revenue, $1.8M or 3.2% below the anchor value. On earnings, OrthoPrint's 17x gives 17 × $3.0M = $51.0M and the ask is 18.0x EBITDA; the seller's number sits between the earnings view and the revenue view, which is where a fair price should.

### 4. Recommendation

Proceed at the seller's number. Nothing sits in Tier 1, the one status recorded Resolved is borne out by signed renewals, and the $54M asked is 3.2% below an anchored value of $55.8M. The second resin source is worth finishing, not worth waiting for.

*What would change this.* A supply interruption before the backup is qualified, or a lost orthodontist group, would be the reasons to revisit.

### 5. Conditions

None to signing. After completion: qualification of the second resin supplier — operations lead, after completion.

## Memo three — Larchmont Stage Company (declined)

**What the file contained.** A producer of touring theatre with a home venue. Register, grades Severe / Material / Minor: performance rights for its three touring productions are licensed from a rights agency, and two legal opinions disagree on whether the licences survive a change of ownership — Severe, Unresolved; a regional arts-council grant worth 28% of revenue may be held only by a registered charity — Severe, Unresolved; the home-venue lease runs three more years — Material, Unresolved; a box-office system migration — Minor, In-Progress. Narrative: revenue $15.0M, made up of $9.8M of ticket and touring income, the $4.2M grant and $1.0M of sponsorship, growing 7%; EBITDA $0.6M, and the grant carries almost no direct cost; seller's number $34M, presented as about 2.3x revenue. Reference group, EV/Revenue with growth: LiveArts Holdings 2.4x, 9%; Curtain Call Group 2.0x, 6%; Proscenium Tours 2.7x, 12%; StageRight 1.8x, 4%; Marquee Global 5.5x, 15%, an international rights owner many times Larchmont's size.

# Larchmont Stage Company — committee memo: decline

### 1. Deal thesis

Larchmont produces touring theatre from a home venue, earning across many venues on a single creative investment and growing 7% a year. The seller presents $15.0M of revenue, but $4.2M of it is an arts-council grant only a registered charity may hold, so a commercial buyer is in fact acquiring a $10.8M business that loses money. Its main risk is rights: every tour runs under a performance licence whose survival on a sale is disputed between two legal opinions.

### 2. Risk table

| Item | Grade | Recorded / read | What it does | What clears it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arts-council grant, 28% of revenue, charity-only | Severe | Unresolved / lost on sale | Base falls to $10.8M; EBITDA turns negative | Nothing a commercial buyer can do |
| Performance rights, conflicting opinions on change of ownership | Severe | Unresolved / unconfirmed | Tours cannot run without the rights | Definitive opinion, or the agency's written confirmation |
| Home-venue lease, three years left | Material | Unresolved / unconfirmed | Relocation risk later | Renewal on agreed terms |
| Box-office migration | Minor | In-Progress / in progress | Routine | Migration complete |

*Haircuts to the register.* Each status matches the narrative. The haircuts here fall on the base, not on the column: the grant is filed accurately as unresolved but is in substance certain to fall away, and the rights question is unresolved because the opinions conflict, not because work is outstanding.

### 3. Valuation range

*The business and the number.* Touring theatre; seller's number $34M. Of $15.0M revenue, the $4.2M grant is confined to a registered charity, leaving $10.8M a commercial buyer acquires. Because the grant carries almost no direct cost, losing it takes EBITDA from $0.6M to −$3.6M; on the transferable base an earnings multiple is not meaningful.

Earnings on the transferable base are negative, so revenue is the basis. Kept: LiveArts Holdings 2.4x, Curtain Call Group 2.0x, Proscenium Tours 2.7x, StageRight 1.8x. Dropped: Marquee Global at 5.5x — an international rights owner whose multiple prices a catalogue Larchmont does not have.

Larchmont grows 7%, and the nearest kept comparable is Curtain Call Group at 6%, so its 2.0x is the anchor. On the $10.8M that transfers: 1.8 × $10.8M = $19.4M; 2.0 × $10.8M = $21.6M; 2.7 × $10.8M = $29.2M. The seller's $34M is 3.15x that base, above the top of the range by $4.8M and above the anchor value by $12.4M, or 57.4%. Presented against $15.0M it reads 2.27x and looks ordinary, because it quietly prices a grant the buyer cannot hold.

### 4. Recommendation

Decline. The grant cannot pass to a commercial owner, so the business on offer is $10.8M of revenue, not $15.0M, and on that base the seller's $34M is 3.15x against a reference group between 1.8x and 2.7x — $12.4M, or 57.4%, above value. The performance rights are unconfirmed as well: two opinions that disagree settle nothing.

*What would change this.* Confirmed rights and a seller's number near $22M would make this worth reopening; the grant will not come back under any structure a commercial buyer can use.

### 5. Conditions

Nothing is offered at $34M. The file reopens only on two things:
- A definitive opinion on the performance rights, or the agency's written confirmation that they survive a sale — deal counsel.
- A seller's number near $22M on the $10.8M that transfers — commercial lead.

With the file read, write the memo; only the haircuts' results appear, never the working. Its first line is the title line, nothing precedes it, and nothing follows the conditions.
