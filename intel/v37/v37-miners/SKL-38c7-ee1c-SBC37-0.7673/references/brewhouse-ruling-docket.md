# Brewhouse ruling docket

This docket holds one invented matter worked from its exhibits to a finished memo, a shorter second matter in which Resolved rows hold, how section 5 shifts with each ruling, and a handful of single-fact rulings. Take from it the shape of the memo, its economy, and the habit of arguing every call from the record. A real memo draws every name, figure and finding from its own artifacts.

## The memo handed down

# IC Memo: Millrace Brewing Group

### 1. Deal Thesis

Millrace is a regional craft brewer with three production breweries, five taprooms and $48.0M of LTM revenue growing 7%, two-thirds of it wholesale through distributors. Both must-address items are documented as closed: the state licences are approved for reissue at closing and a new three-year malt contract is signed. The primary risk is R3, a trademark opposition that names the flagship Millrace Lager (38% of revenue); it is tiered Manageable, but an injunction on the core product carries must-address weight. At $66.0M the ask is 11.0x the $6.0M recurring EBITDA, above the range the comps support.

### 2. Risk Assessment

| Row | Upstream finding | Tier (as given) | Status (as given) | Record note | Effect on the deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | State manufacturer licences must be reissued to the new owner | Must-Address | Resolved | Consistent: all three states approved the reissue, effective at closing | None beyond filing the approvals |
| R2 | Single-maltster base-malt agreement reached its term end in June | Must-Address | Resolved | Consistent: new three-year agreement signed in July | Supply secured; terms to confirm |
| R3 | Earlier-mark owner opposes the trademark and threatens the flagship lager | Manageable | Unresolved | **Apparent mis-tier:** injunction exposure on the core product carries must-address weight; tier kept as given | Flagship revenue at stake: 38% × $48.0M = $18.2M |
| R4 | Two taproom leases end within 12 months | Manageable | In-Progress | — | Rent reset not quantified in the artifacts |
| R5 | Founder and head brewer stepping back within two years | Informational | Unresolved | — | Recipe and quality continuity |

R3 keeps its Manageable tier and Unresolved status; the memo flags the weight of the exposure rather than re-tiering it, and that flag holds the recommendation until counsel's evidence on the opposition is in hand. R1 and R2 stand as Resolved because the narrative confirms both.

### 3. Valuation

Primary multiple: EV/EBITDA, since Millrace is profitable and every comp reports meaningful EBITDA. Base: $6.0M, the reported $6.5M less the $0.5M one-time gain on the canning-line sale, which the buyer does not acquire.

| Comparable | Treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Northgate Ales (11.0x) | Included | Regional brewer, similar growth and margin |
| Sablewood Brewing (9.5x) | Included | Regional brewer |
| Kestrel Craft Holdings (12.0x) | Included | Highest multiple, but its growth and margin sit inside the set's spread |
| Barrelhouse Partners (10.5x) | Included | Regional brewer |
| Foxmoor Brewery (16.0x) | Excluded | Precedent acquisition at a strategic premium, not a trading multiple |

Included EV/EBITDA multiples in order: 9.5x, 10.5x, 11.0x, 12.0x; median (10.5x + 11.0x) ÷ 2 = 10.75x. Included medians: growth 7% (between 6% and 8%), gross margin 41% (between 40% and 42%). An EBITDA step is 1.0x:
- growth, 7% against 7%: no gap, no step;
- gross margin, 37% against 41%: 4 points short, a step down;
- concentration, the largest distributor at 26% of revenue, over the 20% line: a step down;
- size: the comps' revenue scale is not provided, so no step.

Running sum: 10.75x − 1.0x margin − 1.0x concentration = 8.75x. Range, 8.75x ± 0.5x: 8.25x × $6.0M = $49.5M and 9.25x × $6.0M = $55.5M, so **$49.5M–$55.5M**.

The ask of $66.0M sits $10.5M above the high end and implies 11.0x the $6.0M base (10.15x on the reported $6.5M, which still includes the one-time gain).

### 4. Recommendation

**Request more data.** No must-address row is open: R1 and R2 are Resolved and the narrative confirms both. The hold comes from R3, a Manageable row flagged as an apparent mis-tier because an injunction would reach the flagship lager; the recommendation stays here until counsel's opinion or a coexistence agreement is in hand. The comps support at most $55.5M, $10.5M below the $66.0M ask.

### 5. Conditions and Next Steps

- **R1:** Legal diligence to place the three state approvals in the disclosure schedule and confirm, before signing, that no condition attaches beyond closing.
- **R2:** Procurement to file the signed three-year malt agreement in the data room before signing and confirm its price and volume terms.
- **R3:** Trademark counsel to deliver, before signing, a written opinion on the opposition or a signed coexistence agreement. Clean: the verdict moves to Proceed with conditions. Adverse: price a rebrand of the flagship.
- **R4:** Real-estate diligence to obtain signed renewals or final terms for both taproom leases before signing, and price any rent increase into the EBITDA base.
- **R5:** Management to name a successor head brewer and document the core recipes in the 100-day plan.

## How section 5 shifts with the ruling

- **Proceed**: bullets turn into closing protections: the instrument each row rests on (warranty, indemnity, covenant, consent), an escrow or holdback only where an exhibit quantifies the exposure, and the day-one steps. Manageable rows keep their mitigations.
- **Request more data**: for every open row, the precise paper or answer that would close it, who obtains it, by when, and what each outcome would do to the ruling and the range.
- **Pass**: why the blocker cannot be cured within this deal, and what would need to be different before the committee reopened the matter.

## A second, shorter matter: Resolved rows that sound live

Ridgeway Print Works (invented) is a commercial printer. Two must-address rows are marked Resolved: "Master agreement with the largest publishing customer (22% of revenue) came up for renewal this year" and "County opened a review of the plant's solvent-emissions permit after a neighbour complaint". One manageable row, "Two presses are past their overhaul interval", is Unresolved; the narrative says the overhaul is budgeted. Nothing in the narrative or the comps says the renewal or the review is still live, though neither closing document is in the data room.

The ruling: each finding describes a risk, and no other exhibit contradicts either status, so both rows stay Resolved and the gate is met. The missing papers are a gap in proof, not a contradiction. **Proceed with conditions**, with section 5 reading in part:

- **R1:** Deal lead to obtain the signed renewal of the publishing master agreement before signing.
- **R2:** Environmental counsel to obtain the county's written closure of the permit review before signing.
- **R3:** Operations to complete the budgeted press overhaul in the first post-close year, tracked in the 100-day plan.

## Single-fact rulings

- **Critical / Moderate / Low.** The table keeps the upstream labels, and one sentence records that Critical gates the recommendation the way must-address does.
- **Open status words.** Labels like "In-Progress", "substantially resolved" or "deferred", and a status cell left empty, all count as open for the gate; one clause in the memo explains why.
- **A bar on transfer.** If the terms stop a material share of the revenue the thesis needs from passing to the buyer, and nothing in the record shows a way around it, the ruling is Pass; the valuation is still shown on the revenue that does transfer.
- **The seller's arithmetic.** When the banker quotes a multiple on the wrong base, such as gross volume in an industry that prices net revenue or a revenue line still carrying one-time items, restate it on the correct base first and only then set it against the range.
- **Absent exhibits.** A metric or artifact that is missing is written "not provided"; the valuation runs on what was given, and section 5 carries the request.
- **Running long.** Trim adjectives and repeated context first; never trim away a row, a reason for exclusion or an arithmetic line.

The docket closes here. Begin the memo: its opening characters are `# IC Memo: ` and then the target's name. A line like "Now I will draft the IC memo" placed ahead of the title is the commonest slip; the deliberation stays out of the reply.
