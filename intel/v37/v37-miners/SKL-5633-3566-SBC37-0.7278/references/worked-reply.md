# Worked IC memos

These three invented deals illustrate evidence handling and decision structure, not reusable prices or conclusions. All amounts are in USD millions. Actions and protections in the memos are proposals, not facts about completed diligence.

## Contents

- Example 1: Request more data; a disputed resolution and a missing status.
- Example 2: Proceed; resolved blockers and owned mitigations.
- Example 3: Pass; a confirmed structural failure and a nontransferable revenue base.

## Example 1: finished reply

# Brookmere Laboratory Services — Investment Committee Memo

## 1. Deal Thesis

Brookmere offers exposure to utility water-testing demand through an established laboratory generating $25M of LTM revenue and $3.5M of EBITDA. Growth of 12% and gross margin of 46% match the central service peers, supporting an earnings-based acquisition case rather than a proprietary-diagnostics premium. The investment depends on uninterrupted laboratory operations and retention of the enterprise customer supplying 24% of revenue. The primary risk is R1: the upstream table marks the operating permit resolved, but the financial narrative says only the application has been acknowledged. That discrepancy prevents transaction approval today. At the $50M asking EV, the committee would also pay above the earnings-supported range before allowing for the omitted billing expenditure.

## 2. Tiered Risk Table

| ID | Finding and consequence | Upstream tier | Upstream status and conflict | Mitigation or evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Laboratory permit expires before acquisition; expiry without renewal stops operations | Must-Address | Resolved upstream; disputed by financial narrative: no authority decision | Obtain renewal effective through closing; reconcile status with risk author |
| R2 | Customer representing 24% of annual revenue has unsigned renewal terms | Manageable | Not supplied; treated as unresolved | Obtain customer confirmation and signed terms; test loss case |
| R3 | Billing deployment requires $0.4M omitted from cash budget | Informational | Unresolved | Include expenditure in funded implementation budget; retain upstream tier |

R1's tier and reported status are preserved. An acknowledgment is not approval, so resolution is unverified for the decision gate.

## 3. Valuation

| Comparable | Disposition and rationale |
|---|---|
| WaterMark Labs | Include: water-testing services; lower-growth anchor |
| Basin Testing | Include: service model and matching growth/margin |
| ClearAssay | Include: service model; upper operating anchor |
| RapidBio Networks | Exclude: proprietary diagnostic products, materially higher growth and margins; not a testing-service anchor |

The three included peers have median EV/EBITDA of 11x, EV/Revenue of 1.8x, growth of 12%, gross margin of 46%, and revenue of $120M. Brookmere's $25M scale warrants a downward size adjustment; growth and gross margin are neutral against these medians. Customer concentration and pending permit clearance argue against a premium. These are judgmental adjustments, not measured discount coefficients.

Use EV/EBITDA as primary because supplied earnings are positive and the service peers provide usable earnings multiples. An applied 9-10x range sits below the 10-12x peer band, reflecting smaller scale and concentration without pretending a discount resolves the permit. On the supplied LTM base, **9-10x × $3.5M EBITDA yields $31.5-35.0M EV**, conditional on operating continuity and customer diligence.

As a secondary check, 1.4-1.6x × $25M revenue gives $35-40M EV. Its higher indication is consistent with Brookmere's 14% EBITDA margin versus the selected peers' implied approximately 16-17% margins; the earnings range should govern pending cost-quality work. The $50M ask equals 14.3x EBITDA and 2.0x revenue, above both ranges. The primary EV range implies $28.5-32.0M equity after adding $1M cash and deducting $4M debt, before other closing adjustments. The $0.4M billing spend needs funding, not an automatic recurring EBITDA deduction or a second debt deduction.

## 4. Recommendation

**Request more data.** Authorize targeted diligence and price discussions only; defer transaction approval. R1 remains unverified despite its upstream Resolved label, and R2 lacks both status and signed renewal evidence. The ask also exceeds the supported $31.5-35.0M EV range. Reconsider when the authority's renewal decision establishes operating continuity, the customer evidence supports a retained earnings case, and the seller's price expectations can be assessed against that case. Management's expectation of a permit decision is insufficient clearance.

## 5. Conditions and Next Steps

- **R1 — Regulatory diligence lead:** obtain the authority's issued renewal and effective dates before IC reconsideration; acceptance requires coverage through closing and written reconciliation of the upstream status.
- **R2 — Commercial lead:** obtain signed renewal terms and the customer's written confirmation before IC reconsideration; Finance must quantify retained EBITDA under those terms and the loss downside in the same row's workstream.
- **R3 — Integration finance lead:** submit a funded $0.4M billing implementation budget before IC reconsideration; acceptance requires an accountable implementation owner and identification of the cash source.

## Example 2: finished reply

# Harborlane Cold Logistics — Investment Committee Memo

## 1. Deal Thesis

Harborlane supplies temperature-controlled distribution under recurring service agreements, with $60M of revenue and $6M of EBITDA. Its 8% growth and 32% gross margin sit modestly below the selected peer medians, favoring a disciplined earnings price rather than a growth premium. The $52M asking EV falls within the proposed $48-54M range. The investment depends on maintaining routing capability and funding known facility work. The primary remaining execution risk is R2: two managers hold undocumented routing knowledge. R1, the software change-of-control requirement, is confirmed resolved by signed licensor consent covering this buyer. A proceed decision is supportable with owned knowledge-transfer and roof-repair plans; it does not assume those proposed integration actions have already happened.

## 2. Tiered Risk Table

The upstream vocabulary is mapped transparently: Critical → Must-Address; Moderate → Manageable; Low → Informational.

| ID | Finding and consequence | Upstream tier → memo tier | Upstream status and conflict | Mitigation or evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Software license requires change-of-control consent | Critical → Must-Address | Resolved; confirmed by financial narrative | Signed consent covers proposed buyer; retain in closing record |
| R2 | Routing knowledge concentrated in two managers | Moderate → Manageable | Unresolved | COO to own retention, documentation and backup-dispatcher qualification |
| R3 | Depot roof needs $0.6M repair within first year | Moderate → Manageable | In-Progress | Facilities lead to secure scope, funding and completion evidence |
| R4 | Dashboard deployed across depots | Low → Informational | Resolved | No further diligence condition indicated |

No supplied fact contradicts R1's closure. R2 and R3 remain open, with proposed mitigations and milestones below.

## 3. Valuation

Include **FrostRoute, ColdSpan, NorthCrate and PolarFreight**: each provides temperature-controlled logistics with a comparable service mix. No exclusions are warranted. The four-peer median EV/EBITDA is (9x + 10x)/2 = 9.5x; median EV/Revenue is 0.95x, growth 9%, gross margin 33%, and revenue $80M.

Size adjustment is downward: Harborlane's $60M revenue is below the $80M median. Margin adjustment is modestly downward: gross margin is 32% versus 33%. Growth adjustment is also modestly downward: 8% versus 9%. The differences do not justify treating the target as distressed, but the routing dependency and roof funding argue against the upper peer multiples. These adjustments are analytical judgment rather than a quantified regression.

Use EV/EBITDA as primary: earnings are positive, no normalization is identified, and every peer has a usable multiple. Applying 8-9x to $6M gives **$48-54M EV**, below the 9.5x peer median and within the lower part of the 8-11x observed band. This is conditional on retaining the supplied earnings base and implementing the R2/R3 plans.

For a secondary revenue check, a discounted 0.8-0.9x applied to $60M gives the same $48-54M EV range. The $52M ask is 8.67x EBITDA and 0.87x revenue, within both indications; use $54M as the proposed EV ceiling absent new evidence. With $2M cash and $7M debt, the range implies $43-49M equity before other closing adjustments. The separate $0.6M roof expenditure must be funded in the acquisition/integration plan; do not subtract it from recurring EBITDA or count it again as debt.

## 4. Recommendation

**Proceed** within a proposed $54M EV ceiling, with the specific protections below incorporated into the transaction and integration plan. The only Must-Address finding, R1, is explicitly resolved and corroborated within the supplied artifacts by buyer-specific licensor consent. R2 and R3 remain Manageable with named owners, funding requirements and acceptance tests. The $52M ask is supported by the earnings and revenue cross-checks. Reopen IC approval if the earnings base deteriorates, consent no longer covers the transaction, or the mitigation plans prove infeasible.

## 5. Conditions and Next Steps

- **R2 — COO:** agree retention and knowledge-transfer arrangements before closing, then document routes and qualify a backup dispatcher within 90 days after closing; acceptance requires the backup to run an operating cycle independently with COO sign-off.
- **R3 — Facilities lead:** secure an independently scoped roof quote and approved $0.6M funding allocation before closing; complete work within the first year, with contractor completion evidence and independent inspection. Escalate costs above the allocation for approval before commitment.

## Example 3: finished reply

# Linden Archive Tools — Investment Committee Memo

## 1. Deal Thesis

Linden's commercial software licenses generate $9M of revenue, growing 16% with 75% gross margin, alongside $2M of services. That commercial business could be attractive if the buyer acquired continuing rights to the underlying archive. The primary risk, R1, defeats that premise under the offered structure: all commercial products depend on rights that terminate on acquisition, and the rights owner has refused consent for this buyer. R2 also removes the $5M public subsidy. The seller's refusal to change structure or price means these are not simply documents awaiting routine completion. A commercial valuation can describe a counterfactual rights-cleared business, but it cannot establish realizable value for the transaction the committee is being asked to approve.

## 2. Tiered Risk Table

| ID | Finding and consequence | Upstream tier | Upstream status and conflict | Mitigation or evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Core archive rights terminate on acquisition; consent refused; all commercial products depend on them | Must-Address | Unresolved; written refusal confirmed by financial narrative | No cure identified under offered structure; binding replacement rights required for re-entry |
| R2 | $5M public subsidy is nontransferable | Must-Address | Unresolved | Exclude subsidy from valuation base; replacement funding and standalone cost plan needed for re-entry |
| R3 | Engineers' retention proposal covers six months | Manageable | In-Progress | Revisit team continuity only if a rights-cleared acquisition case becomes viable |
| F1 | Commercial earnings/cost bridge missing; blended loss does not establish standalone loss | Memo observation; not an upstream tier | Commercial-only EBITDA not supplied | Obtain segment cost bridge before any renewed valuation approval |

## 3. Valuation

Include **FolioCloud, CatalogWorks and IndexHarbor**: all are commercial archive-software businesses. None is excluded. Median EV/Revenue is 3.6x, growth 16%, and gross margin 76%. Peer scale is unavailable, so a size adjustment cannot be quantified. Linden's licensing growth matches the median, a neutral growth adjustment on that segment; growth of the combined commercial base is not supplied. Licensing margin is slightly lower at 75%, and the $2M services component carries only 35% margin, supporting a downward mix/margin adjustment. Commercial blended gross margin is ($9M × 75% + $2M × 35%)/$11M = 67.7%, although the peers' own service mixes are unknown.

Use EV/Revenue; target EBITDA is negative and no peer EBITDA multiples exist. Bridge the reported $16M base by removing $5M of nontransferable subsidy, leaving $11M of commercial revenue. Applying a judgmental 2.5-3.0x below the 3.6x median yields **$27.5-33.0M hypothetical EV**, conditional on enforceable commercial rights continuing and a viable standalone cost structure. This is a counterfactual analytical range, not an executable bid for the current transaction; without rights, the $11M base itself is at risk.

The $65M ask equals 5.91x commercial revenue, versus 4.06x reported revenue including subsidy. Neither supports the offered terms. No EBITDA cross-check is available, and the $1M blended loss cannot be adjusted by simply deducting $5M subsidy without the underlying cost bridge (F1). Cash and debt are not supplied, so equity value is not derived. A discount cannot cure the confirmed rights failure; the live deal lacks a defensible going-concern valuation on the supplied evidence.

## 4. Recommendation

**Pass** on the offered transaction. R1 is a confirmed structural failure: consent has been refused, every commercial product depends on the terminating rights, and no replacement is identified. The seller also refuses structural changes or a reduction from $65M, well above the conditional commercial valuation. R2 removes subsidy support, while F1 prevents a standalone earnings assessment. Additional routine diligence would not cure those offered terms. Re-entry requires a changed rights position and an economically credible transaction, not a conditional Proceed verdict.

## 5. Conditions and Next Steps

- **R1 — Deal counsel:** before any re-entry, obtain enforceable rights covering this buyer, structure and commercial uses; acceptance requires removal of acquisition-triggered termination and confirmation that every dependent product is covered.
- **R2 — Deal finance lead:** before any re-entry, produce a subsidy-free funding and operating plan; acceptance requires documented funding sources and no reliance on the nontransferable $5M subsidy.
- **R3 — Engineering integration lead:** only after rights viability is restored, obtain a funded retention and succession plan extending beyond the six-month proposal; acceptance requires named coverage for archive operations through transition.
- **F1 — Finance diligence lead:** before renewed IC review, reconcile commercial revenue, direct costs and allocated overhead to the reported blended loss; acceptance requires a reproducible standalone earnings and cash-funding case at renegotiated terms.
