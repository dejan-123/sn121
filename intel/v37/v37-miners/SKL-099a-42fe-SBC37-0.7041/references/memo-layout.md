# Memo layout and one worked memo

Your next message is the memo itself, built from the worksheet you have just written: its verdict, base, retained median, adjustments, range and conditions are copied from there unchanged, not recomputed. No sentence, analysis or remark comes before the first heading, because anything before it is read as part of the memo.

## The five sections, in this order

1. `## Deal thesis`, 100 to 130 words: what the buyer acquires, the economics from the narrative that support it, and the primary risk named from the worksheet, which the table below then carries.
2. `## Tiered risk table`: every row from worksheet step 1, blocking tier first, as a table with columns finding, tier (as given), status (as given), deal implication in one line, and the condition number that carries it. Each contradiction from step 1 follows as a short flagged-observation line beneath the table.
3. `## Valuation range`, 200 to 260 words: base and why it, the multiple and why, then **every comparable by name** — the retained ones each with its multiple, the excluded ones each with a one-line reason carrying the gap and spread figures — the retained median, the adjustment against it with directions, then the two products and the range, then the ask converted to the same multiple on the same base. A comparable that appears in the artifact and not in this section is an omission the committee will notice.
4. `## Recommendation`, 60 to 90 words: the verdict in a sentence naming the gate's reason, then what it rests on. Where the worksheet wrote a conditional line, it appears here.
5. `## Conditions and next steps`: numbered bullets, one per open row, in table order, each naming its row, with owner role, document or action, and the step it must clear.

Under about 1,100 words in all. No go/no-go language outside the recommendation, no numeric risk scores, no invented precedents.

## Arithmetic conventions

- Quote a multiple as the comp set quotes it (3.4x), a money figure as the narrative quotes it ($26.5M), a percentage as given (64%).
- The retained median is the median of the comparables kept after exclusions, not of the whole set. With an even count it is the midpoint of the two middle values and the division is shown: four retained multiples of 2.8x, 3.4x, 3.9x and 5.6x give a median of (3.4 + 3.9) / 2 = 3.65x, not 3.4x. Reporting the lower middle value as the median understates every figure that follows from it.
- Write each product as it is checked: `base x multiple = result`, with the base named. Round the result the way the narrative rounds, and never introduce a precision the inputs do not have.
- A range is two products of the same base at two multiples. Do not mix bases inside one range.
- If the seller states a price, convert it to the same multiple on the same base so the comparison is like for like, and say so.
- The base changes only for an amount the narrative names and the comps exclude. A recurring or segment percentage ("94% recurring", "61% hardware") is composition, not a one-time item: keep the full figure as the base and let mix inform the adjustment in step 4 instead.

## The worked case

**The artifacts.** Acquiring Harlowe Grid, a utility-metering data business. Risk table: four rows under the labels Must-Address and Manageable. Must-Address, Resolved: the metering licence renewal was granted for eight years. Must-Address, In-Progress: a single supplier provides the firmware signing service, and the contract is in renegotiation. Manageable, Unresolved: the head of engineering resigned two months ago, unreplaced. Manageable, Unresolved: two local authority customers are on expired contracts and continue month to month. Financial narrative: LTM revenue $26.5M, of which $3.8M is a one-time meter-replacement programme that ends this year; recurring base $22.7M growing 16%; gross margin 64%; EBITDA $2.2M on the full base and $0.4M on the recurring base; no debt; seller asks $118M on the full revenue figure. Comp set: Meterline 3.9x, 21x, 19%, 67%; Gridsense 3.4x, 18x, 14%, 63%; Flowmark 2.8x, 15x, 9%, 58%; Calibra Data 5.6x, NM, 37%, 74%; Northfell Metering (acquired three years ago, multiple from that transaction) 4.4x, 24x, 12%, 65%.

## Deal thesis

Harlowe Grid supplies metering data services to utilities under an eight-year licence renewed this year, with a recurring base of $22.7M growing 16% at a 64% gross margin. The acquisition buys a licensed position and a contracted customer base rather than a growth asset: $3.8M of the reported $26.5M is a meter-replacement programme that ends this year and does not recur. The primary risk is the firmware signing service, which a single supplier provides under a contract now in renegotiation; the licensed service cannot be delivered without it, and that finding sits in the risk table below as the blocking item.

## Valuation range

The comps are EV/revenue on full revenue, and the right base here is the recurring $22.7M, not the reported $26.5M: the $3.8M replacement programme ends this year, so a buyer does not acquire it. EBITDA on that recurring base is $0.4M, too small to carry an EBITDA multiple, so the range is struck on revenue and the EV/EBITDA column is noted only as a cross-check that cannot be used.

Sorted, the trading multiples run 2.8x, 3.4x, 3.9x, 5.6x. Included: Meterline 3.9x, Gridsense 3.4x, Flowmark 2.8x. Excluded: Calibra Data 5.6x, whose gap to the nearest multiple is 1.7x against a 1.1x spread across the others, and which carries the set's highest growth at 37% and highest margin at 74%; Northfell Metering 4.4x, a multiple struck at a transaction three years ago rather than a current trading multiple. Retained median: 3.4x.

Against that retained set, Harlowe Grid grows 16% (median 14%, above) and earns a 64% gross margin (median 63%, at the median), on a smaller revenue base than two of the three. Growth supports the median; the terminable local authority contracts and the one-time programme inside reported revenue argue against paying above it. The range therefore sits at the median and just below: $22.7M x 3.0 = $68.1M and $22.7M x 3.4 = $77.2M, so **$68M to $77M**.

The seller asks $118M. On the recurring base that is $118M / $22.7M = 5.2x, above every retained comparable and above the excluded transaction multiple; on reported revenue it is 4.5x, which is how the ask was framed. The gap is the one-time programme and the contract terminability.

## Recommendation

**Request more data.** The firmware signing finding sits in the blocking tier and is In-Progress, not Resolved, so a proceed recommendation is not available whatever the valuation shows. The renegotiated signing terms and the two expired customer contracts are both answerable with documents the seller holds, and the $68M to $77M range is $41M below the ask, so price and diligence would need to move together.

## Conditions and next steps

1. **C1, firmware signing (Must-Address, In-Progress).** Deal counsel to obtain the draft renegotiated signing contract and the current one, with pricing and term, before the IC reconvenes; an unsigned or materially worse term keeps this row open.
2. **C2, head of engineering (Manageable, Unresolved).** The deal lead to obtain the retention and recruitment plan for the vacancy and confirm who signs off firmware releases in the interim, before confirmatory diligence closes.
3. **C3, expired customer contracts (Manageable, Unresolved).** Commercial diligence to obtain both expired contracts and written confirmation of intent to renew, and to restate the recurring base excluding any customer that will not confirm, before the range is finalised.

## How the sections change with the verdict

**Proceed** is available only when every blocking row reads Resolved. Then the recommendation names that fact first ("every must-address finding is Resolved"), the lower-tier rows keep their conditions as **mitigations with owners and closing dates**, and the valuation paragraph ends on the price the range supports rather than on the gap. The thesis still names a primary risk, and that risk is now one the conditions mitigate rather than one that blocks.

**Pass** is for a blocking row that no document could cure, or a corrected range so far below the ask that no negotiation closes it. The recommendation says which, in one sentence. The conditions section becomes what would have to change for the file to reopen: the event, the evidence that would show it, and the price at which the asset would merit a second look. It still carries one bullet per open row.

**Where a tier scheme is not the familiar one**, read what each label means. Critical, Must-Address, Deal-Breaker and Blocker say a finding must be cleared first; Moderate, Manageable, Low, Informational and Monitor say it can be managed. The memo uses the table's words throughout, never substituting its own. A table whose severest label is Manageable has no blocking tier, so the gate does not bar proceeding: the recommendation says that in terms, then rests on the mitigations.

**Where a required artifact is absent**, the memo says which one is missing, writes the sections that the remaining artifacts support, and puts the missing artifact first in the conditions. It does not invent a risk table, a comp set or a narrative, and it does not produce a valuation range with no comp set.

Before sending, check the valuation section lists every comparable from the artifact by name, the retained ones with their multiples and the excluded ones with their gap and spread figures: a comp set of five needs five names in that section.
