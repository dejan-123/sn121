# Writing the investment committee memo

A committee seldom loses money on the risk the deal team argued about. It loses it on a row marked resolved while the process behind it was still running, a multiple laid over revenue the buyer will never own, a comp bought in a bidding war that pulled the median up. I write as the member who must defend the vote a year later. Three people wrote the three artifacts at different stages, so they disagree; the memo says so openly and never quietly repairs one with another. Every figure is in the inputs or worked from them in view; one the inputs do not give (an asking price, the comps' size or mix, a comp's EBITDA) is named as absent, with what the memo does without it.

## The risk table is evidence, not a verdict

Each upstream row keeps its tier and status exactly as given: the committee must see what the analyst said. Then I test each status against the other artifacts and the row's own words. Resolved beside a narrative saying a re-bid is live, an auditor has not opined or a review was deferred rather than closed, or beside a claim the row calls pending, is disputed: the memo keeps the status, writes the contradicting fact beside it, and reads the row as unresolved at the gate, as it reads a blank status. A status nothing contradicts stands, even beside a serious risk, and the paper that shows the resolution (a consent, an amended contract, counsel's opinion) becomes a closing deliverable. A tier that looks too low (an informational row the price leans on, a manageable row already breaching a standard) is flagged with the reason and what would settle it, never moved. Other tier words are mapped in one sentence above the table: the top tier is must-address, every tier beneath it manageable.

## The gate decides the call

Proceed needs no must-address row unresolved, in progress, blank or disputed, and a named mitigation on every manageable row (an escrow, a specific indemnity, insurance, a closing deliverable, a retention agreement). When the table allows that, proceed; holding back there is as wrong as proceeding over an open must-address row. Only a must-address row can block. A manageable row that is open, in progress or disputed never holds the call back: it gets its named mitigation in the conditions, and with no must-address row open the call is proceed.

With a must-address row open, request more data when each open row is a question a document can answer (a closure letter, a consent, an audit report, a regulator's acknowledgement, counsel's merits opinion). Pass when the evidence has already answered a blocking row against the deal, so no document could clear it: a counterparty has said no, or a term or rule bars what the thesis needs. An ask above the range is a price condition, not by itself a pass.

## Price the revenue the buyer will own

The base comes before the multiple: net revenue where the reported line passes money on to others, recurring revenue where a one-time project inflated the last twelve months, commercial revenue where grant income cannot move to a buyer. A seller's multiple on the wrong base looks cheap until restated. EV/EBITDA needs a positive EBITDA that survives scrutiny, each add-back tested alone (one-time severance can come out, recurring "strategic" spend cannot); otherwise it is not meaningful. The base is a figure the inputs state, or one stated amount taken from one, and never a figure built from a percentage.

## Comp discipline

Every comparable is named as included or excluded, each exclusion with a one-line reason: a control or strategic premium (an acquisition price, not a trading level), a sponsor's premium, growth or margin so far from the target and the rest that it prices another business, an NM multiple (on that metric only). One comp table carries them all, its last row the included median as plain values. Set the target's growth, margin and size beside that median in words, each with a direction. Then the range: two products, each an included comp's own multiple x base = value: with an even count, the two comps either side of the median; with an odd count, the median comp and its neighbour on the side the adjustments point. Each product is written in millions to one decimal, and the ask as one division on the same base. Those three are the memo's only calculations: no implied margins, no downside case. The range rests on one base and one multiple: the whole company on the comps' own multiple, with a revenue mix the comps do not break out treated as a direction in words, never priced by parts. Where EV/Revenue and EV/EBITDA both apply, one sentence says which sets the range and why; the other is a cross-check at most. The range ends are exactly the two products, as multiplied. The range is first written as the last line of the valuation, right after its products; the header and thesis carry only figures the inputs state, and the range and the ask recur afterwards in the same words, with no new figure and no what-if range. A share keeps the whole the input gives it (a share of tuition stays a share of tuition); where the narrative's figures conflict, the memo says which one it uses.

## The memo

The title is `# Investment Committee Memo: <target> acquisition`, then one line with the call word alone, the asking price (or that none is given) and the revenue base as the input states it; a price condition waits for Section 4 and quotes the range. Five sections follow in this order, each heading carrying its bound:

1. `## 1. Deal Thesis (up to 150 words)`: what we would buy and why, on the base and growth that justify it, and the primary risk named in the words of its risk-table row.
2. `## 2. Tiered Risk Table (one row per upstream finding)`: the tier-mapping sentence when needed, then columns #, finding, tier as given, status as given, and an observation under 40 words stating the deciding fact in plain words; rows in the input's order.
3. `## 3. Valuation Range (up to 300 words, plus the comp table)`: base and multiple and why, the comp table, the adjustments in words, the ask, then the two products and the range as its last line.
4. `## 4. Recommendation (up to 140 words)`: proceed, request more data or pass as its first words, the rows that decide it named by their words, and why the other two calls do not fit.
5. `## 5. Conditions and Next Steps (one bullet per risk row, up to 45 words each)`: each bullet opens with its row's words. Under proceed it is a closing condition or mitigation; under request more data, the document that would clear the row and who provides it; under pass, what would have to change to reopen.

The whole memo runs about 6,000 to 8,000 characters.
