# Three memos written against the failure list

Each deal gives abridged artifacts and then the memo as it should be delivered. Names and figures are invented. Read them for the reasoning: how a status is tested against the other artifacts, how the base is cut to what the buyer will own, how the range is built and where the call comes from. Do not reuse their facts.

### Memo

# Investment Committee Memo: Corvane Industrial Staffing (industrial staffing)

**Recommendation: NO-GO at this time (do not proceed to signing)**, because two Must-Address findings are open, #2 workers' compensation cover and #4 the labour audit, and no price can absorb an uninsured payroll or an unassessed back-pay claim.

**Input check**
- Scale: LTM revenue $84.6M (+9%), gross margin 21%, reported EBITDA $5.6M (6.6% margin).
- Open: #2 Must-Address Unresolved; #4 Must-Address In-Progress; #3 and #5 Unresolved.
- Contradicted label: #1 is marked Resolved, but the narrative puts the largest client (22%, about $18.6M of revenue) on a 90-day rolling extension during a tender, so #1 is open.
- Add-backs: $0.4M lease exit accepted (signed, single payment); $0.7M retention bonuses rejected (also paid the year before). Defended EBITDA $6.0M (7.1%).
- Net debt $12.5M ($14.8M facility less $2.3M cash).
- Missing: the facility's change-of-control terms.

## 1. Deal Thesis (≤ 120 words)
Corvane places light-industrial workers with regional manufacturers. It grew 9% last year at a 21% gross margin, in line with its trading peers, and on a defended EBITDA of $6.0M it would be a sound bolt-on at a peer multiple. The obstacles are not about price. A staffing agency cannot place workers without workers' compensation cover, and Corvane's lapses in 75 days with nothing bound. A state audit of overtime and joint-employer liability is still in field work, so its cost cannot be sized. Both must close on the carrier's and the department's own documents before value matters.
**Primary risk:** #2, the carrier's non-renewal takes effect in 75 days and no replacement cover is bound.

## 2. Tiered Risk Table

| 4 | State audit of overtime and joint-employer liability, three branches | Must-Address / In-Progress | Blocks proceed | Field work continues; no findings or assessment issued |

Deal-breakers: #2 and #4, both open Must-Address findings.

## 3. Valuation Range
Method: EV/EBITDA, because EBITDA is positive and every comp reports it.
Comps: Forgeline Workforce (8.0x) included; Tallow & Pike Staffing (8.8x) included; Ironreach Talent (7.1x) included but farther on growth (4% vs 9%) and margin (17% vs 21%); Meridale Global Workforce (12.6x) excluded, as its $4.4B revenue is about 52 times the target's.
Bracket: Forgeline (7%, 20%) and Tallow & Pike (10%, 23%) sit closest to the target's 9% and 21%; no adjustment.
- 8.0x × $6.0M defended EBITDA = $48.0M
- 8.8x × $6.0M defended EBITDA = $52.8M

**Defended range: $48.0M-$52.8M EV.**
Cross-check only: EV/Revenue 0.57x-0.62x × $84.6M = $48.2M-$52.5M, consistent with the range.
Ask: $68.0M is 11.33x defended EBITDA (10.15x the seller's $6.7M) and 0.80x revenue, $15.2M (28.8%) above the top.
Equity value: EV less $12.5M net debt.

## 4. Recommendation and Consistency Check (≤ 120 words)
The IC should hold at NO-GO at this time (do not proceed to signing); #2 and #4 decide it, not the $15.2M price gap: even inside $48.0M-$52.8M the IC would be buying an uninsured payroll and an open audit. It reverses only when the new carrier's binder (#2) and the department's closing letter (#4) are both delivered; the deal could then return as proceed with conditions at no more than $52.8M. Checks: primary risk #2 is the first table row; $52.8M is the top of the range; $6.0M, $12.5M and $84.6M are the same in every section.

## 5. Conditions and Data Requests

# Investment Committee Memo: Halyard Point Marinas (marina operator)

**Recommendation: PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS at no more than $97.9M EV.** No Must-Address finding is open: the lease renewal (#1) is evidenced, and the fuel-tank row (#2) is questioned rather than open, so its proof becomes a condition precedent.

# Investment Committee Memo: Mossbank Organics (organic fertiliser producer)

**Recommendation: NO-GO at this time (do not proceed to signing)**: the stop-sale order (#1) is an open Must-Address finding, counsel's expectation that the appeal will succeed does not close it, and the ask is far above the defended range in any case.

**Input check**
- Scale: LTM revenue $27.0M (+12%), gross margin 34%, EBITDA -$2.3M.
- Open: #1 Must-Address In-Progress (under appeal); #3 Manageable Unresolved.
- Questioned: #2 is marked Resolved, but its wording still describes a lapsed listing and nothing confirms reinstatement.
- Base: $6.0M private-label revenue ends on a change of control, leaving $21.0M.
- Ask: $64.8M is the seller's 2.4x applied to the full $27.0M.
- Net debt $8.0M ($9.6M term debt less $1.6M cash).

## 1. Deal Thesis (≤ 120 words)
Mossbank turns farm by-products into certified organic fertiliser for retail and farm-direct customers, growing 12% at a 34% gross margin. The appeal is a branded input in a growing category; the business is not yet profitable, so it can only be valued on revenue. What the buyer would own is $21.0M of revenue, not $27.0M, because the garden-centre chain has said it will leave on a sale. A stop-sale order sits over one product line, and the only document that would close it, the department's rescission, does not exist yet.
**Primary risk:** #1, the stop-sale order stays in force while the appeal runs.

## 2. Tiered Risk Table
