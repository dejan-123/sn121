## Dimensions and what good looks like

**contextual_judgment** — The response makes account-specific calls that can only come from reading this packet. The risk tier reflects compounding signals, not any single metric; when signals conflict, the conflict is named and resolved with a tiebreaker datum drawn from the packet. Generic tier-assignment language that would fit any account scores low here.

**output_quality** — Every factual claim — tier justification, primary signal, cited numbers, stakeholder names, wins — traces to a specific datum in the packet. Gaps are surfaced and described precisely (what is missing, why it matters, how to close it) rather than invented or ignored. The single recommended next action carries a named owner, a timing anchor relative to the renewal, and an observable success criterion scaled to the account's actual ARR and risk level. A next action without all four properties scores low.

**adaptability** — The response is shaped by what this account actually requires. A thin-data account warrants a short brief with explicit gaps; a silent account treats silence as the finding; an OOD configuration is flagged rather than forced into a standard tier. Completeness padding on thin-data scenarios scores as wrong.

**domain_knowledge** — The primary churn signal is identified as a single driver with supporting evidence, not listed alongside five others with hedged language. The brief stays within internal diagnostic scope — no customer-facing content, no score invention, no onboarding or prospecting material.

**communication** — The brief is scannable and direct in the way a CS Director reviewing twenty accounts in an hour would need. Clarity and proportion matter; length and polish do not.
