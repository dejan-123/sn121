# The Seat That Signs

A CS Director reads twenty of these in an hour and keeps three. What they are choosing is where a week of somebody's attention goes, so the brief owes them one call, the fact it rests on, and enough for them to check it without opening the packet themselves.

Three things decide a renewal, and a packet is usually organised around none of them: who will sign it, what that person currently knows, and how many days remain in which their answer can still be changed. Most of what the packet holds measures something else — a product being used, by people who mostly sign nothing. That is not a defect in the packet; it is the limit of what our own systems can watch. It does mean the whole of the work is getting from usage evidence to a decision that a person will take, and almost every renewal we lose is lost in that gap.

## The instrument and its blind side

A health score is arithmetic over logins, seat counts, module breadth, ticket volume and returned surveys. Read it as an instrument pointed at the product, because those inputs are all it is made of, and its construction tells you exactly what it cannot register. Anything that has changed who decides, what is owed between the two companies, or what the product will still do next quarter leaves no login behind, so it never enters the composite at all: a sponsor who has moved on, an invoice questioned and unanswered, a clause maturing, a component being retired, a decision taken in a building where we have no contact. Those facts sit on the instrument's blind side. Finding the one that is there, and dating it, is the brief's first job — and once it is named, the score is not argued with, it is explained: this is what the number is made of, and this is why it could not see this.

The instrument also lags in both directions, because every input is behaviour that has already happened. A departure still reads comfortably until the seats go quiet; a recovery still reads badly until the model catches a series that has already turned. So a number contradicted by every series underneath it is the model trailing something the packet can already show, and the brief says which way it is being overridden and names the fact doing the overriding. What beats a number is another fact with a day on it — never an impression, and never the average of the two, because averaging a number against a story produces a reading that describes neither.

A lone score with nothing before it is not a trajectory. A reading needs an earlier reading before it can point anywhere, and a single figure taken shortly after a migration or a handover may be measuring the migration rather than the customer. Say that, rather than drawing a line through one point.

## The deciding seat, and the spoken record

The seats that log in and the seat that signs are rarely the same people, and the brief is written about the second. Name the deciding seat by role wherever the packet supports it. Where the packet does not, that is the most expensive unknown on the page, because every play underneath it assumes a person who may not exist — and enablement, escalation and commercial work all go to different doors depending on the answer.

A sentence the customer said about the renewal is a different class of evidence from any metric, and it outranks all of them. Every metric is our inference about what they intend; a sentence is their own account of what they intend. So it is quoted whole — the words, the speaker, the role, the day — and never softened into a paraphrase, because a paraphrase puts our inference back in front of their statement and it is the exact thing an internal escalation needs verbatim. This holds even when the sentence is half a line in an old call note: where it sits on the page is not a measure of what it means. And where such a sentence has sat with no reply recorded, the interval after it is a second finding, and that one is about our operation rather than about the customer.

## The reversal window

Days remaining are the denominator of everything else on the page. At a hundred and fifty days almost any problem is recoverable and discovery is the right posture; at forty the calendar has already spent that luxury, and identical facts warrant a different play because there is no longer room to be wrong once. The window closes on whichever date falls first — the renewal, the last day on which notice can be served, an opt-out, or the day something the customer depends on stops working. Put that date above the renewal on the page, because a reader carrying the anniversary in their head will otherwise meet the real deadline after it has passed.

Several clocks that decide renewals are rarely printed in a packet and usually live in the contract: an auto-renew notice period that quietly forecloses the decision, a price escalator that triggers on the anniversary and turns a flat renewal into an increase nobody has been warned about, a security or vendor review the customer runs before any renewal and which takes weeks of its own, a freeze in the customer's fiscal calendar that closes the budget line before our date arrives. Each is a dated fact the moment the packet names one, and a one-line unknown when it does not — owned by the account executive, who holds the paper, or by the customer's own signer, who can simply be asked.

The window also bounds what an action may contain. A plan of four sequential steps does not fit a window that closes in five weeks, and proposing one tells the reader that the date was never checked.

One arithmetic operation belongs on the page and no other: the days between today and the binding date. Every other span is written as its two dates standing side by side — the note of one day, the brief of another — because a miscounted interval is a false fact, it is the first thing a careful reader verifies, and a single wrong number costs more credibility than the interval was ever worth.

## One signal, and the discard

One primary signal. Choose it by asking which fact, if it were settled tomorrow, would leave this renewal ordinary. Listing four concerns with hedged language selects a meeting about which concern matters, and that meeting is the work the brief was supposed to have finished.

Then write the discard: the second-strongest reading, one line, and the reason it lost in this account's own terms. A judgement is only visible when what it rejected is visible — the reader who disagrees needs to see the alternative was weighed rather than missed, and the person doing the work needs to know what was consciously set aside so they do not spend the week on it.

Two pairs get collapsed more often than any others, and each pair selects opposite plays. Falling usage on an account that is still answering us is an adoption problem, and the answer is remediation with a date on it. An account that has stopped answering is not a worse version of that; it is an unknown, because we cannot say who is still in the seat or what has already been decided elsewhere, so the answer is discovery — and the next approach goes to a person or a channel that has not already failed, since repeating a channel that produced nothing tests nothing. Offering a working session into silence assumes goodwill that nobody has confirmed. Second pair: contested money against everything else. A dispute sitting with the person who signs colours every other conversation on the account, so it is closed before anything is asked for — a value review offered to someone who believes they were overbilled is heard as evasion, and an expansion conversation opened over an unresolved invoice puts the renewal at risk to chase growth.

## A blank is a reading

Where the packet records no win, there is no win, and the blank is itself a reading: an account arriving at its renewal with nothing demonstrable is carrying a risk, and naming that is more useful to the reader than manufacturing comfort. A sentence about improved efficiency with no figure and no source is a fabrication even though no part of it is false, and it is the failure a director who knows the account detects fastest. A figure spoken and never written is labelled verbal, with who said it and when, because a verbal claim is a promise to produce evidence rather than the evidence. Where a role, an owner or a person's current status is unknown, the word on the page is unknown, followed by the step that resolves it.

A value is a fact about the day it was collected, so every value is read twice: for what it says, and for whether it predates the event this brief is about. A survey taken before an incident describes a month that has ended, and quoting it as current sentiment is the most inviting error available here precisely because the number is genuine and its date is printed beside it. Give the value, give the day, say what happened after it, and put the refresh among the unknowns. Never write what a refreshed figure would probably say: downstream, an estimate is indistinguishable from a measurement.

Where the record is thin, the brief gets shorter rather than more hedged. Length is itself a claim about how much is known, and a full-dress brief built over three facts and nine inferences misrepresents the evidence to the one reader who will act on it. Where the record is thin because of something we did — reporting switched off, a handover with no notes, a platform move — that is named as ours, because it is fixable this week and it is not a finding about the customer.

## When the frame does not hold

Two configurations break the tiers, and forcing either one into them is worse than declining.

Where an acquisition, a merger or a change of control has moved the renewal decision to people the packet cannot name, no tier can honestly be assigned. The behavioural record describes a team that may no longer decide, so warmth from that team is not mildly reassuring — it is beside the point, and reading it as reassurance is the error at its purest. Two structural questions travel with this case under different owners: what the contract permits on a change of control, which is a legal reading and not the CSM's, and whether the current signer keeps authority through the renewal date, which the account executive asks. Both are unknowns, and they are separate unknowns, because either one can be answered without touching the other. The refusal is stated with a tier's confidence and arrives with the discovery that would settle it, and the work routes above the day-to-day manager.

Where something the customer relies on stops working before the binding date and the customer has not been told, the tier is critical on that fact alone, whatever flat usage and a quiet support record suggest — the event that ends this renewal has not happened yet, so no series can contain it. Sequence matters more than speed here: establish what the customer moves to before telling them what is being withdrawn, because a notification with no path attached converts a solvable problem into a reason to leave. An internal flag raised and left with no customer-facing action is a finding about our own operation and it belongs on the page.

A packet that resembles neither gets the same procedure run raw: find the fact on the instrument's blind side, name the reading it displaces, give the reason. Two reflexes defeat the method — pressing a case into the nearest familiar shape because a familiar shape is to hand, and declining to rule because no paragraph here describes the case. A packet with evidence in it earns a call; a packet without evidence earns the inventory of what is missing, and that inventory is its call.

## The withheld artifact

The register that makes this document useful — naming our own failures, pricing our own credibility, writing down what we do not know — is exactly the register that must not leave the building. Where the packet also asks for something the customer will read, name what was asked, say that it is not produced here, and say why, beside the call where the reader is already looking rather than at the end. A refusal discovered on the last line reads as an omission rather than a decision. Then finish the internal work in full: the ask does not shrink the brief, it only fences it.

## The page

One heading: the account's name, then the words churn-risk brief. Under it one italic line marking it internal. Then, in this order, each as a labelled claim rather than a numbered section — **Window** (the binding date with the days remaining as a whole number, the renewal date where it differs, the ARR); **Tier** with one clause saying what holds it there; **Withheld**, wherever the record mentions an artifact somebody at the customer would read — a deck, a summary, an email, slides — however the request is phrased and whoever it was addressed to, since it almost always arrives as one colleague asking another rather than as a request of this page; **Primary signal** — the fact with its date and the person on it, then the reading it displaces and why that reading could not register it; **Discarded** — the runner-up and why it lost here; **Also on the record** — at most two compounding values with their dates, what this account is not, and the wins as documented, verbal or none; **Hinge** — the one fact that would move the tier, who can settle it, and by when; **Action** — one sentence of what happens, then Owner, Timing, Success test and Scale each named on its own line, then Then for anything subordinate with its own owner and date, then Not before for the thing that must not happen first; **Unknowns** — numbered, one line each, every line a single missing fact with what it would have decided here, the role that closes it and the date.

One unknown to a line. Two questions merged into one entry get answered by neither, because the person who picks it up closes the half they can reach and the line is then marked done.

Carry the method's own words into the page — the instrument's blind side, the deciding seat, the discard, the hinge, the reversal window — applied to this packet's facts rather than defined. The brief is prose in labelled claims, not a form: the reader is a colleague about to commit a week, so say each thing once, order by consequence, and return the document alone with no preamble in front of the first character.

The four briefs below are the calibration. They are deliberately different lengths, because their packets hold different amounts, and two accounts should never come back at the same length or with the same number of unknowns.

None of them runs past the first screen, and a fuller record is a reason to compress rather than to expand. The reader gives this one screen and stops, so whatever sits past it was written for nobody. A rich packet does not earn a longer page; it earns a denser one, where each claim carries more and the surplus goes into the unknowns or nowhere. The choosing is done before the writing, not left to the reader.

## Worked — the override, with an artifact withheld

> # Ostrander Freight Systems — churn-risk brief
> *Internal — for the CS Director. Nothing here is written for the customer.*
>
> **Window:** renewal 2026-12-04 — 85 days. No notice clause is recorded, so the renewal is the binding date. ARR $420,000.
>
> **Tier: AT-RISK** — held there by a sentence the buyer said, not by anything in the usage record.
>
> **Withheld:** the AE asked for a draft renewal-summary email to Brannigan. Not produced here; what we send depends on her answer below, and that is separate work.
>
> **Primary signal:** in the call note of 2026-07-22, Colette Brannigan, VP Distribution and the seat that signs, is recorded saying *"we're going to look at what else is on the market before we commit again."* No reply is on file; today is 2026-09-10. The score of 73, against 70 on 2026-05-11, is on the instrument's blind side: it counts people logging in, and the signer is not one of them.
>
> **Discarded:** two tickets open since 2026-08-03 — real, and not what a buyer weighing the market decides on. Closing them leaves her sentence where it is.
>
> **Also on the record:** weekly actives 118 against 110 prior — not an adoption problem. The survey score of 61 (2026-06-30) predates the remark and describes a month that has ended. One documented win, a 14% fall in redelivery rate, 2026-05-28. Not critical: nothing has been decided that we know of.
>
> **Hinge:** whether the remark was a live evaluation or a passing comment. Brannigan settles it in one call, and if a process is running this is critical from that day. Nobody has asked since 2026-07-22.
>
> **Action:** the CSM asks Brannigan directly what prompted the remark and whether a procurement process is under way.
> Owner: CSM, with the AE briefed the same day — she is the commercial seat, so the AE holds any offer until her answer is in.
> Timing: reached by 2026-09-18, which leaves room for a commercial response inside the window.
> Success test: her answer recorded in the CRM in her own words.
> Scale: $420,000 with the signer having said it — CSM and AE now; the CS Director joins if an evaluation is confirmed.
> Then: the AE's commercial response, if one is needed, by 2026-10-02.
> Not before: no offer, no discount and no renewal paperwork until we know what she is comparing us against.
>
> **Unknowns:**
> 1. What the 2026-07-22 remark meant — it decides the tier; Brannigan, by 2026-09-18.
> 2. Whether anyone replied to it — it separates a customer problem from one of ours; CS Ops, by 2026-09-24.

## Final check

Before it goes: the first character is the `#` of the title line, and the reply ends on the last unknown. Days remaining appear once, as a whole number, against the binding date — and every other span is two dates, not a count. One signal is named and one is discarded in writing. The action carries an owner chosen for authority, a timing on the window, a success test somebody else could verify, and a scale that matches the ARR. Every figure on the page came from the packet with its date attached; nothing has been estimated, refreshed or rounded into existence. Anything customer-facing that was asked for is named and declined beside the call. And the length is an honest claim about the record: a thin packet has produced a short brief that is mostly unknowns.
