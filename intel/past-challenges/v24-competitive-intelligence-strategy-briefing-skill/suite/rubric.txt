## Step 1 — Score each dimension independently

Use anchors 0.0 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0. Intermediate values are permitted when quality genuinely sits between levels. Score each dimension before combining.

**contextual_judgment** — Does the submission make the right call on each signal, given the reader's stated position? Assess whether signals are classified with the correct implication type, whether proximity tiers (core / adjacent / noise) reflect actual exposure rather than headline drama, and whether ambiguous or thinly-sourced signals are routed to open questions rather than resolved with false confidence. A response that classifies every signal identically, inflates every signal to "high threat," or projects confident meaning onto genuinely ambiguous data should score low here regardless of how well-structured it looks.

**output_quality** — Does the brief deliver asserted strategic implications traceable to specific provided signals, rather than cataloging events chronologically? Does the synthesis section stay inside the evidence — no smuggled claims? When a thesis relationship is tested, does the output call "contradicts" plainly when warranted? When position is missing, does it degrade honestly rather than fabricate synthesis?

**adaptability** — Does the response handle what is actually in front of it — including signal types or configurations that do not map cleanly onto standard categories — by reasoning from first principles rather than enumerating anticipated rules? A response that works perfectly on textbook cases but visibly fails or hedges on novel configurations (regulatory actions, partner signals, hybrid tags) should score lower than one that generalizes gracefully.

**domain_knowledge** — Does the framing reflect genuine understanding of competitive intelligence — the difference between a GTM change and a product direction signal, what a capacity/leadership signal implies, why proximity to the reader's segment matters more than signal magnitude?

**communication** — Is the brief structured so a busy executive can extract the priority read and strategic questions quickly? Condensed and precise earns more than verbose and hedged.
