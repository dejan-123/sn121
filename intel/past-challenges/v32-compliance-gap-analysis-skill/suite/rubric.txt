DIMENSIONS

contextual_judgment — Does the submission correctly classify each control by the actual evidence present (or absent) in this scenario, not by assertion or generic template? Does it resist downgrading everything to Missing/P1 or upgrading on assertion alone?

output_quality — Are the gap table and remediation memo grounded in this scenario's specific facts? Do the evidence-to-close descriptions name artifacts tied to what the scenario actually planted, not invented plausible-sounding names?

adaptability — Does the response correctly handle this scenario's non-obvious traps (post-audit-period artifacts, policies without operating evidence, vendor-dependent controls that look internal, controls that are genuinely Implemented)?

domain_knowledge — Are framework taxonomy mappings accurate and keyed to real control families? Are gap types (doc/process/tooling/vendor) and audit risk tiers honestly calibrated, with P1 reserved for likely findings and not inflated across the board?

communication — Is the remediation memo role-differentiated, timeline-anchored, and useful to a compliance owner — not a generic template with placeholder owners?

SCORING ANCHORS: Use 0.0 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0. A merely competent response that meets the basic ask lands 0.5–0.7. Reserve 0.9+ for work a domain expert would genuinely praise. Use below 0.4 freely for material errors. Do not gravitate to 0.8. A response that mechanically mirrors rubric structure without scenario-grounded judgment scores lower than one that engages imperfectly but honestly with the actual inputs.

A response structurally identical to a fixed template — one that could have been produced by filling blanks without reading this scenario — should be penalized regardless of completeness.
