# Deliverable layout

Begin the reply with `# ` and the title; nothing before it, and no scratch work anywhere in the reply. Write it the way an experienced compliance lead briefs leadership, in this order.

1. Title: `# <Organisation> — <Framework> readiness triage: control register and action memo`, then a bold line `Internal readiness triage — not an audit opinion or counsel's advice`, then one line giving the tested window or period, the engagement type and the controlling date.

2. **Bottom line**, four sentences (no counts here):
   - every live exposure with its contain-now step, or "no live exposure";
   - the controlling date as the hard deadline, with the time left;
   - one sentence on what this framework's structure demands here (two regimes with their own regulators, 72-hour clocks and transfer instruments; a Type II period whose design must be finished the day before it opens; a Stage 2 audit with no deferral; an insurer's annual assessment sampling the past year);
   - the verdict: not ready, ready with conditions, or ready.

3. `## Part 1: Control register`, one row per control in inventory order. Prefer one row per inventory line; split into sub-rows only when that line's own words show two different outcomes (never invent a vendor or finding). Every row must be complete — never cut a row mid-cell.
   ID | framework reference | inventory's deciding words | evidence pattern | status (claimed → **assessed**) | evidence to close | gap category | vendor? | tier
   - Framework reference: each clause, criterion or article on its own with its short name, taken from the framework file.
   - Deciding words: a short direct quote (at most 12 words) plus the artifact's date and the date it is measured against ("approved Oct 2024; yearly review due Oct 2025"). State dates against due dates; do not convert them into ages.
   - Evidence pattern: the one pattern name that decided the row.
   - Status: the inventory's claim, an arrow, then the assessed status in bold, e.g. `Implemented → **Partial**`.
   - Evidence to close: form + scope + dates, under 15 words; for Implemented rows, the evidence that will keep accruing.

3b. Immediately under the register, one tally line only: `N rows: a Implemented, b Partial, c Missing, d N/A; P1 x, P2 y, P3 z`, counted from the register you just wrote (sub-rows included). N, status totals and tier totals must all equal the same row count. These are the only counts in the reply — do not put counts in the bottom line or the memo.

4. `## Part 2: Action memo` with To, From, Date, Re and Classification on separate lines.

5. `### Summary`: first sentence names each live exposure and its contain-now step (or says there is none); then the verdict, the controlling date with time left, and what decides whether it is met. No counts. Do not call a control "operational" or "Implemented" in the summary if the register assessed it Partial or Missing.

6. `### P1 items`: a table, one row per P1: item | why the assessor will write it up | closing actions | owner role | due date. Cells under 20 words.

7. `### P2 and P3 items`: one line each: fix, owner role, due date.

8. `### Third parties`: table of control | third party | role and instrument | what they must supply | owner | request by | fallback if late. Role and instrument names the relationship and its paper: BA and BAA; supplier and contract clauses; processor (Art. 28 terms), independent controller (data-sharing agreement) or joint controller (Art. 26), with EU SCCs and the UK IDTA or Addendum per regime; service organisation, report type, period coverage, bridge letter and the user-entity controls you must run.

9. `### Calendar`: dated blocks, not generic buckets, ending with one line on anything that cannot be finished by the controlling date and its interim position (pause, restrict, or a dated risk acceptance signed by a named role). Up to the controlling date, `**Week 1 (dates):**`, `**Weeks 2–3 (dates):**` and so on, each line naming its owning role; then one block for the window or period itself and one for fieldwork or the review. When the controlling date is more than 90 days away, use 30-, 60- and 90-day blocks with their end dates.

10. `### Judgement notes`: exactly three numbered notes, each under 40 words, on the calls a reviewer is most likely to question: a control held at Implemented, a downgrade from the inventory's claim, the controlling-date logic (work started after the window opens yields fewer operating records), each third party's role and instrument, a scope boundary. Ground each note in the inventory's own words.

11. `### Decision for leadership`: two or three sentences giving the verdict, the critical path and the approval or resources needed; no counts.

Length: about 1,200 words and never more than 10,000 characters; an executive reads a tight triage end to end. P2 and P3 one line each; the calendar at most six lines; the third-party table only for parties that must supply or sign something. Keep every register row complete — if short on space, cut P2/P3 and calendar lines, never truncate a register cell.

Your reply now begins with `# ` and the title, with no sentence before it.
