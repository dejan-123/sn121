# How the assessor samples, and the follow-up questions

The first question to an owner is rarely the one that decides a row. What decides it is the second or third, asked after the assessor has drawn a sample and found the one item that does not match. This page lists those follow-ups, grouped by area, with the record that settles each.

## Drawing the sample
- How often does this control run? *The frequency sets the sample: annual controls are seen once, quarterly ones two or more times, monthly ones several times, daily or per-event ones by a draw of twenty-five or more.*
- Where does the population come from? *A system-generated list (every ticket, every leaver, every change) whose completeness can itself be checked; a list typed up for the visit is not a population.*
- What if the sample turns up one exception? *The assessor asks whether it is isolated or systemic, extends the sample, and records the root cause the owner gives; an exception with no explanation reads as a pattern.*
- Can an item be swapped for a cleaner one? *No. Once drawn, the sample stands, which is why a gap closed only for the items the owner expects to be picked closes nothing.*

## Joiners, movers and service accounts
- Pick someone who changed teams: did their old rights go when the new ones came? *Two timestamps per person, removal and grant, with the removal not later than the move.*
- Which accounts belong to no person? *A list of service and shared accounts, each with a named owner and a stated reason it cannot be personal.*
- Who may switch the second factor off for an account, and how often has it happened? *The exception register, each entry with an approver and an expiry.*
- How are emergency accounts kept out of daily use? *Credentials held under seal, an alert on every use, and a review after each one.*

## Devices and endpoints
- How do you know a laptop is encrypted before it holds regulated data? *A management-console report listing each device and its encryption state, dated.*
- What happens to a device that falls out of management? *A rule that blocks it, and one instance traced from detection to remedy.*
- Where are the drives that were wiped? *A disposal record per device: serial, method, date, who did it.*

## Backups and restores
- When did you last bring something back, and how long did it take? *A dated restore test with the target time beside the time achieved.*
- Is the backup copy beyond the reach of the account that could delete production? *A separate account, an immutability setting, or an offline copy, shown.*
- Which systems are not in the backup plan, and who agreed to that? *A written exclusion with an owner's sign-off.*

## Continuity planning
- Which business process stops first if this region goes dark? *A ranked impact analysis naming each process, its tolerable outage and its dependencies.*
- Who has the authority to declare a disaster? *A named role and a deputy, written into the plan.*
- What changed after the last exercise? *An action list from that exercise with owners and closure dates.*

## Incidents and breach decisions
- Take your most recent security event: who decided whether it was reportable, and on what facts? *A dated assessment against the notification test, signed by the person who made the call.*
- How quickly did the first responder escalate? *Detection and escalation timestamps from the ticket or chat export.*
- Where is the lessons-learned note? *A post-incident review naming the cause and the fix, with the fix's own evidence.*
