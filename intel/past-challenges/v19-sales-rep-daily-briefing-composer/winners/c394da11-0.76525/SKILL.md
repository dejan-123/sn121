# Control-Return Brief

Turn one seller's calendar and open pipeline into the briefing they should execute today. Return the briefing, not the analysis.

## Deliverable

Use the supplied date. Stay under 430 words and output these six headings in this order:

1. **Pipeline Health** — one sentence with open-deal count, table-summed value, number of deals requiring action today, and dominant exposure.
2. **Top 3 Actions** — exactly three ranked seller-initiated outbound moves, at most 32 words each.
3. **Meeting Prep** — every meeting chronologically in one sentence of at most 32 words, or state that no customer meetings exist.
4. **Tier A — Other Deals Needing Action Today** — urgent deals not already ranked, at most 15 words each, or state that none remain.
5. **Tier B — Monitor** — compact groups with the evidence that makes waiting safe and a useful trigger; at most 75 words when the board exceeds 15 deals.
6. **Needs Record Update** — only records whose defects change a decision, at most five compact bullets.

Put one plain alert before Pipeline Health when a meeting begins within one hour. Use no title, extra sections, nested checklists, method labels, or invented facts. Never emit a separator line such as `---`. Finish the six sections before briefly declining any forecast, quota model, team audit, or message-drafting request outside the briefing.

## Model revenue control, not CRM motion

Treat each opportunity as a path of commitments. A viable path normally needs:

- an acknowledged problem and consequence;
- a decision process, authority, and timing;
- access through people able to mobilize the account;
- proof with an owner and exit condition;
- commercial agreement with reciprocal commitments;
- governance, paper, and implementation work that fits the remaining time;
- observable completion of seller and buyer promises.

Locate the weakest edge that constrains the next transition. A stage or activity count is not proof of progress. The useful move repairs that edge, obtains authoritative evidence, completes owed work, or exposes that the opportunity should not remain viable.

Separate evidence quality from importance. Prefer completed behavior and verified artifacts, then scheduled events with the right participants, dated direct commitments, undated intentions, indirect signals, and administrative records. Higher-quality evidence overrides lower-quality evidence. An uncertain threat can justify verification without becoming a stated fact. Preserve conflicts rather than resolving them with a favorable assumption.

## Read ownership and time together

For the weakest edge, identify who controls the next transition: seller, buyer, both parties, or nobody yet. Seller-controlled debt is usually high leverage. A buyer-owned wait is safe only when supported by a credible owner, event, or time. Joint work needs a mutual next commitment. Unknown ownership requires evidence before confidence.

Read two clocks. The completion clock is the work and sequence remaining before the buyer's real decision or operating deadline. The decay clock is how quickly attention, access, or recoverability deteriorates without a meaningful exchange. A forecast date matters only in relation to those clocks. Recent motion cannot rescue an impossible path; old activity does not make an explicit customer-owned schedule unsafe.

Use a counterfactual test for urgency: if the seller waits through today, does the opportunity become materially harder to complete, verify, or recover? Tier A requires a defensible yes tied to the current path. Tier B requires affirmative evidence that waiting preserves control. Do not manufacture urgency from amount, quota pressure, stage age, incomplete snapshot fields, or a visible close date alone.

Calibrate elapsed time in working days from the latest verifiable human exchange or completed artifact. Interpret silence relative to the deal's decision horizon and remaining path rather than using age as a verdict. Explicit customer constraints govern contact. Internal metadata cannot substitute for buyer behavior. Record defects belong in Needs Record Update; combine repair with outreach only when the same action protects a live deal and obtains the missing truth.

## Choose interventions by control return

Generate one minimum sufficient intervention for every Tier A deal. It should have one leading verb, one recipient or channel when inferable, one primary ask, and one observable outcome. Use synchronous contact to resolve ambiguity, negotiation, or relationship decay; use an asynchronous message for a precise artifact or confirmation. Clear seller-owned blockers before consuming more buyer effort.

Rank interventions by:

- irreversibility of waiting;
- revenue and strategic exposure;
- mismatch between remaining work and available time;
- probability that today's move changes the path;
- effort, friction, and recipient availability.

Scheduled meetings, preparation, internal administration, and record cleanup never fill the outbound Top 3. Prepare each customer meeting around its decision objective, relevant evidence, concrete ask, and fallback. For an internal meeting, state that no deal preparation is required and mention a useful issue only when relevant.

## Selection and classification guards

Do not fill the Top 3 with interchangeable status checks. Before ranking, form three private shortlists from the qualified urgent set:

- **completion leverage** — a small seller repair or direct confirmation that converts existing buyer intent;
- **exposed path** — the most consequential near-term opportunity whose completion sequence is aged, ownerless, or unbounded;
- **recovery decay** — the access, competitive, funding, or attention window most likely to become unrecoverable.

When all three shortlists are nonempty, take the strongest move from each before taking a second routine paper-status check. This diversifies interventions; it never manufactures an urgent deal. Rank the three selected moves by reversibility, exposure, today's access, and effort-to-impact.

A generic recap, summary, qualification reminder, scheduling note, or normal follow-up is not seller debt unless a buyer explicitly requested it or the seller promised it by now. Verbal intent with paper credibly being prepared is a buyer/seller process, not automatically an outreach emergency; escalate when the promised step is overdue, the path is unbounded, or the remaining time cannot absorb it.

As a calibration cross-check, unexplained silence approaching two business weeks normally threatens recoverability. Shorten that tolerance when the current decision window cannot absorb the remaining work. Near-period-end paper checks may remain in Tier A Other even when recent, but they should not displace a higher-leverage completion, exposure, or decay move from the Top 3.

Use source dates instead of publishing an elapsed-working-day number unless the count has been checked. Do not infer a different activity date from the runtime clock or from an ambiguous note.

## Final routing and arithmetic audit

Route every deal once after ranking. Remove Top 3 deals from Tier A Other. A meeting may be the reason an urgent deal is omitted from outbound work, but meeting preparation is not a substitute for classifying the rest of the pipeline. Tier B explanations must name the commitment that makes waiting rational rather than merely describing a stage.

Compute value in integer thousands: add every row once, use small subtotals for a large table, then add those subtotals. Do not copy a total from surrounding prose or a reference. If two totals conflict, use the pipeline-table sum.

After final routing and deduplication, the Pipeline Health action count equals the three Top 3 deals plus Tier A Other; meeting-only and record-only deals do not inflate it. Recheck table arithmetic, customer constraints, exactly three outbound actions, no repeated urgent deal, no separator lines, and all six complete sections before returning.
