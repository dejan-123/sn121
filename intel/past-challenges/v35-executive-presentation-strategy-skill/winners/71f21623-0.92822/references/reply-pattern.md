# Reply pattern

The reader is an executive who reads the titles first and a row of the table only where a title surprises them. Every figure in a real reply comes from that reply's own input; nothing below is reused except the form. A reply that opens with "Now I have the skill", "Let me build" or "Based on the skill" has spent its first line on the writer instead of the reader: the first characters are `## `.

## One complete reply (invented case)

The papers: a brief dated January for the members' board of Brackenfield Dairy, a farmers' cooperative, asking whether to build its own second bottling line ($3.2M, 15-year design life) or go on paying a contract bottler. The brief says the line "saves $0.9M a year, payback 3.6 years" and that volume is "up 16%". The bottler is paid $1.1M a year; running the line would cost $0.5M a year (the maker's estimate). Note 1: the $0.9M includes $0.3M of new retail sales the sales team expects from two buyer conversations. Note 2: a regional grant would pay 25%, but the scheme closes in March and the order cannot be placed before June. Note 3: the 16% includes a one-year school tender that has ended; without it volume grew 6%. Note 4: the bottler's contract renews each December on six months' notice, on terms "agreed at this September's members' meeting". The whole reply:

## Brackenfield Dairy: build the second bottling line

**Ledger**

| ID | Fact as given (status) | Allows or stops |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Line costs $3.2M; "saves $0.9M a year, payback 3.6 years" (modelled). Note 1: $0.3M of that is sales the sales team expects | On fees alone: $1.1M - $0.5M = $0.6M a year; $3.2M / $0.6M = 5.3 years. Stops the 3.6-year claim |
| E2 | Running cost $0.5M a year (maker's estimate, not measured) | Carries the whole saving |
| E3 | Grant pays 25% (conditional). Note 2: scheme closes in March; no order before June | Not in force on the order date: out of the case |
| E4 | Volume "up 16%" (measured). Note 3: a one-year school tender has ended; 6% without it | 6% is the underlying rate; the case needs no growth |
| E5 | Bottler contract renews each December on six months' notice (contracted); terms "agreed at this September's meeting" in a brief dated January | Notice falls due in June, the month of the order; delivery date not supplied. The September date cannot be right as written: reported, not corrected, not relied on |

**Call:** Build. With the expected sales and the grant taken out, the line still repays in 5.3 years of a 15-year life.

**Governing thought:** The board should build the second line: its $3.2M comes back in 5.3 years from $0.6M a year of bottling fees no longer paid, without the grant and without the sales forecast.

**In the room:** Start with what the cooperative pays the bottler today, because that is the whole reason to act. Then take the two soft figures out in front of the board, so that nobody has to trust the sales forecast or the grant in order to vote yes. Close on the calendar: June is when notice and the order both fall due, and the one thing still missing is a delivery date.

**Argument tree**
1. **It pays on fees alone.** $1.1M a year to the bottler against $0.5M to run the line (E1, E2).
2. **It pays with the soft figures left out.** The $0.3M of expected sales and the 25% grant are outside the case, and 5.3 years sits inside the 15-year life (E1, E3). Against it: the $0.5M is the maker's figure, untested here (E2).
3. **The calendar allows it, with one gap.** Notice to the bottler and the order both fall in June (E5); the delivery date is not supplied.

Each branch settles a different question: does it pay, does it pay when the soft figures go, can it be done in time. No fact sits under two of them.

**Slides**

| # | Action title | Shows | Visual and why | Speaker note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Building the second line repays its $3.2M in 5.3 years on bottling fees alone | The thought and its three reasons | Callout: one decisive number | Open on the $1.1M a year paid to the bottler. If a member asks why the brief said 3.6 years, the answer is that it counted $0.3M of sales nobody has signed. |
| 2 | Bottling in-house costs $0.5M a year against the $1.1M paid out today | $1.1M, $0.5M, $0.6M saved (E1, E2) | Two bars: one cost exceeds another | Say plainly that the $0.5M is the maker's estimate. If asked who has run this line at that cost, the papers do not say: not supplied. |
| 3 | The case stands with the $0.3M sales forecast and the 25% grant both left out | $0.9M quoted, $0.6M kept; 3.6 years, 5.3 years (E1, E3) | Bridge from quoted to corrected | Explain that the scheme closes in March and the order cannot go before June. If asked whether it might reopen: not supplied, and the case does not need it. |
| 4 | Volume grew 6%, not 16%, and the line needs none of that growth | 16% reported, school tender removed, 6% (E4) | Bridge: a headline restated by its note | Note that the school tender has ended. If someone calls the line a bet on growth, the saving is on fees paid today. |
| 5 | Approve now, so that notice to the bottler and the order both go in June | June notice, June order, December renewal (E5) | Timeline: deadlines | Walk the dates: notice is due six months before December. If asked what happens when delivery is late, no delivery date is supplied, and it is needed before the vote. |

**Self-check**
Read alone, the five titles say: build, it pays, it pays without the soft figures, growth is not needed, approve now.
- E1: Note 1 moved payback from 3.6 to 5.3 years; the direction held.
- E2: the whole saving rests on the maker's $0.5M; a measured figure from a comparable line would settle it.
- E3: the grant is excluded, not discounted; an extended scheme would be upside only.
- E5: with no delivery date, the overlap between a new line and the December renewal cannot be judged.
- E5: a meeting in "this September" is cited as past in a brief dated January; the renewal terms need confirming before they are quoted to the board.

## How the other replies open

**Contradict.** Had Note 1 said instead that the bottler offers to renew at $0.7M a year, the saving would be $0.7M - $0.5M = $0.2M a year and the payback $3.2M / $0.2M = 16 years, past the 15-year life. After the heading (`## Brackenfield Dairy: the second line does not repay`) and the ledger:

> **Call:** Contradict. The brief asks for approval, and on its own figures the line needs 16 years to return its cost, one more than it is built to last.
>
> **The contradiction:** Owning the line does cut what is paid out each year, and the brief is right about that. But at the renewal price in Note 1 the cut is $0.2M a year, not $0.9M, and no figure in the papers brings the payback inside the line's life.
>
> **Two governing thoughts**
> - *Backed by the evidence (recommended):* The board should decline the line and renew with the bottler at $0.7M a year; the question reopens only if the renewal offer is withdrawn or a measured running cost comes in well under $0.5M.
> - *Requested:* Build now. It holds only if one of those two conditions is already met; neither is in the papers.

Tree, slides and self-check then follow for the first thought only. The deck is four or five slides: the answer, one for each reason the case fails, and what would reopen it. It proposes no pilot, tranche or part-approval that the papers do not themselves size, and each payback is one division of two figures from the papers.

**Rule to its owner.** Had the brief added that the chair told members last spring the cooperative would fund no plant while reserves stand below six months of milk payments, and they stand at four:

> **Call:** Rule to its owner. The line repays in 5.3 years; the chair's reserves rule stands in its way.
>
> **Governing thought:** The figures favour building the line, which repays its $3.2M in 5.3 years, but building breaks the chair's six-month reserves rule, so the decision before the board is whether to waive that rule for this purchase; on the figures, we recommend that it does.

Branch 1 is the cost case, branch 2 the rule in the chair's words and what keeping it costs ($0.6M a year of fees that continue), branch 3 what each choice sets in motion. The last slide puts the two-way choice: waive and build, or keep the rule and renew.

**A review before the point of no return.** Had a trial of the same line at a neighbouring plant been due to report in four weeks, with the order cancellable until then: the call is still Build, the governing thought says to order now, and the trial is named as the point where the order stops if it reads badly, with what is at stake until then taken from the papers.

**Ask.** A parks department sends two years of pool figures and writes "help us tell our story", naming no audience and no decision. The whole reply:

> ## Aldermoor Pools: two things to settle before a storyline
>
> | ID | Fact as given (status) | Allows or stops |
> |---|---|---|
> | E1 | Visits rose from 210,000 to 240,000 (measured). Note 1: a free-entry month added about 20,000 | Paid visits are about 220,000: up 5%, not 14% |
> | E2 | Income per visit fell from $6.10 to $5.60 (measured) | More visits are not yet more income |
> | E3 | Both years fall after the new opening hours began (measured) | No period from before the change: the data cannot show what the change did |
>
> **Call:** Ask. No audience and no decision are named, so there is no claim to test: no governing thought, tree or slides yet.
>
> **The question:** who will be in the room, and what must they decide?
>
> **Decisions this data could serve**
> 1. Whether to repeat the free month. The leisure committee would decide; it rests on E1's 20,000 visits and E2's falling income per visit, and leans against unless free visitors return as paying ones (not supplied).
> 2. Whether to change entry prices. The head of department would decide; it rests on E2, and the direction is open without a price history (not supplied).
>
> **Send back:** the audience, the decision, and the date of the meeting.

**Repair.** After the heading (`## [Name]: the outline, repaired`):
1. **What the outline argues as written:** one sentence with its figures.
2. **Title-only reading:** a table of slide, current title, what it says read alone, the break.
3. **Corrected governing thought:** one sentence, on corrected figures where two slides disagree.
4. **Reordered and retitled:** a table of new position, old slide, new action title, and kept / retitled / moved / merged. Every original slide appears once; the answer comes first and the decision last.
5. **Self-check:** the figures corrected, what the outline still lacks (named, not written), the seam it still carries.

**Explain.** The ledger marks which reasons are measured and which are weaker than they look. However many reasons the brief lists, the tree has two to four branches, never one per reason, and the deck stays within the same length as any other. The governing thought gives the decision and its main reason for the people hearing it. The objection slide's title states the concern and the answer together. Labels assigned for the deck replace real names in every row and note.

## Order of a built reply

Heading with the organisation and the position taken; **Ledger**; **Call**; **Governing thought**; **In the room** (two or three sentences telling the argument in the order the presenter would say it); **Argument tree** with its line on why the branches do not overlap; **Slides** (one table, speaker notes in the presenter's own sentences); **Self-check**. Parts sit under bold labels; the heading is the only heading.

Your reply now begins with `## ` and the organisation's name, and nothing comes before it. That holds for an Ask as well: the reason for asking belongs in its **Call** line.
