# Reply pattern

The reader is an executive who reads the titles first and a row of the table only where a title surprises them. Every figure in a real reply comes from that reply's own input; nothing below is reused except the form. A reply that opens with "Now I have the skill", "Let me build" or "Based on the skill" has spent its first line on the writer instead of the reader: the first characters are `## `.

## One complete reply (invented case)

The papers: a brief dated March for the trustees of Harbour Lights Theatre, asking whether to buy its own stage-lighting rig ($1.8M, 12-year design life) or go on hiring one for each production. The brief says owning "saves $0.5M a year, payback 3.6 years" and that attendance is "up 15%". Hire costs $0.7M a year; running an owned rig would cost $0.4M a year (the supplier's estimate). Note 1: the $0.5M includes $0.2M of extra ticket income the marketing team expects from bigger shows. Note 2: an arts capital grant would pay 20%, but the round closes in April and the order cannot be placed before July. Note 3: the 15% includes one touring musical that will not return; without it attendance grew 5%. Note 4: the hire agreement ends each January on eight months' notice, on terms "agreed at this November's trustees' meeting". The whole reply:

## Harbour Lights Theatre: buy the lighting rig

**Ledger**

| ID | Fact as given (status) | Allows or stops |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Rig costs $1.8M; "saves $0.5M a year, payback 3.6 years" (modelled). Note 1: $0.2M of that is ticket income the marketing team expects | On hire fees alone: $0.7M - $0.4M = $0.3M a year; $1.8M / $0.3M = 6 years. Stops the 3.6-year claim |
| E2 | Running cost $0.4M a year (supplier's estimate, not measured) | Carries the whole saving |
| E3 | Grant pays 20% (conditional). Note 2: round closes in April; no order before July | Not in force on the order date: out of the case |
| E4 | Attendance "up 15%" (measured). Note 3: one touring musical included; 5% without it | 5% is the underlying rate; the case needs no growth |
| E5 | Hire agreement ends each January on eight months' notice (contracted) | Notice falls due in May, before the July order; delivery date not supplied |
| Dates | Brief dated March. To come: grant round closes April, notice due May, order from July, hire ends January. Spoken of as past: terms "agreed at this November's trustees' meeting" | November is later than the brief, so it cannot be past: a clash, reported as written, not corrected, not relied on |
| Test | Does the saving repay the outlay inside the rig's life? $1.8M / $0.3M = 6 years against 12 | Passes on hire fees alone, with E3 left out |

**Call:** Build. The test passes: with the ticket income and the grant taken out, the rig still repays in 6 years of a 12-year life.

**Governing thought:** The trustees should buy the rig: its $1.8M comes back in 6 years from $0.3M a year of hire fees no longer paid, without the grant and without the ticket forecast.

**In the room:** Begin with what the theatre pays in hire today, because that is the whole reason to act. Then remove the two soft figures in front of the trustees, so that nobody has to believe the ticket forecast or the grant in order to vote yes. Finish on the calendar: notice on the hire agreement falls due in May, two months before an order can go, and the one thing still missing is a delivery date.

**Argument tree**
1. **It pays on hire fees alone.** $0.7M a year in hire against $0.4M to run an owned rig (E1, E2).
2. **It pays with the soft figures left out.** The $0.2M of ticket income and the 20% grant are outside the case, and 6 years sits inside the 12-year life (E1, E3). Against it: the $0.4M is the supplier's figure, untested here (E2).
3. **The calendar is tight, with one gap.** Notice falls due in May and the order cannot go before July (E5); the delivery date is not supplied.

Each branch settles a different question: does it pay, does it pay when the soft figures go, can it be done in time. No fact sits under two of them.

**Slides**

| # | Action title | Shows | Visual and why | Speaker note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buying the rig repays its $1.8M in 6 years on hire fees alone | The thought and its three reasons | Callout: one decisive number | Open on the $0.7M a year spent on hire. If a trustee asks why the brief said 3.6 years, the answer is that it counted $0.2M of ticket income nobody has sold. |
| 2 | An owned rig costs $0.4M a year to run against the $0.7M paid in hire today | $0.7M, $0.4M, $0.3M saved (E1, E2) | Two bars: one cost exceeds another | Say plainly that the $0.4M is the supplier's estimate. If asked which theatre runs this rig at that cost, the papers do not say: not supplied. |
| 3 | The case stands with the $0.2M ticket forecast and the 20% grant both left out | $0.5M quoted, $0.3M kept; 3.6 years, 6 years (E1, E3) | Bridge from quoted to corrected | Explain that the round closes in April and no order can go before July. If asked whether a later round is planned: not supplied, and the case does not need it. |
| 4 | Attendance grew 5%, not 15%, and the purchase needs none of that growth | 15% reported, touring musical removed, 5% (E4) | Bridge: a headline restated by its note | Note that the musical will not return. If someone calls the rig a bet on audiences, the saving is on hire paid today. |
| 5 | Approve now, so that notice goes in May and the order in July | May notice, July order, January end of hire (E5) | Timeline: deadlines | Walk the dates: notice is due eight months before January. If asked what happens when the rig arrives late, no delivery date is supplied, and it is needed before the vote. |

**Self-check**
Read alone, the five titles say: buy, it pays, it pays without the soft figures, growth is not needed, approve now.
- E1: Note 1 moved payback from 3.6 to 6 years; the direction held.
- E2: the whole saving rests on the supplier's $0.4M; a measured figure from a theatre already running the rig would settle it.
- E3: the grant is excluded, not discounted; a later round would be upside only.
- E5: with no delivery date, the gap between the hire agreement ending in January and the rig arriving cannot be judged.
- Dates: a meeting in "this November" is cited as past in a brief dated March; the hire terms need confirming before they are quoted to the trustees.

## How the other replies open

**Contradict.** Had Note 1 said instead that the hire firm offers to renew at $0.5M a year, the saving would be $0.5M - $0.4M = $0.1M a year and the payback $1.8M / $0.1M = 18 years, past the 12-year life. After the heading (`## Harbour Lights Theatre: the rig does not repay`) and the ledger:

> **Call:** Contradict. The test fails: the brief asks for approval, and on its own figures the rig needs 18 years to return its cost, six more than it is built to last.
>
> **The contradiction:** Owning the rig does cut what is paid out each year, and the brief is right about that. But at the renewal price in Note 1 the cut is $0.1M a year, not $0.5M, and no figure in the papers brings the payback inside the rig's life.
>
> **Two governing thoughts**
> - *Backed by the evidence (recommended):* The trustees should decline the purchase and renew the hire at $0.5M a year; the question reopens only if the renewal offer is withdrawn or a measured running cost comes in well under $0.4M.
> - *Requested:* Buy now. It holds only if one of those two conditions is already met; neither is in the papers.

Tree, slides and self-check then follow for the first thought only. The deck is four or five slides: the answer, one for each reason the case fails, and what would reopen it. It proposes no pilot, tranche or part-approval that the papers do not themselves size, and each payback is one division of two figures from the papers.

**Rule to its owner.** Had the brief added that the treasurer told the trustees in the autumn that no capital purchase would be made while the roof appeal is open, and the appeal is still open:

> **Call:** Rule to its owner. The test passes, the rig repaying in 6 years; the treasurer's hold on capital purchases stands in its way.
>
> **Governing thought:** The theatre should buy the rig, which repays its $1.8M in 6 years, but only if the treasurer lifts her hold on capital purchases for this one order: that waiver, not the cost case, is the decision in front of this meeting.

Branch 1 is the cost case, branch 2 the hold in the treasurer's words and what keeping it costs ($0.3M a year of hire that continues), branch 3 what each choice sets in motion. The last slide puts the two-way choice: lift the hold and buy, or keep it and renew the hire.

**A review before the point of no return.** Had a safety inspection of the rig been due in three weeks, with the order cancellable until then: the call is still Build, the governing thought says to order now, and the inspection is named as the point where the order stops if it reads badly, with what is at stake until then taken from the papers.

**Ask.** A community college sends two years of enrolment figures and writes "help us tell our story", naming no audience and no decision. The whole reply:

> ## Wexcombe College: two things to settle before a storyline
>
> | ID | Fact as given (status) | Allows or stops |
> |---|---|---|
> | E1 | Enrolments rose from 3,400 to 3,900 (measured). Note 1: a one-off employer contract added about 300 | Without it about 3,600: up 6%, not 15% |
> | E2 | Fee income per enrolment fell from $410 to $380 (measured) | More learners are not yet more income each |
> | E3 | Both years fall after the new timetable began (measured) | No period from before the change: the data cannot show what the change did |
>
> **Call:** Ask. No audience and no decision are named, so there is no claim to test: no governing thought, tree or slides yet.
>
> **The question:** who will be in the room, and what must they decide?
>
> **Decisions this data could serve**
> 1. Whether to seek more employer contracts. The governors would decide; it rests on E1's 300 enrolments and E2's falling income per enrolment, and leans against unless the contract's own margin is known (not supplied).
> 2. Whether to change course fees. The finance director would decide; it rests on E2, and the direction is open without fee levels by course (not supplied).
>
> **Send back:** the audience, the decision, and the date of the meeting.

**Repair.** After the heading (`## [Name]: the outline, repaired`):
1. **What the outline argues as written:** one sentence with its figures.
2. **Title-only reading:** a table of slide, current title, what it says read alone, the break.
3. **Corrected governing thought:** one sentence, on corrected figures where two slides disagree.
4. **Reordered and retitled:** a table of new position, old slide, new action title, and kept / retitled / moved / merged. Every original slide appears once; the answer comes first and the decision last.
5. **Self-check:** the figures corrected, what the outline still lacks (named, not written), the seam it still carries.

**Explain.** The ledger marks which reasons are measured and which are weaker than they look. However many reasons the brief lists, the tree has two to four branches, never one per reason (six listed reasons still make three branches), and the deck stays within the same length as any other. The governing thought gives the decision and its main reason for the people hearing it. The objection slide's title states the concern and the answer together. A label the papers assign for the deck (a code name, "Supplier 2") is the only name used: the ledger, titles, table cells and speaker notes never spell out the real one.

## Order of a built reply

Heading with the organisation and the position taken; **Ledger**, ending on its dates row and its test row; **Call**; **Governing thought**; **In the room** (two or three sentences telling the argument in the order the presenter would say it); **Argument tree** with its line on why the branches do not overlap; **Slides** (one table, speaker notes in the presenter's own sentences); **Self-check**. Parts sit under bold labels; the heading is the only heading.

Your reply now begins with `## ` and the organisation's name, and nothing comes before it. That holds for an Ask as well: the reason for asking belongs in its **Call** line.
