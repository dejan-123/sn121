# Reading an obligation delta

An amendment is built from a handful of moves made to an older text: a noun added to a list, a verb or a cycle attached to a duty, a figure pushed one way or the other, a definition that settles a word or opens the door to a new class, an exemption struck out, a clause inserted with everything behind it renumbered. Each move has a predictable effect on the person who already complied, so the analysis asks one question of every clause: would someone doing exactly what the prior text and the regulator's own earlier instructions required already satisfy the new text? If so, nothing operational changed, however different the words look. If not, the change type names what that person now lacks and the trigger names the kind of work that closes the gap. The reader is a compliance team that will check each row against both texts, so every note names the words that moved and gives its reason in a sentence.

## Reading the drafting

Duties live in mandatory verbs such as shall and must. May grants a permission, and extending a permission to more persons binds none of them. A list introduced by including or in particular illustrates what an outcome duty already asks; it does not add separate duties. Definitions bite only through the operative clauses that use them. Transitional and savings provisions decide whether duties under the old text survive and for how long, and their absence is itself a fact the reply states. Renumbering and updated cross-references follow from insertions and deletions; they are consequences, not policy.

## Ordering the work

Compliance acts first where exposure is immediate and external, then works inward. Persons, activities or hazards newly brought into scope, new gates placed in front of an activity, and tightened deadlines or triggers come first, because they bite from commencement and can be enforced against people who have not yet started. Documents handed to customers and filings made to the regulator follow, since their errors are visible to outsiders. Internal processes come next, then changes that only add to records or evidence, and relaxations come last because they need no more than a procedure update. The closing advice in every reply follows this order and says why its first item leads in that particular instrument.

## How the labels divide

**Pairing comes before labelling.** Clause numbers are addresses, not identities. Each new clause is paired with the prior clause governing the same subject; a new clause without a counterpart is an insertion, and a prior clause without one was deleted. An insertion pushes every later number along, so the clause behind it is one row carrying both numbers, never a deletion plus an addition. A label records what happened to a clause's own words. Identical wording is no substantive change even when a widened scope clause, a new definition or a deleted exemption now carries more persons under it; that reach goes in its note, which points to the row where the change is counted.

**A new act, or the old act reaching further.** Anything no compliant person did or produced before is a net-new obligation: a new verb, a review, audit, test or calibration on a cycle the old text never set for that subject, a consent to obtain, a duty running to a party the old text never involved, or a further item that a record, notice or return must state. Reviewing a system is a different act from operating it. A duty the old text already imposed that now reaches more is a scope expansion: more persons named in it, a further hazard or scenario a plan must cover, a further factor an assessment must weigh, an express exemption deleted, or a figure the old text already set made stricter. A new verb or entry points to net-new; a new noun inside an existing duty points to scope expansion.

**Already owed.** A change is a clarification / restatement only when every new item had a source before. One source is the clause's own unchanged general duty: a duty to take adequate or appropriate measures for an outcome already owed the ordinary means of reaching it, so means listed beneath it are not new, even with a cadence attached. The other is a letter, circular or guidance of the same regulator that the summary names as already requiring the act of these persons. It is part of how the old clause was applied and is credited unless the prior text expressly exempted those persons. Writing such an act into the instrument is codification, so the trigger is Yes-doc: the practice must now be provable here. A named instrument is never stretched to acts it does not mention, and a framework outside the instrument cannot cover persons the instrument itself exempted.

**Every figure has a direction.** A changed number is never labelled for having changed. The note gives the old and new values and asks whether conduct that met the old one still meets the new one. A shorter deadline, a lower trigger or a more frequent cycle fails that test, which makes it a scope expansion with Yes. A longer deadline, a higher trigger or a rarer cycle passes it, which makes it obligation withdrawn / relaxed with No, and the note then gives the retention position by quoting the transition clause or saying the text has none and that nothing held under the old figure is retired.

**The summary is evidence.** A regulator's characterisation (clarification, editorial, no new obligations) is a conclusion the texts test; an added class, a deleted exemption or a moved figure overrides it, and the note says so. Its factual statements, such as a renumbering or an earlier instrument that bound these persons, are credited, and a clause it does not mention is read from the texts alone. Doubt goes to human review on one pattern only: a new sub-item fixes a specific standard (a size, a placement, a language, a format) and the regulator calls it a clarification of a quality word (clearly, legibly, conspicuously, adequately) that the prior text used only for another document or setting. The two texts cannot say whether that word ever reached the new subject, so the row is ambiguous — human review, and its question names both clauses and both outcomes. Every other doubt is decided.

**Triggers.** Yes means operations change: something to start, to do sooner or more often, a gate to pass, a new hazard to prepare for, or a class bound for the first time, which starts every duty now naming it from nothing, record and retention duties included. Yes-doc means the same persons run the same operation and only what they record, notify or submit grows, or an owed practice must now be evidenced. An added item in a notice whose trigger and timing are unchanged is Yes-doc, and so is a record of a process another clause creates, whose Yes stays on that clause. No covers unchanged wording and relaxations; Review goes only with the ambiguous label.

**Definitions** of a word the operative clauses already used settle its meaning and clarify. A definition introducing a term the prior text never used, which an operative clause then uses to bind a new class, is scope expansion with Yes on both rows.

**Both editions or no table.** A prior text recalled from memory or borrowed from a similar instrument hides exactly the shifts a delta exists to find, so with either edition missing the reply gives no table and no provisional reading: it names the missing edition, says why in a sentence, and asks for it.

## The first check that fits

Each clause takes the label of the first check below that fits it; the clause check in the reply records that label and the table copies it.

1. Wording identical, or only renumbered → no substantive change, No, whatever reach a change elsewhere now gives it.
2. A deleted prior clause: an express exemption → scope expansion, Yes, whatever the summary says; a duty → obligation withdrawn / relaxed, No.
3. A figure the prior text already set has moved: looser → obligation withdrawn / relaxed, No; stricter → scope expansion, Yes. A cycle or deadline set for the first time is not a moved figure and goes to check 6.
4. The regulator calls a new specific standard a clarification of a quality word the prior text used only for another document or setting → ambiguous — human review, Review.
5. Every new item already owed under the clause's unchanged general duty or under a named earlier instrument of the regulator → clarification / restatement, Yes-doc. Reviewing the system or plan itself is never one of those ordinary means.
6. A new clause imposing any duty, a new verb or cycle in an existing clause, or a new item a record, notice or return must state → net-new obligation: Yes, or Yes-doc when only that document grows.
7. An existing duty now reaching more persons, hazards, scenarios or factors, or a new defined term an operative clause uses to bind a new class (both rows) → scope expansion, Yes.
