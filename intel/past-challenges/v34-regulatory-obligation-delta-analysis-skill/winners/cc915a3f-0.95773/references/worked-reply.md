# A worked reply

The reply is written for a compliance team that will hold every row against the two texts. It uses the instrument's short name, its edition names and its clause unit exactly as the input gives them. The instrument below is invented for this file, so none of its names, numbers or clauses belong in a reply; it exists to show where each kind of change lands and what the finished reply looks like.

## The papers

Revision B (Conditions 1–8) and Revision C (Conditions 1–9) of a fictional Kestle Fen Irrigation Code (KFIC), made by the Kestle Fen Water Board, arrive with Board Guidance Note 6. The note says Revision C "clarifies existing practice and adds no new requirements"; that the backflow report in Condition 2 "reflects the Board's Fertigation Letter, which already required holders to report any backflow within 6 hours"; and that Condition 4(c) "clarifies the clearly visible standard already in the Code". In Revision B the words "clearly visible" appear once, in Condition 7, which is about the warning sign at each intake.

## The whole reply

## KFIC Revision B → Revision C

### Orientation

This delta compares the Kestle Fen Irrigation Code Revision B (Conditions 1–8, prior text) with Revision C (Conditions 1–9, new text). Board Guidance Note 6 came with the texts: its view that Revision C "adds no new requirements" is tested row by row, while its statements of fact, that the Fertigation Letter already required the 6-hour backflow report and that Condition 4(c) draws on the Code's "clearly visible" wording, are taken as evidence. Condition 8 is inserted, so prior Condition 8 now sits at Condition 9, and clauses were paired by subject rather than by number.

### Clause check

- Cond. 1: adds "operators of shared reservoir schemes" to the persons bound; note: "no new requirements"; owed before: nothing, the class stood outside Revision B → scope expansion, Yes
- Cond. 2: lists a check valve at every injection point, a valve test before each season and a backflow report within 6 hours under the unchanged "adequate measures to prevent backflow"; note: the report reflects the Fertigation Letter; owed before: valves and tests under the general duty, the report under the Letter → clarification / restatement, Yes-doc
- Cond. 3: adds calibration of each meter by a Board-approved tester at intervals not exceeding 26 months; note silent; owed before: nothing, Revision B set no cycle for meters → net-new obligation, Yes
- Cond. 4(a): the drought plan must also cover stopping abstraction when the Board declares an algal bloom; note silent; owed before: no, a new scenario in the plan's object → scope expansion, Yes
- Cond. 4(b): the notice sent when the plan is invoked must also state the hour abstraction stopped; note silent; owed before: no, though what triggers the notice and when it is due are unchanged → net-new obligation, Yes-doc
- Cond. 4(c) (new): the pump attendant's copy of the plan must be fixed beside the pump controls; note calls it a clarification of "clearly visible", which Revision B wrote only in Cond. 7, for intake warning signs → ambiguous — human review, Review
- Cond. 5: season return due 35 → 15 days after the season closes, shorter, so stricter → scope expansion, Yes
- Cond. 6: notice before a night's draw above 50 → 80 megalitres, higher, so looser → obligation withdrawn / relaxed, No
- Cond. 7: identical → no substantive change, No
- Cond. 8 (new): written consent from the Board's channel engineer before opening any new offtake; note silent; owed before: nothing → net-new obligation, Yes
- Cond. 9 (was: Cond. 8): identical, moved down by the insertion → no substantive change, No

**Review-flagged clauses:**
- **Cond. 4(c):** Did the "clearly visible" standard that Revision B Cond. 7 set for intake warning signs already govern where the attendant's copy of the drought plan is kept? If yes → clarification / restatement, No; if no → net-new obligation, Yes.

**Notes for downstream:** Cond. 1 leads, because shared reservoir scheme operators are bound by every Condition from commencement and start from nothing; Cond. 8's consent gate and Cond. 5's 15-day return come next, since they bite on the first new offtake and the first season to close. Cond. 4(a)'s bloom shut-down and Cond. 3's calibration cycle are operational work for holders already bound, while Conds 2 and 4(b) change only the evidence and the notice; Cond. 6 needs a procedure update, Cond. 4(c) waits on the review answer, and Cond. 9 needs its cross-references updated.

## When an exemption is struck out

Had Revision C changed nothing except deleting prior Cond. 6, "Conditions 3 and 4 do not apply to holders irrigating less than 4 hectares", with the Guidance Note calling the deletion a clarification because small holdings already followed a regional farm assurance scheme, prior Conds 7 and 8 would move up one place. The clause check would carry these lines:

- Cond. 3: identical; small holdings now reach it through the deleted exemption → no substantive change, No
- Cond. 6 (was: Cond. 7): identical, moved up by the deletion → no substantive change, No
- Prior Cond. 6 (deleted): express exemption for holdings under 4 hectares struck out; note: "clarification"; owed before: nothing under this Code, the scheme sits outside it → scope expansion, Yes

and the rows that matter read:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cond. 3 | no substantive change | Fit a flow meter at each abstraction point | Identical | No | Wording unchanged; holdings under 4 hectares now reach it through the deletion of prior Cond. 6, counted on that row. |

Cond. 4 takes the same row as Cond. 3. The duties the exemption used to suspend keep their own label, because their words did not move.

Had Revision C instead added Cond. 1A, defining "water broker" as a person who buys and resells seasonal abstraction allocations, written "and water brokers" into Cond. 5 (the season return) and Cond. 9 (record keeping), and left untouched a Cond. 7 permission that holders "may display the Board's water-saver mark", the rows would read:

| clause-ref | change-type | prior-text summary | new-text summary | action-trigger | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cond. 1A (new) | scope expansion | No definition; "water broker" not used | Defines "water broker" as a person who buys and resells seasonal allocations | Yes | A term Revision B never used, which Conds 5 and 9 now use to bind brokers. A definition that opens the Code to a new class is a scope expansion; it is not net-new, since it imposes no act of its own. |
| Cond. 5 | scope expansion | Holders file the season return within 35 days | Holders and water brokers file it | Yes | The same return now reaches a new class, and brokers file it from nothing. Each clause that names them is its own row. |
| Cond. 7 | no substantive change | Holders may display the Board's water-saver mark | Identical | No | A permission, and brokers are not written into it, so nothing extends. |

## When an edition is missing

Had the papers held only Revision C, the whole reply would be:

## KFIC Revision C: no delta without Revision B

**The prior text is missing.** Only Revision C (Conditions 1–9) was supplied, and a delta compares two editions. Rebuilding Revision B from memory or from another catchment's code would be unsafe: editions that read almost alike can still move a deadline, a trigger or a class of persons, and a table drawn against a guessed baseline would misreport those shifts or miss them.

Please send the full text of Revision B, the edition Revision C replaces, and the delta will follow.

## The shape, part by part

1. The first characters are `## `, the instrument's short name and the two editions joined by an arrow. Nothing comes before them.
2. `### Orientation`: three or four sentences giving the editions and their clause ranges; whether a summary, note, circular or memorandum came with them, which of its facts are relied on and that its characterisation was tested, or that the texts alone decide; and any insertion or deletion that moved the numbering.
3. `### Clause check`: one line per clause in table order, deleted prior clauses last. A changed line gives what moved in the instrument's own words, the old and new figure with its direction where a number moved, what the summary says about the clause or that it is silent, and what already owed the conduct or that nothing did, then the label and trigger from the first check that fits. An identical clause takes a short line.
4. `### Delta table` with exactly six columns: clause-ref | change-type | prior-text summary | new-text summary | action-trigger | notes. Rows run in new-text order with each printed sub-item as its own row, then each deleted prior clause. References copy the new text: `(new)` marks an insertion, `(was: …)` appears only where the number moved, and `Prior … (deleted)` marks a deletion. Each row carries the label and trigger its clause check line gave. A note is one to three sentences that name the element that moved in this instrument's words and say what a person compliant with the prior text was or was not already doing; a relaxed row adds its retention position and a Review row points to its question. An unchanged row reads "Wording unchanged." plus its reach or renumbering where a reader might expect a change. Nothing is cited that the input does not contain.
5. `### Summary`: the six change types in the order above, each cell listing the refs that carry that label in the table and then `= ` and their count, `none = 0` for a type that does not occur, and a last row giving the number of table rows.
6. `**Review-flagged clauses:**` with one bullet per Review row and its two-outcome question, or `None.`
7. `**Notes for downstream:**` two or three sentences ranking the work in the skill's order, saying why the first item leads in this instrument and what the unchanged or renumbered clauses still need.

When an edition is missing, the reply is only the stop, with no clause check and no table.

## Before sending

Hold the table once against its clause check and the texts. A shorter deadline, a lower trigger or a more frequent cycle is scope expansion with Yes; a longer deadline or a higher trigger is obligation withdrawn / relaxed with No and a retention line. A new class in a scope list, a new scenario, hazard or factor in a duty's object, a deleted express exemption, a clause rewritten to name a new class and a new defined term that binds one are all scope expansion with Yes, whatever the summary calls them, and a class bound for the first time takes Yes even on a record clause. A new verb, cycle, consent or inserted duty is net-new with Yes, while a new item in a record, notice or return is net-new with Yes-doc. A general duty spelled out, or an act that a named earlier instrument of the regulator already required of these persons, is clarification / restatement with Yes-doc, and its note names the source of each item. A specific standard the regulator ties to a quality word written for another document is ambiguous — human review with its question. Identical wording, renumbered or not, is no substantive change with No, and a permission is never expanded. Every clause, sub-item and deleted clause has exactly one row, each count equals the refs beside it, every Review row has its bullet, and the reply starts with `## ` and the instrument's short name.
