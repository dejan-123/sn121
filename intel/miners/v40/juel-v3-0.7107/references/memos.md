# From a sheet to a memo

The memo goes to an HR lead who will be questioned on it: 550 to 800 words about role families only, one analyst talking to another. Its facts are the sheet's and no others: the paragraphs are the Say lines put in order and joined into prose, with the figures from their own lines. Checks and steps stay on the sheet. Every point the memo mentions carries its id in brackets the first time, beside the name a reader would know it by: an accepted offer at a Series C company, $175,600 (H-01). The id and the title are copied from that point's own row of the paste, never from where it sat in the timeline or the sheet. The first time each kind is weighed it gets the name an HR reader looks for: an accepted offer is revealed preference, pay somebody actually took, and a posted range is aspirational, what an employer says it will pay. "Paid" and "hired" belong to outside companies unless the sentence names the company itself, and the company's name is used for its own bands and its own offers only.

- `# <Company>: ` and the sheet's title line, in its verdict words.
- An opening paragraph: the city and round the bands are held to, the first month in, copied with its year from the sheet's Floor line (2025-10 is October 2025), and what it shuts out, each by id and by the title its own row gives, if it shuts anything out, and which rows are not prices, by id, taken from the sheet's "Not a price at all" line and no other: a range from another city or round is a price that failed a test, not a non-price. A line the sheet does not have gets no sentence here, not even to say there is none.
- Two to four paragraphs, no headings, from the firmest call to the weakest. Families decided by the same kind of evidence share a paragraph, and each paragraph opens on what decides it. A conversion shows its sum and direction. A range the sheet marks volunteered is called volunteered where it comes up, and partial for that. What a candidate said about a rival's pay, or about why an offer was turned down, is named as hearsay where it comes up and is never set beside a gap or said to agree with one. Only a family the sheet defers is called deferred; a Low one has its verdict, and is said to rest on one posting. An offer from another round beside that posting is partial: it leans, and it never turns the call into a deferral. A partial point is worth a clause for its lean, and an open P1 or P2 role a clause where it raises what hangs on the call.
- `## <Company>'s benchmark`: a table, Family and level | Verdict | Gap, worked | Evidence funnel | Confidence, one row per family in snapshot order. The gap is written as its sum, (midpoint − anchor) ÷ anchor, with the anchor's ids. The funnel is the family's funnel line from the sheet: retrieved, then survived, partial and dropped, all four counted even at 0, each with its ids, and why beside the partial and dropped. Confidence is the tier and its deciding reason in a few words. Under the table, one line opening `Pool:` gives the sheet's four totals as plain numbers, retrieved, survived, partial and dropped, with nothing in brackets.
- `## Deferred verdicts for <Company>`, always there: each deferred family, what came close by id, and the evidence that would settle it (kind, city, round, level). With none deferred, that is said, then the verdict resting on least and what would firm it.
- `## Signal caveats on <Company>'s pool`: two to four sentences on the pool as a whole, from the sheet's closing lines, repeating nothing; the first counts the kinds, accepted offers against posted ranges. A gap is called close only if the sheet lists it as close.
- A last line on the number somebody will quote, the loudest of the dropped figures as the sheet's own line gives it, with its id, and the answer to it.

Every figure is in the paste or worked from it in view. No advice on what to pay.

Two invented memos follow. Halvard's was written from the sheet you have seen; Wendover's comes from a shorter pool in Australian dollars, whose paste says above its table that every range was voluntarily posted. How they open, which families they put together and what the paragraphs dwell on differ, because the pools did.

## Halvard

# Halvard Foodservice Systems: two bands lag Milwaukee, Channel Sales is above market, Install Scheduling is competitive, Demand Forecasting is deferred

Halvard is held to Milwaukee and Series C. September 2025 is the first month in. It keeps a Service Operations range from that month (H-06) and shuts out H-08, a Service Operations Manager offer of $108,600 from August 2025. Four rows are not prices: an undated survey median for service managers with no method behind it (H-07), a Channel Sales range given only as on-target pay (H-10), a Senior Channel Sales Manager figure with a retention grant inside it (H-12) and an agency's word on a forecaster's salary (H-17).

Embedded Systems has the firmest evidence and the narrowest gap. Two Milwaukee companies at Series C hired at this level, a Staff Embedded Systems Engineer at $175,600 (H-01) and a Device Software Engineer, IC5, at $172,800 (H-02), a title the paste's scope note maps to Halvard's staff level. Accepted offers are revealed preference, pay somebody took, and their $174,200 average leaves the $167,400 midpoint 3.9% under: lagging, and close to the line. A posted range at the same round (H-03), published under a disclosure law, is aspirational and counts; it centres on $175,800, agrees, and stays out of the sum, because a range never joins an anchor that has offers in it. Halvard's own hires at $170,600 and $172,300 were above its midpoint already, and a P2 role is open. A candidate who turned down $168,900 said a rival offered about 10% more; that is hearsay, and the call does not lean on it. A public company's range (H-04) was left out.

Channel Sales rests on one offer, and it points the other way. H-09 came as $146,900 in total; less the stated $30,300 bonus it is $116,600 base, down, so the $123,800 midpoint is 6.2% over it: above market. A Series D range (H-11) centres on $130,800, higher as a later round usually is, so it says little, and a Milwaukee range filed as Account Executive / Senior Channel Sales (H-20) may be for either job, so it is partial on its title; it centres on $116,300, level with the offer. Halvard had $118,700 turned down; the turn-down is a fact, and the reason given is only what the candidate said.

Service Operations and Install Scheduling are read from postings alone. Service Operations has two Milwaukee ranges that agree, centred on $105,700 (H-05) and $107,900 (H-06); against their $106,800 average the $100,800 midpoint is 5.6% under, and Halvard's own hire at $103,700 sits with that reading. Install Scheduling has a single posting, centred on $68,800 (H-13), which leaves $67,800 1.5% under: competitive, and Low. A Series D company's $73,300 offer (H-19) is higher, as a later round usually is; it leans, and the call still stands on the posting. A remote range from a Series D company (H-18) missed twice and was dropped, and Halvard's offer list has no line for this family.

Demand Forecasting is deferred while its P1 role is open. A Series B offer at $187,700 (H-14) is lower, as an earlier round usually is, and a Milwaukee Series C range centred on $224,800 (H-15) is higher, but that range was volunteered, written to attract with nothing holding the employer to it. The two pull opposite ways and neither is a price that binds at Halvard's round.

## Deferred verdicts for Halvard

Demand Forecasting, Principal. The Series B offer (H-14) came closest from below and the volunteered range (H-15) from above; one is a round early, the other binds nobody. An accepted offer for a principal forecaster at a Milwaukee Series C company would settle it, or two postings there that a disclosure law made public and that agree; with the P1 role open, that is the evidence to ask for first.

## Signal caveats on Halvard's pool

Of 20 points, 6 are accepted offers, revealed preference, and 11 are posted ranges, aspirational; two verdicts stand on postings alone. One feed supplied 17 of the 20, so little outside it checks them. Four ranges were posted because a law asked for them, one was volunteered and was read as partial for it, and six do not say, so how far those six run high cannot be told. The Embedded Systems gap sits near the 3% line, which is a convention, and Install Scheduling has no Halvard offer to set beside its outside reading.

Somebody will quote $267,600 for a demand forecaster. That is the top of a Minneapolis range (H-16), and Halvard does not hire this job in Minneapolis.

## Wendover

# Wendover Acoustics: Signal Processing lags Melbourne, Technical Support and Industrial Design are deferred

Wendover is held to Melbourne and Series B, and its bands are in Australian dollars. March 2026 is the first month in, and it shuts nothing out. The line printed above its evidence table says no law there makes employers publish pay and every range was voluntarily posted unless its row says otherwise; no row does, so each range is partial on its posting before anything else is weighed. A contractor's day rate for an industrial designer (W-07) is not a price at all, and an industrial design range quoted in New Zealand dollars with no rate supplied (W-06) cannot be read in Wendover's money.

Signal Processing has the one hire in the pool. A Melbourne company at the same round took on a senior signal engineer at A$165,900 (W-01), an accepted offer and so revealed preference, and Wendover's A$155,700 midpoint sits 6.1% under it. A Melbourne Series B range (W-02) centres on A$163,700; it is aspirational and volunteered, so it is partial and only leans the same way. Wendover's own hire at A$160,800 was already above its midpoint.

Technical Support is deferred. Its Melbourne Series B range (W-05), centred on A$109,800, was volunteered, so it is partial on its posting; a Series C range (W-04) is volunteered and a round late, two near misses, and a Sydney range (W-03) is another city. Nothing counts at Wendover's round on a footing that binds the employer, so there is no gap to state.

Industrial Design is deferred too. Neither of its rows can price the job: one is a day rate, the other is in a currency nobody has given a rate for.

## Deferred verdicts for Wendover

Technical Support, Manager. The Melbourne Series B range (W-05) came closest and was volunteered. An accepted offer for a support manager at a Melbourne Series B company would open a verdict, or a range there that a law made the employer publish.

Industrial Design, Senior. Nothing survived: W-07 is a contractor's day rate and W-06 has no exchange rate. An accepted offer for a senior industrial designer at a Melbourne Series B company, in Australian dollars, would open a verdict.

## Signal caveats on Wendover's pool

Of 7 points, 1 is an accepted offer, revealed preference, and 5 are posted ranges, aspirational, every one volunteered under the line above the table; the single verdict stands on the single offer. Wendover's one hire tests Signal Processing and nothing else.

Somebody will quote A$138,900 for a support manager. That is the top of a Sydney range (W-03), and Wendover does not hire this job in Sydney.
