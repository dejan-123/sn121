# From a sheet to a memo

The memo goes to an HR lead who will be questioned on it: 550 to 800 words about role families only, one analyst talking to another. Its facts are the sheet's and no others: the paragraphs are the Say lines put in order and joined into prose, with the figures from their own lines. Checks and steps stay on the sheet. Every point the memo mentions carries its id in brackets the first time, beside the name a reader would know it by: an accepted offer at a Series C company, $176,300 (P-01). The id and the title are copied from that point's own row of the paste, never from where it sat on the Dates line or the sheet. The first time each kind is weighed it gets the name an HR reader looks for: an accepted offer is revealed preference, pay somebody actually took, and a posted range is aspirational, what an employer says it will pay. "Paid" and "hired" belong to outside companies unless the sentence names the company itself, and the company's name is used for its own bands and its own offers only.

- `# <Company>: ` and the sheet's title line, in its verdict words.
- An opening paragraph: the city and round the bands are held to, with the snapshot's own words on where its roles sit quoted from the sheet's Placed line and, when that line names something left open, one clause saying so, the first month in, copied with its year from the sheet's Floor line (2025-10 is October 2025), and what it shuts out, each by id and by the title its own row gives, if it shuts anything out, and which rows are not prices, by id, taken from the sheet's "Not a price at all" line and no other: a range from another city or round is a price that failed a test, not a non-price. A figure in another currency with no rate is named with its currency, and the sentence says the paste gives no rate; it is never converted. Then one sentence on the basis, from the sheet's Basis line: that prices are read as yearly base in each band's own money, every figure that had to be worked shown worked out and whether it went up or down, a figure moved into another currency only at a rate the paste prints, or, when nothing needed working, that every price already was yearly base. Any other line the sheet does not have gets no sentence here, not even to say there is none.
- Two to four paragraphs, no headings, from the firmest call to the weakest. Families decided by the same kind of evidence share a paragraph, and each paragraph opens on what decides it. A conversion shows its sum and direction. A range the sheet marks volunteered is called volunteered where it comes up, and partial for that; a point from another city is partial for its place and says how that city pays, not this one. What a candidate said about a rival's pay, or about why an offer was turned down, is named as hearsay where it comes up and is never set beside a gap or said to agree with one. Only a family the sheet defers is called deferred; a Low one has its verdict, and is said to rest on one posting. An offer from another round beside that posting is partial: it leans, and it never turns the call into a deferral. A partial point is worth a clause for its lean, and an open P1 or P2 role a clause where it raises what hangs on the call.
- `## <Company>'s benchmark`: a table, Family and level | Verdict | Gap, worked | Evidence funnel | Confidence, one row per family in snapshot order. The gap is written as its sum, (midpoint − anchor) ÷ anchor, with the anchor's ids. The funnel is the family's funnel line from the sheet: retrieved, then survived, partial and dropped, all four counted even at 0, each with its ids, and why beside the partial and dropped. Confidence is the tier and its deciding reason in a few words. Under the table, one line opening `Pool:` gives the sheet's four totals as plain numbers, retrieved, survived, partial and dropped, with nothing in brackets.
- `## Deferred verdicts for <Company>`, always there: each deferred family, what came close by id, and the evidence that would settle it (kind, city, round, level). With none deferred, that is said, then the verdict resting on least and what would firm it.
- `## Signal caveats on <Company>'s pool`: two or three sentences on the pool as a whole, never family by family or point by point, and with no count or number word in them, since a count is the easiest figure to get wrong. First the families that rest on accepted offers, revealed preference, and those that stand on posted ranges alone, aspirational, as the table's Confidence column has them. Then the sources as the sheet's Sources line gives them: the main source named, and every row from anywhere else by id. Then one sentence on why the pool's ranges were posted, in general words: whether laws made them public, whether some were volunteered, whether some do not say, and what that does to how far they can be trusted. How close a gap sits to the line is said in the body where the sheet marks it close, and never here.
- A last line on the number somebody will quote, the figure on the sheet's "Loudest not counted" line, in the currency and with the id that line gives, and the answer to it.

Every figure is in the paste or worked from it in view. No advice on what to pay.

Two invented memos follow. Pellworth's was written from the sheet you have seen; Quarrow's comes from a shorter pool, whose paste says over its table that every range was published by choice unless its row cites a law. How they open, which families they put together and what the paragraphs dwell on differ, because the pools did.

## Pellworth

# Pellworth Diagnostics: Firmware Engineering and Lab Support lag Columbus, Field Applications is competitive in Dublin, Inside Sales is above market, Data Platform is deferred

Pellworth is held to Columbus and Series C, and its Field Applications team to Dublin and Series C, in euros: the snapshot puts it in "Columbus OH" with "remote roles pinned to the Columbus tier" and says the Field Applications team "sits in Dublin, Ireland". August 2025 is the first month in. It keeps a Lab Support range from that month (P-06) and shuts out P-08, a Lab Support Manager offer of $101,700 from July 2025. Each price is read as yearly base in its family's own money: P-13's $81,400 total less its stated $14,600 bonus is $66,800 base, down, and P-10's dollar range is carried into euros at the rate the paste prints, 1 USD = 0.92 EUR, so its $96,300 centre becomes about €88,600. Four rows are not prices: an undated survey median for lab support managers with no method behind it (P-07), a Field Applications figure with a retention grant inside it (P-12), an Inside Sales range given only as on-target pay (P-14) and an agency's word on a data platform engineer's salary (P-19).

Firmware Engineering has the firmest evidence and a narrow gap that Pellworth's own hiring confirms. Two Columbus companies at Series C hired at this level, a Staff Firmware Engineer at $176,300 (P-01) and an Embedded Software Engineer, IC5, at $173,100 (P-02), a title the paste's scope note maps to Pellworth's staff level. Accepted offers are revealed preference, pay somebody took, and their $174,700 average leaves the $168,300 midpoint 3.7% under. A gap that size is called only when the company's own hires back it, and here they do: Pellworth hired at $172,100 and $174,800, both above its midpoint, so the band is lagging, close to the line. A Columbus range published under a disclosure law (P-03) is aspirational and counts; it centres on $176,100, agrees, and stays beside the offers rather than in their sum. A P2 role is open. A candidate who turned down $171,200 said a rival offered about 12% more; that is hearsay, and it plays no part in the call. A public company's range (P-04) was left out.

Field Applications and Inside Sales each rest on one offer. In Dublin a Series C company hired a Senior Field Applications Scientist at €81,700 (P-09), which leaves the €78,600 midpoint 3.8% short of it; with no Pellworth hire in the family to back a gap that size, the call is competitive, close. The converted Dublin range (P-10) sits higher, as a posted range usually does, and a range filed as Field Applications Scientist / Sales Engineer (P-11) is partial on its two-name title; it centres on €82,700, near the offer. In Columbus the Inside Sales offer (P-13), once its bonus is taken off, leaves the $71,300 midpoint 6.7% over it: above market. A Cincinnati range (P-15) centres higher, on $73,200, but it prices another city, and a Series B range that was also volunteered (P-16) missed twice. Pellworth had $69,400 turned down for this job; the turn-down is a fact, and the reason given is only what the candidate said.

Lab Support is read from postings alone. Two Columbus ranges that agree centre on $102,200 (P-05) and $104,400 (P-06); against their $103,300 average the $95,600 midpoint is 7.5% under: lagging, at the cap for ranges alone. Pellworth's own hire at $98,300 sits above its midpoint, with that reading.

Data Platform is deferred while its P1 role is open. A Series B offer at $184,300 (P-17) is lower, as an earlier round usually is, while a volunteered Columbus range centred on $218,300 (P-18) and a remote range with no city centred on $209,100 (P-20) are higher. They pull apart, and none is a price that binds at Pellworth's round in Columbus.

## Deferred verdicts for Pellworth

Data Platform, Principal. The Series B offer (P-17) came closest from below; the volunteered range (P-18) and the remote range (P-20) sat above, one binding nobody and the other pinned to no city. An accepted offer for a principal data platform engineer at a Columbus Series C company would settle it, or two Columbus Series C postings that a disclosure law made public and that agree; with the P1 role open, that is the evidence to ask for first.

## Signal caveats on Pellworth's pool

Firmware Engineering, Field Applications and Inside Sales rest on accepted offers, revealed preference; Lab Support stands on posted ranges alone, aspirational, and is capped for it. Every row but the survey (P-07), the recruiter's figure (P-12) and the agency's word (P-19) came from one job-data feed, so little outside it checks the pool. Some of the ranges were made public by a disclosure law, the volunteered ones were read as partial, and those that do not say why they were posted may run high by an amount nobody can tell.

Somebody will quote $238,900 for a firmware engineer. That is the top of a public company's range (P-04), and Pellworth is a Series C company.

## Quarrow

# Quarrow Agronomics: Agronomy Data Science and Field Technicians lag Raleigh by a little, Grower Success is deferred

Quarrow is held to Raleigh and Series A; the snapshot says only "Raleigh NC" and is silent on remote roles, so all of its bands are held to that city. March 2025 is the first month in, and it shuts out R-10, a Lead Field Technician range from February 2025. The line over the evidence table says no pay-posting law reaches Raleigh and every range was published by choice unless its row cites one, so a range without such a note is partial on its posting from the start. Prices are read as yearly base in dollars as printed: a Field Technician range quoted in euros (R-07) has no rate anywhere in the paste, so it stays in euros and out, and an hourly contract rate (R-08) is never scaled up into a salary.

Agronomy Data Science rests on one Raleigh hire. A Series A company took on a Senior Agronomy Data Scientist at $137,300 (R-01), an accepted offer and so revealed preference, which leaves Quarrow's $132,700 midpoint 3.4% under. A gap that size needs Quarrow's own hiring behind it, and it has it: Quarrow hired at $135,900, above its midpoint, so the band is lagging, close. A volunteered Raleigh range (R-02) centres higher, on $139,300, and a Durham offer at $141,900 (R-03) prices another city; both are partial and lean the same way.

Field Technicians are read from one posting. A Raleigh range published under a disclosure law (R-09) centres on $63,600, which leaves the $61,300 midpoint 3.6% under. A gap that rests on a posting stands without Quarrow's own hiring behind it: lagging, close, and Low. A candidate turned down Quarrow's $59,700; the turn-down is a fact, and nothing more is read into it.

Grower Success is deferred. The Raleigh range at Quarrow's round (R-04) was filed as Grower Success Manager / Account Lead, so its title spans two jobs and it is partial even though a law made it public; it centres on $89,600, next to the band. A Charlotte offer at $84,300 (R-05) is another city's, and a Series B range that was also volunteered (R-06) missed twice.

Somebody will quote $153,900 for an agronomy data scientist. That is the top of a volunteered Raleigh range (R-02), written to attract; the price somebody took at Quarrow's round was $137,300.
