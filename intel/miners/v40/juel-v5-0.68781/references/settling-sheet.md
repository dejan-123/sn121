# The settling sheet

The sheet is worked out on the side and never sent, and it is written before `references/memos.md` is opened. It opens with the floor, every row's date set against it, the line printed above the table, the basis each price is read on, the company's own offers and the rows by family, takes the families one at a time in snapshot order, and closes with the pool's totals and the verdicts. The lines about the pool as a whole come first, while the rows are being read one by one. Pellworth, its ids and its figures were made up.

## What decides a line

- Placed: the snapshot's words on where the company and its roles sit are quoted, not retold, with one clause on what they leave open; a missing fact the call would need is named there as a gap, never filled in.
- Each point's line opens with its id and its own row's Notes cell, then its kind and Title cell, all copied as the paste prints them, never the family's name in their place; the job and posting tests read those copies, so a note that names a law, a volunteered posting, a currency or a missing city is in view before any test is marked.
- Kind, copied from the paste's own type column: an accepted offer or a posted range can count. Hearsay, a survey median, a contract rate, a grant or buy-out, on-target pay with no split, or a figure in another currency than the family's with no rate in the paste is out, and nothing after it matters. A currency line names the currency and says no rate was given; nothing is converted with a rate from outside the paste.
- Basis: a price is compared as yearly base pay in the money its family's band is kept in. A total minus a stated bonus, or a figure moved into another currency at a rate the paste itself prints, is worked out on the Basis line, and the line says whether it went up or down; where the paste prints no rate, the figure keeps its own money and is never moved. If nothing needed working, the Basis line says that too: every price was already yearly base in its band's money.
- Date: decided by the Dates line alone. Before the floor, or undated, fails on date: the row stays in the pool, is listed under its family and is dropped there, never left off. The floor month and every month after it is in, and any month of a later year is after it.
- Place: the same metro, or remote pinned to it by the paste, is same. Remote with no city is near, and so is any other named city, whatever law made it post: one place apart is one near miss, as one round apart is.
- Stage: count the rounds between the two (Seed, A, B, C, D, E, F). 0 is same, 1 is near, 2 or more is out, never near, and so is public. One round apart is never out on its own.
- Job: the same work and level, or a title the paste maps to it, is same. A neighbouring specialty or one level off is near. Any title with a slash in it is near too, whichever name comes first and however close the two names sound ('Field Applications Scientist / Sales Engineer', 'Inside Sales / Account Coordinator'): one posting spans two names, so its scope is not the family's alone. Word order, a comma, a dash, or a short form standing alone as the whole title (Ops, Quant, PM, SRE) changes nothing.
- Posting, for ranges only, taken from the point's own note and the Above the table line: a range a disclosure law made public, or one whose row says nothing of why it was posted, is same. A note that names a mandate or a pay-transparency law in any words, a state's or a city's ('NY mandate', 'CA mandate'), means a law made it public, and the line says "posting law", not "not said" and never "volunteered", whatever any other line says. A range the paste marks as voluntarily posted, in its own Notes cell or by a line printed above the table (the sheet's "Above the table" line) that marks every range volunteered save those whose rows say otherwise, is near: it was written to attract, and nothing holds the employer to it. An offer has no posting mark.
- Each line counts its near and its out marks apart. All same counts. One near, no out, is partial. Two near, or one out, is dropped: another city with another round, or another city with a volunteered range, is two near.
- A partial point joins no average, even when it is the only other figure. A range joins no anchor that has an offer in it. With one offer and one range counted, the anchor is that offer as it stands, with no sum at all, and the range's centre is written beside it as agreeing or not.
- Call, on the Gap line, in this order: within 3% either way is competitive, close from 2%; past 4.5% decides alone, a minus gap (band under the anchor) lagging and a plus gap (band over it) above market; between 3% and 4.5%, an anchor of accepted offers goes to the family's Own line, an accepted hire on the anchor's side of the midpoint (above it for a band under, below it for a band over) making it lagging or above market, close, and none there making it competitive, close, while an anchor of range centres stands as it falls, lagging or above market, close. A declined offer, or a hire on the other side, backs nothing. The Gap line names the band it falls in before the verdict.
- Two or more offers counted is High. One offer is Medium. No offer and two or more ranges that agree is Medium, the cap for ranges alone. No offer and one range is Low. Nothing counted is Deferred, and only that is: one counted range keeps its Low verdict even with a partial offer beside it.
- Own offers come from that family's own line. They are never counted, and they raise no tier. A declined offer is one the candidate turned down; the reason given, or what rivals were said to pay, is hearsay. It is named as hearsay where it comes up and is never set beside a gap, never sized, never said to agree with one.
- Every row of the paste is on one family's list, the one the Rows line gave it by its own Title cell or the paste's scope note, wherever the row sits in the paste and whatever family the rows printed before it belong to; each family block works its own rows from that line and no others. A row failing on date is worked, and dropped, only in its own family: retrieved = survived (the points that count) + partial + dropped. A row failing on date, or one whose kind is out, still sits on its family's list, has its note copied and gets its own line there ending in "dropped"; a family's retrieved count is every row on its list.

Now the real paste: write its sheet as plain text, every item on one line as above, never a list inside a line and never a rule explained again; when a check finds a slip, the corrected line replaces the wrong one and nothing is written out twice. End it with "Settled; the memo is next." and, in the same message, call read_reference on `references/memos.md`. That file is opened once, in that message and not before: the sheet needs nothing from it, and opening it early spends the token budget the whole run shares.
