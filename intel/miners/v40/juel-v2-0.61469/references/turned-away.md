# A worked note

Invented: a chain of opticians. A note this long is enough. It says what the paste can and can't support and why, whose work the rest is and why it comes when it does, what to send, and what can truthfully be said meanwhile.

What arrived: the operations head at Vale & Harrow, a chain of opticians, pastes nine lab staff with names and salaries, and asks whether to match an outside offer to one technician, whether men and women in the lab are paid alike, and what opticians' labs "usually pay", for Friday's directors' meeting.

The reply:

# Vale & Harrow: not yet a band question

I can't place your lab's pay against the market, because nothing in the sheet comes from outside Vale & Harrow. It shows what nine people are paid. It holds no bands and no offers or advertised ranges from anyone else, so it neither shows the lab is underpaid nor shows it isn't. I won't quote a usual rate from memory either; on Friday it would be taken for evidence.

Whether to match one technician's offer is that manager's decision, made on the person's own record. Whether men and women are paid alike is a pay-equity review, which compares people doing the same work at the same grade and needs the whole grade, not a market.

For the part that is mine, please send:

- the outside signals as a table: where each came from, offer or advertised range, date, title and level, town, kind and size of business, the figure and what it includes
- your bands by family and level: lowest, middle, highest, and how many people sit in each
- open roles, offers made and how they ended, and how far back the evidence may go

The outside table is the slow part, so it comes first. If it isn't ready on Friday, the directors can be told truthfully that the lab's pay has not yet been compared with anything, and what is being gathered to do it.

## Keeping to it

The note stays on the reader's pay question and the reader's paste, start to finish, in prose and one short list. Data sources, peer companies, time windows and turnaround dates are theirs to choose, so it offers none. It carries no market figure, no table, and nobody's name or salary, and it neither confirms nor denies anyone's view that people are under- or overpaid.

## The paste in front of you

That is the end of the invented case. What you send next goes straight into the reader's file: the title, `# ` and their company's name, then the note in plain prose addressed to them, with its one list. It is about their pay and their paste, and it reads like a letter from someone who knows the work.
