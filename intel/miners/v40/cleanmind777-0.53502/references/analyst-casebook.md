# Analyst casebook: two write-ups and six quick rulings

All firms, people, sources and amounts here are made up so the screening can be seen at work. Borrow the reasoning and the layout; never a name or a number.

## Write-up 1: Halvorsen Grid Software

**What the packet held.** A Series D grid-software firm of 380 people, headquartered in Columbus, paying every role to the Columbus tier. Data retrieved 2026-05-11, with the instruction that anything before May 2025 is out of window. The packet calls its adverts voluntary and its grades "bands".

|---|---|---|
| Firmware Engineering (Staff), open, P1 | $175,800 / $191,700 / $209,600 | hired at $198,400 and $203,600 |
| Mobile Engineering (Lead) | $139,600 / $153,700 / $168,900 | none recent |
| Procurement (Buyer II) | $88,400 / $97,300 / $106,900 | one candidate said no to $94,700, taking a higher offer elsewhere |
| Learning & Development (Manager) | $101,700 / $112,300 / $123,400 | none recent |
| Solutions Consulting (Principal), salary only | $131,800 / $144,900 / $160,300 | none recent |

Sixteen comparators, H-01 to H-16: fifteen from one aggregator feed and one from a recruiter's call.

# Halvorsen Grid Software: pay against market (Series D, Columbus tier)

**Bottom line.** Sixteen comparators came in (H-01 to H-16) and ten survived. Staff Firmware Engineering, your P1 opening, sits at 94.5% of market, so it lags, and both recent hires already landed above its $191,700 mid. Procurement reads lagging at 96.0% on one advert, tipped by a candidate who turned down $94,700 for a better offer, so the next acceptance should confirm it before the band moves. Mobile Engineering and Solutions Consulting are competitive, the second only just, and Learning & Development gets no call yet because its only survivors come from firms a funding round earlier.

## How each call was reached

**Firmware Engineering (Staff).** Two of the four comparators describe this job in this market: an acceptance at $199,700 (H-01, Columbus, Series D, January 2026) and an advert at $187,300–$224,900 (H-02, Columbus, Series D, March 2026, centre $206,100). Their average of $202,900 leaves your $191,700 mid at 94.5%, a real shortfall, and both recent hires needed more than the mid: $198,400 is $6,700 above it and $203,600 is $11,900 above. The listed company's advert (H-03) is set aside because liquid stock reshapes its whole pay mix, and an acceptance from March 2025 (H-04) because it predates the window; the old acceptance would only have widened the gap.

**Mobile Engineering (Lead).** All three comparators pass. The packet maps a Lead iOS Engineer acceptance at $160,900 (H-05) to your Lead scope, and an acceptance quoted at $170,900 carries a stated $14,600 sign-on, so its salary is $170,900 − $14,600 = $156,300 (H-07). A Series C acceptance at $157,300 (H-06), one round earlier, is counted but flagged. The median of $156,300, $157,300 and $160,900 is $157,300, which puts the $153,700 mid at 97.7%, competitive. Both exact acceptances sit above the mid, which makes the tier High, though the firm has no recent Mobile hires to check it against.

**Procurement (Buyer II).** One advert survives, $92,700–$110,100 (H-08, Columbus, Series D, centre $101,400); a Cleveland advert (H-09) is another city and a Purchasing Coordinator advert (H-10) is a level down. At 96.0% the table alone cannot tell competitive from lagging, but your own record can: the candidate who turned down $94,700 left for a better offer.

**Learning & Development (Manager).** Both survivors are from firms a round earlier and a recruiter's phone figure (H-13) is not a record, so there is no call; see below.

**Solutions Consulting (Principal).** A package with equity and bonus inside and no salary stated (H-14) cannot be read as salary. Two Series D adverts remain, centred on $148,600 (H-15) and $151,100 (H-16, May 2025, the window's first month), and their average of $149,850 puts the $144,900 mid at 96.7%. The firm has no recent hires or refusals in this family to test it against, so it stays competitive, just under the market, on adverts alone.

## Deferred verdicts

**Learning & Development (Manager).** Nothing at Series D survived. A Series C advert centres on $110,600 and a Series C acceptance came in at $114,700. Both sit a round before yours, so neither is exact and they settle nothing. What would settle it: an acceptance or advert for an L&D Manager at a Series D firm paying to Columbus, salary only, dated May 2025 or later.

## Signal caveats

- Four of the eight comparators behind a market rate are adverts, the employer's asking price; the other four are acceptances, the price actually paid.
- In the one family with an advert beside an acceptance, the advert centre sits higher, so adverts may run high here and the advert-only ratios may understate where those grades stand.
- The packet calls its adverts voluntary: these employers chose to publish a range.
- All eight rate-setting comparators come from one aggregator feed, so whatever it over- or under-samples is in every row.


---

Workings, for checking: Firmware (199,700 + 206,100) ÷ 2 = 202,900, and 191,700 ÷ 202,900 = 94.5%. Mobile, the median of 156,300, 157,300 and 160,900 is 157,300, and 153,700 ÷ 157,300 = 97.7%. Procurement 97,300 ÷ 101,400 = 96.0%. Solutions Consulting (148,600 + 151,100) ÷ 2 = 149,850, and 144,900 ÷ 149,850 = 96.7%. Reverse check: 202,900 × 0.945 is about 191,700.

## Write-up 2: a request with nothing to screen

**What the packet held.** Maple & Thorne Studio, seed stage, 18 people, Halifax. The founder is about to hire a lead animator and asks what to offer, and whether pay is fair across the team. There is no outside data, only a payroll export of eleven names, roles and salaries. The note below speaks to the two things this founder asked for; a note to someone else names their requests instead.

---

# Maple & Thorne Studio: pay benchmark not run, no outside data classified yet

**What you asked and what came with it.** You want an offer figure for a lead animator and a view on whether pay is fair across the studio. What came with it is your payroll: eleven people with roles and salaries. That shows what each person earns, not a grade per role, and there is nothing from outside to set it against.

**Why I am not putting a number on it.** A market call needs comparators that pass four tests: the same job, the same market (city and funding round), the same kind of pay, and a dated, written record. With none to test, any figure I gave would come from general impressions, and such numbers tend to get quoted in a negotiation as if someone had checked them.

**Where the other two requests go.** The offer for one candidate is an offer decision, and the fairness question is a pay-equity review. Both rest on a benchmark's market rates, so they are easier and safer once this is done.

**What to send.**
- Comparators for a lead animator and the other roles on your payroll, from studios paying to Halifax at seed stage first; one round on can support a call but not make one.
- Accepted offers count most, because someone was paid them; an advertised range is the employer's hope and alone cannot give a high-confidence call.
- For each outside comparator: where it came from, whether it is an acceptance, an advertised range or a survey figure, its date, city, funding round, title and level, and the salary with any equity or sign-on shown separately.
- Your own recent offers in these roles, taken or turned down, with the reason when a candidate gave one.
- A grade per role (min, mid and max) and the city you pay to.
- The window you want; if none, the past twelve months is applied.
- When the animator decision is needed.

Until it arrives every role on the payroll stays Deferred; once it does, the benchmark can be ready for the animator decision.

---

## Quick ruling A: a team paid in Canadian dollars

A firm grading its Toronto actuarial analysts at C$96,400 / C$104,300 / C$114,200 has a Toronto advert at its own round, C$97,900–C$118,300 (centre C$108,100), and a Toronto acceptance at its own round of C$107,700. A third comparator is a Buffalo advert in US dollars: it is paid in another city before currency even matters, and with no rate in the packet it could not be converted anyway. The market rate is (108,100 + 107,700) ÷ 2 = C$107,900, and 104,300 ÷ 107,900 = 96.7%, just short of plainly competitive. No neighbour comes in, and with no recent hires or refusals the call is Competitive at the low edge, Medium: one acceptance and one advert. Every amount stays in Canadian dollars.

## Quick ruling B: two near-misses make a miss

Against a Fleet Planner (Level 4) grade at a Series D firm, the pool offers a Route Scheduler advert from a Series E firm in the same city. Scheduling and planning are neighbours, and so are the two rounds. Either gap alone would leave it as a flagged neighbour, but together they describe a different job at a different kind of firm, so it fails on job. The funnel cell records both reasons in a few words.

## Quick ruling C: the exclusion that carries the call

A Customer Insights (Lead) grade has a $118,400 mid. One exact advert survives, centred on $125,300: 118,400 ÷ 125,300 = 94.5%, Lagging, Low. A second advert centred on $117,100 is dated before the window. Kept, it would make the market rate (125,300 + 117,100) ÷ 2 = $121,200 and the ratio 97.7%, which reads Competitive. So the funnel cell adds "if the older advert counted: Competitive", and the reader sees the date rule is deciding this row.

## Quick ruling D: a priority opening with nothing exact

A P1 Security Analyst (Level 5) family has one survivor, an acceptance from a firm a round later. It is Deferred, and because it is P1 it opens the Deferred verdicts section and is named in the bottom line. The ask is specific: one exact acceptance or advert at the firm's own round and city. A priority role is where a borrowed figure does the most damage.

## Quick ruling E: a neighbour that moves the call

A Field Service (Lead) grade has a $97,200 mid. One exact acceptance came in at $98,600, and an advert from a firm one round later centres on $108,300. The market rate is (98,600 + 108,300) ÷ 2 = $103,450, and 97,200 ÷ 103,450 = 94.0%, lagging. The exact acceptance alone gives 97,200 ÷ 98,600 = 98.6%, competitive, so the later-round advert is what makes this row lag. The funnel cell adds "exact only: 98.6%", and the tier reads "Low: one exact acceptance, and a firm a round away tips it".

## Quick ruling F: reading a title against the family name

A Principal Routing Planner acceptance against a Fleet & Routing (Principal Planner) family is the same job: the family's own name covers the title, and it becomes a neighbour only on a dimension the packet flags. A Laboratory Lead Technician advert against a Laboratory (Technician) grade is a level up, because of the extra rank word, so it fails. Use the same title tolerance for every family in one write-up.

## Last look before sending

- Each amount in the write-up comes from the packet or from arithmetic on it that the reader can see; nothing from memory, and nothing from this casebook.
- In equals survived plus failed, the rows add up to the pool, and each failure names one question.
- A subtraction removes only what the packet says was folded in (a sign-on, a bonus, a commission target).
- The window's named month counts as inside.
- Neighbours never carry a call by themselves, and a deferred family carries no verdict anywhere.
- Each ratio survives the reverse check: market rate × ratio is about the mid.
- Whole-pool caveats sit once under Signal caveats, not inside rows or about one family alone, and any direction they give is read off this packet's figures.
- Every own hire is written with its distance from the mid ("$6,700 above it"), and every median with its figures in order.
- A comparator a round away is treated the same way in every family, and the tier counts exact comparators only.
- Every own hire, refusal (with its reason) and "no recent offers" is accounted for in its family's paragraph.
- A flip clause appears only for a comparator that failed a single question.
- No screen words in the prose: no "fails on", no grey zones, no lines, no "joins the median".
- The word count is inside the range, and a deferred family's figures appear only in its own section.
- A foreign-currency grade stays in its currency.

What you write is what the owner of pay reads, from the very first character, so the reply opens with `# ` and the firm's name. Any sentence before it about the casebook, the skill or your next step would sit at the top of their page.
