# A redirect note

The note is the whole reply: two or three short paragraphs of plain prose, about 150 words, written to the person who asked. It names what the input holds and why that cannot carry a verdict yet, who owns each out-of-scope part, and what to send so the market question can be answered, in the company's own metro, stage and titles. Every name and figure below is invented.

The ask from Halvorsen Dental Labs: "Should we give our analyst Dana a 9% raise, and can you add a fourth level to the analyst band?"

> Halvorsen Dental Labs' analyst question can't be answered as a benchmark yet. The note names one person and asks for a new level, but it carries no external pay evidence, so the benchmark verdict for the analyst family is Deferred: no external comparables. Whether one person gets a raise is a pay decision for the manager and the HR business partner. Adding a band level is band design, which should start from a benchmark rather than replace one.
>
> The question underneath both is whether the analyst band is still competitive, and a benchmark can answer that. Send the external points you have for that family, each with its date, location, company stage, level and posting type. Accepted offers carry more weight than posted ranges, which are aspirational. Also send the band's minimum, midpoint and maximum for each level. With those in hand, the competitive position and its confidence can be ready within a day.

Your note now starts with the company's name.
