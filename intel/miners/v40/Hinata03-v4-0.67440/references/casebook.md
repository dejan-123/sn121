# Casebook: two benchmarks worked row by row

Everything here is invented: the companies, the titles, every city's pay and every figure. None of it is evidence about any real market, so no name, figure or verdict from these pages belongs in a reply. What carries over is the order of judgement and the form of the working.

The working is scratch paper. It is written in full before any report, so that every call in the report is copied from a line already settled. Its first line, Over the table, copies word for word whatever is printed between the evidence table's heading and its row of column names; a line standing there often begins "Note" and says how the ranges were posted. That space is looked at before anything else is written, because a line in it decides every range whose own note is blank; "nothing printed there" is written only when the column names follow the heading directly. The second line, Snapshot notes, copies any line beginning "Note" that stands beside the bands (a team's country and currency), or says there is none. The Notes column inside the rows is not meant by either line. The Open roles line copies the roles the snapshot lists as open with their priority, or says none are listed. The Own offers line quotes each family's offer line from the input and only then expands it, one figure per accepted offer: "two accepted at $X, one at $Y" is X, X, Y, three figures, and the average is taken over all of them. Every figure in such a list comes from the quoted line; a figure that belongs to an outside point with an ID is never in it, and a declined offer is context only.

Posting is settled row by row, never from memory of the whole table: every range's line first copies that row's own note inside quotation marks, or writes "note blank", and only then makes the call. A note saying the range was voluntarily posted makes it voluntary, a partial test, also when the same note remarks that the state has no mandate (that is a denial of a mandate). A note naming a mandate, law or disclosure rule that published the range, with no "voluntarily" in it, makes it mandated, a match. A blank note, or one about something else, is decided by the Over the table line: where the line copied there says the ranges were posted by choice unless a row says otherwise, the blank row is voluntary; where nothing is printed there, the range stands, a match.

The Slash titles line names every row whose title has a "/" in it, found by reading down the title column once more after the rows are filed; each of those rows takes one partial test on scope, whatever stands before or after the slash. The Rows line only files: every ID of the input goes under one family, and nothing is judged there. A row that will fail a test, two rounds off or a listed company, is still filed under its family and gets its own line there with its outcome; no row is dropped on the Rows line, and the families' retrieved figures add up to the number of rows in the input. Each row gets one line: its kind, its month set against the first month in, then place, round, scope with the row's title copied in quotation marks, and posting, then the outcome. Another named city is a partial test, like a remote figure with no metro, and so is any title with a slash in it. No partial test and no mismatch is full; exactly one partial test is partial; two partial tests, or any mismatch, is dropped. Under a family's rows comes the family's line: the funnel, the full offers and full ranges listed apart, the anchor, the market gap with its close check (worked in two steps, the difference first and then the division by the anchor, never by the midpoint), the verdict and the tier, then the own hires read the same way, the partials with their lean, and the open role if the family has one. The working closes with the Count line and the Loudest dropped line, the highest figure on any dropped row.
