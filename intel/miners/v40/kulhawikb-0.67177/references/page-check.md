# Page check (read after the draft, before the page)

The draft is finished and stays private. Check each line of it against the input once more as the page is written:

- Placement. Every row was tested on family, date, market, stage, level, pay basis and source, in that order; a row in another city is excluded whatever its stage.
- No IDs. The page goes to readers without the evidence table's numbering, so no row ID appears anywhere on it. Each row is named by what it is: its kind, its role when that is not obvious, the fact that matters (city, stage, date, pay basis) and its figure; two rows that could be confused each get one more fact. IDs stay in the draft.
- Lists. `Excluded:` names each excluded row once, with the first test it fails; a partial row is never in that list, and `Partial:` names every partial row. The lists state results only: no second thought, correction or recount is typed into the page. `Out of window` and `Undated` carry their own counts, "none" included; a row is out of window only when its month is earlier than the first inside month.
- Window. The window is the one this input states, whatever its length, restated as its first inside month with the year the input's own window phrase prints (never the retrieval year by habit). When the input also gives the window's length, count the months from the first inside month to the retrieval month: a count that does not match means the year was miscopied, and a first inside month that is the retrieval month itself always is. Compare months as numbers (2025-12 is 202512): a row is out only when its number is smaller than the first inside month's, so a row dated after that month is inside whatever its year; a row dated in that month itself is inside too, fully comparable if nothing else is wrong, and enters its family's reference.
- Basis. Each conversion uses a split or rate printed in the input and shows its subtraction. A row that could not be converted is excluded, not estimated.
- Rows. One table row per family and level, in the snapshot's order. The Evidence cell counts every evidence row of that family once, by class and kind, rows that are out included, and no row of another family. Rounds are numbered Seed 0, A 1, B 2, C 3, D 4, E 5: a row is partial only when its number differs from the company's by exactly 1 (Series A against Series B differs by 1 and is partial; Series E against Series B differs by 3 and is excluded), and it is counted in the same class, partial or out, in the audit and in the table.
- Reading. Two to four short paragraphs before the table, taken from the draft's reading sentences: one or two sentences per family, under 200 words in all. Every figure, gap, verdict and tier in them is the one in the table, what they say about the company's own offers is what the own-offers cell says, and each row they mention is named without its ID. No paragraph opens with a count of families. A family whose reference is a posted range never stands in the accepted-offers paragraph. No sentence says where a partial row sits against the band, and none adds a count of rows or a date the table does not have. No recommendation and no advice on what to pay.
- Reference and gaps. An accepted offer sets the reference wherever a fully comparable one survives (the median when there are several); a posted midpoint, shown as (low + high) / 2, only where none does. Market gap = (midpoint - reference) / reference. Hiring gap = (midpoint - median of the family's own accepted offers) / that median. Each is given to one decimal with its subtraction.
- Verdict. Copy each family's decision line from the draft; the cell gives the test and then the word. Lagging when either gap is -5.0% or beyond ("market gap beyond -5.0%", "hiring gap beyond -5.0%", or both). Above-market when the market gap is +5.0% or beyond and the hiring gap, if there is one, is +2.5% or beyond. Otherwise Competitive ("both gaps inside 5.0%", "market gap alone, inside 5.0%", or "market gap beyond +5.0% but hiring gap inside 2.5%"). Two gaps of -3.9% and -4.4% are both inside 5.0%, so that band is Competitive; gaps of -4.9% and -5.6% make it Lagging on the hiring gap.
- Tier. Copy the draft's tier line, which counts the family's fully comparable market rows by kind; the cell gives that count first, without IDs, then the tier word at once; a note on a partial row or an own offer that is not counted follows in brackets and never changes the word. An offer with two or more full rows in all: High. One offer and no other full row: Medium. No offer and two or more posted ranges: Medium. No offer and one posted range: Low. A partial row and the company's own offers never enter the count, so one posted range beside a partial offer, or beside an own hire, is still Low. Nothing fully comparable, or no band: Deferred, with an entry under Deferred verdicts.
- Own offers. Each cell starts with the snapshot's whole line for that family, declined offers and candidates' reasons included, or "no recent offers" when the line says so; a family that no line of the offer list names gets "not in the offer list". Each line stands in one cell only: the same offer figures never appear in two rows, a declined offer stays with the line that lists it, and a family without a line never takes its neighbour's.
- Caveats. Two to four bullets, each true of this pool: the evidence mix by family, copied from the draft (which references rest on accepted offers, and which on posted ranges alone; "no reference rests on posted ranges alone" when there is none), with no count of rows; one source, only when every fully comparable row names the same one; pay elements or currencies, only when the pool mixed them; a gap, only when the input itself leaves something open, such as roles with no metro anchoring that it does not name (when the input says remote roles are anchored to a metro, nothing is open). No bullet says how ranges came to be posted: a mandate or a voluntary posting is not a caveat, and the page never says that such ranges run wide, high or low. No bullet restates the basis line.
- Copying. Every date, month, year and figure on the page is the one printed in the input.
