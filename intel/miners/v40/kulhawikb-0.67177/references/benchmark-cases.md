## Next step: the draft, not the page

Do not write the page yet. Your next message is a private draft that the requester never sees, and it ends with a call to read_reference for `references/page-check.md`.

In the draft, follow the reasoning of the cases above, never their facts, through six steps.

One: start with a window line. Find, in the input in front of you, the sentence that says which dates are out of window: it may stand in the snapshot or right above the evidence table, and it never comes from this file or from the skill. Copy it word for word in quotes, then write the first inside month it names as year-month and as a number, the retrieval month as a number, and how many months lie between them (`window: "rows dated before 2025-12 are out" -> first inside month 2025-12 = 202512; retrieved 202609; 9 months back`). The year is the one printed in the quoted sentence, never the retrieval date's; when the input states the window's length and the months back do not match it, the line is wrong: read the input's sentence again and correct it before going on. Then write one line for every evidence row, with its ID and seven tests in this order, each answered from the input. Family: which snapshot family and level is the row's role title for? Name it (a row for no family in the snapshot is excluded). Date: write the row's month as a number, then the window line's number with the sign between them, then the result (`202511 >= 202506: in`, `202504 < 202506: out`, `202512 >= 202512: in`); an undated row is out. The sign alone decides: a row from the year before the retrieval year is inside whenever its number is not the smaller one, and the first inside month itself is inside. Market: the market that family's band is anchored to? Another city, or a remote or national figure with no city, fails. Stage: number the rounds (Seed 0, Series A 1, B 2, C 3, D 4, E 5, later rounds higher) and subtract the row's number from the company's; a difference of 0 is the same stage, exactly 1 either way is one step, 2 or more fails (Series E against Series B is 5 - 2 = 3: excluded), and a listed company always fails. Level: the family's level, or a scope the notes declare equivalent? Pay basis: the band's pay element, period and currency, or brought onto it with a split the row itself states? Source: a salaried offer or posting from a named source, not hearsay, an unnamed survey or a contract rate? A row that passes everything with zero stage steps is fully comparable; a row whose only miss is exactly one stage step is partial; every other row is excluded, under the first test it fails. Then list, family by family, the IDs whose line names that family, each with its class (full, partial or out), the same word the audit and the table will use: each ID belongs to exactly one family, and a table row counts only the rows of its own family.

Two: show each conversion as a subtraction that uses only the row's own stated split.

Three: per family and level, the reference is the fully comparable accepted offer (the median of several), or the fully comparable posted midpoint only when no such offer exists; partial rows and the company's own offers never set it. Work out the market gap, (midpoint - reference) / reference, to one decimal.

Four: copy the snapshot's own-offer list line by line, and after each line write the role it names and the one band-table family and level that role belongs to (`Senior Gameplay Programmer: Gameplay Programming, Senior`). An entry such as "two accepted at X, one at Y" is three offers (X, X, Y); a declined offer and any reason a candidate gave are part of the line. Then write the band table's families in their own order, each followed by its matched line or by "not in the offer list". The offer list is often shorter than the band table and in another order, so a line is matched by the role it names, never by its position, and a family never borrows the line of the family next to it. Five: one decision line per family, the tests first and the verdict last. `market gap -4.9%; hiring gap -5.6%; either at -5.0% or beyond? yes, the hiring gap: Lagging`. `market gap -3.9%; hiring gap -4.4%; either at -5.0% or beyond? no: Competitive`. `market gap +6.9%; hiring gap +1.3%; market at +5.0% or beyond? yes; hiring at +2.5% or beyond? no: Competitive`. `market gap +7.3%; hiring gap +2.6%; market at +5.0% or beyond? yes; hiring at +2.5% or beyond? yes: Above-market`. `market gap -3.8%; no hiring gap; at -5.0% or beyond? no: Competitive`. `market gap -5.0%; no hiring gap; at -5.0% or beyond? yes: Lagging`.
