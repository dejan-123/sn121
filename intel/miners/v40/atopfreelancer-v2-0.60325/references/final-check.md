# Final check

Your working notes are done. They stay where they are; the reader never sees them. The next message is the document and nothing else.

## Check the notes before copying from them

- The cut-off month is the one the request's window sentence names, copied as written, not counted back from the retrieval date.
- Every external row has exactly one outcome: fully comparable, partial match, or dropped with a reason.
- For each family, retrieved equals fully comparable plus partial plus dropped; family totals add up to the rows in the request's table.
- Each market reference rests only on fully comparable rows, and on accepted offers alone where one survives.
- Each gap has its sign the right way round: a midpoint under the reference is negative.
- A family with no fully comparable row has no reference and no direction.
- A gap smaller than 4.0% or larger than 6.0% takes its verdict from the arithmetic alone. Only a gap inside that zone is settled by the family's own offers: accepted offers averaging above the band midpoint, or a decline over pay, make it Lagging; anything else leaves the arithmetic result.
- Company stage and location were read from each row itself, not assumed from its neighbours.

If a check fails, correct the figure in your head and write the corrected figure in the document. Do not write the correction out.

## Then send the document

- It begins at its title line. No sentence comes before the title, and the notes are not repeated, summarised or referred to.
- The order is: title; basis paragraph; the table with pool totals; the calls that needed judgement; deferred verdicts; signal caveats.
- The verdict cell holds one word: Competitive, Lagging, Above-market or Deferred. "Marginal" belongs in the sentence about that call, not in the cell.
- Confidence is High, Medium, Low or Deferred, each with its deciding reason in a few words.
- Anything true of the whole pool appears once, under signal caveats.
- No person is named. No figure appears that is not in the request or computed from it in the table.
- Keep it short enough to read in two minutes. A reader who wants the row-by-row scoring can ask for it.
