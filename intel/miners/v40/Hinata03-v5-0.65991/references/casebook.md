# Casebook: two benchmarks worked row by row

Everything here is invented: the companies, the titles, every city's pay and every figure. None of it is evidence about any real market, so no name, figure or verdict from these pages belongs in a reply. What carries over is the order of judgement and the form of the working.

The working is scratch paper. It is written in full before any report, so that every call in the report is copied from a line already settled. Its first line, Over the table, copies word for word whatever is printed between the evidence table's heading and its row of column names; a line standing there often begins "Note" and says how the ranges were posted. That space is looked at before anything else is written, because a line in it decides every range whose own note is blank; "nothing printed there" is written only when the column names follow the heading directly. The second line, Snapshot notes, copies any line beginning "Note" that stands beside the bands (a team's country and currency), or says there is none. The third line, Rows copied, takes every row in the table's own order and copies two cells of it inside quotation marks, its title and its Notes cell, writing "none" for an empty Notes cell. Nothing is judged on this line. Every row's line further down takes its title and its note from this line, so neither is ever recalled from memory. The Open roles line copies the roles the snapshot lists as open with their priority, or says none are listed. The Own offers line quotes each family's offer line from the input and only then expands it, one figure per accepted offer: "two accepted at $X, one at $Y" is X, X, Y, three figures, and the average is taken over all of them. Every figure in such a list comes from the quoted line; a figure that belongs to an outside point with an ID is never in it, and a declined offer is context only.

Posting is settled row by row, never from memory of the whole table: every range's line first copies that row's own note inside quotation marks, or writes "note blank", and only then makes the call. A note saying the range was voluntarily posted makes it voluntary, a partial test, also when the same note remarks that the state has no mandate (that is a denial of a mandate). A note naming a mandate, law or disclosure rule that published the range, with no "voluntarily" in it, makes it mandated, a match. A blank note, or one about something else, is decided by the Over the table line: where the line copied there says the ranges were posted by choice unless a row says otherwise, the blank row is voluntary; where nothing is printed there, the range stands, a match.

The Rows line only files: every ID of the input goes under one family, and nothing is judged there. A row that will fail a test, two rounds off or a listed company, is still filed under its family and gets its own line there with its outcome; no row is dropped on the Rows line, and the families' retrieved figures add up to the number of rows in the input. Each row gets one line: its kind, its month set against the first month in, then place, round, scope with the row's title copied in quotation marks, and posting, then the outcome. Another named city is a partial test, like a remote figure with no metro. No partial test and no mismatch is full; exactly one partial test is partial; two partial tests, or any mismatch, is dropped. Only the words "partial test" on a row's line are counted: a mandated posting is a match and adds nothing to the count, and a note that only repeats a miss already counted ("no metro declared" on a remote row) adds nothing either. Under a family's rows comes the family's line: the funnel, the full offers and full ranges listed apart, the Range check, the anchor, the market gap with its close check (worked in two steps, the difference first and then the division by the anchor, never by the midpoint), the verdict and the tier, then the own hires read the same way, the partials with the side of the anchor each falls on. The family's heading carries its band and its open role, copied from the Open roles line, or the words "no open role". An earlier round usually pays less and a later round more, so a partial from an earlier round that sits above the anchor, or from a later round that sits below it, is a surprise worth a sentence; the other way round nothing is said about it. The Range check comes before any gap and has one of three answers. A family with any full offer writes "not needed, an accepted offer anchors", and its verdict comes from that offer whatever the ranges say. A family with no full offer and two or more full ranges writes "not needed, two adverts stand together". Only a family with no full offer and exactly one full range is checked: if an outside accepted offer among its partials sits more than 5.0% from that range's midpoint, the advert and the only pay anyone took disagree and the family is deferred; with no such offer, or one within 5.0%, the range stands. The company's own offers are never part of this check. Under the family's line comes its Say line: three or four plain sentences that put the call the way a compensation lead would hear it, every figure and ID taken from the lines above. The report's paragraphs are these Say lines, so they are written with care here. A Say line says a gap is close to the line only when the family's line carries the close flag, a size between 2.0% and 4.0%; a gap of 4.5% or 7% is past the line, never close to it. The working closes with the Count line and the Loudest dropped line: the highest figure on any dropped row, with that row's ID and its own job title.
