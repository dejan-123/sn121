# A band is priced by the hires that match it, not by the pool that mentions it

An evidence pool collected by search always holds more neighbours than matches: the same title in another city, a company two funding rounds further on, a package swollen by stock or bonus, a contractor's day rate, a one-off retention cheque. Averaged, it describes no job the company pays for. Only two kinds of row can price a band. An accepted offer is revealed preference, a figure a candidate actually took. A posted range is aspirational, what an employer chose to advertise, and hires tend to settle below its middle. Where a pay-transparency law forced the posting it is at least a disclosure the employer has to stand behind; a range posted by choice is recruiting copy that nothing holds the employer to. So ranges stand in for missing offers but never outvote one, a family priced on adverts alone is trusted less, and a voluntary advert cannot price a band at all. The company's own recent hires are a second witness: they never set the market price and never move a verdict, but hires that close past the midpoint while the market reads level are a warning the report says out loud. Where nothing comparable survives, the honest answer is a deferral that names the evidence still needed. No exchange rate, bonus share, hours figure, premium or discount is ever brought in from outside the input.

Every reply begins with one read_reference call, and nothing is written before it returns: `references/casebook.md` for a benchmark, `references/note.md` for anything else.

## How each call is made

- **Rows that leave before any test.** Dated before the window, or not dated at all. Hearsay: a recruiter's word, a survey with no named source or method. Pay that is not annual base: retention or acquisition grants, contractor or hourly contract rates (never annualised), equity-heavy or cash-heavy packages, on-target earnings or a total with no stated split; a figure in a second currency when the input gives no rate. A row whose note states its base inside a total keeps that base.
- **The window.** The input's own wording fixes the first month in; that month counts, every earlier month is out, an accepted offer included. Compare the year first, then the month.
- **Four tests for every remaining row.** *Place*: the company's metro, the metro the input assigns that family, or the tier remote roles are pinned to is a match; a remote or national figure with no metro is a partial test, and so is any other named city. *Round*: seed 0, A 1, B 2, C 3, D 4, E or later 5, public 6; the same round matches, one round apart is a partial test, two or more is a mismatch. *Scope*: the same work at the same level, or a title the input maps to it, matches; one level up or down is a partial test when the snapshot has no band for that level, and belongs to that band alone when it has one; another role is a mismatch. *Posting*: an accepted offer matches, and so does a range published under a pay-transparency mandate or one the input says nothing about; a range the input marks as voluntarily posted is a partial test. Each range is read by its own note first: "no mandate here; voluntarily posted" is a voluntary posting and a named mandate or disclosure law is a mandated one. A row that says nothing stands, unless a line over the evidence table says the ranges were posted by choice except where a row says otherwise: then the silent rows are voluntary. One row's note never marks its neighbours.
- **Outcome.** Four matches make a row fully comparable. One partial test makes it partial: listed with its figure and the side of the anchor it falls on, never part of the anchor, never adjusted. Two partial tests, or any mismatch, drop it. An earlier round usually pays less and a later one more; a partial that falls the other way is worth a sentence. A family left with partial rows only has no anchor, so it is deferred, however urgent its open role, and partials pulling opposite ways are a reason to wait, never an average.
- **The range check.** Made before any gap, and only for a family with no full offer and exactly one full range. That advert needs company from its own market, an own hire for the family or a partial row from the band's city: with neither, nothing has tested it and the family is deferred. If an outside accepted offer among its partials sits more than 5.0% from its midpoint, it is deferred too: an advert and the only pay anyone took disagree.
- **One basis.** Annual base in the band's own currency, each conversion written with its direction: a month times 12, a salaried hourly rate times 2,080, a range to its midpoint, a total down to its stated base. A family banded in another currency is judged in that currency.
- **The market anchor.** With any fully comparable outside accepted offer: the average of those offers alone (one offer is the anchor), each named by ID; a full range is quoted beside it and kept out of the sum. With none: the average of the full range midpoints, each named by ID. Partials and the company's own offers never enter it.
- **The market gap.** (anchor - band midpoint) / anchor, so a positive gap means the market pays more than the band; tested against the line before rounding, and within 0.1 of it written to two decimals. Above +3.0%: lagging. Below -3.0%: above-market. From -3.0% to +3.0%, both ends included: competitive. The 3% line is a convention, so a gap whose size is between 2.0% and 4.0% carries the word "close" beside its verdict.
- **Own hires.** Where the snapshot lists the company's own accepted offers for the family, write each one as its own figure, taken from that family's offer line and nowhere else ("two accepted at $96,500" is two offers at $96,500; an outside point's figure is never one of them), average them, and read (own average - band midpoint) / own average on the same 3.0% line. The same word as the market verdict: the hires agree. Another word: they trouble it, and the report says so. Either way the verdict, anchor and tier stay where the outside evidence put them; declined offers and what candidates said rivals pay are context only.
- **The confidence tier,** counted from fully comparable outside rows only: **High**, two or more offers; **Medium**, one offer, whatever ranges stand beside it, or two or more ranges and no offer (the posted-range cap); **Low**, one range and no offer, still a verdict, which own hires beside it never lift; **Deferred**, nothing fully comparable survived, or the family has no band.

## Before the pool

Read the snapshot first: the retrieval date and the window as the input words it, the company's metro and round, where remote roles are pinned, each family's band and currency, the roles listed as open with their priority, the offer line for each family, any line beginning "Note" beside the bands, and whatever is printed between the evidence table's heading and its column names. Missing internal data is named as a gap and never filled: a family with no band, a snapshot that says some roles are remote and unanchored without naming them, a family with no own offer on record, a second currency with no rate. Rows are not always grouped by family: each is filed once, under the family and level its title names, wherever it sits, the last row of the table included. Two levels of one function are two families.

## Requests that are not a benchmark

Pricing one named person, a market lookup with no evidence pool, designing or redrawing bands, a pay-equity or demographic audit, or any paste without classified outside evidence is not a benchmark and gets no table and no figure. It gets a short note that sends each ask to its owner and says what to send for a benchmark; a roster of the company's own salaries neither confirms nor rebuts that anyone is underpaid. Where part of a request is a benchmark with its evidence present, that part is done. Before a word of the note is written, read_reference is called on `references/note.md`: the note's form is there and not on this page.

## How a reply is made

Neither reply can be written from this page: the layout of each is kept in its reference. Whatever the request, the first move after loading this skill is one read_reference call, and nothing is written before it returns.

**Not a benchmark:** call read_reference on `references/note.md`. It holds the note's form and says what the next message is: the note alone, from its `# ` title.

**A benchmark** takes three moves.

1. Call read_reference on `references/casebook.md`. It holds two invented cases worked out row by row; its last lines say what the next message is.
2. For a benchmark, the next message is the working for the input in hand, as plain text in the casebook's form: every family of the snapshot, every row on its own line, each family's line and Say line under its rows, then the count and the loudest dropped figure. It ends with the line "Working done; the report is next." and, in that same message, a read_reference call on `references/reports.md`. None of the report is written in that message.
3. The report is the last message, copied from the working: every verdict, anchor, gap and tier the same as there. It argues before it tabulates: an opening paragraph on basis, window, conversions and what the snapshot leaves out; the families in plain paragraphs, firmest call first; then `## Benchmark table` (verdict, market gap, evidence funnel by ID, confidence), a **Count.** line, `## Deferred verdicts` and `## Signal caveats`. The page goes to a compensation lead as it stands, so it starts at `# ` and holds none of the working.
