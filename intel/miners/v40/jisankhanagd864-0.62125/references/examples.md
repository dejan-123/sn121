## Case three: not a benchmark request

**The input.** From the operations lead at Pinecrest Animation, a 45-person studio in Savannah: "Can you tell me the going rate for a senior rigger? Mina, our lead candidate, wants $96,500 and I need to know whether to match it. While you're at it, could you fold our two design bands into one, and check whether the women on the team are paid less than the men? Staff list with current pay is attached. We haven't pulled any market data yet." Attached: six names, each with a job title and a salary.

**The whole reply is this note: four paragraphs, none longer than three sentences, no list.**

# Not a benchmark request: Pinecrest Animation

**What was asked.** A going rate for a senior rigger, a decision on one candidate's ask, a merger of the two design bands, and a pay check between women and men, with a staff list and no market data.

**Why there is no benchmark.** A band verdict needs external pay evidence that is already classified and a band per role family. Neither is here: the list gives pay per person, and a going rate quoted without evidence would be a guess presented as a finding.

**Where the other parts belong.** A going rate is the one question here a benchmark can answer, once there is evidence to answer it with. The candidate's offer sits with the hiring manager and HR partner. Folding two bands into one is job-architecture design. Pay differences between groups need an equity audit.

**What to send for a benchmark.** External pay points, each classified by source, type, date, metro, company stage and level, with accepted offers kept apart from posted ranges, since an offer is what was paid and a range only what an employer says it might pay; a band (minimum, midpoint, maximum) per role family; and the data window. Each family then gets a verdict, its evidence funnel and a confidence tier from High down to Deferred.

## Writing the reply

Each reply above is the whole of what was sent, from its title to its last caveat. In each benchmark the count of what a point is off by is written before its class, the IDs in a funnel before their count, inside or outside before the verdict, and the kept count before the confidence it earns, partial points never added to it: that order is what keeps a page consistent with itself.
