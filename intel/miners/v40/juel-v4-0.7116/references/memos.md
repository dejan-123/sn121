# From a sheet to a memo

The memo goes to an HR lead who will be questioned on it: 550 to 800 words about role families only, one analyst talking to another. Its facts are the sheet's and no others: the paragraphs are the Say lines put in order and joined into prose, with the figures from their own lines. Checks and steps stay on the sheet. Every point the memo mentions carries its id in brackets the first time, beside the name a reader would know it by: an accepted offer at a Series C company, $175,600 (H-01). The id and the title are copied from that point's own row of the paste, never from where it sat on the Dates line or the sheet. The first time each kind is weighed it gets the name an HR reader looks for: an accepted offer is revealed preference, pay somebody actually took, and a posted range is aspirational, what an employer says it will pay. "Paid" and "hired" belong to outside companies unless the sentence names the company itself, and the company's name is used for its own bands and its own offers only.

- `# <Company>: ` and the sheet's title line, in its verdict words.
- An opening paragraph: the city and round the bands are held to, the first month in, copied with its year from the sheet's Floor line (2025-10 is October 2025), and what it shuts out, each by id and by the title its own row gives, if it shuts anything out, and which rows are not prices, by id, taken from the sheet's "Not a price at all" line and no other: a range from another city or round is a price that failed a test, not a non-price. A figure in another currency with no rate is named with its currency, and the sentence says the paste gives no rate; it is never converted. A line the sheet does not have gets no sentence here, not even to say there is none.
- Two to four paragraphs, no headings, from the firmest call to the weakest. Families decided by the same kind of evidence share a paragraph, and each paragraph opens on what decides it. A conversion shows its sum and direction. A range the sheet marks volunteered is called volunteered where it comes up, and partial for that; a point from another city is partial for its place and says how that city pays, not this one. What a candidate said about a rival's pay, or about why an offer was turned down, is named as hearsay where it comes up and is never set beside a gap or said to agree with one. Only a family the sheet defers is called deferred; a Low one has its verdict, and is said to rest on one posting. An offer from another round beside that posting is partial: it leans, and it never turns the call into a deferral. A partial point is worth a clause for its lean, and an open P1 or P2 role a clause where it raises what hangs on the call.
- `## <Company>'s benchmark`: a table, Family and level | Verdict | Gap, worked | Evidence funnel | Confidence, one row per family in snapshot order. The gap is written as its sum, (midpoint − anchor) ÷ anchor, with the anchor's ids. The funnel is the family's funnel line from the sheet: retrieved, then survived, partial and dropped, all four counted even at 0, each with its ids, and why beside the partial and dropped. Confidence is the tier and its deciding reason in a few words. Under the table, one line opening `Pool:` gives the sheet's four totals as plain numbers, retrieved, survived, partial and dropped, with nothing in brackets.
- `## Deferred verdicts for <Company>`, always there: each deferred family, what came close by id, and the evidence that would settle it (kind, city, round, level). With none deferred, that is said, then the verdict resting on least and what would firm it.
- `## Signal caveats on <Company>'s pool`: two to four sentences on the pool as a whole, repeating nothing, and with no count of points, kinds, sources or postings in them, since a count is the easiest figure to get wrong. They name the families that rest on accepted offers, revealed preference, and those that stand on posted ranges alone, aspirational, as the table's Confidence column has them; then, by id, any row that came from somewhere other than the paste's main source, and the ranges marked volunteered or silent on why they were posted that bear on a verdict. A gap is called close only if the sheet lists it as close.
- A last line on the number somebody will quote, the figure on the sheet's "Loudest not counted" line, in the currency and with the id that line gives, and the answer to it.

Every figure is in the paste or worked from it in view. No advice on what to pay.

Two invented memos follow. Halvard's was written from the sheet you have seen; Corvane's comes from a shorter pool in Canadian dollars, whose paste says above its table that every range was posted by choice unless its row names a law. How they open, which families they put together and what the paragraphs dwell on differ, because the pools did.

## Halvard

# Halvard Foodservice Systems: two bands lag Milwaukee, Channel Sales is above market, Install Scheduling is competitive, Demand Forecasting is deferred

Halvard is held to Milwaukee and Series C. September 2025 is the first month in. It keeps a Service Operations range from that month (H-06) and shuts out H-08, a Service Operations Manager offer of $108,600 from August 2025. Four rows are not prices: an undated survey median for service managers with no method behind it (H-07), a Channel Sales range given only as on-target pay (H-10), a Senior Channel Sales Manager figure with a retention grant inside it (H-12) and an agency's word on a forecaster's salary (H-17).

Embedded Systems has the firmest evidence and the narrowest gap. Two Milwaukee companies at Series C hired at this level, a Staff Embedded Systems Engineer at $175,600 (H-01) and a Device Software Engineer, IC5, at $172,800 (H-02), a title the paste's scope note maps to Halvard's staff level. Accepted offers are revealed preference, pay somebody took, and their $174,200 average leaves the $167,400 midpoint 3.9% under: lagging, and close to the line. A posted range at the same round (H-03), published under a disclosure law, is aspirational and counts; it centres on $175,800, agrees, and stays out of the sum, because a range never joins an anchor that has offers in it. Halvard's own hires at $170,600 and $172,300 were above its midpoint already, and a P2 role is open. A candidate who turned down $168,900 said a rival offered about 10% more; that is hearsay, and the call does not lean on it. A public company's range (H-04) was left out.

Channel Sales rests on one offer, and it points the other way. H-09 came as $146,900 in total; less the stated $30,300 bonus it is $116,600 base, down, so the $123,800 midpoint is 6.2% over it: above market. A Series D range (H-11) centres on $130,800, higher as a later round usually is, so it says little, and a Milwaukee range filed as Account Executive / Senior Channel Sales (H-20) may be for either job, so it is partial on its title; it centres on $116,300, level with the offer. Halvard had $118,700 turned down; the turn-down is a fact, and the reason given is only what the candidate said.

Service Operations and Install Scheduling are read from postings alone. Service Operations has two Milwaukee ranges that agree, centred on $105,700 (H-05) and $107,900 (H-06); against their $106,800 average the $100,800 midpoint is 5.6% under, and Halvard's own hire at $103,700 sits with that reading. Install Scheduling has a single posting, centred on $68,800 (H-13), which leaves $67,800 1.5% under: competitive, and Low. A Series D company's $73,300 offer (H-19) is higher, as a later round usually is; it leans, and the call still stands on the posting. A remote range from a Series D company (H-18) missed twice and was dropped, and Halvard's offer list has no line for this family.

Demand Forecasting is deferred while its P1 role is open. A Series B offer at $187,700 (H-14) is lower, as an earlier round usually is, and a Milwaukee Series C range centred on $224,800 (H-15) is higher, but that range was volunteered, written to attract with nothing holding the employer to it. A Minneapolis range (H-16) centres higher still, on $240,700, and prices another city. The three pull opposite ways and none is a price that binds at Halvard's round in Milwaukee.

## Deferred verdicts for Halvard

Demand Forecasting, Principal. The Series B offer (H-14) came closest from below and the volunteered range (H-15) from above; one is a round early, the other binds nobody, and the Minneapolis range (H-16) is another city's pay. An accepted offer for a principal forecaster at a Milwaukee Series C company would settle it, or two postings there that a disclosure law made public and that agree; with the P1 role open, that is the evidence to ask for first.

## Signal caveats on Halvard's pool

Embedded Systems and Channel Sales rest on accepted offers, revealed preference; Service Operations and Install Scheduling stand on posted ranges alone, aspirational, and are capped for it. Every row but the survey (H-07), the recruiter's figure (H-12) and the agency's word (H-17) came from one job-data feed, so little outside it checks the pool. The counted ranges behind Service Operations and Install Scheduling (H-06, H-13) do not say why they were posted, so how far they run high cannot be told. The Embedded Systems gap sits near the 3% line, which is a convention, and Install Scheduling has no Halvard offer to set beside its outside reading.

Somebody will quote $267,600 for a demand forecaster. That is the top of a Minneapolis range (H-16), partial at most, and Halvard does not hire this job in Minneapolis.

## Corvane

# Corvane Robotics: Perception Engineering lags Toronto, Product Marketing sits a little above market, Fleet Support is deferred

Corvane is held to Toronto and Series B, and its bands are in Canadian dollars. June 2025 is the first month in, and it shuts out C-06, a Fleet Support Manager range from April 2025. The line printed above its evidence table says Toronto ranges in that pull were posted by choice unless a row names a law, so every range without such a note is volunteered and partial on its posting before anything else is weighed. A contractor's day rate for a product marketer (C-09) is not a price at all, and a product marketing range printed in US dollars (C-07) has no exchange rate anywhere in the paste, so it is not converted and is out.

Perception Engineering rests on one hire. A Toronto company at the same round took on a Senior Perception Engineer at C$157,300 (C-01), an accepted offer and so revealed preference, and Corvane's C$148,700 midpoint sits 5.5% under it: lagging. A Toronto range (C-02) centres on C$155,800, near the offer, but it was volunteered, aspirational and partial for that. An Ottawa offer at C$149,300 (C-03) is lower; it is another city's pay, partial for its place, and leans without deciding anything. Corvane's own hires at C$153,900 and C$156,100 were above its midpoint already.

Product Marketing stands on one posting. A Toronto Series B range published under a disclosure law (C-08) centres on C$117,900, which leaves the C$121,900 midpoint 3.4% over it: above market, close to the line, and Low.

Fleet Support is deferred. The only range at Corvane's city and round (C-04) was filed as Fleet Support Manager / Field Service Lead, two names on one posting, so it is partial on its title even though a law made it public; it centres on C$96,500, level with the band. A Hamilton offer at C$93,100 (C-05) is another city's. Neither is a price for this job in this city.

## Signal caveats on Corvane's pool

Perception Engineering rests on an accepted offer, revealed preference; Product Marketing stands on one posted range, aspirational, that a disclosure law made public. Under the line above the table the Perception Engineering range (C-02) was volunteered, so it leans and decides nothing. Corvane's own hires test Perception Engineering and nothing else.

Somebody will quote C$171,700 for a perception engineer. That is the top of a volunteered Toronto range (C-02), written to attract; the price somebody took at Corvane's round was C$157,300.
