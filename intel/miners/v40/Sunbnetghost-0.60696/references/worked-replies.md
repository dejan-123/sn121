# Worked replies

The four cases below are invented: every company, person, place, figure and date was made up for this file.
They show the form of a reply and the reasoning behind it only. In a real reply every fact comes from the input in front of you.

# Quillfern Logistics: pay bands against the market

**What the input fixes.** The window excludes anything dated before August 2027. That removes M-10 (2027-07); the other fifteen points run from 2028-02 to 2028-07 and sit inside it. Bands are annual base, and two points had to be brought onto that basis. M-08's $112,600 total, less its stated $9,400 bonus, leaves $103,200 base. M-12's US$76,300 becomes C$104,531 at the supplied 1 USD = 1.37 CAD (US dollars multiplied by 1.37), because the Toronto band is read in Canadian dollars. Remote roles are paid on the Columbus tier, so Columbus is the market for every US family. Carrier Procurement is missing from the own-hires list altogether. That is a gap in the snapshot, not a record of no hiring; Dispatch and Warehouse Automation are listed as having no hires.

## Family by family

**Route Optimisation Engineering, Staff ($183,400 / $199,600 / $216,800).** Two points vote. M-01 is a Columbus offer at a round-C company, accepted at $207,500. M-02 is a Columbus range of $192,500–$231,300 whose different title is mapped to the same staff level by its scope note. The offer is the anchor. M-02's midpoint of $211,900 stays out of the sum but agrees that pay runs above the band's middle. Against $207,500 the midpoint is −3.8% ($199,600 − $207,500 = −$7,900). The company's own two hires, at $209,100 and $211,700 (mean $210,400), put it −5.1% behind what its own candidates accepted. Both readings are below −2.5%, so the band is **Lagging**. Confidence is **High**, on an offer with a range beside it. M-03 leans the other way with a $196,400 midpoint, but it is Indianapolis pay and carries no weight. M-04's $238,900 comes from a round-E employer, two rounds off, and is set aside.

**Carrier Procurement, Manager ($121,600 / $133,800 / $146,500).** No offer survives, so the anchor comes from the two Columbus round-C ranges. M-05's midpoint of $144,800 and M-06's of $143,300 average to $144,050. The midpoint of this band is $10,250 under that, −7.1%. With no own hires on record the outside gap stands alone, and it clears the 5.5% line: **Lagging**. Confidence is **Medium**, because two advertised ranges are as far as ranges can carry a call. M-07's $148,500 was heard on a recruiter's call and is set aside unweighed. The anchor sits inside the band, near its $146,500 top.

**Fleet Maintenance Planning, Senior ($96,300 / $105,900 / $116,200).** Only the converted M-08 votes, at $103,200 base. The midpoint is +2.6% over it and +1.0% over the company's own hire at $104,800. The two readings do not both pass +2.5%, so the band is **Competitive**, at **Medium** on a single offer. M-09 (round B, midpoint $102,800) leans toward the same place but counts for nothing, and M-10 falls outside the window.

**Dispatch Operations, Lead, Toronto (C$88,400 / C$97,200 / C$107,100).** M-12, converted to C$104,531, is the anchor. M-11's Toronto range, midpoint C$110,600, agrees that the market sits above the band's middle. The midpoint falls C$7,331 short of the anchor, −7.0%. No Dispatch hires are recorded, so the outside gap decides alone and passes 5.5%: **Lagging**, **High** on an offer plus a range. M-13 is a contractor's C$64.50 an hour. The input gives no hours, so it cannot be annualised and is set aside.

**Warehouse Automation, Principal ($171,800 / $187,300 / $203,900).** Nothing votes. M-15 names no metro, so its $189,600 midpoint gives direction only. M-14 misses twice, on place and on round, and M-16 was hired at senior level, not principal. The verdict is **Deferred**.

## Deferred verdicts

- **Warehouse Automation, Principal.** M-14 fails on place and round, M-15 on place, and M-16 on level. One accepted offer, or two posted ranges, for a principal role in Columbus at a round-C company, dated August 2027 or later, would settle it.

## Signal caveats

- Of the seven voting points, three are accepted offers (M-01, M-08, M-12) and four are posted ranges (M-02, M-05, M-06, M-11). Ranges state what employers hope to pay and stretch at the top, which is why Carrier Procurement, resting on M-05 and M-06 alone, stops at Medium.
- Fifteen of the sixteen points come from one job-board feed. The only exception, M-07, was set aside, so the surviving pool reflects whichever employers advertise there.
- The notes never say whether a range was posted under a pay-range law, so no range is read as legally wide or as voluntarily generous.

**Calls a quick read would get wrong.** M-04's $238,900 would make Route Optimisation look far further behind than it is, but it is a round-E price; the Lagging call rests on M-01 and the company's own hires. Warehouse Automation has three rows of evidence and not one comparable point, so it gets no verdict.

## Case 4: a paste that cannot be benchmarked

The input:

```
Hi, we're Pellucid Aquafarms (seed stage, 31 staff, Duluth MN). Three things before Friday's board meeting:
can you tell us whether our hatchery technicians are underpaid compared with the market, set a starting
salary for the tank systems lead we're hiring Monday, and build proper bands for the ops team?
We don't have any outside pay data, just payroll below.

| Name | Title | Grade | Salary |
| Ines Halvorsen | Hatchery Technician | II | $58,400 |
| Kofi Abernathy-Reyes | Hatchery Technician | I | $51,700 |
| Rutger Sandoval | Tank Systems Technician | II | $63,900 |
| Wren Okonkwo | Water Quality Analyst | I | $55,300 |
```

The reply:

# Pellucid Aquafarms: no benchmark can be built from this paste

This paste holds a payroll extract of four named people in three titles and no outside pay evidence. A benchmark by job family cannot be drawn from it, and I will not offer market figures from memory, because an unsourced number tends to get repeated as a finding.

Your three asks belong to three different pieces of work:

1. Whether hatchery technicians are underpaid is a benchmark question, and it needs classified outside points. Two individual technician salaries can neither confirm nor rule out underpayment.
2. A starting salary for the tank systems lead is an individual offer decision for the hiring manager and HR, made once a band exists.
3. Building bands for the operations team is band design, which follows a benchmark rather than replacing it.

To get a benchmark back, send:
- outside pay points, each with an ID, source, type (accepted offers kept apart from posted ranges), date, title and level, location, funding round, and the figure with its basis;
- your pay grouped by job and level (min, midpoint, max, headcount) instead of by name;
- any offers made in the past year, accepted or declined;
- the date window to apply.

Each family then comes back with a verdict, an evidence funnel and a confidence tier, or a deferral that names the missing evidence.

For Friday, gather accepted offers for hatchery technicians in Duluth at seed and round-A employers first. Keep the named salaries out of the board pack.

## Writing the reply

The family paragraphs carry the reasoning; the table only restates what they decided. Every figure is either copied from the input or produced by a sum written next to it, and no rate, bonus share or hours figure is ever supplied from outside the input. The funnel counts must add up to the pool line. Points about the whole pool stay in the caveats and are not repeated in rows. Change the phrasing from one family to the next so that no two paragraphs read alike. The message starts at its title and ends with the calls a quick reader would get wrong.
