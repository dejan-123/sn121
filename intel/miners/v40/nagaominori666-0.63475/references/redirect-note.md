# The redirect note: one written out

Tessaly Marine Systems is invented. Take the form and the reasoning, never a fact.

## The request

"Here is our design team at Tessaly Marine Systems (Series B, Tampa FL)", followed by four names, each with a title (Naval Architect Senior, Naval Architect Mid, Marine Systems Engineer Senior) and a salary. "Our COO says our naval architects are paid under market. What should we be paying? We also want to merge the Mid and Senior bands into one. I need something for Friday's leadership meeting."

There is no classified evidence table and no band, so there is nothing to screen.

## The note as sent

## Not a benchmark request — Tessaly Marine Systems

Nothing here can be benchmarked yet: the paste is a list of four people and their pay, with no classified outside evidence, and it bundles four different jobs.

1. **What naval architects and marine systems engineers earn in the market.** That is a market-rate lookup, not benchmarking. A pay-data provider or your recruiting team collects it. I do not quote market pay from memory, because a figure nobody can trace is a guess.
2. **Whether your naval architects are underpaid.** A list of salaries neither confirms nor rebuts the COO's claim. A benchmark reads bands by role family and level, never a named person; what an individual should earn stays with their manager and your people lead.
3. **Merging the Mid and Senior bands.** That is band design, for whoever owns your pay structure. A finished benchmark is an input to it, not a substitute for it.
4. **Something for Friday.** The analysis waits on evidence that has not been collected. Start with Tampa points at Series B for Naval Architect, accepted offers first, since they carry the most weight. If they are not in hand by Friday, tell the leadership team the benchmark is waiting on data.

**What to send.** Your bands (minimum, midpoint, maximum) for Naval Architect Senior, Naval Architect Mid and Marine Systems Engineer Senior, in place of the named salaries. Outside points, one row each: source, type (accepted offer or posted range), date, role and level, place, company stage, and the figure with its pay basis. Your recent offers, accepted and declined, and the data window to apply.

**What comes back.** For each of those three families: a verdict (lagging, competitive or above market), the evidence funnel behind it and a confidence tier, or a deferral where too little survives, with accepted offers weighed ahead of posted ranges. Until the evidence exists, all three are deferred.

## Before sending yours

Every separate thing the request asks has its own numbered line with the kind of work and its owner. No salary from the paste is repeated, no figure of any kind appears, no data vendor is named, and no person is named. The families and levels, the place and the stage are the request's own.

Now send the note for the request in front of you. Its first characters are "## Not a benchmark request — ". Nothing comes before the title, not even a horizontal rule, and nothing about this skill, its references or these checks appears in it.
