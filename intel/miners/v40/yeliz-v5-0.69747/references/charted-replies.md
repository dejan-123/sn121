# Finished replies: the vertical farm and the kayak works, and a note for an ask with no chart

Every company, city, figure and date here is invented. The first two replies are what the reader received once the sheets in `references/sounding-sheets.md` were closed; the third is the note for a request that needs no sheet. They show form and size only. What carries over to a live reply:
- a `## ` title naming the company and its verdicts in words, then an opening paragraph: the home metro and round, the opening month and what it struck, the rows setting no price, and every conversion written as a sum with its direction;
- two to four argued paragraphs, firmest call first, each opening on what decides it, with accepted offers named revealed preference and posted ranges aspirational the first time each is weighed, volunteered ranges called partial, a partial's lean given in a clause, and hearsay named as such;
- a confidence tier read off the counted points: High once a counted offer has any other counted point beside it, Medium for an offer alone or for agreeing ranges alone, Low for one range;
- one table whose gap cell shows its sum with the anchor's IDs and whose funnel counts all four piles by ID, then a `Pool:` line;
- Deferred Verdicts, always present, and Signal Caveats said once for the pool;
- a last line answering the loudest figure that was dropped;
- for a request outside the chart, a short note giving a judgment, what the paste shows, and what to send.

No company, city, job title, figure or date carries over. Each comes from the request in hand, never from this file.

## Reply three: a pay-equity question and a promotion with no market evidence

What the general manager at Mossgiel Mushroom Farm sent. Mossgiel is a Series A grower of specialty mushrooms with 40 staff in Burlington VT. "Are our day and night harvest crews paid fairly against each other? And I want to promote Ailsa Brennan from crew lead to harvest supervisor at $64,500. Bands and our full payroll by name are below; we have no outside data." Bands given (minimum / midpoint / maximum): Harvester $36,400 / $39,500 / $42,900; Crew Lead $44,800 / $48,600 / $52,800; Harvest Supervisor $53,900 / $58,500 / $63,600. The payroll names 19 people: twelve harvesters split across day and night crews, five crew leads and two harvest supervisors. No evidence table came with it, so no sheet was kept.

The note:

## Mossgiel Mushroom Farm: a pay-equity question and one promotion, not a benchmark

You asked whether the day and night harvest crews are paid fairly against each other, and whether to promote one crew lead to harvest supervisor at $64,500. A benchmark can settle neither. It places role-family bands against classified outside evidence, and none came with the request; comparing pay between crews is a pay-equity review, which needs the people records and the reasons behind each rate; and a promotion figure is a decision about one person, which sits with whoever owns pay decisions.

What the paste does show, by family and level: the proposed $64,500 is $900 over the Harvest Supervisor maximum of $63,600 and $6,000 over its $58,500 midpoint, so it would sit above the band before any market question arises. The payroll is read by family and level only: twelve harvesters, five crew leads and two supervisors.

To benchmark the Harvester, Crew Lead and Harvest Supervisor bands, send:
- the evidence, one line per point: its source, accepted offer or posted range, the month, the role and its level, the metro, the employer's funding round, the figure with its pay basis, and whether a range was posted under a law or by choice;
- the home metro and any roles with no metro tie, the months the evidence may span, and the date it was pulled;
- Mossgiel's own offers in that window, accepted or declined, with any reason given.

With those, each family gets a verdict, a gap and a confidence tier. The promotion and the crew comparison stay with the people who own them.
