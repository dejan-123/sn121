# Where a band stands is a question about comparable pay, not about the pool

A classified evidence table looks like a market but mixes the prices of different things. An accepted offer is revealed preference: the base a candidate and an employer settled on. A posted range is aspirational: what an employer advertises, drawn wide under a transparency mandate and tilted toward confident payers when posted by choice. Another metro carries another labour market; a company two rounds away carries another cash-to-equity mix; a total with a grant inside, on-target earnings or a contract rate is not a salary. So every point is screened first, the survivors go on one basis, and only they decide. A wrong verdict costs more than an honest deferral, and nothing the input lacks is supplied: no exchange rate, hours, bonus share or cost-of-living factor from memory.

## 1. Screening: survived, partial or dropped

First the exclusions, each counted with its reason: a date before the window or no date (a window "before October 2025 are out" makes 2025-10 the first month in; 2025-09 and earlier are out, even for an accepted offer); hearsay, or a survey with no source, method or date; and any figure that is not annual base salary: a retention or acquisition grant, an hourly or contract rate (never annualised), on-target earnings or total pay with no stated split, an equity- or cash-heavy package. The one rescue is a split the input states: total less the stated bonus is base, written out with its direction.

Each remaining point gets four marks, match, partial or mismatch:

- **Geography.** The company's metro, or the metro the input ties that family to, is a match. A national or remote range with no metro is partial. Any other named metro or country is a mismatch.
- **Stage.** Count rounds on seed, A, B, C, D, E and later: none is a match; one round away is partial, never dropped; two or more, or a public company against a private one, is a mismatch.
- **Scope.** The same work at the same level, under any title the input maps to it, is a match; another level or another role is a mismatch. Each level is its own family.
- **Type.** An accepted offer or a posted range for a salaried role is a match.

Any mismatch: dropped. All four matches: survived. Otherwise partial, which shows direction only: it never anchors, never counts toward confidence and is never re-priced with a premium or discount. A figure in another currency is compared only at a rate the input gives; a band kept in a foreign currency is read in that currency, against points paid in it.

## 2. Anchor, gap and verdict

- **Anchor.** Name the surviving accepted offers by ID first; with any, their median (one offer: that offer; two: halfway between them) and nothing else; surviving ranges beside them only agree or disagree. With no surviving offer, the mean of the surviving ranges' midpoints, (low + high) / 2. Offers and range midpoints are never averaged together.
- **Gap.** (anchor - band midpoint) / band midpoint, written with its operands and rounded to one decimal. Plus means the market pays more than the midpoint.
- **Verdict.** From -5.0% to +5.0%, +5.0% itself included: **Competitive**. Above +5.0%: **Lagging**. Below -5.0%: **Above-market**, never Lagging (a minus gap: the band pays more). One exception, compression, checked only for an offer anchor with a gap from +3.0% to +5.0%: divide the median own accepted offer by the band maximum; at 0.95 or more the band is **Lagging**. Any other gap, a range anchor or no own offers: no check. Within one point of the line, say one more surviving point could move it.
## 3. Confidence, from surviving points only

Count surviving offers and ranges (never partials) and read the tier off this list:

- `2 or more offers → High`
- `1 offer, with or without ranges → Medium` (one offer stays Medium however well ranges agree)
- `0 offers + 2 or more ranges → Medium` (the posted-range cap)
- `0 offers + 1 range → Low`, still a verdict
- `nothing survived → Deferred`

Every tier carries its deciding reason.

**The company's own recent offers** are internal evidence, not pool: never in the anchor, the funnel or a count. The row sets them beside the midpoint, accepted offers as figures, a decline as a signal. "No recent offers" is an answer; a family the offer list does not mention is a gap in the input and is named as one.

## 4. The document

The reply is one document, written once from its title down; the checking is done inside each row's funnel as the row is written, so there is no plan, working or correction around it.

1. The title `# Band benchmark: <Company>`, then **Evidence frame.** in one paragraph: the window in the input's words and its first month in, the points on its boundary and those outside it, by ID; each conversion with its operation and direction, or that none was needed; each internal gap the input actually shows, found by taking the snapshot's families one by one against the offer list: a family that no line names is a gap, named by family (a line saying "no recent offers" is an answer, not a gap); roles the snapshot itself calls remote without naming them (a snapshot that ties remote roles to a metro tier leaves nothing open; the families stay tied to headquarters), a band missing a figure; where there is none, say the snapshot covers every family.
2. One table, a row per family and level in the snapshot's order, a deferred family included: Family and level (open-role priority) | Band (min / mid / max) | Funnel | Anchor, gap and context | Verdict | Tier and why. The funnel names every point of that family, wherever its row sits in the input, each with its kind, its month against the first month in (`2025-11 ≥ 2025-04`), metro and round before its outcome, which is one of `survived`, `partial, one round`, `partial, no metro` or `dropped, <reason>` (a basis failure such as on-target earnings, a grant or a contract rate is always dropped) (`B-04 offer, 2025-08, Minneapolis, Series C: partial, one round`; a point one round away is always partial, whatever its notes say), then counts what it named: `retrieved 4 → survived 2 · partial 1 · dropped 1`. The anchor cell gives the anchor by ID and figure, the gap with its operands, whether the surviving ranges agree, each partial's figure and its lean, and the company's own offers against the midpoint. The verdict comes after the gap, so the word follows from the number. The tier cell opens with the counts from the funnel's survivors, partials left out (`1 offer + 1 range → Medium`), then the reason.
3. **Totals.** One line adding the funnels up: retrieved, survived, partial, dropped.
4. `## Deferred verdicts`: a bullet per deferred family: what ruled out each point, by ID, what its partials suggest as direction only, and the evidence that would settle it. With none deferred, one sentence saying so that names the verdict resting on a single surviving point.
5. `## Signal caveats`: two or three bullets on the pool as a whole, each counted by ID: offers against ranges, mandate against voluntary postings, one source or one stage behind most points, what stale or off-basis points removed. Said once, never repeated in a row.
6. **Where a quick read goes wrong.** Two or three sentences on the verdicts a fast reading would get wrong, and the point that decides each. No raise, band change or hire is recommended.

## 5. When the request is not a band benchmark

Pricing one person, looking up what a role earns, designing or redrawing bands, auditing pay equity, or any paste with no classified external evidence: the reply is a short note of four brief paragraphs, under 200 words, with no table, no figures and no advice beyond what to send. It carries the same title, `# Band benchmark: <Company>`, and its first sentence says the request is not a band benchmark. It says what was asked and why each ask is different work, and where it belongs; why the paste cannot carry a benchmark (no classified points, and a roster neither confirms nor rebuts that someone is underpaid); and exactly what to send: external points with ID, source, type, date, title, metro, stage and figure; bands with minimum, midpoint and maximum per family and level; the data window; the company's recent offers.

## Before you reply

For every input, a request that is not a benchmark included, you first use read_reference to open `references/casebook.md` (invented companies, for form and judgment only; every fact comes from the input). Then you reply. The reply is the document itself, pasted as it stands into the compensation lead's report: its first characters are `# Band benchmark: `, for a benchmark and for a redirect note alike, with no announcement such as "Now I will produce the benchmark table", no remark about the skill, the casebook or your method, and no `---` rule before the title.
