# Finished replies: the chocolate maker and the hearing-instrument maker, and a note for an ask with no chart

Every company, city, figure and date here is invented. The first two replies are what the reader received once the sheets in `references/sounding-sheets.md` were closed; the third is the note for a request that needs no sheet. They show form and size only. What carries over to a live reply:
- a `## ` title naming the company and its verdicts in words, then an opening paragraph: the home metro and round, the opening month and what it struck, the rows setting no price, and every conversion written as a sum with its direction;
- two to four argued paragraphs, firmest call first, each opening on what decides it, with accepted offers named revealed preference and posted ranges aspirational the first time each is weighed, volunteered ranges called partial, a partial's lean given in a clause, and hearsay named as such;
- one table whose gap cell shows its sum with the anchor's IDs and whose funnel counts all four piles by ID, then a `Pool:` line copied from the sheet's totals;
- Deferred Verdicts, always present, and Signal Caveats said once for the pool, naming families and IDs rather than tallying kinds;
- a last line answering the loudest figure that was dropped;
- for a request outside the chart, a short note giving a judgment, what the paste shows, and what to send.

No company, city, job title, figure or date carries over. Each comes from the request in hand, never from this file.

## Reply three: a pay-equity check and a band redesign with no market evidence

What the operations manager at Kettlebrook Laundry Co. sent. Kettlebrook is a Series A commercial laundry with 85 staff in Tulsa OK. "Can you check whether press operators at our east and west plants are paid fairly against each other, and add a lead-operator step to the press-operator band? Our roster by name and our bands are below; we have no outside numbers." Bands given (minimum / midpoint / maximum): Press Operator $33,800 / $36,700 / $40,000; Route Driver $38,900 / $42,300 / $46,100; Plant Supervisor $52,400 / $56,900 / $62,000. The roster names 31 people across the two plants. No evidence table came with it, so no sheet was kept.

The note:

## Kettlebrook Laundry Co.: a pay-equity check and a band redesign, not a benchmark

You asked whether press operators at the east and west plants are paid fairly against each other, and for a lead-operator step to be added to the press-operator band. A benchmark can settle neither. It places role-family bands against classified outside evidence, and none came with the request; comparing pay between plants is a pay-equity review, which needs each person's record and the reasons behind each rate; and a new step is band design, which belongs to whoever owns the pay structure.

What the paste does show, read by family and level rather than by name: press operators, route drivers and plant supervisors spread across two plants, each family on a single band. A roster shows what Kettlebrook pays; it cannot show what Tulsa's market pays for the same work, which is the question a benchmark answers.

To benchmark the Press Operator, Route Driver and Plant Supervisor bands, send:
- the evidence, one line per point: its source, accepted offer or posted range, the month, the role and its level, the metro, the employer's funding round, the figure with its pay basis, and whether a range was posted under a law or by choice;
- the home metro and any roles with no metro tie, the months the evidence may span, and the date it was pulled;
- Kettlebrook's own offers in that window, accepted or declined, with any reason given.

With those, each family gets a verdict, a gap and a confidence tier. The plant comparison and the new step stay with the people who own them.
