# The benchmark report

## Layout

1. `# <Company>: <the overall finding in a few words>`, then one line: stage and headquarters, evidence retrieval date, the window, and the basis compared (annual base, in each family's own currency).
2. `## Benchmark by role family`: one table, one row per role family and level, never per person. Columns: Role family and level | Band midpoint | Market anchor (what it is built from) | Gap | Verdict | Evidence funnel | Confidence and why. The funnel reads retrieved → excluded → incomparable → partial → comparable, with the IDs that survived.
3. `## How each verdict was reached`: one or two sentences per family with a verdict: the gap worked out, what the company's own offers say, and any partial point that pulls the other way. Name every excluded or dropped point once, with its reason, here or in the funnel.
4. `## Basis conversions`: every conversion, written with the input's own figures and its direction. Write "none needed" if there were none.
5. `## Deferred verdicts`: every family without a verdict: why (what failed and where), and exactly what evidence would settle it. Never leave this section out; write "none" if every family has a verdict.
6. `## Signal caveats`: skews that touch the whole pool (one dominant source, mostly posted ranges, mandate-driven ranges, a supplied exchange rate, thin recency), each stated once here and nowhere else.

Keep it lean: the table carries the verdicts, the prose carries only the reasons a reviewer would ask for.

## A worked report (same invented company as the ledger)

# Halcyon Grid Systems: Firmware and Toronto Data Platform lag the market; Solutions Consulting is on it; Field Service cannot be called yet

Series C, Denver CO; evidence retrieved 2026-11-02; points before November 2025 are out of window; compared on annual base, Data Platform in CAD.

## How each verdict was reached

- **Firmware Engineering.** ($157,400 − $164,700) ÷ $164,700 = −4.4%, between 3% and 5%, so it needed corroboration: our own accepted offers average $163,100, 3.6% above the midpoint, so the band lags. The Series D range (E-04) is partial and leans higher, as a later-stage company should. E-05, a recruiter's verbal figure, is excluded as unverifiable; the Colorado-mandated range E-03 (midpoint $168,100) is context, since offers survived.
- **Solutions Consulting.** The converted offer equals the midpoint, a gap of 0.0%; our own accepted offer at $138,600 sits 1.4% above it. E-06 quotes OTE with no base split, so it is excluded rather than guessed.
- **Data Platform (Toronto).** (C$184,600 − C$194,330) ÷ C$194,330 = −5.0%, at the 5% line, so the anchor decides. Our own accepted offer at C$189,700 is above the midpoint too. E-13 is a Seattle offer in USD for a Toronto team: incomparable on geography.

## Basis conversions

- E-07: $153,900 total cash less the stated $17,200 annual bonus = $136,700 base (down).
- E-14: $143,600 × the stated rate of 1.35 = C$193,860 base (up).

## Deferred verdicts

- **Field Service Operations, Manager.** E-10 predates the window, E-08 is a contractor's hourly rate, and E-09 is two steps off (a Series B company and a voluntarily posted range). To settle it: an accepted offer, or a range published under a pay-transparency mandate, for a Field Service Operations Manager at a Denver Series C company, dated November 2025 or later.

## Signal caveats

- Thirteen of the fourteen points come from one feed.
- Mandated ranges are wide; with comparable offers in hand they served as context, not anchors.
- One Toronto offer depends on the input's own exchange rate of 1.35.
