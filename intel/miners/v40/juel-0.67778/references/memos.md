# From a sheet to a memo

The memo goes to an HR lead who will be questioned on it: 550 to 750 words about role families only, one analyst talking to another. Its facts are the sheet's and no others: the paragraphs are the Say lines put in order and joined into prose, with the figures from their own lines. Point ids, checks and steps stay on the sheet; a point is named the way a reader would know it, as an accepted offer at a Series C company, $161,000. "Paid" and "hired" belong to outside companies unless the sentence names the company itself, and the company's name is used for its own bands and its own offers only.

- `# <Company>: ` and the sheet's title line, in its verdict words.
- An opening paragraph: the city and round the bands are held to, the first month in and what it shuts out, if it shuts anything out, and which rows are not prices, if any are. A line the sheet does not have gets no sentence here, not even to say there is none.
- Two to four paragraphs, no headings, from the firmest call to the weakest. Families decided by the same kind of evidence share a paragraph, and each paragraph opens on what decides it. A conversion shows its sum and direction. Only a family the sheet defers is called deferred; a Low one has its verdict, and is said to rest on one posting. A partial point is worth a clause for its lean, and an open P1 or P2 role a clause where it raises what hangs on the call.
- `## <Company>'s benchmark`: a table, Family and level | Verdict | Midpoint against anchor | Evidence funnel | Confidence, one row per family in snapshot order. Confidence is the tier and its deciding reason in a few words.
- `## Deferred verdicts for <Company>`, always there: each deferred family, what came close, and the evidence that would settle it (kind, city, round, level). With none deferred, that is said, then the verdict resting on least and what would firm it.
- `## Signal caveats on <Company>'s pool`: two to four sentences on the pool as a whole, from the sheet's closing lines, repeating nothing. A gap is called close only if the sheet lists it as close.
- A last line on the number somebody will quote, the loudest of the dropped figures, and the answer to it.

Every figure is in the paste or worked from it in view. No advice on what to pay.

Two invented memos follow. Larkmead's was written from the sheet you have seen; Tolka's comes from a shorter pool in euro. How they open, which families they put together and what the paragraphs dwell on differ, because the pools did.

## Larkmead

# Larkmead Mobility: three bands lag Columbus, Depot Scheduling is above market, Route Science is deferred

Larkmead is held to Columbus and Series C. August 2025 is the first month in. It lets in a Fleet Planning range from that very month and shuts out a $103,000 Fleet Planning offer from July, which would have been the best thing that family had. A contractor's $71 an hour, an agency's word on a Depot Scheduling salary and a Partner Accounts range quoted as on-target pay are not prices at all.

Telematics Engineering is the call to trust. Two outside companies in Columbus, at the same round, hired leads at $161,000 and $158,000, so the price is $159,500 and the $149,500 midpoint is 6.3% under it. The range advertised beside them centres on $160,500 and adds only its agreement. Larkmead's own three hires, two at $154,500 and one at $156,000, were above its midpoint already, and a P1 role is open. The Series F range was left out; three rounds is a different employer.

Partner Accounts has one price. The price came as $146,500 in total; less the stated $24,000 bonus it is $122,500 base, down, and the $117,500 midpoint is 4.1% under it. A Series B range centres on $118,500, lower as an earlier round usually is, so it says little. Larkmead had $116,000 turned down, which is a fact; the reason given is only what the candidate said.

The last two calls rest on advertising. Fleet Planning has two Columbus ranges that agree, centred on $97,500 and $100,500, and against their $99,000 average $94,500 is 4.5% under; Larkmead's own hire at $98,500 makes that easier to believe. Depot Scheduling is called, and only just: a single posting centred on $72,000 leaves $75,000 at 4.2% over, above market and Low; a remote range from an earlier company missed twice and was left out, and Larkmead's offer list has no line for this family, which is a gap to fill. Route Science is deferred. The Series D offer at $214,500 is higher and the Series B range at $187,500 lower, as those rounds usually are, and neither is a price at Larkmead's round, which leaves an open P2 role without a market.

## Deferred verdicts for Larkmead

Route Science, Principal. The Series D offer and the Series B range came closest and stopped a round short on either side. An accepted offer for a principal at a Columbus Series C company would settle it, or two such postings that agree.

## Signal caveats on Larkmead's pool

One feed supplied nearly every point, so nothing outside it checks them. Two verdicts stand on ranges alone. One range was posted because a law asked for it, one was volunteered and eight do not say, so how far they flatter cannot be told. Two families have no offer of Larkmead's own to set against the outside reading, and the 3% line is a convention.

Somebody will quote $262,000 for a route scientist. That is the ceiling of a Cincinnati range, and Larkmead does not hire this job in Cincinnati.

## Tolka

# Tolka Instruments: Firmware is competitive, Field Applications lags, Calibration is above market

Tolka is held to Dublin and Series B, and two of its three families have a price there. The other points are Dublin ranges or come from somewhere else, one of them in pounds with no rate supplied.

Firmware and Field Applications each have one hire behind them. A Dublin company at the same round paid €78,500 for a firmware lead in March, and Tolka's €77,000 is 1.9% under, inside the 3% convention; a range centred on €78,250 agrees, and Tolka's own hire at €76,500 was just below its midpoint. Another paid €66,500 for a senior field applications engineer, which leaves €64,000 at 3.8% under: lagging, and close enough to the line that a second offer could move it. A remote range with no city centres on €66,000 and leans the same way without counting. The London firmware range was left out, for the place and for the currency.

Calibration is a reading and no more. One Dublin range centres on €46,000, which leaves the €49,000 midpoint 6.5% over: above market, and Low, because a single advertisement is all there is. A Cork range was left out for its city.

## Deferred verdicts for Tolka

None is deferred. Calibration rests on least, a single Dublin posting; an accepted offer from a Dublin Series B company at senior level would firm it or overturn it.

## Signal caveats on Tolka's pool

Tolka's one hire in a year tests Firmware and nothing else. Two verdicts rest on one outside hire each and the third on one advertisement, so nothing here is High.

Somebody will quote £81,000 for a firmware lead. That was the top of a London range, and nobody has said what London money is worth in Dublin.

Both memos start at their title and say nothing of sheets or examples. The sheet is written, so your next message is the memo for the real company and nothing else: its first characters are `# ` and the company's name.
