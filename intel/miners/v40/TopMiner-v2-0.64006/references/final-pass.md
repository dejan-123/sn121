With the worksheet complete, write the report as your final message. It goes to the requester as a finished document: bring across the figures, IDs and dates you worked out, and none of the working. Line one is `# <Company>: role-family benchmark`, with nothing above it (no rule, no preface).

## Sections, top to bottom

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| Opening (no heading) | One or two sentences: the company, the number of external points retrieved (the evidence table's row count) and the number of role families, then the headline of what lags, what is competitive or above market, and what is deferred. Leave the full / partial / dropped split to the table. |
| `## Benchmark table` | One row per family and level, in snapshot order, columns \| Role family / level \| Band mid \| Funnel \| Anchor (basis) \| Gap \| Verdict \| Confidence: deciding reason \|. |
| `## Verdict notes` | Two to four plain sentences per row. |
| `## Deferred verdicts` | Per deferred row, what failed and the evidence that would close it; one line if none. |
| `## Signal caveats` | Two to four bullets, each one pool-wide skew stated once. |
| Close (no heading) | One short paragraph on what to act on first. |

## How each part is filled

**Table cells.** Funnel is written "4 retrieved → 2 full (1 offer, 1 range), 1 partial, 1 dropped". The anchor shows its value, basis and IDs, such as "$170,000 (mean of 2 offers: A-01, A-03)", "$160,000 (1 offer: A-01)" or "$100,000 (1 range midpoint: A-06)"; one anchor never mixes offers with ranges. A deferred row has "—" as anchor and gap.

**Verdict notes.** Start each note with what settles the row (the offers behind the anchor, the drop that changed it, the boundary it sits by, the gap that defers it), so no two notes open alike. Then give the anchor's IDs and why they qualify; for an offer anchor, whether a full range agrees, and that it stays out of the anchor; each dropped or partial point by ID, with all its reasons; any conversion, with its direction; and finally, as a separate read on where the company pays now, this family's own offers or declines (never another family's) and whether they agree. Flag a boundary case in a few words, and say when a row rests on a single point.

**Deferred verdicts.** The sourcing brief names role, level, metro, stage, type (accepted offer preferred) and window.

**Signal caveats.** Use this pool's own counts. Candidates: one dominant source; ranges outnumbering offers, an aspirational lean; a thin offer sample; a mix of stages or metros; which ranges the Notes column marks as posted under a pay-transparency mandate and which as voluntary, which are self-selected and lean toward employers proud of their pay. Give mandate status as the Notes state it, neither adding to it nor disputing it from memory. Never repeat a row note here.

**Close.** Order by the open-role priority tiers, a lagging band on a critical-path role first. Do not propose new band figures.

Throughout: role family and level only, never individual employees, and no figure that is not in the input.

# Ashgrove Biologics: role-family benchmark

Ashgrove Biologics (Series B, Salt Lake City UT) has 10 external points retrieved across three role families. Assay Development lags the market on two accepted offers, Facilities is competitive on one posted range, and Process Engineering is deferred.

## Verdict notes

**Assay Development, Senior.** Two accepted offers from Salt Lake City Series B companies carry the anchor: A-01 at $160,000 (2026-03) and A-03 at $180,000 (2026-06), mean $170,000, so the band midpoint sits 11.8% below. A-02, a full posted range of $150,000–$190,000 (midpoint $170,000), points the same way but stays out of the anchor because offers exist. A-04 is dropped as a Phoenix posting, another metro. Ashgrove's own two recent hires both came in above the midpoint, which matches the external lag.

**Facilities, Manager.** One aspirational range is all this row has, so it is Low. A-06, posted at $80,000–$120,000 (midpoint $100,000), is the only full point and puts the band 4.0% below. A-05, a Series C posting one rung up, stays as a partial that overstates the market. A-07, an accepted offer dated before the cutoff, is dropped.

**Process Engineering, Staff.** Nothing survives in full, so there is no verdict. A-08, a remote posting with no metro, is a partial and cannot anchor. A-09 is dropped as an OTE figure with no base split, and A-10 as a Public-company range.

## Deferred verdicts

- **Process Engineering, Staff:** no fully comparable point survived. To close it: an accepted offer, or else a posted base range, for a Staff process engineer at a Salt Lake City Series B company, dated inside the window.

## Signal caveats

- 8 of the 10 points come from one job board, so the source mix is narrow.
- Ranges outnumber offers 6 to 3, so the pool leans aspirational. The Notes column marks two postings as made under a transparency mandate and the rest as voluntary, which are self-selected.

Assay Development is the critical-path hire and the clearest lag, so it comes first. Facilities needs one more comparable point before anyone acts on it.
