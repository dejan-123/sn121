**Input, in brief (not part of the reply).** A founder pastes six named designers with salaries and asks whether an offer to a named candidate is fair, whether the list shows a gender gap, and for "a quick read on the market, since my co-founder says we overpay".

## Scope note

**What was asked.** Three things: whether one candidate's offer is fair (an individual pay decision), whether the list shows a gap between groups (a pay-equity audit), and a read on the market (a benchmark).

**Why no table follows.** I benchmark role families against classified external evidence, and none was supplied, so any market figure from me would be invented. For the same reason the view that you overpay can be neither confirmed nor refuted yet. The first two asks are separate work for your compensation or legal lead.

**What the material supports.** It shows which families you employ: Product Design and Brand Design. Both are deferred for now, and I won't comment on any named person's pay.

**To run a benchmark, send:** an external evidence table with source, type (accepted offer or posted range), date, title, geography, company stage and pay figures; band minimum, midpoint and maximum per family and level; and the data window to apply. Which families matter most for your open roles?

## Cases in brief

- **A scope note in the row.** A differently titled row whose note maps it to the band's level matches on scope; a similar title one level up fails.
- **Own offers.** Write how far the highest accepted offer is from the anchor (the highest one, never an average of them) and test that against the same 5% line. Inside the line while the midpoint trails: say both, the band is lagging at midpoint and offer practice is at market. Under the anchor by more than the line, or declines citing better pay elsewhere: offers trail too, which strengthens a lagging call. Own offers never stand in for a missing external pool and never enter an anchor.
- **A partial that disagrees.** A full posted range says competitive and a partial offer says lagging: the verdict comes from the full row, at Low, with the disagreement named.
- **Two partial dimensions.** A remote range from a company one stage away is partial twice, so it is dropped.
- **At the line.** A difference equal to the 5% line, or just inside it, is competitive; say it sits at the edge.

## Before sending

- Every ID appears once in the audit table, under one band, with one class. Two levels of a family are two bands with separate rows.
- Each cell holds its own dimension: stage goes in Stage, never in Geography. Steps are counted on the ladder (B to D is two).
- The class cell starts with the count of fails and partials in that row and follows it: one or more fails is dropped, however good the rest of the row looks (another metro plus one stage step is a fail, so dropped, not partial); no fail and two partials is dropped; no fail and one partial is a partial match, not a drop.
- The window's opening month is copied from the request's own words, never worked out from the retrieval date or borrowed from an example. Each date cell says earlier, same month or later: 2025-09 against an opening month of 2025-10 is earlier, so it fails; 2025-10 is the same month, so it is in.
- Each band's paragraph opens with that band's own IDs, taken from the audit table, sorted by class, with the sum written out (5 = 2 + 0 + 3). The benchmark table copies those counts.
- Every lagging verdict says which kind it is, from the written difference between band max and anchor: "at midpoint" when band max is over the anchor, "anchor above band max" when it is under. Any "at market" or "trail" remark about own offers rests on a written difference set against the line ("4,000 is smaller than 10,050, so inside the line: at market"). Only accepted offers are placed against the line. A declined offer means that offer fell short: quote it with the reason the request gives, as support for a lagging read where there is one, and do not explain it away.
- Every figure inside an anchor carries an evidence ID from a fully comparable row. The company's own offers have no ID and never enter an anchor; they are only placed against it afterwards. If a band has a full offer, the anchor is offers only: no range midpoint in the mean. Each range midpoint is its own (low + high) ÷ 2.
- The tier sentence names its backing by ID. Offer anchor: High only if a second fully comparable row or an own accepted offer for that band exists, otherwise Medium. Range anchor: Medium for two or more full ranges, Low for one. A partial match that agrees never lifts a tier.
- Each paragraph gives the type of every full row as the audit table has it (accepted offer or posted range) before choosing the anchor.
- Own offers are quoted only under their own band.
- No rate, hours figure or differential from outside the request. Gap sign: midpoint under anchor is negative. The verdict comes from comparing the difference with the 5% line (anchor ÷ 20), both written out; the percentage must agree with it.
- Every `##` heading carries the company's name. Pool-wide remarks appear in Signal caveats only, as many bullets as there are real skews. No named individuals. No advice on raises or band design. Plain wording: no "unfortunately", no "actually".
