# Verdict and confidence

Use this for close calls, conflicting survivors, and deciding whether to defer.

## What each kind of evidence can carry

| Surviving evidence | What it shows | What it cannot show |
|---|---|---|
| Accepted offer | a price at which a hire actually closed | the spread of the market; one offer is one negotiation |
| Posted range | what an employer is prepared to advertise | where inside the range hires land |
| Partial match | direction, once its known bias is allowed for | a reference level |

An offer is a revealed price. A posted range is a stated intention, and hires commonly close in the lower half to middle of it. That is why an offer outranks a range of equal comparability, and why a pool of ranges alone cannot support the top confidence tier.

## Building the reference

- One fully comparable offer: that figure.
- Several: the median. Give the low and high as well when they are more than a few percent apart.
- Offers and ranges both fully comparable: the offers set the reference. Then check where the offers fall inside the ranges. Offers in the middle of the ranges mean the evidence agrees. Offers at or beyond an edge mean the ranges are aspirational or stale; say which.
- Ranges only: midpoint of each, then the median of midpoints. Report the reference as approximate.
- A fully comparable range and a partial-match offer that disagree: the range sets the reference, because the offer's unmatched dimension may explain the whole difference. State the direction that dimension should push the offer and whether the disagreement fits it.

Never build a reference from a mean across fully comparable and partial rows together, and never include a dropped row "for context".

## Reading the gap

Compare the band midpoint with the reference, as a percentage of the reference. The midpoint is the band's intended price for a fully competent incumbent, which is what the market figure represents.

- A gap inside 5% is competitive. Evidence this thin cannot resolve finer differences.
- Check the band edges as well. A reference above the band maximum means new hires cannot be made inside the band at all: lagging, with urgency. A reference below the band minimum means the band starts above market.
- A gap between 4% and 6% is marginal. Say so, and use the internal signals to settle it:
  - own accepted offers clustering above midpoint: the band is already being stretched, which supports lagging
  - own declined offers where the candidate cited pay: supports lagging
  - own offers closing at or below midpoint without difficulty: supports competitive
  - no internal signal: keep the arithmetic result and label it marginal
- Internal signals settle marginal calls only. They do not create a verdict where no external row survives, and they do not overturn a clear gap.

State the verdict in the band's currency and for the level in the snapshot. If a family has two levels, each gets its own row and its own funnel.

## When to defer

Defer when a verdict would say more than the surviving rows do.

1. **Empty pool.** Every row was excluded or incomparable.
2. **Single partial match.** One row with an unknown bias is not a position.
3. **Partial matches that disagree.** One implies lagging and another competitive or above-market. Typical case: one row a stage earlier and one a stage later, with the band between them. The rows bracket the band; they do not locate it.
4. **Fully comparable ranges that straddle a threshold.** One range's midpoint puts the band inside 5%, another puts it outside, and no offer survives to settle it.
5. **No internal anchor.** The family has evidence but no band, or the band's basis or currency cannot be reconciled with any surviving row.

Two or more partial matches that agree in direction, each biased the way its unmatched dimension predicts, can carry a Low-confidence verdict. Say that it rests on partial matches only.

A deferred entry contains: the family and level; the funnel for it; the precise reason from the list above; and what would unlock a verdict, stated as evidence someone could go and get. Good: "one accepted offer for this level, same metro, same stage, dated inside the window". Weak: "more data". Do not add a lean such as "probably lagging"; that is a verdict by another name.

Deferral is not a failure of the analysis. A wrong direction costs the reader more than a named gap.

## Confidence tiers

| Tier | Requires | Typical deciding reason |
|---|---|---|
| High | two or more fully comparable rows that agree, at least one an accepted offer | "offer and range agree" |
| Medium | one fully comparable accepted offer; or two or more fully comparable ranges that agree | "single offer", "ranges only" |
| Low | one fully comparable range; or two or more agreeing partial matches | "one posted range", "partial matches only" |
| Deferred | any deferral condition | the condition itself |

Adjust down one tier, never up, when: the verdict is marginal; the deciding row is a boundary row at the edge of the window; or more than half of the family's retrieved rows were dropped. Give the reason for the tier in a few words beside it. An offer and a range agree when the offer sits inside the range; two ranges agree when their midpoints lead to the same verdict. Figures do not have to be identical.

## Pool-wide caveats

A caveat belongs in the closing caveats section only if it tilts the whole pool or a whole evidence type. State each once, with the direction of the tilt. Common ones:

- every row comes from one source or one retrieval
- the pool is mostly posted ranges, with few closed offers
- all ranges are voluntary because the jurisdiction has no mandate, so they lean high
- mandated ranges in the pool are wide, so midpoints are soft
- survivors lean toward later-stage or earlier-stage employers
- a large share of the pool was dropped, and for what main reason
- the retrieval date sits late in the window, so older rows are close to expiry

A condition affecting one row or one family goes in that family's rationale or the drop log, not here.

## Before sending

- Each verdict cites only row IDs that survived for that family.
- Funnel counts add up for each family and for the pool.
- Every conversion is written out with its direction.
- No figure appears that is not in the input or derived from it on the page.
- No named individual appears anywhere.
- Each deferred family names evidence that could be collected.
