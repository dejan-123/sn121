# The redirect note

Some requests arrive without what a benchmark needs, or ask for work a benchmark does not do. The usual shapes: a payroll export or roster of named people with no external evidence; a question about one person's raise, offer or counteroffer; a request to add, merge or redesign levels in a band; a pay-equity audit; a bare "what does the market pay" with nothing classified behind it. A table built from such input would look like a benchmark and be a fabrication, so the reply is a short note instead.

## What the note does

It is written to the person who asked, in plain sentences, 180 to 280 words, under a `## ` title that names the company and says what the request needs. Four short paragraphs:

1. **What came in and why it cannot be benchmarked yet.** Name what was asked and what was supplied. Explain the gap in one or two sentences: salaries of current staff show who stayed, not what the market pays, because people paid well below market tend to leave quietly; without classified external evidence there is nothing to hold a band against. A deadline changes when evidence is needed, not what counts as evidence.
2. **What belongs elsewhere.** Each ask that is not a benchmark, and the work it belongs to: redesigning levels is job-architecture work (defining what changes in scope between levels), one person's pay is a manager-and-HR decision drawing on performance, internal equity and budget, an equity audit is its own analysis. One sentence each, no lecture.
3. **What the paste does show.** Only what is internal and only in aggregate, by role and level: how many people sit at each level, the spread within a level, whether the current structure has the levels the request mentions. Never a person's name or a person's salary, and never a claim about the market.
4. **What to send so the benchmark can run.** Classified external points, each with its type (accepted offer or posted range, kept apart), date, title and level, metro or remote anchoring, the employer's funding stage, the figure and its basis (base, total, on-target), and source; the internal snapshot by role family and level with band minimum, midpoint and maximum rather than names; the company's own recent offers, accepted and declined; open roles and their priority; and the window the evidence should fall in. Close by saying what comes back: a verdict, an evidence funnel and a confidence tier for each family, or a deferral where the evidence is too thin.

No table, no bullets, no market figure the input does not contain, no promise of a date.

## An invented example

Request: a COO at Brannock Assay Labs (Series A, 40 staff, Omaha) pastes a list of twelve lab staff with names and salaries, asks whether two senior chemists are underpaid compared with "the market", asks for the chemist band to be split into four levels, and needs it all for an investor update on Friday.

## Brannock Assay Labs: what this request needs before a benchmark

You asked whether your senior chemists are paid below market, for the chemist band to become four levels, and for both by Friday, and you sent a list of current staff and their salaries. That list cannot answer the market question. Salaries of the people who stayed describe the survivors, since chemists paid well below market tend to leave quietly, and nothing in it says what other Omaha employers at your stage pay. Friday changes when evidence is needed, not what counts.

Splitting the band into four levels is a job-architecture question: what changes in scope, autonomy and expertise from one level to the next, which no market figure can decide. Whether either chemist should get a raise is a decision for their manager and HR, weighing performance and internal equity alongside any benchmark.

What the list does show is internal: the chemist family has three levels today, with most people at the middle one, and the senior level spans a narrow range. That describes your structure, not your position against the market.

To benchmark, send classified external points for chemists at each level, keeping accepted offers apart from posted ranges, each with its date, title, level, metro, the employer's funding stage, the figure and whether it is base or total pay; your band minimum, midpoint and maximum by level; your recent offers, accepted and declined; and the window to use. Each family then comes back with a verdict, an evidence funnel and a confidence tier, or a deferral where the evidence is too thin.
