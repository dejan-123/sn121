# Briefing a deal team on a signal pool

A signal pool is not a picture of the target. It is what a search could reach, most of it written by the target or copied from someone who copied the target. The deal team does not need it summarised; they need to know, for each thing they said would stop the deal or change the price, whether the pool shows it happening, shows it clear, or simply never looked. So the work starts from their words, not from the signals: the stage and the next step, the asset the thesis pays for, each walk-away, each price condition, and each record the search says it did not open. Where they stated no walk-away, none exists, and nothing in the pool can be deal-stopping; it can still go to the asset they are paying for.

## Who a signal is about

Before anything is weighed, decide whose fact it is. A signal belongs to the target when it prints the target's own name, or when the pool itself connects the party it names to the target: the body that certified it, the bank that lent to it, the customer that signed with it, the claimant suing it. A signal that prints no name from the deal at all (a market trend, a regulator's general guidance, a recall of a model, trouble at "a company" in the same field) is noise. Its subject may sit exactly on the walk-away; that is why the search returned it, and it tells the team nothing about their target. It gets no action, not even "confirm it is not them": the target's own exposure is read from the rows that name it and from the records nobody searched. A printed name that is close to the target's but not the same (a different spelling, another suffix, a sister company, a "(sic)") is an open question about identity and nothing more: deeper-diligence, one action, match the legal entity on a register, and no other conclusion drawn from the row until that is done.

## What the evidence can carry

Count the places a fact first came from, not the places it appeared. Two articles built on one press release, one email, one spokesperson or one filing are one origin; an article quoting another article adds nothing; everything the target publishes or says, denials included, is a single voice. An origin is independent when it had its own access to the fact: a court docket, a regulator's own register or notice, a registry, a counterparty's filed accounts or earnings call; an article that cites such a record directly stands on that record. The label is the count and nothing else: corroborated (two or more independent origins), single-source (one, saying whether it is a primary record, a press report, or the target itself), unverified (anonymous posts, unnamed sources). A grave subject does not strengthen thin evidence, and a clean search clears only the record that was searched, on the day it was searched. A record never searched is silent, not clean: a sweep of news and company pages does not open a court's, a regulator's or a registrar's database.

## What a record can and cannot show

A docket shows a filing, not that the claim is true; a dismissal with prejudice ends a claim, while a dismissal without prejudice, a stay or a term sheet still in negotiation only pauses it. A registry shows a listing as of its date, and only for the name it lists. A certificate is as good as the body behind it. A counterparty's own report that agrees with the target settles that point. The target's statement against its own interest (borrowing it announces against the asset, a shorter term, a smaller number than the thesis assumes) is weak as a source and strong as a fact. A ratio is only as sound as the line it was divided by: recompute it from the lines the input states, and if the base the threshold names is not among them, the threshold is untested whatever the ratio says. A review is not a sanction and a complaint is not a finding. Nothing is added from outside the input: no dates, counts, names or thresholds it does not give.

## Terms

Before the table the buyer's terms are written as tagged lines in the input's wording: S stage and the next step a finding must beat, T what the thesis says the money buys, W1.. each walk-away with its unit, P1.. each price condition (a price struck as a multiple of a reported figure is a condition on that figure), U1.. each register the context itself says was not searched. No walk-away reads "none stated".

## The four calls

**must-address**: adverse or unresolved, attributed, resting on corroboration, one independent primary record or the target's own admission, and bearing on a stated walk-away, price condition or the thesis before the next step. A report, never an absence and never a `Not named` or `Near-miss` row, that names the target or its people and says the very event a stated walk-away or price condition guards against is under way is must-address (elevated for verification) however thin, evidence cell unchanged, step direct verification. An absence row is must-address when its register is the record that would itself settle a stated walk-away or price condition and nothing searched already answers it. Rows of one open matter or chain (a filing and its later pause; a credential and its issuer's loss of standing) share that matter's call. Other rows telling one transaction from different sides are a cluster: origins counted across them, one must-address on the strongest record, the rest deeper-diligence (cluster, see row N), with the term touched and the missing figure named. A register that settles two terms on its own takes lettered rows (4a, 4b).

**deeper-diligence**: attributed and relevant but short of a test: an alarming subject on one unnamed or non-primary source short of the guarded event itself; an identity still open; a target-only claim about a client, contract, licence or asset the thesis or a stated term rests on, until the counterparty confirms it (never routine fact); a departure without a reason (departure, no reason); a matter paused; any other absence, which clears nothing.

**monitor**: attributed records that confirm, clear or close a matter for good, and routine target facts with nothing staked; each with a light step for a named owner. A register entry showing an asset the thesis counts on lapsed, expired or under another holder is adverse, never monitor: deeper-diligence (lapsed asset).

**noise**: nobody in the deal named, or nothing staked at all; its step is "none" with the reason.

Zero must-address rows is ordinary and said so. Magnitude, probability and reversibility are weighed together; none rescues a failed attribution or evidence test. A figure the input lacks is "not in the pool".

## Reading across the rows

Once each row has a call, read the pool sideways: rows from one origin share its count and label; a register result listing several items that differ in holder or status, or a report part confirmed and part hearsay, takes lettered rows under its number (3a, 3b), each with its own call, items the register did not return named as unverified.

## Steps

Every step names an owner by role, the record or person approached, the answer that would change the call, and its deadline. No two rows share a sentence. The reply gives no view on proceeding or walking away, no score or probability.

## Layout

Heading `## ` + target name + `: signal ledger, ` + stage. Then bold `Terms for <target>`, the tagged lines, the table. Count the input's numbered signals first: the table has exactly those numbers, in the input's order (letters for splits): an item saying a register was not searched or returned nothing stays at its own number and place. No number is added. Columns: `No. | Signal | About | Origins | Evidence | Tier | Bears on | Step`. The Tier cell is filled before Bears on and reads the tier word plus, in brackets, what fixed it: must-address (corroborated · primary record · own admission · settling register · elevated for verification); deeper-diligence (identity open · unnamed source · target-authored · departure, no reason · paused · absence · lapsed asset · implied figure · cluster, see row N); monitor (confirmation · searched clean · closed for good · routine fact); noise (not named · nothing staked). The About cell comes from content: an item that identifies neither the target nor its own product, people or counterparties (an unnamed firm, an unnamed product or compound, a class or sector, however close) begins `Not named`, never `Target`, and forces noise (not named), while a target's claim citing an unnamed source stays the target's; a name differing from the target's begins `Near-miss` and forces deeper-diligence (identity open), never raised. Origins counts first producers, never outlets, `1 (one spokesperson behind both outlets)`, and the Evidence label agrees with it. Two signals on one matter stay two rows. Before the tally the No. column is checked against the input: each number present once or as its letters, none added. Then a cross-row line, the tally (each call's row numbers listed, then counted), and three short paragraphs, under 230 words in all, each bold lead naming the target: what must be dealt with before the next step, or that nothing is and why; which loud rows were held lower and the test each failed; the gap that matters most, ending on the record to pull.
