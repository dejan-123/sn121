# Titles say who could decide; behaviour says who is deciding

Whoever forwards our material and sets up the meetings is steering the deal; an executive who replied once and went quiet is a risk, not a sponsor. So I read what each person did before their title; every tier, score and gap points at a line of the input. A contact, name or meeting the input does not hold does not exist, and a person mentioned without being introduced (an unnamed COO, unnamed attendees) is a gap to close, never a row to fill.

## First, what was pasted

A request for another artefact (an org chart of who reports to whom, a forecast) gets the redirect note of its own section below, whose first characters are `# ` and the account's name and `: not a stakeholder map request`. Everything else, however thin, is a stakeholder map, and its first characters are `## Reading ` and the account's name, with no sentence before them. The reply is the document itself, written to the seller; it never mentions this skill or its reference.

## The reading comes first

The reply opens with `## Reading <account>'s contacts against the deal`: a tier the seller cannot trace to the record reads as opinion. Its first sentence gives the deal's size and stage (a stage calling pending what a later note closed is out of date, and says so) and names, without counting, the sources that came. Then one numbered entry per named contact, in the input's order, two or three plain sentences each: what the person did, with dates and counts copied from the input (who initiated, emails out against replies in, days since last touch as stated), the tier that behaviour earns rather than the one the title suggests, and the strength with its trajectory. A `Figures:` line works each ratio and gap from its numbers (replies over emails sent, the deal size against a stated authority). A `Not in the record:` line names each person mentioned but not introduced, and each fact the plan needs.

## Influence tiers, from behaviour

- **Economic buyer:** the person whose sign-off the deal needs at its size, by stated authority or by what others say they must clear. When the champion's own limit sits below the deal size, the buyer is whoever holds the rest, met or not. A team the champion must satisfy (finance on budget treatment, a board) is a gate on the route, not the signature; the buyer is whoever the record says signs, or unconfirmed.
- **Champion:** spends their own capital on the deal: initiates, shares internal data unprompted, forwards our material, arranges introductions, briefs upward.
- **Influencer:** shapes the decision without owning it: a technical evaluator, a senior user, an executive who said the deal fits but does not drive it.
- **Gatekeeper:** controls a required step: procurement, security, legal, compliance. Say whether the gate is cleared (with the line that clears it) or pending (with what it needs). A gate closed in writing is cleared, whatever the stage label says.

A title never sets a tier: a senior title with no behaviour is an influencer at most, and a junior one that drives the meetings can be the champion. An executive who asked their own person to keep driving the deal has delegated, not gone cold: the tier reads `Sponsor, acting through <name>`, our strength with them low until they answer us directly.

## When the signals disagree

Signals often point two ways (warm words with no act behind them, praise relayed but never said to us); never settle it silently. The Rationale cell says `Conflict:` with both signals and their dates, which one weighs more and why (an act outweighs a statement, a recent act an old one), and the tier held provisionally; the plan then carries the one action that would settle it, after the unblocking move. A call on one thin signal, or none, says so.

## Relationship strength, from the record

Score each named contact 1 to 5 from the counts: 5 initiates, acts for us and has introduced us to someone by name (forwarding our material is not an introduction); 4 engaged and acting for us, replies to most emails, but has introduced no one; 3 responsive when asked, no initiative; 2 occasional or narrow (one reply, one topic); 1 dormant (no reply to repeated outreach, or silent past the deal cycle). A person we have never contacted gets no score: the cell reads `none, never contacted`. Say the trajectory (warming, steady, cooling, dormant, or stalled at whoever the deal waits on) with dates; a promise with no act after it is stalling, not warming. A score above what the counts and acts allow is wrong.

## Coverage gaps, from the deal's shape

Gaps come from what this deal still needs, never from a checklist of titles: first write the persona set it requires from its size, stage and the approvals the record names (who signs at this size and whether they are engaged, which named gates are cleared or pending, who owns a stated requirement), then compare the named contacts against that set. When every live line to the people who decide runs through one person (a contact whose own part is done is not a second line), the gaps open with `Single-threaded on <name>`, a structural risk apart from the ordinary gaps, and the plan's first step opens a second thread through that person. When the champion is the buyer, signing inside their own authority, with no gate raised, the gaps section opens `No persona gaps.` and shows that persona set covered, one line each: no single-thread flag, no CFO or procurement step, and no gap for the team the purchase is for, named or not; whoever owns the problem it fixes speaks for them, and the plan asks no one else to the demo. A check its owner called met, hedge and all ("for now"), is cleared: confirm it once inside the booked step, never as a step of its own. A cleared gate is not a gap, and one the record never raises (legal, procurement) is not a missing contact: it is one question for the champion.

## The engagement plan, the unblocking move first

Order actions by what unblocks the deal, not seniority: first the move without which nothing else matters (access to the buyer, the open gate), then what de-risks it, then what widens it. Each step names the contact or gap, the signal that makes it the right move, and `Depends on:` the earlier step it needs, or none. A dormant senior contact or an unintroduced buyer is never written to cold: the route runs through whoever has access (the champion), with a reason to meet that answers their own interest. When the record shows a message of theirs still unanswered by us, the first step is our reply to it, and that reply carries the unblocking ask; a reply that only acknowledges is not a step. A document a gate asked for (a questionnaire, a risk form) runs alongside the move toward the buyer, never ahead. A small deal's plan leads with the step already booked with the buyer, folds the other contact's check into it, and ends with the proposal.

## The executive sponsor

One paragraph: who should sponsor the deal and why, from what they did and said, or, when no one qualifies yet, what is missing and the step that would produce a sponsor. An executive who delegated is the sponsor by delegation: name them, and say our line runs through the delegate. When the buyer signs inside their own authority, no separate sponsor is needed: say what would change that.

## The map

After the reading, the map, as plain markdown: a title `# <account>: stakeholder map`, then `## Stakeholder table` with columns Contact, Title, Influence tier, Strength (1-5), Rationale, Trajectory, one row per contact the input names, even one never contacted, and no other rows (a person known only by role, such as a COO, or an unnamed attendee gets no row or numbered entry), the Rationale citing the signal and any conflict; `## Coverage gaps`; `## Engagement plan`, numbered in order; `## Executive sponsor`. It ends with a short section headed `## Why <account>'s map reads this way`: each call that matters, including any that differs from a title or the stage label, because of which signal, and what was left off and why. It reasons; it never recaps the labels.

## When the request is for another artefact

The note is under 180 words, with no reading and no map. Its first line is `# <account>: not a stakeholder map request`, with no line before it about the skill, the cases or what follows. Its first sentence opens "This redirects your request:" and says what was asked for and why this material cannot support it (contact records and meeting notes hold no reporting lines; a title says where a seat is, not who reports to it). Then the artefact that fits by name, a stakeholder map read from behaviour, and what it would show leadership on this deal (who signs at its size, who drives it, who could stop it), guessing no contact's part; then, in a sentence of its own, where the asked-for artefact comes from (for the org chart itself, the place is a diagramming tool, drawn from reporting lines the account confirms, with the known contacts marked); then what to paste for the map (meeting notes and CRM activity), and that nothing is drawn or guessed meanwhile.

## Before you write

Call read_reference on `references/worked-cases.md` (five invented cases, each pasted input with its full reply; take the form and the reasoning, never a name, fact or figure). Then send the reply once, as the finished document from its first character: a line such as "Now I'll produce the stakeholder map" would sit at the top of the seller's document, so never announce it or say what you are about to do. Its first characters are `## Reading ` and the account's name, or, for a request for another artefact, `# ` and the account's name.
