# Field notes: one worked map and three short cases

All names, figures and organisations here are invented for teaching. Reuse the reasoning, not the wording.

## Worked map

The record, summarised:

- City of Arnsby, a municipal government of about 3,000 staff. Deal: $430K a year for a building-permit processing platform. Stage: final evaluation; the deal sheet still says "accessibility audit pending".
- Gareth Imbo, Permits Program Manager: contacted us after a trade show, forwarded our process map to the planning office the same week, arranged our session with the city's digital team, and drafted the internal funding request himself. Sends a question or an update most weeks; answers within the day. Approval limit: $150K; anything larger goes to the Deputy City Manager.
- Rosalind Achebe, Deputy City Manager: opened the August steering meeting, called the project "overdue", and told Gareth "you own it from here". Our three emails since: one reply, a one-line thanks, five weeks ago.
- Pieter Valk, Digital Services Lead: ran the integration review and asked for our API limits, which we sent. Replies to most messages.
- Nkem Osterberg, Accessibility Officer: tested the public forms and wrote on the 9th: "Accessibility standards met, no further audit needed."
- Gareth mentions that "council finance will want to see the five-year cost" before approval. Nobody from council finance is named.
- Two people from the planning office sat in on the digital-team session; nobody introduced them.
- Nobody has mentioned who reviews the contract on the city's legal side.
- We still owe Gareth the five-year cost model he asked for last week.

How the map reads, as a note to the seller with no labels after the title:

**Stakeholder Map — City of Arnsby | $430K a year, final evaluation**

Gareth Imbo is carrying this deal for you, but $430K is above his $150K limit, so the decision rests with Rosalind Achebe, who handed the project to him in August and has barely written to you since. The accessibility audit is done: Nkem confirmed in writing that the standards are met, whatever the deal sheet says. The next move is yours, because the five-year cost model Gareth asked for last week is the same document council finance will want. The people line up like this.

| Name | Title | Role in this deal | Strength (0–5) | Direction | What they have done |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gareth Imbo | Permits Program Manager | Champion, below the approval line | 5 | Rising | Contacted us first; forwarded the process map to planning; arranged the digital-team session; drafted the funding request; limit $150K |
| Rosalind Achebe | Deputy City Manager | Approver; delegated to Gareth | 2 | Quiet | Opened the August steering meeting ("overdue"); told Gareth to own it; 3 emails from us, 1 one-line reply five weeks ago |
| Pieter Valk | Digital Services Lead | Technical reviewer | 3 | Steady | Ran the integration review; asked for API limits; replies to most messages |
| Nkem Osterberg | Accessibility Officer | Accessibility gate, closed | 3 | Done | Tested the public forms; wrote "Accessibility standards met, no further audit needed" on the 9th |

Council finance, who "will want to see the five-year cost", is a group without a named person in your notes, and the two planning-office people at the digital-team session were never introduced, so none of them has a row. No legal contact appears anywhere in the record.

Four things are still missing for a deal this size. This deal is single-threaded on Gareth: every line to the people who decide runs through him. Council finance has no face: approval waits on a cost review by people you cannot name, and the two planning-office people from the session are just as anonymous. Rosalind's attention is borrowed: she backs the project through Gareth, not through you, with one reply in five weeks. And nobody has said who reviews the contract, which a purchase this size will go through.

This is the order I would work in, since each move opens the way for the next:

1. Send Gareth the five-year cost model this week. He asked for it, it is overdue from you, and it is what council finance will review.
2. When he has it, ask Gareth to name and introduce the council finance reviewer and the two planning-office people from the session, and offer to walk the finance reviewer through the model with him in the room.
3. In the same conversation, ask Gareth to get you ten minutes with Rosalind at her next steering meeting this month, with him leading and you presenting the model. Do not write to Rosalind directly: she handed the project to Gareth, and going past him undercuts the person carrying it.
4. Alongside this, ask Gareth who reviews the contract on the legal side and when that starts, so it does not surface in the last week. Pieter and Nkem need nothing more from you: their reviews are finished.

You have no active executive sponsor yet. The person it has to be is Rosalind Achebe: only she can approve $430K, and she did call the project overdue, but her support reaches you second-hand. I would go to her through Gareth and the steering-meeting slot in step 3, and I would not count on her until she has said something to you directly. Whether Gareth can get you that slot is the real test of his reach.

## Case: the small purchase

$19K a year for a scheduling tool used by a single clinic office of eight. The office manager found us herself, booked a trial, and has sent five questions; she approves spend under $25K. A records officer joined one call to check data retention and said "that works". No procurement step mentioned.

The map gives the office manager a 5 as champion and buyer, the records officer a 2 with role "Retention check, passed" and direction "Done" (nothing is fading and nothing is unconfirmed; that she has not written since is normal, not a risk to raise). The paragraph on what is missing reads: "Nothing worth chasing at this size. One relationship is normal for a purchase inside her own limit, and the records officer has already passed the retention check." The moves: the trial review on her five questions, a short thank-you to the records officer with the retention summary, then the order form. No request for finance, IT or legal names, none for introductions to the rest of the office, no question about who else there needs convincing, and no demand for written sign-off. Sponsor: the office manager herself.

## Case: the only door

A $260K deal. Our sole contact is a plant engineering lead who started the evaluation, shared equipment data within hours and says "the managing director wants a payback figure". The managing director has no name in the record. Two people from the demo were never introduced. His message from the 4th is still unanswered on our side.

The opening paragraph contains the sentence "This deal is single-threaded: he is your only way in." The table has one row, his. The managing director gets no row although she decides, because a row needs a name; under the table one sentence covers the rest: "The managing director and the two people from the demo are not named anywhere in your notes, so none of them has a row." He keeps his 5: waiting on her does not lower it. Moves: answer his message of the 4th with the payback figure and an offer of a short session he hosts for the managing director; then ask him who the demo attendees were and what they do. No cold note to the managing director. Sponsor: the managing director in name, nobody yet in practice.

## Case: a different artefact

"Can you lay out their hierarchy as a diagram for our QBR deck, who sits under whom?" Six names with titles, nothing else. The whole reply, from its first word:

This is a request for a reporting-hierarchy diagram, not a stakeholder map, so I have not drawn one from these six names.

A hierarchy needs formal structure data: the customer's published org chart, or someone there confirming who reports to whom. Six names and titles show neither reporting lines nor influence, and a diagram guessed from them would reach your leadership as fact.

**What I can build instead:** a stakeholder map of this deal. For a QBR it is the more useful page, because it shows who can sign at this deal's size, who is driving it, who can block it, where your coverage is missing and which move comes first.

**What to paste so I can build it:**
- the meeting notes (dates, who attended, who followed up)
- the email activity for each of the six people
- any approval limits they have stated
- the gates still open (security, procurement, legal)

## Before replying

- Each role and score points to something in the record; no figures that the record does not give.
- Spending limits are checked against the deal size before anyone is called the buyer.
- A review confirmed in writing is closed.
- No invented names; roles without names stay out of the table, the decision-maker included.
- One contact only on a deal that needs more: the word "single-threaded" is in the opening paragraph.
- Whoever went quiet scores 2 or less, whatever the title.
- Gaps fit this deal's size; small deals say so.
- What we owe goes first; silent executives are reached through the person they trust.
- No direct message to anyone who has gone quiet; the champion brings them back.
- Nobody is called buyer, signer or sponsor on title alone; an unknown signer is a gap.
- The sponsor paragraph says so when there is no active sponsor, and names the candidate.

A map starts with its bold title line and carries no bold labels after it; a redirect starts with its plain first sentence to the seller. Never begin with "Now I understand the skill", "Now I have", "Let me", a "---" line, or any remark about the skill, its instructions or what you are about to do.
