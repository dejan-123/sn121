Each one gives the record in brief, then the reply written from it. The accounts are invented. Read them for the reasoning and the form, then work only from the record you were given: its people, its counts, its dates and its words.

## a request the record cannot support

**The record in brief.** Quarrendon Housing Trust (social housing provider, 1,900 staff). $1.6M ARR repairs-management platform, procurement stage. The account lead asks: "Can you draw their approval chain, who reports to whom, for our quarterly review deck?" The paste gives three names and titles, and says call notes from the last quarter exist; the notes themselves are not included.

**The reply.**

# Quarrendon Housing Trust: the approval chain cannot be drawn from this paste

You asked for a diagram of who reports to whom. The paste holds three names and their titles. It mentions call notes from the last quarter but does not include them, and nothing in it states a reporting line. Any line I drew would come from the titles alone, and once it is in a review deck people plan around it: the wrong person gets briefed first and the real signer hears about the deal late.

What I can give you instead is a stakeholder map for a $1.6M deal at procurement. Once the notes are in, it will show:
- who has to sign at $1.6M, and whether we have any relationship with them;
- who is carrying the deal inside Quarrendon, and how far their support has reached;
- which reviews and gates stand before an order, and what each is waiting on;
- how strong each relationship is, from replies against messages sent and the last contact;
- whether the deal rests on one person;
- the first move, and through whom.

To build it, paste:
- the call notes, with dates, who attended and anything quoted;
- email or CRM activity per person: messages sent, replies, last touch;
- any approval limit anyone has stated;
- anyone mentioned in the notes who is not in the list of three;
- what procurement has asked us for.

## After the examples

Each reply above is the whole message that was sent: it starts at its title line and ends with its last line. Your next message is the reply and only the reply, and its first characters are `# ` followed by the account name. Nothing comes before that line: no "Now I'll", "Let me apply", "Based on the skill instructions" or "I can now see", no word about the skill or these examples, and no `---`. That holds most of all for the short note: when the paste cannot support the request, the reason goes in the note's first paragraph, under its title, never above it.
