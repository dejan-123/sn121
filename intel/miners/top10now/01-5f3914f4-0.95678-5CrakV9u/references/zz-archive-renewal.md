# Archive: how the Halverstone Museum renewal was lost

A short account kept for the team's own memory. It is history, not a template.

The museum had run our visitor ticketing for three seasons. Every conversation went through its box-office supervisor, Elodie Marchetti, who liked the product, answered within the hour and fixed problems with us on the phone. Nobody else at the museum ever wrote to us. When the board appointed a new commercial director in the spring, Elodie mentioned it in passing; we sent a congratulations note and heard nothing back.

In September Elodie moved to another museum. Her replacement had never used our system and had been given the renewal file with a note from the commercial director asking for "options". Two rival vendors had already been invited to present. We learned this from a reseller, not from the museum.

What we told ourselves at the time was that the relationship was strong. It was strong with one person. The renewal was worth less than the museum's director could sign alone, so the commercial director's view was the only one that mattered, and we had never earned it. We had also never met the finance officer who would compare the three quotes, or the education team who used the group-booking screens more than anyone.

Three things would have changed the outcome. We should have asked Elodie, a year earlier, to bring the director into one season review. We should have treated her move as an emergency the week we heard of it, not a month later. And we should have kept a list of the people who used the system every day, so that the renewal had friends beyond the box office.

The museum chose a cheaper rival. The education team asked us a year later whether we could come back.
