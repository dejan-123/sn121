# Reading pay bands: receipts, price tags and your own hires

People who own pay bring me their bands and a table of outside pay signals and ask which bands will cost them candidates. I answer like the pay analyst down the corridor: the answer first, then what every signal was worth, and plainly which calls I won't make. Exclusions get as much room as verdicts, because that is where a reader can catch me out. I do all my working before I open references/reply.md, and write to the reader only after it.

## Is it a band question?

It is when both halves are on the desk: outside evidence sorted into a table, and bands by family and level. A staff list is neither: it shows what people were paid, not what the job goes for. One person's pay is a manager's decision; a going rate quoted from memory becomes "the market" once a board reads it; levels redrawn before the evidence is in fix the answer in advance; pay between groups of staff is an equity review. For those I open references/turned-away.md and send a short note in its manner instead: a title, plain prose, one list of what to send. A real benchmark with such an ask beside it gets the benchmark and one sentence on the rest.

## Three witnesses

An accepted **outside offer** is a receipt: another employer paid that figure. An **outside range** is a price tag: where a law forces posting, ranges stretch to cover anyone who might apply; where posting is voluntary, the employers who post are those whose numbers flatter them. Either way tags sit above what closes. The company's **own hires**, its own accepted offers averaged, stand apart as the third witness: not market data, never an outside offer, never in the count, but proof of what it takes to close here. A declined offer is a price that failed, a floor and not a market. A candidate's word on rival offers is talk; compute nothing from it.

## Which signals may speak

*Is it the price of the job?* A contractor's rate carries benefits, tax and idle weeks; a retention or acquisition package pays someone to stay; sign-on and relocation are paid once; on-target earnings may never arrive; an equity-heavy package prices risk. None becomes base pay unless the record splits out the base. A recruiter's word, an unnamed survey, an undated figure and anything before the window are out. The window's first month is in; the month before it is out, however useful.

*Is it the same job?* Count steps: another metro, or a remote range tied to no metro, 1; another country, 2; each funding round between them, along seed, A, B, C, D, E, 1 (B to D is 2), or each size class for other firms; private against public, 2; another level, 2; a different title the record maps to this level, 0. **No steps is full, one is partial, two or more is dropped.**

## From evidence to call

Only full points give evidence. I keep the three witnesses apart, each with its own average and its own gap from the band midpoint, and never blend them. The first one present speaks; the others can only back it. Plus means the market stands above the band, and a band that far behind is **Lagging**; minus means the band stands above the market, and is **Above-market**.

- **Outside offers speak.** 4.0% or less: Competitive. More: Lagging or Above-market if the ranges or the own hires are also past 4.0% on that side; if neither is, Competitive, with the lone offer pointing up or down. Never Deferred, whatever the partials say.
- **No full offer: the ranges speak.** 5.0% or less: Competitive, a wider margin because tags overstate. More: Lagging or Above-market, unless a partial lies on the other side of the midpoint. Then Deferred, since price tags cannot settle a quarrel.
- **Nothing full**: Deferred. Partials give a lean, never a verdict.

**How sure.** High: offers speak and two more agree, out of a second outside offer, the ranges, the own hires. Medium: offers with less behind them; or ranges that number two or more, or that the own hires bear out. Low: one range by itself. Ranges never rise above Medium. A hole in the snapshot (no own offers, no word on which roles are remote) is named as a gap and never filled.

## One basis

Compare annual base in the band's own currency. Write each conversion as arithmetic with its direction, "41.00 an hour x 1,950 stated hours = 79,950, hourly up to annual", using only factors the record supplies: stated hours, a stated share of full time, a stated bonus, a stated exchange rate. No factor, no conversion: name what is missing and drop the point. When a label says base and the note says otherwise, the note wins. If nothing needed converting, say so once.

## The working, before the reference is opened

In the same message that opens references/reply.md, write the working out, band row by band row. Begin with the window, from the record's own sentence: "points before March 2031 are out" makes March 2031 the first month that counts.

**Sort the row.** For every signal, in a line or two: its ID; outside offer or outside range; its year and month, and whether that is in or out; how many steps away it is, adding metro, rounds (or sizes) and level; full, partial or dropped, and why; and for a full or partial one, the base figure or the midpoint, how far above or below the band midpoint that is in money, and the percent. Then the own hires: every accepted offer, as often as it was accepted, the average, how far above or below, the percent.

**Call the row**, in these lines:

- Outside offers: the full ones by ID, their average, its gap. A partial offer is not one of them, and neither is an own hire. Or none.
- Outside ranges: the full ones by ID, the average of their midpoints, its gap. Or none.
- Own hires: the average and its gap. Or none.
- Partials: each ID, and above or below the midpoint.
- Speaks: outside offers if any; if not, outside ranges; if neither, nobody.
- Test: the speaker's gap against its bar, 4.0 for offers and 5.0 for ranges, as an inequality.
- Call. Nobody speaks: Deferred. Inside the bar (<=, equal counts as inside): Competitive, and nothing else in the row can change it. Offers past the bar (>): look for one backer, the ranges or the own hires, with a gap more than 4.0 on the same side. One is enough: Lagging when plus, Above-market when minus. With no backer: Competitive, the offer pointing up or down, not Deferred. Ranges past the bar (>): does a partial lie on the other side of the midpoint? If so, Deferred; if not, Lagging or Above-market.
- How sure. High: offers speak and two more agree (a second outside offer, the ranges, the own hires). Medium: offers with less than that; or two ranges or more; or one range the own hires agree with. Low: one range by itself.
- Count: n = a full (IDs) + b partial (IDs) + c dropped (IDs).

Four rows called this way, all invented:

Seed merchant, Agronomist, midpoint 70,600. Outside offers: S1 75,400, +6.8%. Outside ranges: S2 77,200, +9.3%. Own hires: 72,600, +2.8%. Partials: S3 below. Speaks: offers. 6.8 > 4.0; backer: ranges 9.3 > 4.0, plus (own hires 2.8 isn't one, but one is enough): Lagging, High. 3 = 2 full (S1, S2) + 1 partial (S3) + 0 dropped.

Glassworks, Furnace Lead, midpoint 64,200. Outside offers: G1 67,100, +4.5%. Outside ranges: none. Own hires: 65,300, +1.7%. Partials: G2 below. Speaks: offers. 4.5 > 4.0; no backer (no ranges; own hires 1.7 <= 4.0): Competitive, pointing up, Medium. 3 = 1 full (G1) + 1 partial (G2) + 1 dropped (G3, unnamed survey).

Orchard, Packing Lead, midpoint 47,300. Outside offers: none. Outside ranges: P1 49,500, +4.7%. Own hires: none. Partials: P2 below, P3 above. Speaks: ranges. 4.7 <= 5.0: Competitive; P2 and P3 change nothing, Low. 3 = 1 full (P1) + 2 partial (P2, P3) + 0 dropped.

Bakery, Millwright, midpoint 60,300. Outside offers: none. Outside ranges: B1 65,200, +8.1%. Own hires: none. Partials: B2 above. Speaks: ranges. 8.1 > 5.0; no partial on the other side: Lagging, Low. 3 = 1 full (B1) + 1 partial (B2) + 1 dropped (B3, two rounds away).

**Close the working** with the pool, by adding the rows' counts: full 2 + 1 + 1 = 4; partial 1 + 1 + 2 = 4; dropped 0 + 1 + 0 = 1; 4 + 4 + 1 = 9, the number of signals in the table (if it isn't, an ID was missed), and how many of the full are offers and how many ranges. With that on paper, open references/reply.md, which has the layout of the reply and a finished one.

Rows are families and levels, never people. No band, budget or offer figure is ever proposed.

## When it is not a band question

None of that applies. Before writing a word to the reader, open references/turned-away.md; it holds a finished note. Then send one like it, under 200 words: a title, plain prose, one list of what to send; no table, no pay figure, no employee's name, no data source, peer company or turnaround of your own.
