# The reply

It is written fresh from the working and is complete in itself: someone who has seen nothing else can follow every call and check it.

- Title `# <Company>: ` plus place and month. Then two sentences: the call on the most urgent open role's band, and how many signals came (first ID to last) and from which month they count. The other calls wait for their own paragraphs.
- `### <family, level>` for each band row, urgent open roles first, in 50 to 70 words of ordinary prose: the witness that speaks and its arithmetic, what backs it, why each other ID was left out (the city, the round, the date, the basis, never a step score), the call.
- `## Benchmark table`: Family and level | Band midpoint (min-max) | Outside offers | Outside ranges | Own hires | Verdict | Confidence and reason | Evidence. The three witness cells are copied from the call lines, one gap each or "none"; a partial's gap never goes into one, so a row whose only offer is partial has "none" under outside offers. Below it, the whole pool as three numbers.
- `## Deferred verdicts`: the exact evidence that would settle each (role, level, place, stage, offer or range, date). If none, say so and name the weakest call.
- `## Signal caveats`: two or three points on how the whole pool leans, such as offers against ranges, forced or voluntary posting as the record's notes give it, a single feed, what base leaves out. Nowhere else.

It stops there, at 650 words or so for four or five families. Every call and every number, in the prose and in the table, is the working's, unchanged.

# Kestrel Lock Lines: Thunder Bay bands, March 2027

The Chief Vessel Engineer's band, behind the berth you can't fill, lags: people are signing for a tenth more than its middle. You sent thirteen signals, K1 to K13, and they count from March 2026.

### Vessel Engineering, Chief
Someone here signed at 61.50 an hour (K1): 61.50 x 2,080 stated hours = 127,920, hourly up to annual, 12,420 over your 115,500 midpoint, +10.8%. The range advertised in town (K2, midpoint 125,500, +8.7%) and your own hire at 123,700 (+7.1%) stand on the same side. K3 is another port and a bigger carrier, two differences at once, so it is out. Lagging.

### Dispatch Planning, Lead
K4 was signed for 0.8 of full time: 60,800 / 0.8 = 76,000, part time up to full, 2,500 under your 78,500, -3.2%. K7 (midpoint 80,200, +2.2%) is dated March 2026, the window's first month, so it counts; K6, from February, doesn't. K5 is another port's range (74,500), worth a glance and no more. Nobody is far from your midpoint: competitive.

### Terminal Operations, Shift Supervisor
No one has signed, so the ranges speak: K8 (midpoint 85,600) and K9 (86,800) average 86,200, which is 5,300 under your 91,500, -5.8%. K11 is signed, but in Sarnia, so it is partial and stays out of the offers; at 84,900 it leans the same way. K10 folds relocation into its 111,500 with no split, so it prices a move and not a job. The band is above the market, and since tags run high, probably by more than that.

### Harbour Safety, Officer
K12 is signed and local, but 48.20 an hour with no paid hours recorded can't be made into a salary. K13 is a national carrier's, two sizes up from yours. With nothing full left I make no call.

## Deferred verdicts

Harbour Safety, Officer: the paid hours behind K12 would settle it at once; failing that, one offer or range for an officer in Thunder Bay at a regional carrier, March 2026 or later.

## Signal caveats

- Four of the six full signals are price tags and only two are receipts, and the supervisors' row is nothing else, so that band's true distance from the market is probably wider than 5.8%.
- Sea allowances, large in this trade, appear nowhere; only base is compared.
- One family in four has an own hire to check against.
