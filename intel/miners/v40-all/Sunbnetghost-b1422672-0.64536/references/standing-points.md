The reply:

# Saltgrass Island Ferry Co.: no benchmark from this paste

What came in is a crew list of five rows from a company that gives its size as 70 staff. It covers vessel captain, deckhand and purser, carries no as-of date, and comes with no outside pay evidence. A benchmark is priced only from classified outside points, so the captain, deckhand and purser cells are all Deferred: nothing outside stands to anchor any of them. The four questions also belong to different pieces of work. Whether captains are paid below other ferry lines is the benchmark question proper, and it waits on outside evidence. What deckhands earn across the country is a market lookup, and a national figure would not price an island route anyway. Whether one captain is underpaid next to the other is an individual pay decision for whoever sets crew pay. A gender pay gap on the deck crew calls for a pay-equity audit, which compares people inside the company and needs no market data.

Read internally, and only internally, the list says this. It holds two captains, two deckhands and one purser. The captains sit $3,300 apart, 3.7% above the lower of the pair, and the deckhands sit $1,300 apart, 3.2% above the lower. A spread inside a pair, or a step between two roles, is not evidence that anyone is underpaid or overpaid, because tenure, licences and seasons the list does not show can explain it. Groups of one and two cannot carry an average, five rows may not be the whole crew, and the figures are base pay only, with no overtime, seasonal bonus or profit share.

To answer the captain question, send each outside point with an ID, source, kind, date, title, place, type and size of employer, figure and pay basis. Accepted offers weigh more than posted ranges, because an offer is a wage someone took and a range is what an employer chose to advertise. Hourly charter rates, packages built on profit share, and figures with one-off awards folded in will not count. Add pay bands by role and level, using role codes in place of names, the offers made over the past year, and the date window to apply. Two outside points that agree would give a cell a Medium reading. Three points, two of them accepted offers, would give it a High one. A single point gives only a Low one.

Before the 1st, the realistic aim is the captain cell, because that is the claim in dispute. Two or three accepted captain offers from comparable New England ferry operators would give it a first reading. The other cells can follow as points arrive, and the crew list itself should stay with the owners.

## A note on writing the reply

Each reply opens with its decisive call, then lets each family's paragraph show the reading the table only restates. The first time an ID appears, its row comes with it (kind, date, place, stage, figure), so anyone can check the call without the table beside them, and the basis paragraph says how many rows the table holds. The gap is always our midpoint minus the anchor, over the anchor, so a minus sign means the band sits below the market. A gap between 3% and 5% is settled by the company's own hires in that family and nowhere else, and the paragraph says which way they pointed. A partial never turns a standing reading into a deferral. Each funnel sums to its retrieved count, and the Pool line sums to the rows in the paste. Own offers come from their own family's line, appear as percentages of the midpoint, and never move a tier. Where the paste is silent, the reply says so and moves on. The reply is written once, with no working sheet in it. A redirect names each cell as deferred, describes the roster by role and level only, and calculates nothing about the market.
