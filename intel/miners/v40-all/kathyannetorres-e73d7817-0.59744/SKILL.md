# Pay position review: reading a band against its market

## What a salary signal has to share with the band

A band is a statement about one job, at one level, paid as base salary, in one labour market, at one kind of company, now. A signal earns a place in the comparison only when it shares all of that, and the first thing it fails to share is the reason it is set aside. Most wrong calls come from one signal that should have gone: an out-of-town range, on-target earnings set against base, a listed company beside a private one, a grant folded into a package.

**The same pay.** Bands are base. A signal that bundles more is unbundled only with the input's own numbers: a total that states its bonus becomes base once the bonus comes off. Without the split it goes: an on-target-earnings range, a package carrying a retention or sign-on grant, a contract or hourly rate (nothing in the input says how many hours make a year, and I never supply that number). Money in another currency stays out unless the input gives the rate; I never convert from memory, in either direction, and a team paid in pounds or euros is read in pounds or euros against its own city.

**The same moment.** Signals with no date go. The first month of the window counts: if signals from before June 2025 are out, June 2025 is in and May 2025 is out. Compare each signal's month with that first month; equal or later stays.

**A real source.** Accepted offers and published ranges from a named source count. A recruiter's word, a verbal report, a survey with no name or method does not.

**The same labour market.** The market is the home metro, or the cost tier the company says its remote staff are pinned to. A signal from any other city goes, however good it looks: an accepted offer in another city is set aside, not half-counted. A national or remote range is half-comparable unless the input says that job is remote with no anchor.

**The same kind of company.** The same funding stage is comparable. One stage either side (Series B read against A or C) is half-comparable: it can confirm a reading, never make one. Two stages away, or a listed company read against a private one, goes; listed-company equity is liquid, so even its base is set for a different pay mix.

**The same job.** Same family, same level. A different title counts only when the input maps it to the company's level; a combined title that includes the role counts; a different level does not. Senior and Staff are two bands, two paragraphs, two rows.

## The company's own offers

The snapshot's recent offers are internal evidence, kept apart from the external count. Each accepted offer joins its family's market figure, counted one by one ("two accepted at $144,600 and one at $141,600" is three); a decline is a warning sign, and loudest when it sits at or above the midpoint and the candidate points to current pay or a better offer; a family the snapshot lists with no offers has none.

## What people took beats what employers advertise

An accepted offer is revealed preference: someone said yes at that number. A published range is an employer's opening position, and where the law forces ranges into job ads they are often drawn wide. So when a family has comparable accepted offers, external or its own, they make the market figure: line them up from lowest to highest and take the middle one (with an even count, halfway between the two middle ones; one offer is the figure by itself). Only a family with no comparable accepted offer is read off its comparable ranges, the same way, using each range's midpoint. When a family has even one comparable accepted offer, its ranges stay out of the line-up altogether: offers and range midpoints never share one. Half-comparable signals never make the figure; I only say whether they agree.

## How far is far

What decides the call is the gap between the band midpoint and the market figure, as a share of the midpoint, and what the market figure rests on. Accepted offers are what it costs to hire, so a gap of 3% to them already counts. Advertised ranges are drawn wide, so a figure that rests on ranges alone needs 5%.

- Market figure from accepted offers: 3% or more above the midpoint is **Lagging** ("slightly" under 5%), 3% or more below is **Above market** ("slightly" under 5%), anything inside is **Competitive**.
- Market figure from ranges alone: 5% or more above is **Lagging**, 5% or more below is **Above market**; inside 5% it is **Competitive**, and a gap of 3% to 5% is noted as "at the low edge" or "at the high edge".

A midpoint under the market figure can only be Lagging or Competitive; one over it only Above market or Competitive. A lean also needs the signals on one side: when a gap under 5% has a comparable or half-comparable signal on the other side of the midpoint, the midpoint sits inside its market and the position is **Competitive**, at the low (or high) edge. Where a lagging family has a P1 opening, the row says so.

## How much weight the call can bear

The grade is earned by external evidence; the company's own offers decide only the top grade. **High**: two or more comparable external signals, at least one an accepted offer, that agree, with the company's own accepted offers pointing the same way. **Medium**: one comparable external offer backed by the company's own; an external offer and range with no own offers; two or more agreeing external ranges; or one range lifted by an agreeing half-comparable signal. **Low**: one comparable external range alone, or comparable ranges more than 10% apart. **Deferred**: no comparable external signal at all; one is enough for a Low call and is never a reason to defer.

## How the note goes

Read references/casebook.md once, then write the note once, from its title down, as your entire reply: each signal is weighed inside its own band's row as the row is written, so there is no plan, draft or correction around the note. It begins with the line "# Pay position — <Company> (<stage>, <home metro>)".

1. **Opening**, two sentences: how many external signals, retrieved when, the first month the window includes (as YYYY-MM) and the market the bands are read against; then where the bands sit overall.
2. **## Where each band sits**: one table, Family and level | Band midpoint | Signals weighed | Market figure | Position | Confidence. The signals cell names every external signal for that band, each with its own city, stage and month written before the call: "T-7 (Raleigh, C, 2025-06) stays, $166,600–$198,600, midpoint $182,600; T-8 (Raleigh, C, 2025-05) set aside, before the window". The market figure cell starts with "from offers" or "from ranges alone", which decides the margin, then lists the values behind it in order, the company's own offers marked "(own)", and the middle value. The position cell says which way the midpoint sits, in money and as a percent to one decimal, then whether every signal sits on one side or one sits across the midpoint, then the word ("midpoint $6,000 under, 3.9%; all signals above → Lagging slightly"). The confidence cell gives the grade and what decided it.
3. **## Not called yet**: bands with no external signal that stays, what is missing and what would settle it; "Every family has a call" if none.
4. **## What skews this pool**: two short sentences on patterns across the whole pool, said once, ending on the one most particular to this pool.

Before a row is final: a signal one stage away in either direction is half-comparable and never the market figure; a band with no signal that stays is Deferred, its market figure "none", whatever its half-comparable signals say. Every figure comes from the input; the only figures I make are midpoints, middle values, gaps and percents.

## When the note cannot be written

The note needs bands per family and level and a classified external evidence table. Without external evidence there is no table, no position and no market salary, because any number would be mine and not evidence. A list of individual salaries is not a band, and people are never averaged into one. Designing bands or levels, pricing one person, or projecting next year's market is other work. The reply is then a short note: what is missing, which parts of the request are other work and why, each family and level in the list named as not called yet, with the reason stated (no external signals, so nothing stays), and exactly what to send, each item described in words with no figures and no sample rows; a sample band written with numbers is still a figure and does not belong.
