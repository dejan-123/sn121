Your worksheet is done. Write the deliverable now as your final message, carrying over the figures, IDs and dates you worked out. Its first line is the title `# <Company>: role-family benchmark`, with no rule (---) or other text above it, and the opening sentence follows. It reads as a finished report for the requester; the working stays behind.

## Layout

Role-family-and-level grain only: no individual employees, and no figure from outside the input. After the title and opening, the headings are exactly Benchmark table, Verdict notes, Deferred verdicts, Signal caveats.

- **Opening.** One or two sentences: the company, how many external points were retrieved (the evidence table's row count) across how many role families, and the headline: what lags, what is competitive, what is deferred. Leave the full, partial and dropped counts to the table's funnel column.
- **Benchmark table.** One row per family and level, in snapshot order. The columns are | Role family / level | Band mid | Funnel | Anchor (basis) | Gap | Verdict | Confidence: deciding reason |.
  - Funnel: "5 retrieved → 3 full (2 offers, 1 range), 0 partial, 2 dropped".
  - Anchor names its points: "$169,250 (mean of 2 offers: X-02, X-04)", "$167,000 (1 offer: X-02)" or "$126,000 (1 range midpoint: X-06)". An anchor never mixes an offer with a range.
  - A deferred row shows "—" for the anchor and the gap.
- **Verdict notes.** Two to four sentences of plain prose per row. Open each note with whatever decides that row (the offers that anchor it, the drop that changed it, the boundary it sits on, the gap that defers it), so the notes do not all start the same way. Cover:
  - the IDs that set the anchor and why they qualify;
  - when an offer anchors, any full range corroborates but stays out, and the note says so;
  - each dropped or partial point by ID, with every reason;
  - any conversion with its direction;
  - last, as a separate check on where the company pays today: this family's own offers or declines (never another family's), and whether they agree with the external verdict.
  Flag a boundary case in a few words in that row's note, and say when a row rests on one point.
- **Deferred verdicts.** For each deferred row, say what failed and give a sourcing brief that would close it: role, level, metro, stage, type (accepted offer preferred) and window. If nothing is deferred, say so in one line.
- **Signal caveats.** Two to four bullets of one or two sentences each, each a pool-wide skew stated once and never a repeat of a row note, written about this pool's own counts:
  - a single source;
  - ranges outnumbering offers (an aspirational lean);
  - a thin offer sample;
  - a stage or geography mix;
  - which ranges the Notes column marks as posted under a pay-transparency mandate and which as voluntary (self-selected, so they lean toward employers proud of their pay). Report mandate status as the Notes give it, without adding or disputing it from memory.
- **Close.** One short paragraph on what to act on first, by the open-role priority tiers. A lagging band on a critical-path role comes first. Do not prescribe new band figures.

## Shape, for an invented company

# Halvorsen Labs: role-family benchmark

Halvorsen Labs (Series C, Minneapolis MN) has 11 external points retrieved across three role families. Security Engineering lags the market on two offers, Field Marketing is competitive on a single range, and Mobile Engineering is deferred.

## Benchmark table

| Security Engineering / Senior | $157,500 | 5 retrieved → 3 full (2 offers, 1 range), 0 partial, 2 dropped | $169,250 (mean of 2 offers: X-02, X-04) | −6.9% | Lagging | High: two offers agree |
| Field Marketing / Manager | $120,500 | 3 retrieved → 1 full (0 offers, 1 range), 1 partial, 1 dropped | $126,000 (1 range midpoint: X-06) | −4.4% | Competitive | Low: one range only |
| Mobile Engineering / Staff | $187,500 | 3 retrieved → 0 full, 1 partial, 2 dropped | — | — | Deferred | Deferred: no full point |

## Verdict notes

**Security Engineering, Senior.** Two Minneapolis Series C accepted offers, X-02 at $167,000 (2026-05) and X-04 at $171,500 (2026-04), set the anchor at $169,250, and the band midpoint sits 6.9% below it. X-03's range ($159,500–$186,500, midpoint $173,000) is fully comparable and points the same way, but it corroborates rather than anchors because offers exist. X-01 is dropped as a Milwaukee posting, a different metro. X-05 is dropped because its "$191,500 total comp" has no base split. Halvorsen's own two recent hires both came in above the midpoint, so internal pay points the same way as the lag.

**Field Marketing, Manager.** This call rests on a single aspirational range, so it is Low. X-06, a posted range of $113,000–$139,000 (midpoint $126,000), is the only fully comparable point and puts the band 4.4% below. X-07, a Series B posting one stage below, is kept as a partial that understates the market. X-08, an accepted offer dated 2025-03, is dropped as before the cutoff.

**Mobile Engineering, Staff.** Nothing survives in full, so there is no verdict. X-09, a Series D posting one stage above, is a partial, and a partial cannot anchor. X-10 is dropped: its $88/hour contract rate prices a different relationship and is never annualised. X-11 is dropped as a Public-company range.

## Deferred verdicts

- **Mobile Engineering, Staff:** no fully comparable point survives. To close it: one accepted offer, or a posted base range, for a Staff mobile engineer at a Minneapolis Series C company, dated inside the window.

## Signal caveats

- 9 of 11 points come from one aggregator, so the source mix is narrow.
- Ranges outnumber offers 6 to 4, so the pool leans aspirational. The Notes column marks two postings as mandate-driven and the rest as voluntary, which are self-selected.

Security Engineering is the critical-path hire and the clearest lag, so it comes first in the review. Field Marketing needs one more comparable point before anyone acts on it.
