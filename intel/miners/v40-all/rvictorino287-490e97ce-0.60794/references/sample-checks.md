# Sample checks: two full replies and one note

Three invented cases that show the form of the reply. Every company, data point and figure here is made up and has nothing to do with the input in front of you. Take the layout and the order of reasoning; never a figure, an ID or a sentence. Each role family gets its own small table, and each data point is screened against that family's own anchor metro, stage and currency. The Class cell holds one of four things only: fully comparable, partial (geography), partial (stage), or dropped with its reasons; a data point that is off on geography and off on stage is dropped as incomparable, and "partial (geography and stage)" is not a class. In each Position line every comparison is one figure against one figure. The Benchmark table at the end is copied from the Funnel and Call lines above it. IDs differ by case: cite the IDs your own input uses.

Cost tiers for US metros: two metros match on geography when they share a tier. Tier 1: San Francisco Bay Area, New York City, Seattle. Tier 2: Austin, Boston, Chicago, Denver, Los Angeles, Miami, Philadelphia, Portland, Sacramento, San Diego, Washington DC. Tier 3: every other US metro.

## Case 3: a request that is not a benchmark

### What was pasted

We have not pulled any outside data yet." A roster of six named people follows, with role, level and salary: four Lab Technicians (two Senior, two Mid) and two Quality Inspectors (Senior).

### The reply

# Tamarack Dental Labs: benchmark not run

No classified external evidence table came with this request, so there is nothing to screen and no market position to state. Any market figure given now would be invented, so none is given. The salaries on the roster are internal pay, not market evidence, and are not benchmarked here.

**Request by request**

1. What Senior Lab Technicians make in Tucson: this is the benchmark itself, and it cannot run until the evidence below arrives.
2. Whether one technician is underpaid: outside a benchmark, which speaks about a band and never about a person.
3. A pay-equity audit: a separate internal analysis comparing like roles across groups; a market check cannot stand in for it.
4. A band for Lab Manager: out of scope here. Band design is job-architecture work, which a finished benchmark can inform.

**Position now:** Lab Technician (Senior), Lab Technician (Mid) and Quality Inspector (Senior) are all Deferred: 0 data points retrieved, 0 survived, 0 dropped.

**What the input does give:** role families with levels and headcount: Lab Technician (Senior and Mid, four people) and Quality Inspector (Senior, two people). No bands, no own offers.

**To run the benchmark, send:** external data points (accepted-offer records, posted ranges, or a cut from a pay survey such as Pave, Carta or Radford), each with posting type, date, role and level, metro, company stage, figures and pay basis; the data window; a band (minimum, midpoint, maximum) per role family and level; own offers, accepted and declined.

**What comes back:** a verdict, evidence funnel and confidence tier for each role family, with deferred verdicts and signal caveats.
