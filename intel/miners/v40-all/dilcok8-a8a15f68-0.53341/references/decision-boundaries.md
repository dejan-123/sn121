# Check the meaning of the numbers

These arithmetic examples illustrate the method. Their numbers and conclusions are not defaults for another task.

## Equal midpoint, different ability to reach the comparison interval

Suppose a supported external anchor is 120, in thousands of annual base, and the declared comparison tolerance is -4% to +6%. The comparison interval is [115.2,127.2]. Two existing bands share midpoint 111:

- A band from 90 to 135 intersects the interval at [115.2,127.2]. Its midpoint still lags: (111-120)/120 = -7.5%. The band contains the entire comparison interval, but midpoint competitiveness and actual hiring success have not been established.
- A band from 108 to 114 has no overlap. Its upper endpoint is 1.2 below the lower comparison edge. The midpoint has the same -7.5% gap; here existing band capacity is also below the declared interval.

Changing the second maximum to exactly 115.2 creates a one-point intersection. Do not say that an amount at the band maximum is outside the band. Neither example authorizes moving the band or offering a particular salary.

## Conversion and date boundary are independent checks

An eligible employee offer states 7,200 source-currency units each month, 13 paid periods per year, and no variable pay. With 0.75 target units per source unit, annual target-currency base is 7,200 × 13 × 0.75 = 70,200. Do not substitute twelve periods or invert that quote. If the input instead quotes 1.333333 source units per target unit, division is the appropriate direction; retain the precision of the supplied quote.

“Exclude months before May” admits a May observation. “After May 31” does not. A current retrieval date does not renew an old observation. The date decision remains independent of a correct pay conversion.

## Amount offered and amount paid are different evidence

A documented offer at 88 that was declined proves an offer was made at 88. If eligible under the selected policy, it can enter an offer-made comparison, with that limitation stated. It does not prove a paid salary, the candidate's reservation price, or what a competing employer offered.

If an adjacent-stage offer at 88 is only partial and a full posting spans 92 to 108, the partial does not supply an offer anchor. The posting midpoint is 100, with Low confidence for a single posting and an explicitly advertised-pay claim. A pay-citing decline of the company's own offer may explain hiring friction without turning either partial evidence or internal pay into a full outside observation.

## A rounded display does not decide a boundary

For a 100 anchor with the default inclusive -5%/+5% policy, midpoint 95 is Competitive; midpoint 94.99 is Lagging. Both can display as about -5% if rounded too early. Decide with the original amounts, then display enough precision to explain the distinction.
