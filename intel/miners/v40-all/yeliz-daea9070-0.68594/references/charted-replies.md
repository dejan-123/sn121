# Finished replies: the rope maker and the hull-coatings maker, and a note for an ask with no chart

Nothing here is real; the companies, cities, figures and dates were made up. Replies one and two are what each reader got once the matching sheet in `references/sounding-sheets.md` was closed; reply three is the note for a request that needs no sheet. Take form and size from them, nothing else. What a live reply keeps:
- a `## ` title naming the company and its verdicts in words, then an opening paragraph: home metro and round, the opening month and what it struck, the rows that price nothing, and each conversion as a sum with its direction;
- two to four paragraphs of argument, firmest call first, each opening on what decides it; accepted offers are called revealed preference and posted ranges aspirational where each is first weighed, a volunteered range is called partial, a partial's lean takes a clause, hearsay is called hearsay, and a gap from 3% to 5% names the own offer that settled it;
- when a point first appears, its sentence gives what kind it is, its month or round, its city and its figure next to the ID;
- one table whose gap cell shows its sum with the anchor's IDs and whose funnel counts all four piles by ID, then a `Pool:` line;
- Deferred Verdicts in every reply, and Signal Caveats spoken once for the pool, naming families and sources rather than counting kinds;
- a last line answering the loudest figure that was dropped;
- for a request the chart cannot serve, a short note in plain prose: a judgment, what the paste shows, what to send.

No company, city, job title, figure or date carries over; each comes from the request in hand.

## Brindle Rope & Rigging: braiding and rigging bands lag Tacoma, rope engineering sits above it, tensile testing holds narrowly, inside sales waits

Inside Sales Lead. Nearest from above sits the slash-titled range (B-17), nearest from below the Series B hire (B-18); one may be priced for another job and the other comes from a round earlier. ## Reply three: a pay-equity check and a going-rate question with no market evidence and no bands

What the owner of Quillon Pottery Supply sent. Quillon is a Series A maker of kiln parts and glazes with 45 staff in Asheville NC. "Can you check whether the women and men on our kiln crew are paid alike, and tell us the going rate for a glaze lab manager? Our kiln supervisor is sure the crew is underpaid next to the tile plant across the river. Our staff list with names and salaries is below; the bands are still being drafted, and our lender wants the numbers by next Thursday." The staff list names fourteen people: nine kiln technicians, one glaze lab manager and four warehouse leads. No bands, no open roles and no evidence table came with it, so no sheet was kept.

The note:

## Quillon Pottery Supply: a pay-equity check and a going-rate question, not a benchmark

You asked whether women and men on the kiln crew are paid alike, and what a glaze lab manager earns elsewhere. A benchmark can settle neither: it sets role-family bands against classified outside evidence, and neither the evidence nor the bands came with the request. Comparing pay between groups of staff is a pay-equity review for whoever owns pay at Quillon, since it needs each person's record and the reasons behind each rate.

Your supervisor's view of the tile plant is neither confirmed nor denied here: a staff list shows what Quillon pays the people who stayed, never what the market pays. Thursday changes how soon the evidence is needed, not what can stand in for it, so no going rate is offered meanwhile. The paste shows nine kiln technicians, one glaze lab manager and four warehouse leads, and no one is judged by name.

To benchmark those three families, send the band for each family and level with its minimum, midpoint and maximum; classified outside evidence, one line per accepted offer or posted range, giving its month, role and level, metro, the employer's funding round, its base figure and whether a law required the posting; and Quillon's open roles and recent offers in the window. Each family then gets a verdict, a gap and a confidence tier.
