# Scope redirect

Use this when the request cannot be answered as a band benchmark.

## Recognising the request

Ask two questions of the input, in this order.

1. Is there an external evidence table with typed, dated rows? A promise that data exists, an attached file you cannot see, or a request that you go and find figures does not count.
2. Is there an internal snapshot with bands by role family and level? A roster of people with their salaries is not a band snapshot. It is individual data, and working from it would put the answer at the wrong grain.

If either answer is no, there is nothing to filter and nothing to compare against, so no verdict can be earned.

Then identify what is actually being asked. Requests often bundle several of these:

| The ask sounds like | What it really is | Why this method does not serve it |
|---|---|---|
| "Is this person underpaid", "what should we offer this candidate" | individual pay decision | verdicts here describe a band against a pool, not a person against a band |
| "What do these roles pay", "give me market numbers" | raw salary lookup | the method weighs supplied evidence; it has no evidence of its own |
| "Add a level", "rebuild our ranges", "set new midpoints" | band design | positioning says where a band sits, not what the structure should be |
| "Are we paying people fairly across groups" | pay-equity audit | needs individual records, protected attributes and a different statistical method |
| "Full market analysis by a deadline" with no evidence | a benchmark without inputs | a deadline does not create evidence |

## Writing the redirect

Keep it short: four or five plain sentences, under about 150 words, with no table, no headings, no bold labels and no numbered list. If several asks are bundled, deal with each in a clause. It should let the requester act immediately.

- Say plainly that you cannot produce the benchmark from what was sent, and why, in terms of what is missing from this request.
- Name each out-of-scope ask you found and where it belongs (a compensation lead or manager for an individual decision, a band-design exercise, an equity audit with counsel involved).
- Say exactly what to bring back for a benchmark: an evidence table with source, type, date, role, location, company stage and pay figures per row; band minimum, midpoint and maximum per role family and level; the data window; the company's stage and location policy.
- Say that every verdict stays deferred until those arrive, and which part of the request becomes answerable then.

The list of what to bring back is one sentence, not an itemised checklist. The requester needs the names of the two inputs and their key fields, nothing more.

Write as the analyst replying to a colleague. Do not refer to "this method", a skill, or instructions.

## An invented example

The request: a small biotech pastes six named scientists with their salaries and asks what process scientists earn locally, whether one named person should get a raise, and whether two scientist grades can become three. No external evidence is attached.

The reply, in full:

You asked for local market pay for process scientists, a view on one colleague's salary, and a third scientist grade. What arrived is a list of people and their pay, with no external evidence and no bands by role family and level.

A benchmark sets a band against screened market evidence, and neither side of that comparison is here, so I can't give a market position and I won't quote figures from memory. The individual question belongs with that person's manager and your compensation lead. The third grade is band design, a separate exercise that a benchmark can later inform.

For a benchmark I would need the external data points you hold, each with its type, date, role, location, company stage and base figure, plus each family and level's band minimum, midpoint and maximum, and the window to apply. Until then every verdict stays deferred.

Take the length and the plain voice from this, never its facts.

## What not to do

- Do not produce a table, even a partial or illustrative one.
- Do not quote market figures from general knowledge, and do not confirm or deny a claim that a group is underpaid.
- Do not comment on the people in a pasted roster, repeat their names, or rank their pay.
- Do not sketch a new band structure "as a starting point".
- Do not pad with a description of the whole method. The requester needs to know what to send, not how filtering works.

## Mixed cases

- Evidence table and band snapshot both present, plus a request to redesign a band: benchmark first, then one sentence saying the redesign is separate work that the verdicts can inform.
- Evidence table present but the snapshot covers only some role families: benchmark the families that have bands; list the rest as lacking an internal anchor.
- Snapshot present, evidence table empty or all rows excluded: this is not a redirect. Run the method; every family is deferred, with the evidence needed stated.
