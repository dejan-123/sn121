# Where a salary grade sits against its market

A comparator table answers more than "are we paying enough for this role?": an advert from a dearer city, an acceptance at a listed company, a figure a recruiter mentioned on the phone are each true about another job. I keep only comparators that describe this job in this market, say what I set aside and why, and let the tier hold the doubt.

## Four questions for every comparator

I ask them in order and stop at the first no; that answer is the reason I report.

1. **Same job?** The same function at the same level, whatever the wording. A title from another function or ladder passes only when the packet maps its scope to our level; a dual title passes when one half is ours. A level up or down fails.
2. **Same market?** The city tier the firm pays to. The same funding round passes; one round off is a neighbour, kept and flagged; two rounds off, or a listed company against a private one, fails. A national remote range passes only for a role the packet says is paid nationally; if it says some roles are remote without naming them, the range fails, with a clause on whether it would have moved the call.
3. **Same kind of pay?** Salary against salary, in the grade's currency. On-target earnings, total compensation, anything with a retention, sign-on or equity grant inside, and contractor rates fail, unless the packet itself states the salary or the folded-in amount, which I subtract. No exchange rate, annual hours or commission split from outside the packet.
4. **Sound record?** Dated inside the stated window, its boundary month counted in; undated fails. Written down somewhere: a remembered recruiter number or an anonymous survey with no method fails.

A neighbour on two questions at once (one round off and a neighbouring role) fails.

## Acceptances against adverts

An acceptance is the market's price, paid by someone. An advertised range is the employer's hope, often stretched at the top, so it enters at its centre. A candidate who said no over money or a better offer is pressure; a no without a reason is not. The firm's own recent hires are not comparators, yet they show where its grade closes: hires above our mid say the mid sits under the market.

## Making the call

- **Market rate**: the median of the surviving comparators, acceptances as stated and adverts at their centre. Neighbours join the median only once an exact comparator is in. With 2 survivors, their average.
- **Market ratio**: grade mid ÷ market rate, as a percentage to one decimal; the bands below apply to that shown figure.
- **Competitive** from 97.0% to 103.0%. **Lagging** at 95.0% or less. **Above-market** at 105.0% or more.
- **The grey zones**, above 95.0% and below 97.0%, or above 103.0% and below 105.0%, go to the firm's own hiring. Low side with hires above our mid, or a money-driven no: Lagging; otherwise Competitive, low edge. High side with hires under our mid: Above-market; otherwise Competitive, high edge.

A 3% difference is what a small pool wobbles by; at 5% candidates feel it. In between, the firm's own offers know more than the table.

## How sure the call is

Count exact comparators only; neighbours never raise a tier. One acceptance is a data point; two that agree are a market.

- **High**: 2 or more exact acceptances agreeing in direction (all above our mid, all below, or all within 3%); adverts can add, never stand in.
- **Medium**: 2 or more exact comparators with at least 1 acceptance, or 2 or more exact adverts backed by the firm's own hiring (a hire above the mid, a money-driven no).
- **Low**: a single exact comparator, or adverts with nothing from the firm's hiring behind them.
- **Deferred**: no exact comparator survives. No call, and no direction read into leftovers.

Write the tier with its reason, as in "Low: one advert".

## Skews across the whole pool

A skew that touches every family is written once, never row by row: adverts outnumbering acceptances, adverts that were legally mandated (broad ranges) or voluntary (self-selected employers), one source behind most comparators, more than one currency, neighbours carrying weight. Count only comparators that set a market rate. Take any direction from the packet's figures: advert centres above same-family acceptances mean adverts run high and lags look deeper; below, the reverse; mixed, no direction. Facts about mandates come only from the packet, and a rule it states for a place covers every survivor there.

## Situations to handle

- **One family at two levels**: two rows; a comparator counts only in its own level's row, and a staff advert never prices a lead grade.
- **A family with no grade**, or only individual salaries: Deferred, "no grade supplied". I never rebuild a grade from salaries; a blank mid with min and max given is (min + max) ÷ 2, shown.
- **Hires, the market rate or a money-driven no at or above the grade's top**: the grade has stopped reaching the market; the verdict cell says so.
- **A comparator that decides the call**: if counting one that failed a single question, or leaving out a neighbour, would move the verdict, the funnel cell says so ("if X-nn counted: Lagging", "exact only: 94.0%"); otherwise nothing. Not for one failing two questions or a deferred family, and never in the bottom line.
- **One advert listed twice** counts once.
- **No window given**: say which dates were treated as current.
- **Counting rounds**: seed, A, B, C, D, E, later growth; count the steps: B to D is two rounds.
- **Failures named in pay terms**: a listed company because liquid stock reshapes the pay mix, a contractor rate because it is not a salary, a retention grant because it is one-off money.
- **Priority openings**: a lagging P1 leads the bottom line; a deferred P1 leads the deferred section.

## When it is not a benchmark request

With no classified outside data there is nothing to screen, and a salary from general knowledge is an unsourced number that travels. A request to set one candidate's offer, design grades or levels, or audit pay equity is a separate job that uses a benchmark's market rates, so it follows one; mention only the ones actually asked for. Reply with a note of 180 to 280 words, titled "# [Firm]: pay benchmark not run, …", in four bolded parts: what was asked and what came with it (salaries are not a grade's min, mid and max); why no call can be made, including on the worry they raised; where the other requests go; and what to send, naming their own roles, levels, city and round, acceptances first and the firm's own recent offers with any reason for a no, and that the benchmark follows by their date, never one they did not give. Answer only what they asked.

## Writing it up

Read `references/analyst-casebook.md` once with read_reference. Then the write-up is your entire reply, and its first characters are `# ` and the firm's name. Nothing goes before that: no remark about this skill, the casebook or what you plan. Write as a colleague to the people who own pay, using the packet's word for grades, in plain sentences ("your only recent hire needed more than the mid"), not rule labels such as "grey-zone test applies". Keep screen words out of the prose ("fails on market", "neighbour joins", "grey zone", "95.0% line"); say "paid in another city".

- `# [Firm]: pay against market ([round], [city] tier)`
- `**Bottom line.**` three or four sentences: how many comparators came in and their ID range, the calls that matter, lagging priority roles first, and any deferral. Call a verdict clearest only if its tier is highest; a Low lag is one to confirm.
- `## Role-family benchmark`: Role family (level) | Verdict | Confidence | Mid ÷ market rate | Funnel. Keep every cell short: the verdict in a few words, the tier with a brief reason, the ratio with its division ("$143,300 ÷ $149,300 = 96.0%", checked from the difference: −$6,000 ÷ $149,300 = −4.0%), and the funnel as counts ("4 in, 2 survived, 2 failed"). Deferred families keep their row.
- `**Screen tally.**` one line: comparators in, survivors (neighbours), failures by plain reason with their IDs, and any conversion.
- `## How each call was reached`: one paragraph of two or three sentences per family: what survived with its type, date, city, round and figure (neighbours named as such), what failed and its one deciding reason in pay terms (a listed company: liquid stock reshapes the pay mix; never a mandate), any conversion with its arithmetic, the median with its figures in order and whether acceptances or adverts carried it, and every own hire or no the packet lists, with its distance from the mid ("$7,000 above it") and any reason, or their absence named as missing data. A deferred family gets one sentence pointing below and no figure.
- `## Deferred verdicts`: per family, why nothing exact survived, the leftovers as plain facts with no direction drawn, and the precise data that would settle it. If none, one sentence.
- `## Signal caveats`: the whole-pool skews as at most four short bullets.

Amounts stay in the grade's currency. Every figure is in the packet or worked from it. Four or five families run 500 to 750 words; cut restated table facts first.
