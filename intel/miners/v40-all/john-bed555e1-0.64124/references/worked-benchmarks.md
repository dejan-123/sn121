## Case three: an input that is not a benchmark job

The input for Osterley Print Works (Series A, 40 employees, Tucson AZ): "Quick one. We're about to make an offer to a press operator lead and I pulled three numbers from job boards, below. What should we offer, and can you write the offer letter as well? We don't have formal bands yet, the founder just decides." Three rows follow, each a posted range for a press operator lead in Arizona, with no dates.

The reply, from its first character:

# Osterley Print Works: no benchmark without bands, and what to send for one

You asked what to offer a press operator lead and for the offer letter. You supplied three posted ranges, none of them dated, and no pay bands: the founder decides each case.

A benchmark sets a band against the outside evidence that survives checking, and says whether the band is competitive, lagging or above-market. With no band there is nothing to give a verdict on, and with no dates the three ranges cannot be placed in any window. Picking a figure for one candidate from them would be a guess presented as a benchmark, so no figure is given here.

To get a benchmark, send: the band for this job (minimum, midpoint, maximum) even if it is only the range the founder would accept; the three rows with their dates, employer stage and whether each is a posted range or an accepted offer; the window you want applied; any offers of your own in that period. The offer letter is a separate job for whoever owns hiring, once the figure is set.
