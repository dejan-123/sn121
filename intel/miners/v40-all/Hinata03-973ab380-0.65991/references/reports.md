# The reports sent for cases A and B

Invented, like the casebook: take the form, never a fact. Each report was written after its working and copies every verdict, anchor, gap and tier from it. The working itself never appears in a report; the reader is a compensation lead who will be questioned on the page, so it argues each call in plain sentences before it tabulates anything. The paragraphs are the working's Say lines, firmest call first, with the word "Say" gone; the table only records what the paragraphs have shown.

## Last look before sending

- Every family and level in the snapshot has a paragraph sentence and a table row, deferred or not; no row names a person. A family the snapshot lists as an open role carries "(open P1)" or "(open P2)" after its name in the table, and no other family does.
- The paragraphs are the working's Say lines in order of firmness, the firmest verdict first and the deferred families last; families may share a paragraph. Nothing is worked again in the report: every figure, percentage and ID in a paragraph or a cell is copied from the working.
- Each funnel names every row filed to that family once, by ID, with the reason for each drop and the miss for each partial; a row listed away from its family, the last row of the table included, still sits in its own family's funnel and in no other; the Count line adds up to the number of rows in the input.
- Another named city, a remote or national figure with no metro, one round apart, one level off and a voluntary posting are each one partial test. One of them makes a row partial; two of them, or any mismatch, drop it.
- A blank posting note stands unless a line over the table says the ranges were posted by choice; a mandated range always stands. What the opening paragraph says about a line over the evidence table is the working's Over the table line and no more: where the working found nothing printed there, the paragraph says only that, and nothing in the report claims the ranges were posted under a mandate, or by choice, as a group.
- A full range beside an offer anchor is still full: it stays in the funnel's full count, is quoted beside the anchor and is kept out of the sum and the tier.
- The Market gap cell is the working's division step, the difference over the anchor, with the working's own two-decimal percentage and the anchor's IDs. A market above the midpoint is a positive gap and means lagging past +3.0%; "close" stands beside a verdict only when the gap's size is between 2.0% and 4.0%.
- The tier word in a Confidence cell and in a paragraph is the word on that family's Tier line in the working, copied and never chosen again: "1 offer: Medium" in the working is "Medium: 1 full offer" in the cell. Every tier counts fully comparable outside rows only: two or more offers, High; one offer, Medium, whatever ranges stand beside it; two or more ranges and no offer, Medium under the posted-range cap; one range alone, Low. A family with partial rows only is deferred, and so is a family whose one full range has no own hire and no partial row from the band's city beside it, or is disputed by a partial accepted offer more than 5.0% away, open role or not.
- `## Deferred verdicts` is always there. With no family deferred it says so in one line and names the verdict that rests on least and what would firm it.
- The opening paragraph names only what the snapshot in hand leaves out: a family with no band, roles said to be remote and unanchored without being named, families with no own offer on record, a second currency with no rate. A remote gap is named only when the working's Remote roles line quotes the snapshot saying some roles are unanchored; where that line says none are noted, or that they are pinned to a tier, the paragraph says nothing about remote roles.
- Pool-wide skews are said once, under signal caveats: two or three bullets that name rows by ID and add no new figure.
