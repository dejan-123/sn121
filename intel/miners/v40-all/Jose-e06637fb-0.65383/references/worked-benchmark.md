## How Each Verdict Was Reached

**Mobile Engineering, Senior.** Four points came back. The Calgary Series B accepted offer at CAD 156K (X-1) and the Calgary Series B posted range of CAD 146K-CAD 174K (X-2) match on all four dimensions. The Toronto Series C range (X-3) is another metro and a stage apart, and the recruiter's "about CAD 160K" (X-4) has no date or stage, so both drop. Anchored on the offer, the CAD 151K midpoint sits 3.2% below market, past the 3% line. That is close, but Halden's own offers at CAD 157K-CAD 159K land above the midpoint and a candidate declined for more pay, so the company's own hiring settles it: lagging, not borderline. High confidence: a full offer with a full range agreeing.

**Product Marketing Manager.** The Calgary Series B offer (X-5) was reported as CAD 141K total including a stated CAD 12K bonus; subtracting it gives CAD 129K base (total to base, downward). The Series A range (X-6, CAD 118K-CAD 140K) is a round below, so it is partial and only corroborates. The CAD 127K midpoint is 1.6% under the anchor: competitive. High: the full offer has the partial range on the same side beside it.

**Solutions Consulting, Senior.** The CAD 150K-CAD 190K range (X-7) is on-target earnings with no base split, so it cannot be put on a base basis and is excluded rather than estimated. The surviving Series B base range (X-8, CAD 128K-CAD 156K) gives a CAD 142K anchor; the CAD 139K midpoint is 2.1% below: competitive, but borderline: one posted range is all that survives, so competitive and a deferral are both defensible. The 3% threshold gives competitive; a Calgary Series B senior accepted offer would confirm or flip it. Low confidence: a single posted range and nothing beside it.

**Talent Acquisition Manager.** The Series B range (X-11) is dated 2025-06, before the 2025-09 cutoff month, so it drops. What remains is a Series A range and a Series C range, each partial, sitting on opposite sides of the band: deferred.

## Signal Caveats

- Posted ranges outnumber offers 8 to 2 in the retrieved pool, so most verdicts lean on what employers advertise rather than what candidates accepted.
- Alberta does not require posted ranges, so every range here was posted voluntarily and may sit wherever the employer chose to advertise.
- Stage coverage is thin outside Series B; the one deferral comes from that gap.

## A request that is not a benchmark

Input: "Can you tell me what a product manager should earn in Calgary, and whether we're underpaying Maria? Also redesign our PM ladder into four levels." No external evidence table is attached. The reply is this, and nothing more:

# Halden Freight Software: request outside benchmarking scope

This skill benchmarks role-family pay bands against a classified external evidence table. Your note asks for three other things: a market rate lookup, a judgement on one person's pay, and a new level structure. Individual pay decisions belong with your manager and HR review process, and ladder design is a band-design exercise; neither should be read off a benchmark. If you want a benchmark, send the internal band snapshot by role family and level together with a classified external evidence table (each point's source, type, date, geography, company stage and figures), and the bands will be checked family by family.
