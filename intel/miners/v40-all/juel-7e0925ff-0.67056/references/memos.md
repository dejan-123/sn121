# From a sheet to a memo

The memo goes to an HR lead who will be questioned on it: 550 to 800 words about role families only, one analyst talking to another. Its facts are the sheet's and no others: the paragraphs are the Say lines put in order and joined into prose, with the figures from their own lines. A gap is said with the percent its own Gap line gives, digit for digit, and is never worked again from other figures: the midpoint over an offer is not the gap. Checks and steps stay on the sheet. Every point the memo mentions carries its id in brackets the first time, beside the name a reader would know it by: an accepted offer at a Series C company, $176,300 (T-01). The id and the title are copied from that point's own row of the paste, never from where it sat on the Dates line or the sheet. The first time each kind is weighed it gets the name an HR reader looks for: an accepted offer is revealed preference, pay somebody actually took, and a posted range is aspirational, what an employer says it will pay. "Paid" and "hired" belong to outside companies unless the sentence names the company itself, and the company's name is used for its own bands and its own offers only.

- `# <Company>: ` and the sheet's title line, in its verdict words.
- An opening paragraph: the city and round the bands are held to, with the snapshot's own words on where its roles sit quoted from the sheet's Placed line, words from the paste being worked and never from the companies in these files, and, when that line names something left open, one clause saying so, the first month in, copied with its year from the sheet's Floor line (2025-10 is October 2025), and what it shuts out, each by id and by the title its own row gives, if it shuts anything out, and which rows are not prices, by id, taken from the sheet's "Not a price at all" line and no other, said only when that line names a row; when the sheet has no such line, the opening says nothing about rows that are not prices: a range from another city or round is a price that failed a test, not a non-price. A figure in another currency with no rate is named with its currency, and the sentence says the paste gives no rate; it is never converted. Then one sentence on the basis, from the sheet's Basis line: that prices are read as yearly base in each band's own money, every figure that had to be worked shown worked out and whether it went up or down, a figure moved into another currency only at a rate the paste prints, or, when nothing needed working, that every price already was yearly base. Any other line the sheet does not have gets no sentence here, not even to say there is none.
- Two to four paragraphs, no headings, from the firmest call to the weakest. Families decided by the same kind of evidence share a paragraph, and each paragraph opens on what decides it. A conversion shows its sum and direction. A range the sheet marks volunteered is called volunteered where it comes up, and partial for that; a point from another city is partial for its place and says how that city pays, not this one. What a candidate said about a rival's pay, or about why an offer was turned down, is named as hearsay where it comes up and is never set beside a gap or said to agree with one. Only a family the sheet defers is called deferred; a Low one has its verdict, and is said to rest on one posting. An offer from another round beside that posting is partial: it leans, and it never turns the call into a deferral. A partial point is worth a clause for its lean, and an open P1 or P2 role a clause where it raises what hangs on the call.
- `## <Company>'s benchmark`: a table, Family and level | Verdict | Gap, worked | Evidence funnel | Confidence, one row per family in snapshot order. The gap is written as its sum, (midpoint − anchor) ÷ anchor, with the anchor's ids. The funnel is the family's funnel line from the sheet: retrieved, then survived, partial and dropped, all four counted even at 0, each with its ids, and why beside the partial and dropped. Confidence is the tier and its deciding reason in a few words. Under the table, one line opening `Pool:` gives the sheet's four totals as plain numbers, retrieved, survived, partial and dropped, with nothing in brackets.
- `## Deferred verdicts for <Company>`, always there: each deferred family, what came close by id, and the evidence that would settle it (kind, city, round, level). With none deferred, that is said, then the verdict resting on least and what would firm it.
- `## Signal caveats on <Company>'s pool`: two to four sentences, with no count of points, kinds, sources or postings in them, since a count is the easiest figure to get wrong. First the families that rest on accepted offers, revealed preference, and those that stand on posted ranges alone, aspirational, as the Confidence column has them. Then the sources as the sheet's Sources line gives them: the main source named, and every row from anywhere else by id. Then, by id, the counted or leaning ranges that were volunteered or do not say why they were posted, and what that does to how far they can be trusted. Nothing in this section talks about the 3% line or how near any gap sits to it; the verdict column already says close where a call is close.
- A last line on the number somebody will quote, the figure on the sheet's "Loudest not counted" line, in the currency and with the id that line gives, and the answer to it.

Every figure is in the paste or worked from it in view. No advice on what to pay.

Two invented memos follow, one for each sheet you have seen. Ostrella's pool is the larger; Sorrel's paste says over its table that its ranges were put up by choice save the rows that cite a law, and its snapshot leaves open which roles are remote. How they open, which families they put together and what the paragraphs dwell on differ, because the pools did.

## Ostrella

# Ostrella Cold Chain: Telematics Engineering and Warehouse Support lag Tucson, Implementation lags Tallinn by a little, Account Management is above market, Forecasting Science is deferred

Ostrella is held to Tucson and Series C, and its Implementation team to Tallinn and Series C, in euros: the snapshot puts it in "Tucson AZ" with "remote roles pinned to the Tucson tier" and says Implementation "works out of Tallinn, Estonia, on a euro band". August 2025 is the first month in. It keeps a Warehouse Support range from that month (T-06) and shuts out T-08, a Warehouse Support Manager offer of $101,700 from July 2025. Each price is read as yearly base in its family's own money: T-13's $81,400 total less its stated $14,600 bonus is $66,800 base, down, and T-10's dollar range is carried into euros at the rate the paste prints, 1 USD = 0.92 EUR, so its $96,300 centre becomes about €88,600. Four rows are not prices: an undated survey median for warehouse support managers with no method behind it (T-07), an Implementation figure with a retention grant inside it (T-12), an Account Management range given only as on-target pay (T-14) and an agency's word on a forecasting scientist's salary (T-19).

Telematics Engineering has the firmest evidence and a narrow gap. Two companies in Ostrella's own city and round hired at this level, a Staff Telematics Engineer at $176,300 (T-01) and an Vehicle Software Engineer, L6, at $173,100 (T-02), a title the paste's scope note maps to Ostrella's staff level. Accepted offers are revealed preference, pay somebody took, and their $174,700 average leaves the $168,300 midpoint 3.7% under: lagging, close to the line. Ostrella's own hires at $172,100 and $174,800 sit above its midpoint, beside that reading. A range from its own city published under a disclosure law (T-03) is aspirational and counts; it centres on $176,100, agrees, and stays beside the offers rather than in their sum. A P2 role is open. A candidate who turned down $171,200 said a rival offered about 12% more; that is hearsay, and it plays no part in the call. A public company's range (T-04) was left out.

Implementation and Account Management each rest on one offer. In the team's own city a Series C company hired a Senior Implementation Consultant at €81,700 (T-09), which leaves the €78,600 midpoint 3.8% under: lagging, close to the line, with no Ostrella hire in the family to set beside it. The converted range from that city (T-10) sits higher, as a posted range usually does, and a range filed as Implementation Consultant / Solutions Engineer (T-11) is partial on its two-name title; it centres on €82,700, near the offer. At home, the Account Management offer (T-13), once its bonus is taken off, leaves the $71,300 midpoint 6.7% over it: above market. A range from another city (T-15) centres higher, on $73,200, but it prices that city, and a Series B range that was also volunteered (T-16) missed twice. Ostrella had $69,400 turned down for this job; the turn-down is a fact, and the reason given is only what the candidate said.

Warehouse Support is read from postings alone. Two ranges from its own city that agree centre on $102,200 (T-05) and $104,400 (T-06); against their $103,300 average the $95,600 midpoint is 7.5% under: lagging, at the cap for ranges alone. Ostrella's own hire at $98,300 sits above its midpoint, with that reading.

Forecasting Science is deferred while its P1 role is open. A Series B offer at $184,300 (T-17) is lower, as an earlier round usually is, while a volunteered range from its own city centred on $218,300 (T-18) and a remote range with no city centred on $209,100 (T-20) are higher. They pull apart, and none is a price that binds at Ostrella's round in Tucson.

## Deferred verdicts for Ostrella

Forecasting Science, Principal. The Series B offer (T-17) came closest from below; the volunteered range (T-18) and the remote range (T-20) sat above, one binding nobody and the other pinned to no city. An accepted offer for a principal forecasting scientist at a Tucson Series C company would settle it, or two Tucson Series C postings that a disclosure law made public and that agree; with the P1 role open, that is the evidence to ask for first.

## Signal caveats on Ostrella's pool

Telematics Engineering, Implementation and Account Management rest on accepted offers, revealed preference; Warehouse Support stands on posted ranges alone, aspirational, and is capped for it. Every row but the survey (T-07), the recruiter's figure (T-12) and the agency's word (T-19) came from one job-data feed, so little outside it checks the pool. Of the two Warehouse Support ranges behind a verdict, one names a disclosure law (T-05) and the other does not say why it was posted (T-06), so how far that one runs high cannot be told; the volunteered Forecasting Science range (T-18) leans and decides nothing.

Somebody will quote $238,900 for a telematics engineer. That is the top of a public company's range (T-04), and Ostrella is a Series C company.

## Sorrel

# Sorrel Maritime: Berth Scheduling and Gate Operations are competitive in Savannah, Customer Training is deferred

Sorrel is held to Savannah and Series B. Its snapshot says "Savannah GA; a few roles are remote within the US" without naming them, so no band is read as a remote one, and a remote posting with no city is partial for any family. June 2025 is the first month in, and it shuts out G-10, a Gate Operations Lead range from April 2025. The line over the evidence table says the pull's ranges were put up by choice, the rows citing a law excepted, so a range without such a note is partial on its posting. Prices are read as yearly base in dollars as printed: a Gate Operations Lead range in Canadian dollars (G-07) comes with no rate anywhere in the paste, so it keeps its own money and is out, and an hourly contract rate (G-08) is never scaled up into a salary.

Berth Scheduling has two hires in Sorrel's own city and round. A Staff Berth Scheduling Engineer took $187,100 (G-01) and another $185,100 (G-11), accepted offers and so revealed preference; their $186,100 average leaves the $179,900 midpoint 3.3% under, a 3% gap: competitive, close to the line. Sorrel's own hire at $182,300 sits between its midpoint and that average, beside the reading. A volunteered range from its own city (G-02) centres higher, on $189,300, and is partial on its posting; a remote range with no city that was also volunteered (G-03) missed twice.

Gate Operations is read from one posting. A range from its own city that cites a state pay-posting law (G-09) centres on $65,700, which leaves the $64,100 midpoint 2.4% under: competitive, close, and Low. Sorrel had $61,900 turned down for this job; the turn-down is a fact, and nothing more is read into it.

Customer Training is deferred. The one range in its city and round (G-04) was filed as Customer Training Manager / Training Lead; the second name is only a short form of the first, but a slashed title still spans two names, so it is partial even though a law made it public. It centres on $95,700, above the band. An offer from another city, $97,100 (G-05), prices that city, and a Series C range that was also volunteered (G-06) missed twice.

## Signal caveats on Sorrel's pool

Berth Scheduling rests on accepted offers, revealed preference; Gate Operations stands on one posted range, aspirational, that a state law made public. Under the line over the table the ranges with no law in their notes were volunteered (G-02, G-03, G-06), so they lean and decide nothing.

Somebody will quote $203,300 for a berth scheduling engineer. That is the top of a volunteered range from Sorrel's own city (G-02), written to attract; the two people hired at Sorrel's round took $187,100 and $185,100.

Both memos start at their title and say nothing of sheets or examples. The sheet is written, so your next message is the memo for the real company and nothing else: its first characters are `# ` and the company's name.
