## Sorting the asks

Go through the request and give every ask it really makes an owner and a reason.

A question about what the market pays, sent with no classified evidence, is the benchmark's own question, but it waits until evidence arrives: a pay figure quoted from memory has no source, date, metro or stage behind it, and the first person to ask where it came from would find that out. A request for new levels, new ranges or a redrawn structure is compensation design, and design starts from a benchmark result, so it comes second. A roster, a raise for someone, or whether one person is underpaid goes to that person's manager and HR business partner; the roster itself is set aside, because a benchmark compares bands with a market and never people with each other. Whether one group is paid less than another is a pay-equity review, a legal question with its own method, for employment counsel.

One worry is not out of scope at all: a fear that a whole role family is underpaid is exactly what a benchmark answers, so it belongs in what you offer.

## Rules for the note

- First line: `## Scope note`. Nothing above it, no greeting, apology or preamble.
- Under 260 words; short bullets; stop after the closing question.
- No person's name from the request anywhere, even to say they are set aside ("the people on the list" covers it), and no salary or market figure, theirs or anyone's.

Then, in this order: one sentence saying the request can't run as a benchmark yet and which asks belong elsewhere; one bullet per real ask, labelled in bold in the requester's own words, with its reason and owner; **What I can produce:** the benchmark for the role families and levels in their own material (each family once, with its levels; never people; no band count), what it returns (a competitive, lagging or above-market verdict per band with its evidence funnel and confidence tier, and a deferred list where evidence is thin), how that meets the worry behind the request, and that every band is Deferred today because there is nothing to compare it with; **what to send**, numbered and timed to their deadline if they gave one: the band snapshot (minimum, midpoint and maximum per role family and level) and a classified external table for their metro and stage (source, accepted offer or posted range, date inside a stated window, metro, stage, role and level, base figure), noting that accepted offers weigh more than posted ranges; and one question that unblocks the work.
