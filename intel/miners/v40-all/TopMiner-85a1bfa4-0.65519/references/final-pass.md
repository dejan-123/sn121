The worksheet is settled; now turn it into the report, which is your final message. Its very first line is the `# ` title: no `---` rule, no "here is the report", nothing before it. Picture the reader: an HR lead who will be asked "why that number?" and needs the argument as well as the verdicts. Everything in the report comes from the worksheet (figures, IDs, dates, classes, anchors, gaps, verdicts, tiers), and none of the worksheet's working is pasted in. About 600 to 900 words, role families and levels only, never a person, and no figure that is not in the input or worked from it in view.

## Shape of the report

**Title line.** `# <Company>: ` followed by the headline in verdict words, for example "two bands lag Madison, one is above market, one is deferred". It is line one of the message.

**Opening paragraph, no heading.** Say what the bands are held to: the metro and funding stage, and the first month in (the cutoff month, with its year). Name by ID any row the window shuts out, and any row that is not a price at all (hearsay, an undated or method-free survey, contractor pay, a grant, OTE with no split, a currency with no rate), each with its reason. Close it with the count of outside points retrieved, the evidence table's row count, across how many role families.

**`## Reading the evidence`.** One short paragraph per family and level, firmest call first and deferred last. Open each on what decides it (the offers behind the anchor, the single range that is all there is, the line it sits near, the gap that defers it), so no two paragraphs start alike. Then give the anchor's IDs and why they qualify, and the sum that makes the gap; for an offer anchor, whether a surviving range agrees, and that it stays out; every partial or dropped point by ID with all its reasons, calling a range held out for being voluntarily posted exactly that; any conversion with its sum and direction; and last, as a separate read on where the company pays today, this family's own offers or declines and whether they agree. Say so when a row rests on one point or is close to the ±3.0% line.

**`## Benchmark table`.** One row per family and level, in snapshot order, columns | Role family / level | Band mid | Funnel | Anchor (basis) | Gap | Verdict | Confidence: deciding reason |. The funnel reads "4 retrieved → 2 survived (1 offer, 1 range), 1 partial, 1 dropped". The anchor names value, basis and IDs: "$150,000 (mean of 2 offers: P-01, P-03)", "$100,000 (1 offer: P-05)", "$110,000 (1 range midpoint: P-08)"; one anchor never mixes offers with ranges. A close gap carries the word in the verdict cell ("Lagging, close"). A deferred row has "—" for anchor and gap. Under the table, one line beginning `Pool:` gives retrieved, survived, partial and dropped for the whole table.

**`## Deferred verdicts`.** For each deferred row: what came nearest, by ID, why it could not decide, and the sourcing brief that would settle it (role, level, metro, stage, type with accepted offers preferred, and window). With nothing deferred, one line saying so and naming the verdict that rests on least.

**`## Signal caveats`.** Two to four bullets, each one skew across the whole pool, stated once and never a repeat of a row paragraph: which families rest on accepted offers (revealed preference) and which on posted ranges alone (aspirational, and capped for it); one dominant source; a thin offer sample; a mix of stages or metros; which ranges the Notes mark as posted under a pay-transparency mandate and which as voluntarily posted, the latter self-selected advertising from employers proud of their pay. Give mandate status as the Notes state it, never from memory.

**Close, no heading.** One short paragraph on what to act on first, in the order of the open-role priority tiers, a lagging band on a critical-path role at the front. Propose no new band figures.

## A finished report (invented company and figures)

# Quillon Marine: Hull Engineering and Field Service lag Madison, Technical Recruiting is above market, Simulation is deferred

Quillon Marine's bands are held to Madison WI and Series C, and March 2026 is the first month in; no row falls before it. Two rows are not prices at all: a figure a recruiter mentioned for a field service lead (P-07, hearsay) and a recruiting manager range given only as on-target pay (P-09, OTE with no base split). That leaves 11 outside points retrieved across four role families to be weighed.

## Reading the evidence

**Hull Engineering, Senior** rests on the firmest evidence in the pool. Two Madison Series C companies hired at this level, at $140,000 (P-01, 2026-05) and $160,000 (P-03, 2026-08); accepted offers are pay somebody took, and their mean of $150,000 leaves the $130,000 midpoint 13.3% short, (130,000 − 150,000) / 150,000. A surviving range of $130,000–$170,000 (P-02, midpoint $150,000) agrees and stays out of the anchor because offers exist. P-04 is dropped as a Milwaukee offer, another metro. Quillon's own two hires this year both landed above the midpoint, the same pressure the outside offers show.

**Field Service, Lead** turns on one offer just past the line. P-05 at $100,000 (2026-06) puts the $96,000 midpoint 4.0% below: lagging, but close. P-06, a $90,000–$130,000 range at the right metro and stage, is held out as a partial because its Notes mark it voluntarily posted; it is advertising and would overstate the market. With a single point behind it, this row could move on the next offer.

**Technical Recruiting, Manager** has one range and nothing else. P-08, posted at $100,000–$120,000 (midpoint $110,000), puts the $120,000 midpoint 9.1% above it, (120,000 − 110,000) / 110,000: above market, and Low, since one range is all the support there is.
