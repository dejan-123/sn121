# The benchmark memo: layout and a finished example

The memo is written in the order the work happens, so nothing is claimed before the part that establishes it:

1. A `## ` title with the company, its metro and its stage, and no verdicts. Then a basis paragraph of facts the input already gives: what the bands are held against, the first month in, how many points the pool holds, which teams sit elsewhere or in another currency.
2. `### Evidence screen — <Company>`: one row for every external point, in ID order. Fill the test cells left to right and stop at the first ✗; the call names that test. Under the table, a `Pool:` line counting what counts and what fell out, by reason, adding up to the number of rows.
3. `### Benchmark — <Company>`: one row per role family in snapshot order, read off the screen: funnel, anchor with its IDs, gap written as a sum, verdict, confidence with its deciding reason.
4. `**What decides the calls.**`: two short paragraphs of reasoning, firmest evidence first, naming offers as revealed preference and ranges as aspirational the first time each is weighed.
5. `### Deferred verdicts — <Company>`: only families whose verdict is Deferred. With none, one sentence naming the thinnest call and what would firm it.
6. `### Signal caveats — <Company>'s pool`: two or three bullets about the whole pool, never one per row.
7. One closing sentence on the figure most likely to be quoted back and why it does not price the job.

Below are an invented input and the memo it should produce. Copy the order of work and the shape; take no fact from it.

**What decides the calls.** Firmware and Mobile rest on revealed preference, pay that candidates actually accepted, and Kestrel's own hires closing above both midpoints say the same thing from inside: with a P1 firmware seat open, finalists will keep landing in the upper half of a band whose midpoint sits 4.5% under the market. The declined mobile candidate's "12% higher" rival offer is hearsay; it confirms the direction without sizing the gap. Treasury runs the other way: a Toronto hire at C$98,700 and a Toronto range centred on C$99,600 both sit below the C$103,800 midpoint, and the Minneapolis treasury range stays out because it is another city and another currency, with no rate to bridge them.

Field Service and Technical Writing stand on posted ranges alone, aspirational evidence of what employers will advertise. Field Service's single volunteered range points to a modest lag; Technical Writing's two ranges straddle its midpoint, and K-12, dated in the window's first month, counts like any other, though its dual title may cover a broader documentation job.

### Deferred verdicts — Kestrel

- **Supply Planning (Lead), P1.** K-14 is one round earlier and lands lower, as an earlier-stage employer would; K-15 is a public company with an RSU-heavy package; K-16 prices the planner level. Kestrel's own $126,800 hire can test a verdict but cannot make one. One accepted offer or posted range for a Supply Planning Lead at a Minneapolis Series C company, dated March 2027 or later, would settle it.

### Signal caveats — Kestrel's pool

- Ranges outnumber offers among the survivors, and two of them were posted voluntarily, which tilts the advertised side toward employers keen to show their pay.
- All but two points come from one job-data feed, so any skew in its coverage runs through every verdict.
- Field Service Operations is missing from Kestrel's own offer list, so that call has no internal test.

The figure most likely to be quoted back is the public company's $149,800 for a Supply Planning Lead (K-15); it prices a listed employer's pay mix, not a Series C lead.

## Reads that are easy to get wrong

- The window column names the first month in at its head and compares every point's month with it. A point dated in that month counts; only earlier months fall out.
- Remote passes only with an anchor the input states; remote with no metro declared fails place, however close its figures look.
- The stage column names the company's own stage at its head and counts steps from it. A point at the company's own stage is never "one below".
- The company's own offers appear only in the test and the confidence reason. They never get an ID, a funnel count or a seat in the anchor.
- One offer with a surviving range but no own hire is Medium, not High; High needs two offers, or one offer backed by both a range and an own accepted offer.
- An own offer that was declined is internal evidence that the band did not land; only the candidate's claim about a rival's pay is hearsay.
- One surviving range is enough for a Low-confidence verdict. Deferral is only for a family with nothing left, and the deferred section lists only those families. When none is deferred, that section is one sentence: "None. The thinnest call is <family>, resting on <evidence>; <the evidence> would firm it."
- Write the gap as (band mid − anchor) ÷ anchor every time and let its sign pick the verdict word.
