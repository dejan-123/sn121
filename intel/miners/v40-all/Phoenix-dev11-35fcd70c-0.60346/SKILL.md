# Salary benchmarks: filter the pool before you weigh it

An evidence table looks like a market, but much of it describes another job: another city's cost tier, a company two rounds older, commission or a retention grant folded in. I test each point against the internal role, count every drop with its reason, and let only the survivors speak.

## The like-for-like test

A point is like-for-like when it matches the internal role on every line below; the first miss is the reason I record.

- **Metro.** The cost tier the company prices the role to; another metro is out, however close. A remote national range fits only a role priced nationally; if the input says some roles are remote but not which, it stays out, with a clause on whether it would change the call.
- **Stage.** Same funding stage matches; one stage away is adjacent, kept and flagged; two or more away, or public against private, is out, because cash mix, equity and scale move with stage.
- **Level and scope.** The same function at the same level is the same job, however worded. Another function's or ladder's title counts only when the input maps its scope to our level; a dual title naming our role counts. Another level is out.
- **Pay basis.** Base against base. OTE, total comp, a figure carrying a retention or sign-on grant, and contractor rates are not base. I convert only with figures the input states (a total with its bonus named: subtract the bonus): no annualised hourly rate, exchange rate from memory or assumed commission split. Anything else unconvertible is out.
- **Date.** Inside the stated window, its named boundary month counted in. Undated is out.
- **Provenance.** A figure someone can show; a recruiter's verbal remark or an unnamed survey with no method is out.

Adjacent on two lines at once (function and stage) is out.

## Offers, postings, declines

An accepted offer is revealed preference: where the market cleared. A posted range is aspirational, often stretched at the top, so I read it at its midpoint. A decline is pay pressure only when the stated reason was pay or a competing offer. Our own recent hires are not market points, but they show where our band clears: hires above our midpoint mean it sits under the market.

## From survivors to a verdict

1. **Anchor**: the median of the like-for-like points, offers at their figure and ranges at their midpoint; two points, their average.
2. **Gap**: (band midpoint − anchor) ÷ anchor, one decimal; the bands apply to the shown figure.
3. **Within ±3.0%: Competitive.** At −5.0% or lower: **Lagging**. At +5.0% or higher: **Above-market**. A clear gap stands whatever adjacent points say, unless it rests on one point and our recent hires land within 3% of our midpoint (then step 4).
4. **Beyond 3.0%, short of 5.0%:** add the adjacent points and read the gap again. Still in between, our own hiring decides: below the anchor, with our hires above our midpoint or a pay-driven decline, Lagging, otherwise Competitive at the low edge; above it, with our hires under the midpoint, Above-market, otherwise Competitive at the high edge.

Three percent is noise in a small pool and five a gap a candidate notices; an adjacent point settles near calls, never clear ones.

## Confidence tiers

Count like-for-like points only; adjacent points never raise a tier. One accepted offer is a data point; two that agree are a market.

- **High**: two or more like-for-like accepted offers agreeing in direction (all above our midpoint, all below, or all within 3%); postings can add, never stand in.
- **Medium**: two or more like-for-like points with at least one accepted offer, or two or more like-for-like postings backed by our own hiring (a hire above the midpoint, a pay-driven decline).
- **Low**: a single like-for-like point, or postings with nothing from our hiring behind them.
- **Deferred**: no like-for-like point survives. No verdict, and no direction drawn from leftovers.

Give each tier its reason, as in "Low: two postings, no offer".

## Pool-wide caveats

Some skews touch several rows, so I write them once and never per row: the share of posted ranges against offers, whether postings were mandated or voluntary (self-selected employers), one source behind most points, currencies, and how many kept points are adjacent-stage. I count only points that fed an anchor. A direction must show in the pool: posting midpoints above same-family offers mean postings run high, so lags may read deeper; if mixed, no direction. A stated mandate rule covers every kept point in its jurisdiction; import none.

## Edge cases that change the call

- **Two levels of one family** are two rows; each point counts only in its own level's funnel, and a staff point never stands in for a senior band.
- **A missing band.** If the snapshot gives no band for a family, or only individual salaries, the internal side is missing: the row is Deferred with "no band supplied", and I never build a band from salaries. A blank midpoint with min and max given is (min + max) ÷ 2, shown.
- **Hires above the band maximum** mean the band no longer reaches the market for that role; the paragraph says so.
- **A load-bearing exclusion.** When keeping a point that failed one line would change the verdict, the paragraph says so in one clause; otherwise nothing, and never in the opening paragraph.
- **The same posting twice** (same employer, title and range) counts once.
- **No window stated.** Say which dates were treated as current, and why.
- **Counting stages.** Step along seed, Series A, B, C, D, E and later growth: B to E is three stages.
- **Say why in the job's terms.** A listed company fails because liquid stock changes its whole pay mix; a contractor rate because it is not employment pay; a retention grant because it is a one-off.
- **Priority roles.** A lagging P1 family leads the opening paragraph; a deferred P1 family leads the deferred section.

## When the input is not a benchmark

With no classified external evidence there is nothing to test, and a market salary from general knowledge is an unsourced figure that ends up in a board deck. A request to redesign bands or add levels is structure work that uses a benchmark's anchors, so it comes after one. Then the reply is a note of 180 to 280 words with the same title start ("# [Company] compensation benchmark: not built yet, …") in four bolded parts: what was asked and what arrived (individual salaries are not bands); why no verdict can come from it, including on any specific worry raised; what happens to the redesign; and exactly what to send, naming their own roles, levels, metro and stage, and that it follows by their stated deadline; invent no cutoff day. Answer only the requests they made. No table, no figure that is not theirs.

## The reply

Read `references/worked-benchmarks.md` once with read_reference. Then the benchmark is your whole reply, and its first characters are `# ` and the company name. Nothing comes before it: no line about this skill, the reference or your plan. Write to the comp reader as a colleague, in plain sentences: "your last three hires all needed more than the midpoint", not rule labels such as "zone test applies", nor this skill's phrases ("joins as adjacent", "hiring decides", lines, edges).

- `# [Company] compensation benchmark: [headline]`, the headline naming the calls that matter.
- An opening paragraph of three or four sentences: the basis (metro tier, stage, base pay, window from the retrieval date), the pool count with its ID range (in, kept, out), and the sharpest calls, any lagging priority open role first.
- `## Benchmark table`, one row per role family in the snapshot: Role family (level) | Band midpoint | Market anchor | Gap | Verdict | Confidence | Evidence funnel. Short cells: the verdict in a few words, the tier with a brief reason, the gap that decided, the funnel as counts ("4 in → 2 kept, 2 out") plus the other gap when there is one ("with adjacent: −1.3%"). A deferred family keeps its row with "Deferred" as verdict and confidence.
- `## What sits behind each call`: a paragraph of two or three sentences per family, the way an analyst explains a call to a colleague: the kept points with type, date, metro, stage and figure (adjacent ones named as such), each exclusion and its reason in a few words, any conversion with its arithmetic, the anchor's figures in order and whether offers or postings carried it, and each own hire or decline with its distance from the midpoint ("$7,000 above it"), or their absence named as a gap. A deferred family gets one sentence pointing below, and its points appear only there.
- `## Deferred verdicts`: per deferred family, why nothing like-for-like survived, the leftover points as plain facts with no direction drawn, and the exact evidence that would settle it (metro, stage, level, type). If none, one sentence saying so.
- `## Signal caveats`: the pool-wide skews as at most four short bullets.

Keep figures in the band's own currency. Every number is in the input or computed from it in view. Four or five families run 500 to 750 words; cut method narration first.
