# Sent replies: the e-bike maker, the marine-sonar maker, and a note where no chart can be drawn

Nothing on this page is real; companies, cities, figures and dates were made up. Replies one and two are what each reader received after the matching sheet in `references/sounding-sheets.md` had been written and closed in a message of its own. Reply three answers a request that needed no sheet. Read them for shape and length, never for content. A live reply keeps this shape:
- a `## ` title carrying the company and its verdicts in words; under it an opening paragraph with the home metro and round, the opening month and the rows dated ahead of it, the rows that set no price, and every conversion as a sum with its direction;
- two to four paragraphs of argument, strongest call first, each opening on the point that decides it; accepted offers are called revealed preference and posted ranges aspirational where each is first weighed, a range offered by choice is called partial, a partial's lean takes a clause, and hearsay is called hearsay;
- a single table, its gap cell showing the sum with the anchor's IDs and its funnel showing all four piles by ID, followed by a `Pool:` line taken from the sheet's totals;
- Deferred Verdicts in every reply, and Signal Caveats spoken once for the whole pool in plain words that name families and sources, with no tallies and no row IDs;
- a closing line on the loudest figure that was dropped;
- and for a request the chart cannot serve, a short note: a judgment, what the paste shows, what to send.

The request in hand supplies every company, city, title, figure and date; none is borrowed from here.

## Reply three: a raise for one person and a going-rate question, with no market evidence

From the general manager at Corrie Glasshouses, a Series A greenhouse grower with 70 staff in Lexington KY: "Our head grower says she's underpaid and wants a raise to market. What do head growers make around here, and how big a raise should she get? Our staff list with salaries and our bands are below; we haven't collected any outside pay data." Bands given (minimum / midpoint / maximum): Head Grower $52,800 / $57,400 / $62,500; Grower $35,200 / $38,300 / $41,700; Packhouse Operative $31,900 / $34,700 / $37,800. The staff list names nine people: one head grower inside her band, five growers of whom one is paid above the Grower maximum, and three packhouse operatives, all inside theirs. No evidence table came with it, so no sheet was kept.

The note:

## Corrie Glasshouses: a single raise and a going-rate question, not a benchmark

You asked what head growers earn in Lexington and how large a raise your head grower should get. A benchmark can answer neither. It sets role-family bands against classified outside evidence, and the request brought none, so any going rate quoted here would be invented; and the size of one person's raise turns on her record and your pay policy, which a benchmark never judges.

What the paste does show, by family and level rather than by name: the head grower's pay sits inside the Head Grower band; one of the five growers is paid above the Grower maximum of $41,700, which is worth a look on its own; the three packhouse operatives all sit inside their band.

To benchmark the Head Grower, Grower and Packhouse Operative bands, send:
- the evidence, one line per point: its source, accepted offer or posted range, the month, the role and its level, the metro, the employer's funding round, the figure with its pay basis, and whether a range was posted under a law or by choice;
- the home metro and any roles with no metro tie, the months the evidence may cover, and the date it was pulled;
- Corrie's own offers in that window, accepted or declined, with any reason given.

With those, each family gets a verdict, a gap and a confidence tier; the raise itself stays a decision for Corrie.
