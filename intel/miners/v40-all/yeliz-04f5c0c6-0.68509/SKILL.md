# Only soundings that survive may fix a band; the struck ones stay visible

Treat each evidence row as a depth some other ship took in unfamiliar water: it goes on the chart only once it proves it was measured from this band's datum. The reply is a short memo that argues before it tabulates: which families can be fixed on a price somebody accepted, which only on an advertisement, which not at all, and why. Each row charts one job at one level; no person is ever charted. Only classified evidence read against an internal snapshot makes a chart; anything else gets the short note further down.

## Reading the home port

From the snapshot I take the home metro and any roles it pins elsewhere or frees from a metro; the funding round; the first month the window admits, in the input's own words ("before June 2024" makes 2024-06 the first month in and 2024-05 out); each band's currency and basis; open requisitions; and each family's own offers. An internal number a call needs and the snapshot omits is named as a gap, never invented.

## The kind of each point

An accepted offer is revealed preference: money a hire agreed to. A posted range is aspirational: the pay an employer advertises, drawn wide where a law forces it into the open and pitched to attract where it is volunteered. Both may count, but they never share an average. Nothing else sets a price: word of mouth, a survey naming neither date nor method, an hourly contractor rate, a sum folding in a retention or buy-out award, on-target earnings with no base split, a package weighted to equity or to cash; each drops whatever else it passes.

## Four tests: same, a step off, or out

- Place. Home metro, or remote pay the input pins to it or a role it names as metro-free: same. Remote or national with no city: a step off. Any other city: out, accepted offers too.
- Stage. Rounds run Seed, Series A, B, C, D, E. None apart: same. One apart: a step off. Two or more apart, or a listed company: out.
- Scope. Identical duties and level, or a title the input links to that level: same. A neighbouring specialty, one level away, or a title leading with another job's name and adding the family's after a slash: a step off.
- Posting, ranges only. Posted under a disclosure law, or with nothing said about why: same. Marked as voluntarily posted, in its own row or by a line above the table covering every range its row does not exempt: a step off, since an advertisement written to attract binds nobody.

No step off: the point counts. One step off: partial. Two steps off, or any out: dropped. Undated, or dated before the first month in: dropped. Each drop is reported by ID with its reason and counted.

## Weighing what counts

The anchor comes from a single kind of evidence: counted offers, averaged when there are several; only if no offer counts, the centres of the counted ranges, averaged. A counted range beside an offer is quoted next to the anchor, never added in. Partials join no average. Each partial is read for its expected lean (an earlier round tends to pay less, a later one more, an advertisement runs high): falling the way its lean predicts, it adds nothing; on the far side, it earns a sentence.

## Taking the fix: verdict and confidence

Gap: anchor less midpoint, in money and as a percentage of the anchor; positive means the market pays more than the band. Inside 3% on either side the band is Competitive; past it, a positive gap reads Lagging and a negative one Above-market. Three percent is a convention, so any gap from 2% to 4% carries the word close beside its verdict. Confidence follows what counted, never partials or own hires:
- High: two or more counted offers.
- Medium: one counted offer, or two or more agreeing counted ranges with no offer.
- Low: one counted range and no offer; it keeps its verdict even beside a partial offer.
- Deferred: nothing counts, or the snapshot omits the band figure the call needs. Two partials pulling opposite ways mean wait, never an average.

## One basis for every number

1. Before comparing anything, show each conversion as a sum with its direction, e.g. $91,500 total cash − $7,000 stated allowance = $84,500 base, down. No stated base, no figure: splitting a bundle by guesswork is dead reckoning.
2. A currency is crossed only at the rate printed in the input (1 A = x B: multiply A by x for B, divide B by x for A), always carrying the evidence into the band's currency. With no printed rate, a figure is compared only within its own currency.
3. Contract rates are never scaled up into salaries; sign-on and relocation sums are left out entirely.
4. If nothing needed converting, the opening paragraph says the basis was checked and is already annual base.

## Grain and caveats

Rows go family by family and level by level; individuals never get rows and levels never merge. The company's own offers never enter the anchor or the counts: they test the verdict, and a reason given for a turn-down is hearsay, never sized. A deferral is a finished call, priority families included: partials are never promoted and other cities never borrowed to force one. Signal caveats speak once for the whole pool and name things rather than tally them: which families rest on offers and which on ranges alone, why the ranges were posted, one source dominating, a priority requisition resting on a single point. A count is the figure most easily got wrong.

## When the ask is off the chart

A raise for one person, a bare market-rate lookup, a band redesign or an equity audit across staff is no benchmark. Write a short note that says the benchmark cannot answer, with one or two sentences of reason; does what the paste genuinely allows, summed by family (for instance an offer above a band maximum); and lists exactly what to send so a benchmark can follow. A roster of named people is described in prose by family and level, never as a list of people or a table, and the note says benchmarks judge no individual's pay. No bare refusal, no invented market figure.

## Shape of the reply

1. `## ` title: the company and its verdicts in words, no numbers.
2. Opening paragraph: the home metro and round every band is measured from, the opening month and the points it struck by ID, the rows that set no price by ID, and each conversion or exchange rate applied.
3. Two to four paragraphs without headings, firmest call first, each opening on what decides it. Offers are named revealed preference and ranges aspirational the first time each is weighed; a volunteered range is called volunteered, so partial; a partial gets its lean in a clause; hearsay is named as hearsay; an open P1 or P2 role is flagged where it raises the stakes.
4. One table: Family and level | Verdict | Gap, worked | Funnel | Confidence. The gap cell shows (anchor − midpoint) ÷ anchor with the anchor's IDs; the funnel gives retrieved, counted, partial and dropped, all four even at zero, with IDs and a reason beside each partial and drop; confidence gives its tier and reason. Under it, a `Pool:` line with the four totals.
5. `### Deferred Verdicts`, always present: each deferred family, its nearest points by ID, and the sounding that would chart it (offer or range, city, round, level); with none deferred, say so and name the verdict resting on least.
6. `### Signal Caveats`: two to four sentences on the whole pool, never per row, naming families and sources, with no tallies and no row IDs.
7. A last line on the loudest dropped figure: the highest figure on any dropped point, its ID, and why it does not price this job here.

Length: about 600 to 850 words for a memo, table included; 150 to 250 for a redirect note.

## Order of work

1. Off the chart: open `references/charted-replies.md` with read_reference, follow its third case, write the note.
2. Otherwise make one read_reference call, for `references/sounding-sheets.md` alone; it shows a working sheet and supplies no facts. Keep `references/charted-replies.md` shut until the sheet exists: opened in the same call, it leaves the sheet no message of its own, and the sheet then spills into the reply.
3. Write the sheet for the request in hand as one plain-text message, in the order that file shows: every row's month against the first month in, and every row's Notes copied as printed, come before any family is worked. End it with "Sheet closed; the reply follows." and, in that same message, make the read_reference call for `references/charted-replies.md`. The sheet is working paper and never part of the reply.
4. Send the reply as its own message, built from the sheet alone: its first characters the `## ` title line, nothing above it, no remark about files or method, no `---` rule.
