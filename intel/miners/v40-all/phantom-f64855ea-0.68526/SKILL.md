# What market evidence can and cannot say about a pay band

A pay benchmark is only as good as the points that were let into it. Most bad ones fail the same way: a big-city range from a company three rounds further on, a recruiter's remark, a contractor's rate and a package with a bonus folded in all land in one average, and the average looks precise. A careful analyst judges each point before looking at what it would do to the answer, compares only what survives, and lets the weakest link set the confidence. Where nothing comparable survived, the honest result is a deferral that names the missing evidence, and that is worth more than a verdict in every row.

Two kinds of evidence sit in every pool and they do not weigh the same. An accepted offer is a price somebody paid. A posted range is an opening position: employers stretch the top, a transparency law pushes them to publish wide good-faith bands, and a voluntary posting is also an advertisement. So an offer sets the number by itself, and a range is never averaged into it: the range supports it, contradicts it, or, when no offer survived, stands in for it with a ceiling on confidence.

## Which points are let in

Comparability has four sides, and each is scored on its own.

- **Geography.** The same metro, or the anchor the input declares, matches. Another metro in a comparable cost tier is partial. A different cost tier or another country fails, and so does a remote role with no declared metro: it cannot be placed anywhere, so it is not even partial.
- **Company stage.** Along seed, A, B, C, D, E, public, the two neighbours of the company's own stage are partial, its own stage matches, and everything else fails. The neighbours are named once, in the ground rules, and the ledger goes by them.
- **Role scope.** The same role and level matches, and so does a different title whose note maps it to that level, or a compound title that names the role. A neighbouring specialism is partial; a different or unknown level fails.
- **Posting type.** An accepted offer or a posted range matches. Hearsay, an unnamed survey, or anything that is not ordinary employment pay fails.

A point is **full** when all four match, **partial** when one is partial and the rest match, and **dropped** when two are partial or any fails. Three things drop a point whatever its scores: no date; a date before the boundary month the input prints (write both as year-month: "before October 2031" keeps 2031-10 and everything later and drops 2031-09 and everything earlier); and pay of another kind, such as a retention or acquisition grant, an hourly or contractor rate, on-target earnings, or an equity-heavy package with no base stated. The reason is always named. A contractor rate is never annualised and a split is never guessed.

Everything is compared as annual base in the band's own currency. A figure is converted only with a number the input supplies, with the working and its direction shown ("total to base: total − bonus = base"); with no rate or split supplied, the point is dropped instead.

## What the survivors can carry

Only full points set the anchor: the offer, or the average of several offers; with no offer, the posted midpoint, or the average of several midpoints. Partial points are context and never move it. The anchor is set beside the company's own band midpoint, and the evidence decides, not a formula: the band is **lagging** when the surviving pool sits clearly above that midpoint, **above-market** when it sits clearly below, and **competitive** when the difference is too small for a pool this size to mean anything. As private judgement, a difference of up to about 3% of midpoint is that small and anything past it is clear; the document gives the two figures, the money between them and that as a percentage of midpoint. It also says when the anchor is beyond the band's maximum or minimum.

A verdict is **deferred** when no full point survives (partial points cannot carry one, whether or not they agree), when full points of the same kind put the band on opposite sides of the line, or when the internal band is missing. The missing item is named, never filled in.

Confidence is counted, not felt: the funnel prints how many full offers and full ranges there are, and the tier is looked up from those two numbers, however well the figures happen to agree. Two or more full offers that agree: **High**. Exactly one full offer: **Medium**, and ranges that agree with it do not raise it. No offer and two or more full ranges that agree: **Medium**, the ceiling for posted evidence. No offer and one full range: **Low**. Where an offer and a full range point different ways, one tier lower. The reason given is that count.

The company's own offers are its side of the story, not the market's: never counted, never averaged, never beside an anchor. They appear once, in the last caveat, as hiring experience that fits a verdict or sits against it.

Partial points still say something. Points leaning both ways are themselves a reason the anchor should not be moved.

## When it is not a benchmark

A benchmark needs outside evidence that is already classified (source, type, date, place, stage, figure) and bands by role family and level. One person's pay, a raw "what does this role earn" lookup, designing or splitting bands, and a pay-equity audit belong elsewhere, and a list of named people with salaries is not a set of bands. If either input is absent or the request is one of those, the whole reply is a redirect of at most 180 words that sends each ask to where it belongs says that a list of people's salaries shows what some are paid and not the range set for a role, and lists what to send, with no table, no person named, no salary or example figure of any kind, and no view on whether anyone is underpaid. A real benchmark with one side question gets the benchmark, and the side question is sent away in a line.

## The reply

The document is also the place where the thinking is done: points are judged in its ledger, the verdicts are read off its table, and its notes say why. Drafting any of it ahead of the document would only say everything twice, so the first message after the references is the document, from its title on. Its parts, in order, the last two headings finished in words that belong to this company:

- `# <Company>: compensation benchmark`, then the basis in two or three short lines: the input's own words for the window and, from them, the first month kept and the last month dropped, home metro, own stage and its two partial neighbours, currency and conversions, how confidence is counted.
- `## Evidence ledger`: one row per point, headed by its family and level; each side shows its fact with a mark, ✓ matches, ~ partial, ✗ fails, the stage mark copied from the basis; a range says whether it was mandated, voluntary or unstated; then its class and reason, and its base figure (a range as its worked midpoint) with < or > the company midpoint. The class is final and everything after copies it. Two levels inside one family are two separate families, and a point belongs to the level its title names.
- `## Benchmark table`: a row for every family in the snapshot, open P1 roles first, each cell read off the one before it: the funnel (the family's IDs, then where each went, with its count), what the verdict rests on (built only from the IDs under full offers, or under full ranges when there is no full offer, set against the company midpoint, saying whether the market is higher or lower and by how much), the verdict (market higher, lagging; market lower, above-market), the confidence. A row with no full offer and no full range rests on nothing and is Deferred.
- `## How the evidence was weighed`: the heart of the document, a paragraph of two or three sentences for every family, in the table's order. An analyst explains to a head of people which point carries the verdict and why it deserves to, what supports it, why each other point is out or only context, and what a careless reader would have concluded from it. Each family has its own story; no paragraph is built like the one before. It repeats the table's calls, changes none, and compares only what the ledger's < and > compare. The company's own offers are not in it.
- `## Deferred verdicts: <which, or none>`: each deferred family, the gap in the evidence, what would close it; with none, the family that came closest and the one point that would firm it up.
- `## Signal caveats: <the main skew>`: three short bullets on the whole pool: which points are accepted offers and which ranges mandated or voluntary, listed by ID before they are counted, and how that skews the reading; source and thinness; and, last, anything in the company's own offers that bears on a verdict, marked as outside the pool.

Role families, never individuals; every figure from the input or worked from it in view; nothing guessed about trends or causes and no pay figure recommended; under about 5,500 characters. Read `references/worked-benchmark.md`, then `references/before-sending.md`; both apply to the redirect too.
