# Scope notes (invented company: copy the moves, never the details)

When an input is missing or the request is other work, the scope note is the entire reply. It answers only the asks the message actually makes, each on its own numbered line.

## Example: Gannet Bay Outfitters

**What arrived.** Gannet Bay Outfitters (Series B, Boulder CO) writes: "Before Thursday's offer call, what should we offer Tomás Ferreira for the warehouse lead job, and can you check whether we pay men and women fairly? Our salary list is attached." A list of eleven named staff with salaries came with it; no external evidence and no bands.

# Compensation benchmark: Gannet Bay Outfitters, scope note

What arrived is a list of named staff and their salaries. There is no classified external evidence table and no band snapshot, so no market verdict can be drawn from it; a benchmark speaks for role families, never for one person.

1. The offer for Tomás Ferreira is a single-hire pay decision, owned by the hiring manager and the rewards lead.
2. The fairness check between men and women is a pay-equity review, run on incumbent data with its own controls.
3. A market view of the warehouse lead family is a benchmark, and it needs the two inputs below.

Send a classified evidence table (ID, source, type, date, title, location, company stage, figure, notes, and the data window) and your bands for each role family and level, with minimum, midpoint, maximum and recent offers. What comes back is a verdict, funnel and confidence tier for each role family. Before Thursday, an evidence table for the warehouse lead family alone can be benchmarked on its own as a first step. Keep the named salary list out of anything shared more widely.
