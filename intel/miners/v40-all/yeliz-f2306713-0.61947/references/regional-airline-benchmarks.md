# Three charted passages: pay benchmarks for invented regional carriers and ground handlers

Every carrier, ground handler, airport, figure and date below is made up. The replies are kept for their shape and their length: how a finished benchmark is laid out, how much each table cell carries, and how a note reads when the request is not a benchmark at all. Each reply opens on its very first characters with its `## ` title, with nothing above it. What a live reply takes from them:
- a title with the company's name and what was benchmarked, then a short paragraph on the home metro, funding round, window, the day the pool was gathered, the pay basis and the pool count split into full, partial and dropped;
- a Point-by-Point Grading list right after that paragraph, one line per point (its family, offer or range with the figure, the dimension that settled it, and where it landed), where all the grading is done;
- one table row for each family at each level, its funnel running retrieved → full → partial → dropped by ID, with a one-word reason for each drop;
- range midpoints worked on the page, full offers leading the anchor, partials only backing or weakening it, and each conversion written as arithmetic with its direction;
- a gap in money and in percent of the anchor, a verdict, and a confidence cell carrying the reason that settled it, with ranges alone never above Medium;
- a Deferred Verdicts entry for each uncharted family, giving what knocked out every point, the partials that pulled apart, or the internal figure that never arrived, and the sounding that would chart it; Signal Caveats spoken once for the whole pool;
- for a request outside the chart, a short note that gives a judgment, what the paste does show, and a precise list of what to send.

Nothing else crosses over: no company, airport, title, figure or date. Each of those comes from the request being answered, never from this file.

Revenue Accounting holds inside its band on one offer, and Crew Scheduling on two points that agree.

## Case three: an offer approval with no market evidence

What the inflight director at Marrowby Island Air sent. Marrowby is a Series A island-hopping airline with 85 staff at Bangor ME. "Can you sign off on this offer? We want to hire an Inflight Training Manager at $98,500 base plus a $7,500 sign-on, and our chief of inflight says the band is too low, so tell us whether to lift the maximum to $104,000. Our bands are below; we haven't pulled any market data yet." Bands given (minimum / midpoint / maximum): Inflight Training Manager $81,000 / $88,500 / $96,000; Crew Scheduling Supervisor $67,000 / $73,000 / $80,000; Station Manager $84,000 / $92,000 / $100,000. No evidence table came with it.

The reply:

## Marrowby Island Air: an offer approval and a band change, not a benchmark

You asked me to approve a $98,500 base offer with a $7,500 sign-on for one Inflight Training Manager candidate, and to say whether the band maximum should rise to $104,000. This benchmark cannot answer either question. It places role-family bands against classified outside evidence, and none came with the request; it also answers for families, never for one person's offer, and a new maximum is band design, a decision for whoever owns compensation.

What your bands do show: the proposed $98,500 base sits $2,500 above the Inflight Training Manager maximum of $96,000 and $10,000 above its $88,500 midpoint. Whether that is the market's price or a premium is exactly what evidence would decide. The sign-on is a one-off payment and stays out of any base comparison.

To benchmark the Inflight Training Manager, Crew Scheduling Supervisor and Station Manager bands, send:
- the evidence, a line for each point: its source, offer or posted range, when, the role and its level, the metro, the employer's round, and the figure with its pay basis;
- the home metro and any roles with no metro tie, plus the months the evidence may span and the date it was pulled;
- Marrowby's own offers in that window, accepted or declined, with any reason given.

With those, each band gets a verdict, a gap and a confidence tier: footing an approval or a band change can then stand on.
