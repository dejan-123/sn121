# Give a useful intake note when evidence is missing

Write to the client in plain sentences. Begin with a company-specific title, then usually three short paragraphs totaling about 120-170 words. Name what the supplied material supports, route distinct requests to the right workflow, and specify the inputs needed for a market comparison. Use actual job titles, location and maturity where supplied. A deadline sets a dependency; it does not supply evidence.

Do not print a market table, reproduce individual employee names/pay, invent a going rate or end with a generic offer to help. If the client has a valid market pool but lacks a band, use the benchmarking route for that market estimate and defer only the band-position question. The examples here are for genuinely missing market evidence.

## Example: a roster, a new level and a board deadline

Westmere Packaging, an early-stage business in Derby, supplies named employee salaries and asks whether Production Technicians are below market, whether to add a supervisor level and what to tell its board on Friday.

# Westmere Packaging: prepare the evidence before pricing the roles

The roster identifies Westmere's current jobs and pay, but it cannot establish Derby market rates or confirm that Production Technicians are underpaid. Friday's board note can accurately describe the comparison scope, missing evidence and collection plan.

The proposed supervisor level needs job architecture first: define responsibilities, autonomy and progression before attaching a market comparison. Individual adjustments belong with managers and HR compensation review. The named salary list should remain in that internal process rather than appear in a market brief.

For the benchmark, provide bands by family and level, headcounts, the pay-zone policy and recent offer outcomes. Add classified, dated Derby early-stage employer offers or postings for equivalent Technician and defined Supervisor work, with recurring base separated from other compensation. Numerical conclusions depend on receiving those inputs; the board deadline does not make an unsupported rate defensible.

## Example: an individual request without a market pool

Harbor Arts Cooperative in Dundee asks for one employee's raise and a current salary figure for a Programme Coordinator, supplying no outside evidence or bands.

# Harbor Arts Cooperative: separate the pay decision from the market question

The employee's request belongs in management and HR's compensation-review process. A Programme Coordinator going rate requires salary research; no classified outside observations or defined band were supplied for a market-position conclusion.

Define the Coordinator's responsibilities, level and Dundee pay zone, then collect dated employee offers or attributable employer ranges for comparable work and organizations. Separate recurring base from allowances, bonuses and other components.

With those observations and the internal band, a benchmark can explain the band's position and confidence. It would inform the internal review rather than decide the employee's raise. Until then, the useful output is this evidence request and workflow handoff, with no invented salary or personal-pay recommendation.

## Apply to the current request

For this wholly unsupported intake-only route, use the current company's facts and requested workflows. The next response is only its concise company-titled intake note. Do not include these fictional examples, employee details, a market table or instructions being followed. A mixed request retains its supported benchmark and identifies the separate handoff.
