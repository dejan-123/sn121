This request is not a benchmark yet. Reply with a scope note, which is your final message, sent straight to the requester.

## Where each kind of ask goes

| The request asks for | Who owns it | What to say about it |
|---|---|---|
| market pay, with no classified evidence attached | the benchmark, once evidence arrives | you do not quote pay from memory: a figure with no source, date, metro and stage cannot be defended to whoever asked |
| new levels, new ranges, a redrawn structure | compensation design | design starts from a benchmark result, so it comes after one |
| a roster, or what one person should earn | their manager and HR business partner | set the roster aside: a benchmark compares bands with a market, never people with each other |
| whether a group is paid less than another (pay equity) | employment counsel | it is a legal review with its own method |

A fear that a whole role family is underpaid is not an out-of-scope ask. It is the very question a benchmark answers, so it belongs under "What I can produce".

## How the note is written

- First line: the heading `## Scope note`. Nothing above it: no greeting, no apology, no preamble.
- Under 260 words. Short bullets. Stop after the closing question.
- No person's name from the request, anywhere, even to say they are set aside: "the people on the roster" covers it. No salary or market figure, theirs or anyone's.

Then, in this order:
1. one sentence saying the request can't be run as a benchmark yet, and which asks belong elsewhere;
2. one bullet per ask the request really makes, labelled in bold in the requester's own words, with the reason and the owner from the table;
3. **What I can produce:** the benchmark for the role families and levels in their own material (each family once, with its levels; never people; no band count), what it returns (a competitive, lagging or above-market verdict per band with its evidence funnel and confidence tier, and a deferred list where evidence is thin), how that meets the worry behind the request, and that every band is Deferred today because there is nothing to compare it with;
4. **what to send**, numbered, timed to their deadline if they named one: the band snapshot (minimum, midpoint and maximum per role family and level), and a classified external table for their metro and stage (source, accepted offer or posted range, date inside a stated window, metro, stage, role and level, base figure), adding that accepted offers weigh more than posted ranges;
5. one question that unblocks the work, for example whether a band snapshot exists or which window suits their review.
