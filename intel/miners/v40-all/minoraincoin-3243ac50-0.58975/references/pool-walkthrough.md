# Pool walkthrough: one invented case

Everything below is made up to show the procedure. Its company, labels and figures belong to this page only and are never reused in a reply. In real work, labels and numbers come from the input.

## Invented input

Series C medical-device software company, bands anchored to Raleigh NC, base pay. Window: rows dated before April 2025 are out. Retrieved April 2026.

| Family and level | Band midpoint |
|---|---|
| Quality Systems (Senior) | $112,750 |
| Firmware Engineering (Senior) | $141,250 |
| Clinical Training (Lead) | $98,250 |
| Procurement (Manager) | $104,750 |

Internal signal: one accepted Quality Systems offer at $119,750. Nothing for other families.

Eleven evidence rows, labelled by tree:

- Ash: accepted offer, Quality Systems senior, Raleigh, Series C, $118,250 base.
- Birch: posted range, same role, Raleigh, Series C, $108,250 to $128,750 base, dated April 2025.
- Cedar: posted range, same role, Charlotte, Series C.
- Dogwood: accepted offer, same role, Raleigh, Series C, dated February 2025.
- Elm: posted range, senior firmware, Raleigh, Series C, $139,250 to $159,750 base, dated June 2025.
- Fir: hourly contractor rate, senior firmware.
- Hazel: accepted offer, clinical training lead, Raleigh, Series C, "$101,750 total, includes $9,500 bonus; base $92,250".
- Ivy: posted range, same role, Raleigh, Series C, on-target earnings, no split.
- Kestrel: posted range, same role, Raleigh, Series B, $88,250 to $104,750 base.
- Larch: accepted offer, procurement manager, Raleigh, Series B, $99,750 base.
- Maple: posted range, procurement manager, Raleigh, Series C, undated.

## Working each family

**Quality Systems.** Four raw. Cedar fails geography. Dogwood fails the date window: February is before April. Birch is dated in the cut-off month, so it stays. Ash and Birch survive as full matches. The accepted offer anchors: reference $118,250. Gap = ($112,750 - $118,250) / $118,250 = -4.7%. The reference rests on an accepted offer, so the tolerance is 3%, and -4.7% is more than 3% below: Lagging. Birch's midpoint is $118,500, the same story. High: two full survivors including an offer. The internal $119,750 offer corroborates and is not counted outside.

**Firmware Engineering.** Two raw. Fir fails on kind: an hourly contractor rate is not salary and is never annualised. Elm is the only fully matched survivor, a posted range. Reference is its midpoint, ($139,250 + $159,750) / 2 = $149,500. Gap = ($141,250 - $149,500) / $149,500 = -5.5%. The reference rests only on a posted midpoint, so the tolerance is 6%, and -5.5% is inside it: Competitive. The verdict is given, but one posted range is aspirational with no offer behind it: Low.

**Clinical Training.** Three raw. Ivy fails pay basis: on-target earnings with no split. Hazel states its base, so use $92,250, not $101,750. Kestrel is one stage away: partial, context only. Reference $92,250. Gap = ($98,250 - $92,250) / $92,250 = +6.5%. Offer-based, 3% tolerance: more than 3% above, so Above-market. One accepted offer, no second full match: Medium. Using the total would have turned a 6.5% premium into a 3.4% discount, a wrong-direction call.

**Procurement.** Two raw. Maple is undated: out. Larch is an accepted offer but one stage away: partial. No fully matched survivor, so Deferred. No verdict, no gap, even though an offer exists.

## Finished reply

# Band Position Review — (company)

Anchors: Series C, Raleigh tier, base pay, window from April 2025, retrieved April 2026.

| Role family and level | Evidence funnel | Market reference | Band midpoint | Verdict (gap) | Confidence: reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quality Systems (Senior) | 4 raw -> excluded (Cedar other city; Dogwood before window) -> survivors (Ash accepted offer; Birch posted) | $118,250 (offer) | $112,750 | Lagging (-4.7%, offer-based, 3% tolerance) | High: offer plus a second full match |
| Firmware Engineering (Senior) | 2 raw -> excluded (Fir hourly contractor rate) -> survivors (Elm posted) | $149,500 (posted midpoint) | $141,250 | Competitive (-5.5%, posted-only, 6% tolerance) | Low: single posted range |
| Clinical Training (Lead) | 3 raw -> excluded (Ivy on-target, no split) -> survivors (Hazel accepted offer, stated base; Kestrel posted, stage partial) | $92,250 (offer base) | $98,250 | Above-market (+6.5%, offer-based, 3% tolerance) | Medium: one offer, second point partial |

## Deferred verdicts

- **Procurement (Manager):** 2 raw, none fully matched. Maple is undated; Larch is an offer one stage away, context only. One dated Series C Raleigh offer or posted range would settle it.

## Signal caveats

- Posted ranges make up most of the fully matched survivors; they read high and wide, so offer-free verdicts lean weakest.
- Only one internal signal exists, and it runs above midpoint.

Quality Systems is the call a quick reader gets wrong: -4.7% looks small, but offer-based verdicts allow only 3%, so it is Lagging. Firmware is Competitive at -5.5% only because posted-only references get 6%, and it rests on one posted range, so treat it as a lead and not a finding. Clinical Training looks Above-market only because the base, not the total, was used.

## Mistakes this case shows

1. Issuing a verdict from the raw pool instead of survivors.
2. Dropping Birch for its April date, or keeping Dogwood.
3. Using a total or on-target figure against a base band, or guessing the split.
4. Annualising the hourly rate.
5. Letting a partial-stage offer (Larch) drive a Procurement verdict.
6. Reading a posted range by its top.
7. Counting the internal offer as external evidence.
8. Rating Firmware higher than Low because the verdict looks settled.
9. Repeating the posted-range caveat in each row.
10. Applying the 3% tolerance to a posted-only reference, or the 6% tolerance to an offer-based one.
