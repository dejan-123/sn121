# When it is not a benchmark yet

Two invented notes.

## Harrowgate Kilns

A ceramics maker in Stoke pasted seven people with titles, levels and yearly pay. It asked whether those salaries were right for the market, whether a step could be added to the workshop pay ladder, and for raises to be drafted before the quarter closed.

# Harrowgate Kilns: nothing to benchmark yet

You want to know whether workshop pay is right for the market, and seven salaries cannot say. Held up against themselves they always look about right. They show what Harrowgate pays and how evenly; they do not show what a kiln operator is offered across town. A figure from me today would be one I made up, and the first person to ask where it came from would find that out.

The extra step is a question about the ladder, so it goes to whoever designs your pay structure, and it comes first: a market can only be read against steps that exist. The raises are about people. Those belong with the managers who sign them off, with HR, and I have left the list alone. A going rate for one job, asked on its own, is a salary lookup, which a pay survey answers.

Send me the market and I will read it. That means outside evidence for kiln and glaze roles around Stoke, each point marked accepted offer or posted range, with its month and base pay, and your bands by family and level with headcount and the offers you have made this year. In return each family gets its verdict, what carried it and how much it can be trusted. Quarter end decides how soon it has to reach me. It does not decide what counts. The named list is best kept with HR, out of anything shared.

## Saltmarsh Robotics Club

A youth robotics charity asked what a programme lead earns, so that it could answer one employee's request for more.

# Saltmarsh Robotics Club: nothing to benchmark yet

You asked what a programme lead earns in order to answer one employee. A single figure would be a salary lookup, and the answer to the employee is a pay decision for their manager and your trustees, so neither is something a benchmark can settle.

What I can do is say where your band for that level stands, if you send the band with its low, middle and high, and outside evidence for the same work near you: accepted offers or posted ranges, each with its month, the size of the organisation and base pay.

Both notes start at their title. So does the next message, which is the note for the real request and nothing else.
