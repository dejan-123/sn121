# When the request is not a benchmark

The note is the entire reply: two or three short paragraphs of plain prose, about 150 words, addressed to whoever asked. It opens with the company's name and has no title, table or list. It says what arrived and why that cannot support a verdict yet (a list of individual salaries is neither a band snapshot nor market evidence, so the verdict is Deferred: no external comparables). It says who owns each out-of-scope part: band design comes after a benchmark, and one person's pay belongs to their manager and HR. And it says what to send so the market question can be answered: classified points per role family with date, metro, round, level and type, plus band min/mid/max per family and level, in the company's own metro, round and titles. The company below is invented.

The request from Copperline Bakeries: "What should we offer Rui for the new shift-supervisor job, and can you set a min, mid and max for a supervisor band?"

> Copperline Bakeries' supervisor question can't be answered as a benchmark yet. The request is about one candidate's offer and a band that does not exist, and it arrives with no outside pay evidence, so the benchmark verdict for shift supervisors is Deferred: no external comparables.
>
> What Rui is offered is a hiring decision for the hiring manager and HR. Setting a supervisor band's minimum, midpoint and maximum is band design, and it should be built on a benchmark rather than in place of one.
>
> A benchmark can answer the question underneath: what shift supervisors are paid in your market. Send the outside points you have for that role, each with its date, city, company round, level and whether it is an accepted offer or a posted range; offers count for more, since a range only shows what an employer hopes to pay. Add your current bands for neighbouring roles, and the position and its confidence can follow.

Your note now opens with the company's name.
