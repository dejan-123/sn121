# Three sample replies (invented companies; form only)

None of these companies exists. Take the form from them: the yardstick paragraph, the sort that places every point once, the sum behind each gap, a tier with its reason, caveats said once and without family names, counts or IDs, two closing lines. Take no figure, ID, reason or phrase: in a real reply each comes from the input supplied, and the reasons are the ones its own rows give.

# Brackenridge Veterinary Partners: band benchmark, five role families

**Yardstick.** The input sets a 15-month window, so August 2025 is the cut-off month: a point dated 2025-08 is in, one dated 2025-07 is out. ## Deferred verdicts

- **Pharmacy Purchasing, Formulary Buyer.** The guide median has no date or sample, K-14 prices the lead level, and K-13 is from a Series B employer. ## Signal caveats

- The ranges whose notes say how they were posted were all published under a legal mandate; such ranges are written wide to cover any hire, so their centres read high.
- Accepted offers and posted ranges both survive here; a verdict that rests on a range alone is the softer kind, because ranges advertise and offers settle.

**What the verdicts rest on:** Emergency Medicine, Diagnostic Imaging and Client Care are anchored on accepted offers, which show what was paid; Hospital Operations rests on one posted range, which shows only what was advertised.

## Sample three: nothing to benchmark

Input for Pellham Roasters (Series A, 45 employees, Tulsa OK): "We need next year's merit budget. Our three bands are below. Can you look up what the market pays for these jobs, tell me which bands to raise, and build the merit matrix by performance rating? I saw on a salary site that head roasters make about $70k." A table of three bands follows. There is no evidence table.

The reply:

# Pellham Roasters: nothing to benchmark yet

You asked which of your three bands to raise, for market pay for those jobs, and for a merit matrix. What arrived is the bands and one figure remembered from a salary site.

A benchmark sets each band against outside pay evidence that has been classified and dated, and keeps only what is like-for-like. Here there is none: the remembered figure has no source row, date, location or level, and looking up or recalling market pay would put a guess in the place of evidence. So there is no verdict on any band and no view on which to raise.

Send an evidence table (source, type, date, title, location, employer stage, figures), the window to apply, and any offers you made in that period; the bands you have already supplied will do. The merit matrix is pay-review design, a separate job for whoever owns the review, and it comes after the benchmark.

## Writing the reply

The samples end here. Write the document for the input in front of you.

**Format: the message begins with `#`.** The title line is its first line. Nothing is written ahead of it: no "Now I will produce the benchmark table", no "Based on the skill", no step list, no rule line. The working is the document's own `## Sorting the evidence` section and appears once, there. When a half is missing, the message is the short note and begins the same way. Sample three's note began `# Pellham Roasters: nothing to benchmark yet`; yours begins with `# `, the company in front of you, and `: nothing to benchmark yet`, then three plain paragraphs with no headings or lists.
