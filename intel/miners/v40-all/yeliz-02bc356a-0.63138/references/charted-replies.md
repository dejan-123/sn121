# Finished replies: the telescope maker and the frame works, and a note for an ask with no chart

Every company, city, figure and date here is invented. The first two replies are what the reader received once the sheets in `references/sounding-sheets.md` were closed; the third is the note for a request that needs no sheet. They show form and size only. What carries over to a live reply:
- a `## ` title naming the company and its verdicts in words, then an opening paragraph: the home metro and round, the opening month and what it struck, the rows setting no price, and each conversion with its sum and direction;
- two to four argued paragraphs, firmest call first, each opening on what decides it, with accepted offers named revealed preference and posted ranges aspirational the first time each is weighed, volunteered ranges called partial, a partial's lean given in a clause, and hearsay named as such;
- a verdict read on the counted points against the 5% line, even where dropped rows would make the raw pool look far behind;
- one table whose gap cell shows its sum with the anchor's IDs and whose funnel counts all four piles by ID, then a `Pool:` line;
- Deferred Verdicts, always present, and Signal Caveats said once for the pool;
- a last line answering the loudest figure that was dropped;
- for a request outside the chart, a short note giving a judgment, what the paste shows, and what to send.

No company, city, job title, figure or date carries over. Each comes from the request in hand, never from this file.

## Reply three: a retention figure and a going-rate question with no market evidence

What the founder of Glimmerwick Candle Co. sent. Glimmerwick is a Series A maker of hand-poured candles with 48 staff in Asheville NC. "Our lead chandler, Wren Ashby, says she's underpaid and has another offer. What should we pay her to stay, and what do candle makers earn around here anyway? I've pasted everyone's name, title and salary, plus our bands. No market data yet." Bands given (minimum / midpoint / maximum): Lead Chandler $47,600 / $51,800 / $56,300; Production Associate $33,900 / $36,800 / $40,100; Wholesale Coordinator $44,200 / $48,000 / $52,200. The roster names eight people: two Lead Chandlers at $52,900 and $53,400, five Production Associates between $34,100 and $39,600, and one Wholesale Coordinator at $47,200. No evidence table came with it, so no sheet was kept.

The note:

## Glimmerwick Candle Co.: a retention figure for one person and a going-rate question, not a benchmark

You asked what to pay one lead chandler so she stays, and what candle makers earn in Asheville. A benchmark can settle neither. It places role-family bands against classified outside evidence, and none came with the request; it speaks for a family at a level, never for one person's pay, so a retention figure belongs to whoever makes pay decisions; and a going rate stated without evidence would be a guess wearing a number.

What the paste does show, read by family and level rather than by name: two Lead Chandlers, both above their $51,800 midpoint and under the $56,300 maximum; five Production Associates and one Wholesale Coordinator, all inside their bands. That places pay against Glimmerwick's own bands only; a band can look comfortable from inside and still trail the market.

To benchmark the Lead Chandler, Production Associate and Wholesale Coordinator bands, send:
- the evidence, one line per point: its source, accepted offer or posted range, the month, the role and its level, the metro, the employer's funding round, the figure with its pay basis, and whether a range was posted under a law or by choice;
- the home metro and any roles with no metro tie, the months the evidence may span, and the date it was pulled;
- Glimmerwick's own offers in that window, accepted or declined, with any reason given.

With those, each family gets a verdict, a gap and a confidence tier. What one person is offered stays a decision for Glimmerwick.
