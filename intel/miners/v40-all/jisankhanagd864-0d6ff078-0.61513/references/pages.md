**Reading rule.** The gap is the anchor less the band midpoint, held against a margin of 5% of that midpoint, or 3% where the company's own accepted offers already average more than 4% off it on the anchor's side: inside the margin is Competitive, an anchor above it Lagging, below it Above-market, and own accepted offers averaging within 2.5% of the midpoint settle the call as Competitive. ## Case four

**What arrived.** From the office manager at Pellham Awnings, a 40-person awning shop in Tulsa OK: "What does a senior installer make around here? Renata, the one we want to hire, is asking for $71,000 and I have to decide whether to match it. Can you also fold our two installation bands into one, and check whether the women here are paid less than the men? I need something for the owners' meeting on the 14th. The staff list with current pay is attached; we haven't gathered any market data." Attached: seven names, each with a job title and a salary.

**The whole reply is this note: four short paragraphs, no table, no list.**

# Not a benchmark request: Pellham Awnings

**What was asked.** A local rate for a senior installer, a decision on one candidate's ask, one band in place of two, and a pay comparison between women and men, wanted for the owners' meeting on the 14th, with a staff list and no market data.

**Why there is no benchmark.** A band verdict needs outside pay evidence that is already classified and a band per role family, and neither came with this request. A staff list shows what each person is paid, not what the market pays, so it settles no question about the market either way, and a local rate given without evidence would be a guess the owners would repeat as a finding.

**Where the other parts belong.** Where the installer band stands against the market is the one question here a benchmark can answer, once there is evidence for it. The candidate's ask sits with the hiring manager and whoever signs off pay. Merging two bands is job-architecture design, and a comparison between women and men is a pay-equity audit.

**What to send, and what can be ready by then.** For each family and level: accepted offers kept apart from posted ranges, each with its source, date, metro, company stage and an annual base figure; a band (minimum, midpoint, maximum) per role family in place of names; recent offers of your own, accepted or declined; and the window the evidence should fall in. A recruiter's word or an undated survey will not count. If the date is close, gather accepted offers for senior installers in Tulsa first, since they carry the most weight, and tell the owners the benchmark follows the evidence. Keep the named salaries out of anything shared.

## Writing the page

Each page above is the whole of what was sent, from its title to its last caveat. A row has five cells. The verdict, the gap cell and the confidence cell are the page cells that close the family's line in the audit, copied word for word: the anchor first with its distance from the midpoint, then the reading of own accepted offers, then the margin and whether the gap is inside it; the tier and what it rests on. The funnel is the one that opens that line, IDs before their count, each partial and dropped ID with its short reason. A page with no deferred family still carries the deferred heading, under it a line saying so. The own-offers section lists each family's accepted and declined offers as the snapshot gives them, a decline with its figure and any reason for it as hearsay, the candidate's own account, which is said there once and confirms no verdict, then the family's open role with its priority, or that it has none; what the offers do to the margin is worked in the family's row and is not repeated there. The caveats are the pool's own skews, four of them in this order, the first two written from the audit's two closing lines: which verdicts stand on accepted offers (revealed-preference evidence) and which on posted ranges alone (aspirational evidence), family by family as the Standing line sorts them; whether the kept ranges were posted under a mandate or by choice, as the Posting basis line has it from a line above the table or a row's note, a kept range with no note being said to have none; the single feed; and the points set aside that a reader of the raw table would notice first, each with its ID, the figure its own row gives and why it moves no verdict. They carry IDs and family names, and no tally the audit does not hold.
