## How Each Verdict Was Reached

**Data Platform Engineering, Senior.** The Melbourne Series C offer at AUD 172K (K-1) and the Melbourne Series C range of AUD 160K-190K (K-2) pass all four tests. The Sydney range (K-3) is another metro and the Series E offer (K-4) is two rounds ahead, so both drop. The AUD 166K midpoint sits 3.5% under the offer and 4.0% under Korrin's own accepted offers, which average AUD 173K; both gaps clear 2.5% on the same side, so the band lags. High: a full offer with a full range beside it.

**Customer Success Manager.** The full offer at AUD 114K (K-5) puts the midpoint 5.3% under the market, but Korrin's own hire accepted AUD 109K, only 0.9% above the midpoint. One outside offer is one person; the company's own hiring shows the band is landing, so the call is competitive. The Series B range (K-6) is a round behind and only corroborates. Another Melbourne Series C offer near AUD 114K would flip this to lagging.

**Account Executive, Mid-market.** K-7 is quoted as on-target earnings with no base split, so it has no base figure and is left out rather than estimated. K-8 came in as an AUD 152K total with a stated AUD 30K commission guarantee inside it; taking the guarantee off leaves AUD 122K base (total to base, downward). With no own offers on file, the outside gap decides alone: 4.1% under is inside the 5% line, so the band is competitive. Medium: a single offer.

**Security Engineering, Staff.** The one Series C range (K-9) is dated 2025-09, before the 2025-11 cutoff, so it drops. The Series B and Series D ranges are each one round away and only partial, and partials never anchor.

## Deferred Verdicts

**Security Engineering, Staff.** Only partial matches survive, a Series B range and a Series D range, so no gap is computed. One Melbourne Series C accepted offer or posted range for a staff security engineer, dated 2025-11 or later, would settle it.

## Signal Caveats

- Offers and ranges are close to even here (5 offers, 6 ranges retrieved), so most verdicts rest on what candidates accepted.
- Victoria does not require posted ranges, so every range was posted by choice and may sit wherever the employer wanted to advertise.
- Series C coverage is thin for security roles; the one deferral comes from that gap.

## Case two: not a benchmark

The note: "What should we offer our new head of sales? Can you also check our gender pay gap, and flatten engineering into two bands?" A staff list with salaries is attached; there is no outside evidence table. The whole reply:

# Korrin Labs: not a benchmark request

None of this can be answered as a role-family benchmark, so each part is pointed where it belongs.

1. An offer for the new head of sales is an individual pay decision for the hiring manager and your HR review.
2. A gender pay gap check is a pay-equity audit, run on the full roster by people who can see it.
3. Flattening engineering into two bands is band and level design, which comes after a benchmark, not out of one.

A staff list records what Korrin pays and nothing about comparable employers, so it can neither confirm nor rebut that anyone is underpaid.

For a benchmark, send a classified outside evidence table with accepted offers kept apart from posted ranges, each point dated and carrying its ID, role, level, metro, stage and annual base; the band minimum, midpoint and maximum for each family and level; your latest offers; and the window the data should cover. Each family then comes back with a verdict, an evidence funnel and a confidence tier, or a deferral naming what is missing.

If time is short, gather Melbourne Series C accepted offers first.
