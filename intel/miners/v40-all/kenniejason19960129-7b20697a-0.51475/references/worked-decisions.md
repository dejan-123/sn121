# Three decisions from a compensation desk

These fictional requests illustrate how to write an answer from the evidence at hand. The amounts belong only to their own cases.

## 1. Two offers exceed the band even though the advertised range overlaps it

**Question**

We make precision glass in Leeds. Our journey-level kiln technicians have an annual base band of £29,000–£35,000, midpoint £32,000. Our senior optical inspectors have £46,000–£52,000, midpoint £49,000. How are the two bands positioned? The following records concern separate employers similar to us in stage and scale, the same city, and the corresponding duties and level. They all date to this quarter.

| ID | Local pay evidence |
|---|---|
| Ash | A kiln technician accepted £39,600 annual cash, comprising base plus a £2,400 annual shift premium. Those are the only two components. |
| Birch | Another kiln technician accepted £37,800 annual base. |
| Hazel | A kiln vacancy advertised £30,500–£34,500 annual base. |
| Yew | A kiln offer was accepted at £41,000 annual cash including a production incentive. The employer did not disclose the split. |
| Elm | A senior optical inspector accepted £43,500 annual base. |
| Lime | A vacancy for a senior optical inspector advertised £41,000–£45,000 annual base. |

**Answer**

| Role family and level | Internal annual base | Verdict and decisive evidence | Confidence and reason | Evidence accounting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kiln technicians, journey | £29,000–£35,000, midpoint £32,000 | **Lagging.** Ash's £39,600 annual cash − £2,400 annual shift premium = £37,200 annual base. Birch accepted £37,800 base. The two accepted bases exceed our ceiling by £2,200 and £2,800 respectively. Hazel's advertised £30,500–£34,500 overlaps our band. Ash and Birch show the higher amounts candidates agreed to. | **High.** Two independent local accepted offers at the same level support the direction. | 4 retrieved = 3 full + 0 partial + 1 dropped (3 survived). Full: Ash, Birch, Hazel. Dropped: Yew, undisclosed incentive split. |
| Optical inspection, senior | £46,000–£52,000, midpoint £49,000 | **Above-market.** Our floor is £2,500 above Elm's accepted £43,500 base. Lime's posted ceiling of £45,000 supports the same direction. | **Medium.** Only Elm reports an accepted offer. The second employer supplies an advertised range. | 2 retrieved = 2 full + 0 partial + 0 dropped (2 survived). Full: Elm, Lime. |

**Deferred verdicts.** Both families have a direct local comparison.

**Signal caveats.** All observations fall in one quarter. They support a current comparison and leave the trend over time uncertain.

## 2. A hiring decline gives us a reason to investigate, but no external price

**Question**

We run one cold-storage depot in Cork. Shift dispatch leads have an annual base band of €44,000–€50,000, midpoint €47,000. One applicant declined our €48,000 base offer, citing shift scheduling. Depot inventory clerks have €32,000–€36,000, midpoint €34,000. Dispatch is the more urgent hire. What can we tell the operations director about each band? Use January through June inclusive. External figures below are annual base for the same local duties and level.

| ID | External observation |
|---|---|
| Harbour | February dispatch lead vacancy at a much smaller owner-operated depot, €38,000–€42,000. |
| Quay | May dispatch lead vacancy at a national chain, €53,000–€57,000. |
| Ferry | A dispatch lead accepted €46,500 at a comparable single-site operator in the preceding December. |
| Gate | January inventory clerk posting at a comparable single-site depot, €32,500–€35,500. |

**Answer**

| Role family and level | Internal annual base | Verdict and decisive evidence | Confidence and reason | Evidence accounting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dispatch, shift lead | €44,000–€50,000, midpoint €47,000 | **Deferred.** Our band sits above Harbour's €38,000–€42,000 and below Quay's €53,000–€57,000. The two peer groups give opposite answers. The applicant who declined our €48,000 cited scheduling. | **Deferred.** Neither retained employer matches our operating scale. A current accepted offer from a comparable depot would resolve the disagreement. | 3 retrieved = 0 full + 2 partial + 1 dropped (2 survived). Partial: Harbour, smaller owner-operated depot, and Quay, national chain. Dropped: Ferry, preceding December. |
| Inventory, depot clerk | €32,000–€36,000, midpoint €34,000 | **Competitive.** Gate advertised €32,500–€35,500 for the same local job. Our band covers that range and both midpoints are €34,000. | **Low.** One matched vacancy gives only an advertised price. | 1 retrieved = 1 full + 0 partial + 0 dropped (1 survived). Full: Gate, within the window from January. |

**Deferred verdicts.** For dispatch, ask for a January–June accepted base offer for a shift lead with the same responsibility at a single-site Cork depot. The urgent vacancy makes that evidence worth collecting first.

**Signal caveats.** Every surviving external figure comes from an advertisement. The ranges establish what employers were willing to publish. They leave the amounts actually agreed with candidates uncertain.

## 3. The board has payroll records and wants an individual decision

**Question**

Our museum board has job evaluations, service histories and payroll records for two conservators. It wants to know whether their pay is equitable and whether one should receive an adjustment. Can you give us a recommendation for the board paper?

**Answer**

Send the two evaluations, pay histories and documented reasons for pay differences to the person leading your pay-equity review. That review can assess the employees' circumstances and advise the board on an adjustment. If the board also wants a market comparison for conservation roles, provide the existing bands by level and dated external base-pay evidence from comparable institutions in your hiring market.
