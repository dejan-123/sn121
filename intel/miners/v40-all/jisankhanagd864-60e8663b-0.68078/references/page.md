## Case four

**What arrived.** From the office manager at Marlow Signworks, a 40-person sign shop in Omaha NE: "What does a senior fabricator make around here? Dalia, the one we want to hire, is asking for $71,000 and I have to decide whether to match it. Can you also fold our two fabrication bands into one, and check whether the women here are paid less than the men? I need something for the owners' meeting on the 14th. The staff list with current pay is attached; we haven't gathered any market data." Attached: seven names, each with a job title and a salary.

**The whole reply is this note: four short paragraphs, no table, no list.**

# Not a benchmark request: Marlow Signworks

**What was asked.** A local rate for a senior fabricator, a decision on one candidate's ask, one band in place of two, and a pay comparison between women and men, wanted for the owners' meeting on the 14th, with a staff list and no market data.

**Why there is no benchmark.** A band verdict needs outside pay evidence that is already classified and a band per role family, and neither came with this request. A staff list shows what each person is paid, not what the market pays, so it settles no question about the market either way, and a local rate given without evidence would be a guess the owners would repeat as a finding.

**Where the other parts belong.** Where the fabricator band stands against the market is the one question here a benchmark can answer, once there is evidence for it. The candidate's ask sits with the hiring manager and whoever signs off pay. Merging two bands is job-architecture design, and a comparison between women and men is a pay-equity audit.

**What to send, and what can be ready by the 14th.** For each family and level: accepted offers kept apart from posted ranges, each with its source, date, metro, company stage and an annual base figure; a band (minimum, midpoint, maximum) per role family in place of names; recent offers of your own, accepted or declined; and the window the evidence should fall in. A recruiter's word or an undated survey will not count. If the date is close, gather accepted offers for senior fabricators in Omaha first, since they carry the most weight, and tell the owners the benchmark follows the evidence. Keep the named salaries out of anything shared.

## Writing the page
