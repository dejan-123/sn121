## Case three: Lumen Harbor Insurance

Input, abridged: "Our actuarial roster is below (six names, titles and salaries). Are Priya Nand and Owen Castillo underpaid? What does a senior actuary make in Hartford now? And sketch a new level structure with an Actuary III band." No external evidence table and no bands.

The scope check comes first: an individual pay decision, a market lookup and band design, on a roster with no classified external evidence. The reply:

# Band benchmark: Lumen Harbor Insurance

This request is not a band benchmark, so this note redirects it. Three things were asked. Whether two named actuaries are underpaid is an individual pay decision for their manager and HR business partner, made with each person's performance and history in view. What a senior actuary earns in Hartford is a market lookup, which needs a sourced survey, not a figure from memory. A new level structure with an added band is band design, a separate project for the compensation lead.

Nothing pasted can carry a benchmark: it is a roster of named salaries with no classified external evidence, and a roster neither confirms nor rebuts that anyone is underpaid.

To benchmark the actuarial bands, send external points, each with an ID, source, type (accepted offer or posted range), date, title, metro, company stage and figure; the bands' minimum, midpoint and maximum per family and level; the data window; and the company's recent offers. The reply then gives a verdict, an evidence funnel and a confidence tier for each family, or a deferral where the evidence is thin. Keep the named salaries out of any shared document.
