# Benchmark position and approved-band coverage

A midpoint answers where the band is centered. Its approved endpoints answer what the band can accommodate. Work from the same evidence register and keep those two decisions separate.

## Decide what the request supports

For a roster alone, use short prose. State role-level coverage and the owner and next step for each requested action. List only the requested work. Market benchmarking needs approved min/mid/max, currency and pay basis by family, level and zone, plus classified external records with ID, source, type, observation date, role/level, location, employer stage and comparable compensation terms. Give a feasible deadline brief and make the benchmark contingent on validated inputs. Route individual decisions to compensation review, internal equity to a confidential audit and band or level design to job architecture with documented duties. Leave out tables, employee salary details and proposed grades. In mixed work deliver supported comparisons and route other work.

Define each target by family, level, pay zone, funding stage, window and compensation basis. Use the supplied midpoint. If missing, derive it from valid band endpoints and label it. When only the HQ anchors a local comparison, say its results depend on that band's applying there. Keep headcount at its reported grain. Unmapped remote populations require a pay-zone and band mapping. Currency conversion alone cannot map labor markets.

## Record admissible evidence

Assign each incoming record to one intended comparison before grading. Keep related but wrong-level observations in its retrieved count and flag any truly unassigned ID. For every ID record four scores in this order:

| Test | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market | Same metro or established shared pay zone | Broad or national market without a specific zone | Other market without pay-zone equivalence |
| Stage | Same stage | One adjacent funding stage | More distant stage or incompatible public/private peer |
| Work | Equivalent level and duties or a supplied mapping | Limited overlap | Different level or materially different work |
| Type | Documented actual offer or primary fixed/range posting | Attributed dated secondary evidence with a stated method | Hearsay, anonymous or unsupported estimate |

Use ? for an unknown. State when the match rests on titles and levels. Where the request requires verified duties, grade or pay basis, defer use until it is supplied. Full means all four scores are 2 and the row passes timing, identity and usable-basis checks. Limited scores without a disqualifier are partial context. A zero, unresolved essential field or failed admissibility check drops the record. Partial evidence carries neither anchor nor confidence weight.

Use the date of the underlying observation and the requested cutoff, inclusive of a named eligible boundary month. If only a retrieval snapshot sets the current date, use it consistently. Trace repeated listings to a single best-documented original. Separate distinct events from shared employers or retrieval channels. Record one primary reason per dropped ID and keep other defects as flags.

Use recurring annual employee base. Drop contractor rates, one-time or retention/acquisition awards, and inseparable total cash, OTE or equity packages. Keep explicit or exactly recoverable base. A general total-compensation label cannot establish the base or confirm a cash-only total. Apply any explicit common basis in the scenario and state essential assumptions. Unknown required terms block comparison. Give the original labels and show each exact conversion once:

- Annualize with supplied pay periods or eligible annual employee hours. Preserve both endpoints.
- Subtract a known compatible annual non-base component. From total annual cash T with bonus rate r of base use T ÷ (1 + r). If r is of total use T × (1 − r). An unspecified denominator supplies no rate.
- For FX multiply by target units per source unit or divide by source units per target unit. Use only supplied rates, retaining native facts and comparable same-currency evidence when an observation cannot convert.

Reconcile R (retrieved) = F (full) + P (partial) + D (dropped). S (survived) = F + P. Attribute each full record to offer rank, advertised rank or context. Include all drops in D and keep internal hires outside R and the external ranks.

## Establish the midpoint position

Use the median of all full documented actual offers, including recorded candidate declines. Mark outcomes. Full postings supply context if offers qualify. With no full offer, use the median of full employer fixed amounts or normalized range midpoints and label the result advertised base. Preserve the range behind any posted midpoint. Sort normalized amounts and IDs. For odd n select rank (n+1)/2. For even n add ranks n/2 and n/2+1 and halve the total. Verify twice the reported median equals the sum of those two original inputs before calculating the gap.

For internal midpoint M and positive anchor A, the signed percent gap is 100 × (M − A) ÷ A. Use ±5% inclusive by default and honor compatible supplied cutoffs. Before rounding, compare 100 × (M − A) directly against each cutoff multiplied by A, preserving any strict inequality. Report competitive within the permitted limits, lagging below and above-market above. Near a cutoff give a useful margin against the applicable floor or ceiling. Preserve a supported external anchor if the internal basis or midpoint is missing. Conflicting partials or missing comparable evidence require a specific deferral.

High needs multiple recent independent matched offers with established basis and coherent evidence. One matched offer generally supports Medium. Multiple postings or unverified independence cap at Medium, and one posting is Low. Use Low for weak supported evidence and Deferred when there is no supported verdict. Apply supplied stricter rules. A shared retrieval platform cannot establish employer independence.

## Measure what the approved band can cover

For a hiring opening with comparable accepted offers and a valid approved min L and max U on that basis, classify each eligible accepted amount a as below L, from L through U inclusive, or above U. Reuse the normalized accepted-offer rows and reconcile the three counts to the eligible acceptance total. For each active opening with an above-cap acceptance, identify the highest amount, its ID and its difference from U. Coverage is of the observed sample. Do not include internal outcomes, declined offers or contextual observations.

When a requested target is to equal or exceed k documented acceptance amounts, take the kth in ascending order. For a fractional request p, set k to the smallest whole count at least p times the accepted total. Retain equal amounts as distinct documented events. The lowest feasible approved salary is the greater of L and the kth amount, if at or below U. Check how many it reaches and report the basis and ID of the controlling observation. If impossible, report the count reachable at U and the amount by which the kth offer exceeds it. A zero target needs no rank and the band minimum suffices. Defer a positive target if there is no accepted sample or the approved band is incomplete. Describe feasibility, never a predicted acceptance rate or a pay recommendation.

Compare advertised endpoints with approved endpoints when useful. Their overlap runs from the larger lower endpoint to the smaller upper endpoint when ordered. An ad never adds an acceptance to the coverage calculation. Use qualitative language for unsupported rival-pay claims.

## Present the finding

Lead with the decision that matters to the request and its decisive ID or amount. Use one compact row per segment with band, R/F/P/D/S, anchor type and ranked IDs, signed position and reason for confidence. Include a short hiring coverage finding for active openings that have eligible acceptances, or answer the requested coverage target. State basis and shared limits once. Name concrete deferral evidence and pool-wide caveats where applicable. Attach a compact ID audit with scores, disposition and reason, placing conversions there so the summary remains readable. Keep the headline focused on the materially different results and avoid repeating the same facts in prose.
