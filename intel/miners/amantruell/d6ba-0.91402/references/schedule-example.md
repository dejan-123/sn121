# Schedule layout and two invented deals

The reply has six parts, in this order:
1. The heading `## <Target> (<stage>): deal-risk schedule`, and nothing before it.
2. The **Terms** block: stage and next step, what the value rests on, each walk-away and price condition with its number, and what the input says was not searched.
3. The table: `# | Signal | Whose fact | Origins and label | Bears on | Tier | Action`. There is one row per numbered signal, in the input's order, and split rows take letters.
4. A **Cross-signal check** line.
5. A **Count** line.
6. Three short paragraphs: **Must-address picture**, **Elevation rationale**, **Most important evidence gap**.

## Deal one: findings to raise

The input, condensed. Acquiring Kinlochard Helicopters Ltd, post-LOI; signing is next. The value rests on the air operator certificate for the Lochview routes and the health board's air-ambulance contract. The deal has four conditions:
- Walk-away W1: any suspension or revocation of the operating licence by the Aviation Safety Directorate.
- Walk-away W2: mortgages over more than 4 of the 14 helicopters.
- Price P1: the price assumes the fleet is unencumbered.
- Price P2: the price assumes the continuing-airworthiness certificate is valid.

Scope: news and company sources, 24 months; the aircraft charges register, the Directorate's enforcement notices and the airfield authority's grounding list were not searched.

The fourteen signals:
1. Rotor Business Weekly (2027-02-03) and Highland Aviation News (2027-02-04) report a £8M aircraft loan from Tolquhon Bank to add two helicopters; Highland Aviation News cites Rotor Business Weekly.
2. Kinlochard release (2027-02-03): "£8M secured to grow the fleet to 14 helicopters".
3. Tolquhon Bank half-year report (2027-03-30) lists Kinlochard among its aviation-loan clients, with no terms.
4. No aircraft charges register results; that register was outside the sweep.
5. The Brevik Airworthiness register (retrieved 2027-03-02) shows Kinlochard's continuing-airworthiness certificate valid to 2028.
6. Air Operator Gazette and Airfield Journal (2027-04-12): the Directorate withdrew its recognition of Brevik Airworthiness. Neither article names Kinlochard.
7. Strath Herald (2027-04-20): unnamed airfield officials say the Directorate has opened an inspection of Kinlochard's maintenance organisation.
8. In the same article, a Kinlochard spokesperson calls the report "speculation".
9. Directorate notice (2027-01-18): "Kinlocherd Helicopter Co (sic)" cited for a lapsed pilot licence; the matter is referred for further review.
10. Rotorcraft Monitor (2027-03-08): an unnamed operator's helicopter, of the same type Kinlochard flies, was grounded after an incident.
11. Kinlochard release (2027-03-15): a new flight operations director has been appointed.
12. No signals in the Directorate's enforcement notices; not searched.
13. No signals found on ramp-inspection findings for Kinlochard helicopters.
14. No signal on whether the health board has renewed Kinlochard's air-ambulance contract.

**Cross-signal check:** rows 1-3 are one finding against P1. The press, the target and the lender all describe one aircraft loan, and a loan for aircraft is ordinarily secured on them, so P1 is at risk now. W2 is a separate test: 5 or more of the 14 helicopters charged trips it, and no count exists until row 4's search or the security agreements arrive. Rows 5 and 6 are one finding against P2, carried by row 6. Row 8 does not answer row 7. Row 9 stays apart until its identity is known. Row 10 joins nothing.

**Count:** 14 rows from 14 signals, none split. Must-address 7 (rows 1-4, 6, 7, 12; four findings) · Deeper diligence 4 (rows 5, 9, 13, 14) · Monitor 2 (rows 8, 11) · Noise 1 (row 10).

**Must-address picture.** Four findings need answers before signing:
- The fleet the price treats as unencumbered carries an £8M aircraft loan that the lender itself confirms (P1).
- The two registers that would test W1 and W2 were never searched.
- The issuer of the certificate P2 relies on has lost the Directorate's recognition.
- A report places Kinlochard inside a Directorate inspection, the process that ends in W1.

**Elevation rationale.**
- **Raised.** Row 7 rests on one paper and unnamed officials. It is raised because it names Kinlochard inside the process that produces a suspension, and the spokesperson's denial in row 8 does not answer it.
- **Held.** Row 9 describes the kind of matter W1 guards against and says it is still under review, but it names a different entity, so it is not elevated and only its identity check runs. Row 10 is the very event the deal fears, in Kinlochard's own helicopter type, and stays noise because it names no operator. Row 5 stays below the top because it is the register in hand: it shows the certificate current, and the finding against P2 sits in row 6. Row 11 is an appointment, not a process under way against Kinlochard. Row 13 found nothing in a record W1 does not turn on, so it is a request, not a gap at the top. Row 14 asks after a named client's contract, which only the parties hold: a document to request, never a missing register at the top.

**Most important evidence gap.** The aircraft charges register. One search shows how many of the 14 helicopters Tolquhon holds, which settles P1's extent and W2 together. The airfield authority's grounding list, named only in the scope, has no row of its own; it is searched with the same request.

## Deal two: nothing to raise
