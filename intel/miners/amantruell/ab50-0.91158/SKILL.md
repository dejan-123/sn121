# Underwrite the deal, not the news

Two questions decide every signal: does it touch a term of this deal, and whose paper is it? Materiality comes from what the buyer wrote. Verification comes from the signal's own provenance.

## The terms come first

From the buyer's words only:
- **Stage and the next step.** Pre-LOI clears before the letter of intent, post-LOI before signing, pre-close before completion. Every action is dated to that step.
- **What the value rests on.** The contracts, licence, asset, person or revenue line the price is paying for.
- **Walk-aways (W1, W2) and price conditions (P1, P2)**, each with the record that would breach it: "a suspension notice on the operating licence", not "regulatory risk". A price set as a multiple of a figure is a price condition about that figure. "Standard reps and warranties" adds no condition; it leaves the thesis and any price condition as the tests.
- **Not searched:** only what the input itself says was outside the sweep.

## Whose fact is it?

Settle this before any tier.
- **The target**: its exact entity, or a person or subsidiary the brief names.
- **A party the pool ties to the target**: its certifier, lender, a named customer, an opponent in a filed case. A fact about that party is evidence here. A certificate stands on its issuer, so an issuer that loses its own accreditation puts the certificate in question even when no story names the target.
- **Nobody in the deal**: a sector report, guidance, or an event at an unnamed company. (The target's own claim about clients it does not name is still the target's word.) When the story adds that its subject shares the target's product, class, sector or district, that overlap is why the sweep returned it, not a fact about the target. The row is Noise with the action "None", and nothing asks whether the unnamed party might be the target, even when the event is exactly the one a walk-away guards against.
- **A near-match**: a name a letter, suffix or legal form away, or marked "(sic)". It is Deeper diligence with one action, confirming the legal entity from a register, and nothing else is decided on it, however grave its subject.

## Count origins, then label

An origin is whoever first produced the fact. Outlets repeating one release, one email or one spokesperson are one origin; an outlet citing another adds nothing; everything the target publishes is one origin. Independent origins reached the fact by their own route: a docket, a regulator's notice or register, a counterparty's filing or call. Labels follow the count, never the gravity: **corroborated** (two or more independent origins, one outside the target), **single-source** (say whether primary, the record itself, or reported), **unverified** (anonymous posts, unnamed sources). Where a register and a paper the target wrote disagree, the register governs and the disagreement is a finding.

## Silence comes in three kinds

- **Never searched**, on the input's own words. When it is the record that would show a stated walk-away or price condition breached, the condition is untested: Must-address, with the search as the action. When it is a different kind of record from the one the condition turns on (a registration list when the walk-away is a violation finding), it is a search at Deeper diligence. A register the scope names but no numbered signal reports gets no row; it goes in the evidence-gap paragraph.
- **Searched or swept, nothing found.** Weak comfort for that sweep and date only: Deeper diligence with the named register search as its action, never Must-address.
- **A register in hand.** A retrieved entry that shows the condition met (a current certificate, a trial listed with no hold, a filing with no charges) answers that condition as of its date.
- **Papers only the parties hold** (contracts, client status, renewal or churn data, payroll): never a missing register. The row they would settle asks for them.

## Conditions written as an absence

"Unencumbered", "no liens", "no sanctions", "a valid certificate", "no active challenge" are met only while nothing exists. Once attributed evidence shows the thing exists at all, the condition is at risk: financing raised for vehicles, plant or equipment is ordinarily secured on what it bought, and a dismissal without prejudice leaves a challenge able to return. How much exists is a separate test. When a walk-away turns on a quantity the pool does not give, work the threshold once in the deal's units, say it is not yet measurable, and name the record that would supply the count. Say both things.

## Figures

Test every stated threshold the input's figures allow, once, showing the working. A figure the pool takes from a filed record stands as filed; the row tests it against the threshold and moves on. Doubt a base only when the pool itself shows the figure was struck on a different quantity than the condition names, such as a one-off gain inside an earnings base. Then the condition is untested, and that is Must-address even though the number appears to clear.

## How far a row climbs

**Never Must-address**, however material the row feels. Check every row against this list first:
- **A near-match name.** It stays at Deeper diligence with only the identity check, even when its matter is "under review" or "referred", even when its event is exactly a walk-away's, and even when the walk-away covers any breach "regardless of size". "If it is the target" is the question the identity check answers first; until it does, nothing on that row is decisive.
- **Papers or figures only the parties hold.** A named client's status, contract terms, churn, renewals or revenue by customer stay at Deeper diligence, and the action requests them, even when the value rests on that client book.
- **A searched-but-empty sweep.** It stays at Deeper diligence.
- **A register in hand that shows the condition met.** It stays at Monitor, or at Deeper diligence when it must be re-read.
- **A row about nobody in the deal.** It is Noise, with the action "None"; Deeper diligence never holds it, because there is nothing about the target to check.
- **A departure, a hire or a post with no walk-away stated on it.** It is never elevated.

**Must-address** needs four things at once: attributed; corroborated or one primary record; decisive for a stated walk-away or price condition, weighed for magnitude (could it settle the condition alone), probability (does the evidence show it happening) and reversibility (is it still live); and needing settlement before the next step. Two further shapes climb:
- **A thin report of the process under way.** A report is elevated only when all three hold: it names the target's exact entity or the person the condition is about; it says the very process that produces the stated walk-away or price breach is already under way against that party (talks about leaving, an inspection or review of the target, a notice being drafted against it); and no record in the pool answers it. Its label stays unverified, its action is written confirmation from whoever holds the record, and a denial from the target's own spokesperson does not lower it. A report about an unnamed company or a near-match is never elevated.
- **One finding across rows.** Rows carrying one fact (press, the target's release, the lender's own disclosure) take the same tier and say "read with row N".

With no walk-away and no price condition stated, only a live adverse claim on what the value rests on, on an independent record, reaches the top: a filed suit alleging the asset is overstated.

**Deeper diligence**: touches a condition or the thesis but misses a test, and one verification settles it. This covers the target's own word on a contract the value rests on, a certificate whose issuer another row puts in doubt (that row carries the finding), and a register that confirms the core of the thesis while other claimed items lapsed or sit under another name: write the lapse as a loss already taken, the other name as an identity question, and ask for the item-by-item schedule. A claim ended with prejudice on ground the deal stakes is Deeper diligence too: obtain the order and the terms.

**Monitor**: about the target but leaving every condition intact, or already confirmed by an independent record: a counterparty's filing confirming a contract, a routine appointment, a funding round, a denial, a counsel retention, an anonymous post touching only the thesis. It still gets a light action that keeps it in view to the next step.

**Noise**: names nobody in the deal. Its action is "None".

A pool of alarming topics can end with no Must-address row; say so first. Tiers and actions differ row by row.

## Writing it

Call read_reference on `references/schedule-example.md` before writing: it has the layout and two invented deals answered in full, one with nothing to raise. One row per numbered signal, in the input's order and numbering; a signal with parts at different statuses splits into lettered rows (4a, 4b). Every later row keeps the input's own number and place: after 1a and 1b the next row is 2, an absence stays where the input put it, and a second source inside one numbered signal stays on that signal's row. Each action names a role, a verb, the exact record or party and the step it must clear, written for its own row. Every name, date and figure comes from the input. Do the working silently: the reply begins with the `## ` heading line.
