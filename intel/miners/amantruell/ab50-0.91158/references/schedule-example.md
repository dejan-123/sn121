# Schedule layout and two invented deals

The reply has six parts, in this order:
1. The heading `## <Target> (<stage>): deal-risk schedule`, and nothing before it.
2. The **Terms** block: stage and next step, what the value rests on, each walk-away and price condition with its number, and what the input says was not searched.
3. The table: `# | Signal | Whose fact | Origins and label | Bears on | Tier | Action`. There is one row per numbered signal, in the input's order, and split rows take letters.
4. A **Cross-signal check** line.
5. A **Count** line.
6. Three short paragraphs: **Must-address picture**, **Elevation rationale**, **Most important evidence gap**.

## Deal one: findings to raise

The input, condensed. Acquiring Corrie Bay Ferries Ltd, post-LOI; signing is next. The value rests on the operating licence for the Skerray crossing and the island council's service contract. The deal has four conditions:
- Walk-away W1: any suspension or revocation of the operating licence by the Marine Safety Directorate.
- Walk-away W2: mortgages over more than 4 of the 14 vessels.
- Price P1: the price assumes the fleet is unencumbered.
- Price P2: the price assumes the safety management certificate is valid.

Scope: news and company sources, 24 months; the ship-mortgage register, the Directorate's enforcement notices and the port authority's detention list were not searched.

The fourteen signals:
1. Ferry Business Weekly (2027-02-03) and Coastal Transport News (2027-02-04) report a £9M vessel loan from Ardmore Bank to add two ferries; Coastal Transport News cites Ferry Business Weekly.
2. Corrie Bay release (2027-02-03): "£9M secured to grow the fleet to 14 vessels".
3. Ardmore Bank half-year report (2027-03-30) lists Corrie Bay among its shipping-loan clients, with no terms.
4. No ship-mortgage register results; that register was outside the sweep.
5. The Halden Classification register (retrieved 2027-03-02) shows Corrie Bay's safety management certificate valid to 2028.
6. Maritime Gazette and Port Journal (2027-04-12): the Directorate withdrew its recognition of Halden Classification. Neither article names Corrie Bay.
7. Island Courier (2027-04-20): unnamed harbour officials say the Directorate has opened an inspection of Corrie Bay's safety management.
8. In the same article, a Corrie Bay spokesperson calls the report "speculation".
9. Directorate notice (2027-01-18): "Corie Bay Ferry Co (sic)" cited for a lapsed crew certificate; the matter is referred for further review.
10. Shipping Monitor (2027-03-08): an unnamed operator's ferry, of the same class Corrie Bay runs, was grounded and detained.
11. Corrie Bay release (2027-03-15): a new fleet operations director has been appointed.
12. No signals in the Directorate's enforcement notices; not searched.
13. No signals found on port-state inspection deficiencies for Corrie Bay vessels.
14. No signal on whether the island council has renewed Corrie Bay's service contract.

**Cross-signal check:** rows 1-3 are one finding against P1. The press, the target and the lender all describe one vessel loan, and a loan for vessels is ordinarily secured on them, so P1 is at risk now. W2 is a separate test: 5 or more of the 14 vessels charged trips it, and no count exists until row 4's search or the security agreements arrive. Rows 5 and 6 are one finding against P2, carried by row 6. Row 8 does not answer row 7. Row 9 stays apart until its identity is known. Row 10 joins nothing.

**Count:** 14 rows from 14 signals, none split. Must-address 7 (rows 1-4, 6, 7, 12; four findings) · Deeper diligence 4 (rows 5, 9, 13, 14) · Monitor 2 (rows 8, 11) · Noise 1 (row 10).

**Must-address picture.** Four findings need answers before signing:
- The fleet the price treats as unencumbered carries a £9M vessel loan that the lender itself confirms (P1).
- The two registers that would test W1 and W2 were never searched.
- The issuer of the certificate P2 relies on has lost the Directorate's recognition.
- A report places Corrie Bay inside a Directorate inspection, the process that ends in W1.

**Elevation rationale.**
- **Raised.** Row 7 rests on one paper and unnamed officials. It is raised because it names Corrie Bay inside the process that produces a suspension, and the spokesperson's denial in row 8 does not answer it.
- **Held.** Row 9 describes the kind of matter W1 guards against and says it is still under review, but it names a different entity, so it is not elevated and only its identity check runs. Row 10 is the very event the deal fears, in Corrie Bay's own vessel class, and stays noise because it names no operator. Row 5 stays below the top because it is the register in hand: it shows the certificate current, and the finding against P2 sits in row 6. Row 11 is an appointment, not a process under way against Corrie Bay. Row 13 found nothing in a record W1 does not turn on, so it is a request, not a gap at the top. Row 14 asks after a named client's contract, which only the parties hold: a document to request, never a missing register at the top.

**Most important evidence gap.** The ship-mortgage register. One search shows how many of the 14 vessels Ardmore holds, which settles P1's extent and W2 together. The port authority's detention list, named only in the scope, has no row of its own; it is searched with the same request.
