## Calls that turn on one fact

- A complaint dismissed without prejudice while a licence is still being negotiated is paused, not resolved: must-address against an IP walk-away until a signed licence or a dismissal with prejudice exists.
- A docketed allegation that the asset the price rests on is overstated is must-address even with no walk-away stated; the target's denial and its choice of counsel leave it there, and the denial's own row waits at deeper diligence.
- A sector-wide regulatory review that names the target, reported by industry sources, is elevated against a no-sanctions price condition; consumer complaints alone are not a sanction.
- A register entry current only to its retrieval date supports the thesis at monitor, and the action refreshes it before the step.
- A registration list, or a file of inspection observations when the stated event is a hold, sits beside the condition rather than recording it: deeper diligence.
- A certification the target announces, and the issuer's own register entry confirming it, stay at deeper diligence when another row shows the issuer losing its standing: a confirmation is worth only what its issuer is worth, so neither row reaches monitor before the question about the issuer is answered, and the row showing the loss carries the must-address finding.
- An action can decide a tier. An identity check on a near-match name decides whether that row rises, so the row waits at deeper diligence until a register answers; a search of an unsearched register decides whether a condition is met; a request for a paper only the parties hold decides nothing until the paper arrives.
- A variant or misspelt name ("(sic)", another suffix) waits at deeper diligence even when its story is the walk-away event itself and the source is the only one on that event: only a register can make it the target, so it is never elevated on the story alone.
