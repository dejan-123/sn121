# An earlier invented week, kept for the record

Another made-up week and the page written for it. It is not part of the method and nothing in it is needed for a reply.

## The input (invented)

The manager runs Mid-Market Renewals at Halcyon Fieldworks, an agricultural-sensor subscription business, and is preparing the weekly 1-on-1 with Ravenna Kostova, Account Manager. The manager's draft agenda read: "open with a thank-you, she's been great; coaching, she has been missing deadlines on renewal paperwork; status on the Q4 renewal cohort; she asked about a hybrid schedule for a health reason; and she wants to talk about the senior account-manager track." The action-item log showed Ravenna's renewal-risk list for Finance overdue since Thursday, the manager's approval of the discount matrix done, and the churn-signal review with Client Services in progress. The cohort table showed 13 renewals closed this month, with 7 accounts flagged at risk, 3 of them waiting on Legal's contract redline. Slack: Monday, Ravenna in #renewals listing the three accounts stuck in redline and asking Legal for a date; Wednesday, the Client Services lead in #renewals confirming that the save plan Ravenna wrote kept a 40-seat account from cancelling; Thursday, Ravenna to the manager by direct message: "I missed the paperwork cut-off for one renewal by a day; I have asked Finance to backdate it and it is sorted." Ravenna's submitted topic: "Is the senior track about book size or about save work? I want to know which I should be showing."

## The brief

# 1-on-1 brief: Ravenna Kostova

Account Manager, Mid-Market Renewals · weekly 1-on-1, this week

**Context**

A slow month on closes with the cohort partly stuck in Legal. Action items: one done (the manager's discount-matrix approval), one in progress (the churn-signal review with Client Services), one overdue (Ravenna's renewal-risk list for Finance, due Thursday). Renewals closed: 13 this month in the cohort table, with 7 accounts at risk and 3 of those, 43% of the at-risk set, waiting on Legal's redline (Monday, #renewals). Before the meeting: the manager asks Legal's lead for a redline date, since the three have waited since Monday. One 40-seat account that was at risk stayed, on a save plan Ravenna wrote (Wednesday, Client Services lead in #renewals). One paperwork cut-off was missed by a day and repaired (Thursday, direct message).

**Agenda**

The draft wanted to open with a thank-you, but no message or figure in the week names what it would be for; the saved account is an outcome inside the cohort item, and it sits there with its number.

1. [Status] The Q4 cohort and the three accounts in Legal
Three of the seven at-risk accounts cannot move until Legal returns the redline Ravenna asked for on Monday (cohort table; Monday, #renewals). The one question that moves it: has Legal given a date, and if not, should the manager raise it with Legal's lead this week? Agree which of the other four at-risk accounts she works while the three wait.

2. [Status] Renewal-risk list for Finance, overdue since Thursday
The list is Ravenna's one overdue item (action-item log). Ask whether the Legal hold is what is delaying it or whether the list can go to Finance now with the three marked pending; set the new date in the meeting.

3. [Quick feedback] The missed paperwork cut-off
The draft called this coaching; the week shows one cut-off missed by a day, repaired the same day with Finance and reported by Ravenna herself (Thursday, direct message), and one owned slip is feedback, not a pattern. Say that the repair and the report were the right moves and that the standard is the cut-off itself; agree a reminder two days ahead of each cut-off in the renewal tracker.

4. [Status] The 40-seat save and the churn-signal review
The Client Services lead credited Ravenna's save plan for keeping the 40-seat account (Wednesday, #renewals), and the churn-signal review with that team is in progress (action-item log). Ask which signal in the review would have flagged that account earlier, and when the review can be closed.

5. [Career] The senior track: book size or save work
Ravenna asked which of the two she should be showing (her submitted topic). Questions: when she reads the senior criteria, which of them does the save plan already evidence, and which would only be met by a larger book? If the next quarter were shaped so that one of the missing criteria got a real example, which would she choose? What should the manager make visible to the people who decide, and to whom?

**Parking lot**

- The hybrid schedule for a health reason: a private conversation, with HR for the accommodation policy; the manager sets it up this week and keeps it off the agenda.

## A second input (invented), where the manager sends the record of a meeting already held and asks for a 1-on-1 brief from it: "Here are the notes from Monday's 1-on-1 with Oskar Brandvold, can you prep next week's from these?" followed by the notes (topics discussed, three action items with due dates, a line that Oskar mentioned a bereavement). The reply:

# Not a 1-on-1 prep input: notes from the Monday 1-on-1 with Oskar Brandvold

What arrived is the record of a meeting already held, which calls for a meeting summary with an action-item tracker, not a brief for the next one. A brief needs this week's signals, and the notes carry none.

Carry-over from the record, status unknown until updated:
- Oskar: circulate the territory-split proposal, due Thursday
- Manager: confirm the training budget line, due Wednesday
- Oskar: close the two stalled trial accounts, due Friday

The bereavement mentioned in the notes belongs in a private conversation, not on an agenda.

To get next week's brief, send: the status of the three items above, the draft agenda for next week, any figures for the period, and any threads worth reading.

In both cases the reading of the signals happened before the reference was opened; the reply itself begins at the `# ` title line, with no sentence before it and none after the last line.
