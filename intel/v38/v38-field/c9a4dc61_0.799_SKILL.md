# The weekly 1-on-1 brief: class every item by its evidence, not its label

The manager's draft agenda is a set of claims. Each label on it (status, feedback, coaching, shoutout, career, a priority) is read against the week's evidence: the action log, the figures, the messages, the report's own topic. A label the evidence supports is kept; one it contradicts is changed, with the reason in a clause; no signal, name, day, duration or figure is ever supplied that the inputs lack, and a day is copied from the stamp on its own message or row. The reply is the brief and nothing else, 350 to 700 words: its first characters are `# ` and the report's name, and the readings below show their results only inside the brief's own lines, never as notes, an analysis, a draft, a word count or a checklist before or after it. Outside quotation marks the brief contains no "you" at all: the manager is "the manager" (or the manager's name when the input gives it), and the report is the name or the pronoun the input itself uses. Only a quoted question speaks to the report.

## 1. Six classes and the tests between them

- **Recognition** opens the meeting only when, in this period, a named person or a measured result credits a specific piece of the report's work; quote who said what. A vague "great work" in the draft is replaced by that signal. With no such signal there is no opener, and the header says so in a clause. A result that earlier meeting notes record as already discussed is header context, never an opener.
- **Unblock** or **Status.** A draft "status check" is an Unblock when the evidence shows waiting: tickets blocked on another team, requests unanswered, output far under its trailing average, a queue one person cannot clear. Its talking point is the manager's own move (escalate, broker, reassign, decide), never "how is it going"; when the report has already proposed the fix or begun the escalation it is still an Unblock, and the manager's move is to back it. Load or structure mislabelled as feedback to the report is also an Unblock, and a draft priority the evidence contradicts is corrected in the same clause. A status check on work the log shows done is retired: if someone praised that work it becomes the Recognition item, whether or not the draft asked for one, and the rest, like in-progress work nobody waits on, is a header clause.
- **Feedback** or **Coaching: count the dated instances of the same behaviour** (each dated message or event is one). One instance is Feedback: the observation, its effect, what is already agreed. Two or more are Coaching, and a coaching slot holds questions only. One behaviour gets one item, never both. So a coaching label on one rough moment drops to Feedback, and a feedback label on a first occurrence the report has already owned stays Feedback however visible the damage: severity sends the process fix to another venue; it does not make one instance a pattern. Zero instances ("seems", "lately") is a perception, never an item of any class, even when a blocker would explain the impression: it is left off, with what the signals show instead and what observed instance would bring it back. Dated behaviour that follows from an uncleared blocker comes after the Unblock, as Coaching questions about the escalation route.
- **Career** items are questions that hold a named signal up against the path the report asked about. A topic the report submitted gets protected minutes, never leftover ones. Asking what a level takes is Career and puts nothing in the parking lot; only a decision already in motion (a promotion case, a calibration slot) is routed (section 2).

## 2. The venue reading

Pay, a promotion decision, health or family circumstances, a formal performance concern, friction with a named colleague, a process review or conversation that a third party has asked for: each is listed in the parking lot with its venue (a dedicated conversation, HR or the pay cycle, skip-level or calibration, the process owner) and the one booking step. No talking points, no detail and no advice on it anywhere in the brief; a personal matter is named in the input's own few words with its date, and nothing more. The part that is the report's own next action may stay on the agenda. When nothing qualifies, the parking lot says so in one line; it is never padded, and a task the manager owes is never a parking-lot line.

## 3. Header and figures

Three or four sentences that combine the inputs: what moved, what is stuck and on whom, the state of the action log, which item lacks evidence.

- The action log is summed up in words, not as a ratio: what is closed and what is open. An open row that is the manager's own goes on the `**Manager owes.**` line and nowhere else: never an agenda item, never a parking-lot line.
- At most three derived figures, all in the header, each with both of its numbers beside it: a count the input states, as a fraction with its percent; percent change = (new - old) / old, as a whole percent; two percentages compared in points. The item lines compute nothing new and repeat a figure only as the header printed it.

## 4. Order and shape

- Title: `# <Full name>, <role>: <the week in a few words drawn from the signals>`.
- `**Context.**` paragraph.
- `**Manager owes.**` Always written: each action-log row owned by the manager that is still open, with its status and "bring it or name the day"; when the manager's rows are all closed, "nothing open".
- `**From the draft.**` One line, written before any item, that gives each topic exactly one destination with its reason. A label the evidence overturns reads `"<the manager's words>" -> not <that label>: <the signal>, so <Class>`; a label that holds reads `-> kept as <Class>, <signal>`; the rest read `-> dropped: <reason>` or `-> parking lot, <kind>`. It takes every item of the draft in the draft's own order, then each further topic the signals raise: the report's own submitted topic, a request a third party makes, a personal matter. Action-log rows are not draft items. A draft item about a check-in, a programme or development is Career and is kept. The agenda then follows this line. With old notes it reads `**From the notes.**` and takes each commitment and topic recorded there.
- `## Agenda for <first name>: <the order in a clause>`, then item lines `**N. Class: topic**`, each followed by at most three sentences. Recognition is item 1 whenever it is earned; then Unblocks; Feedback or Coaching; Status; Career. An item that explains another goes before it. Use as many items as the evidence supports; two is a valid agenda.
- `## Parking lot for <first name>`: only what the venue reading routes; never a manager's task, an action-log row or a status.

Under an item line:

- Recognition: who said what about which work, and what it made possible.
- Unblock: what waits, on whom, since when; then "Manager's move:" and the action. Give a day only if the inputs give one; otherwise "this week".
- Status: one question naming the live work item.
- Feedback: what happened, what it caused, what the report has already said or done; then the one standard to restate. Three sentences. When anyone in the inputs asks for a process conversation or review about the incident, that request is a parking-lot line and the item says it is routed.
- Coaching: two questions and nothing else, each one sentence that names the thread, ticket or figures it comes from. When the report has already named the cause, ask about that cause, not the symptom in the draft.
- Career: one or two questions, each one sentence that sets a named signal from this week beside the role or criteria in question. A draft career item with no topic from the report is kept, and its question uses the week's strongest signal.

A question that could be put to any report at any company is rewritten until it names something only this input contains.

## 5. Thin or unusual inputs

When signals are missing, the brief stays near 350 words and gets plainer. Name the missing inputs in the header and say what to pull before the meeting, item by item. Keep the draft's items in the class the draft gave them, each as one question tied to the piece of work the draft does name (a career item asks which part of that named work the report wants more of), marked as having no signal behind it; never write a signal, a trend or praise to make an item look anchored.

When the only input is last meeting's notes, the agenda is the commitments recorded there and nothing else: no opener, no coaching, no career item the notes do not contain. A request the report made in those notes is answered by the commitment that came out of it, never by a Coaching item. Each commitment is a Status item with its owner and due date, marked "confirm before the meeting", the manager's own first because the manager can close them before walking in. Nothing is called done, late or overdue, no count of done rows is given, and no day is promised.

Call read_reference on `references/three-briefs.md` (three invented briefs, from full inputs, from a bare draft and from old notes; form only, every fact comes from the inputs in front of you). Then send the brief, once. Do not announce it or say what you are about to do: the very first characters sent are `# ` and the report's name, and the last are the final line of the parking lot.
