# Worked example: a production planner at a wholesale bakery

The brief opens on its `# ` title with no lead-in line and no word about what comes next. The readings stay silent; they show only as the From-the-draft lines and the classes.

## The input (invented)

Report: Halvard Brenna, production planner on the wholesale bread line at Copperloaf Bakery.

Manager's draft, typed on a phone:
- "Status on the allergen-changeover checklist rewrite"
- "Status on the weekend batch schedule for the new rye contract"
- "Coaching: Halvard seems careless lately, the Tuesday schedule had to be redone"
- "Feedback: he was sharp with maintenance this week"
- "He wants to talk about his pay after the shift-pattern change"
- "Start with a quick well-done"

Action log: checklist rewrite (Halvard) DONE, signed off by QA on Friday. Weekend rye schedule (Halvard) BLOCKED, waiting on maintenance to name the oven 4 downtime window. Oven-maintenance review booked (Manager) DONE. Next quarter's line-capacity plan for the planners (Manager) OVERDUE.

Oven-downtime log: 6 hours this week against a trailing four-week average of 13. Tuesday entry: oven 2 down before dawn, logged after the day's batch schedule was locked.

Allergen-changeover checklist: first-time passes 82% the month before, 91% this month.

Chat excerpts:
- Tue, Halvard to the night-shift channel: "Oven 2 is down. Seeded batches move to oven 1, sourdough goes to the afternoon run. Updated sheet is pinned."
- Tue, the shift supervisor: "Every wholesale order left the dock on time."
- Mon, Halvard to the maintenance lead: "I need the oven 4 window before I can lock the rye weekend."
- Wed, Halvard to the same lead: "Still nothing. I cannot plan around silence."
- Thu, the QA lead to the production channel: "Halvard's changeover checklist caught a sesame cross-contact step nobody had ever written down."

Halvard's own topic: "Is the planning work I do building toward the site scheduling lead role?"

## The brief

# 1-on-1 brief: Halvard Brenna, production planner

**Context.** Halvard's week turned on two things: a changeover checklist that QA credits with catching an unwritten sesame cross-contact step, and a weekend rye schedule he cannot lock because maintenance has not named the oven 4 window. First-time changeover passes rose from 82% to 91% (+9 points) and oven downtime ran 6 hours against a 13-hour trailing average (-54%). Tuesday's redone schedule traces to an oven 2 failure logged after the lock, not to a planning error. Not in the input: next week's order volumes.
Manager still owes: the next quarter's line-capacity plan for the planners (overdue).

**From the draft**
- "Status on the allergen-changeover checklist rewrite" -> not Status: done and signed off by QA on Friday, so Recognition.
- "Status on the weekend batch schedule" -> not Status: blocked until maintenance names the oven 4 window Halvard has already requested, so Unblock.
- "Coaching: seems careless, Tuesday schedule redone" -> not Coaching: the downtime log puts the oven 2 failure after the lock and the oven 2 re-plan kept every wholesale order on its slot, so dropped; the save joins item 1.
- "Feedback: sharp with maintenance" -> not Feedback: his Wednesday message follows a request maintenance left unanswered, so it folds into item 2 (Unblock).
- "Quick well-done" -> kept as Recognition, the QA lead's message, merged into item 1.
- "Pay after the shift-pattern change" -> parking lot: compensation conversation with HR.
- Halvard's topic, the scheduling lead path -> kept as Career.

**Agenda**

1. **Recognition: the changeover checklist and the Tuesday re-plan.** The QA lead wrote that the checklist caught a sesame cross-contact step nobody had written down, and first-time passes are up 9 points. Both come from named people this week, so they replace a general well-done.

2. **Unblock: oven 4 window for the rye weekend.** Halvard has already asked the maintenance lead in writing; another ask from the planner will not move it. Manager's move: get the window from the maintenance lead directly. Question for Halvard: when a request to another team stalls, at what point should it come to the manager instead of a second, sharper message?

3. **Career: the site scheduling lead path.** Which parts of the checklist rewrite and the Tuesday re-plan felt like lead work, and which did not? What would Halvard want to own next to test that? If it needs more than this slot, book a dedicated career conversation.

**Parking lot**
- Pay after the shift-pattern change -> compensation conversation with HR.

## Why each line went where it did

- Two draft "status" items, two different moves: the finished one turned into recognition because the log says done and QA praised it; the stuck one turned into the manager's errand because the report had already chased it.
- The coaching label failed the evidence test. The only incident has a logged cause outside Halvard's control and an on-time outcome, and nothing else in the chat repeats it. A trait word with no supporting pattern never becomes an agenda item.
- Pay went to the parking lot with no figures, no reasons and no talking points. The weekly slot cannot settle it and a printed brief should not carry it.
- No completion rate appears; the open item is named instead. Every figure comes from a pair in the input.

## What would change the call

**A real pattern keeps coaching.** Suppose the downtime log showed no stoppage on Tuesday, the redo came from an order change Halvard heard about and did not pass on, and a second schedule was redone the week before for the same reason. Two instances make a pattern, so the item stays as **Coaching**, written as questions on those two schedules: when did the change reach him, and what would flag it to the shift before the lock? If he had already named the slip himself, the questions start from his words and aim at the hand-off moment, not at a trait like carelessness. With one redo and nothing else, it would be at most a one-question **Follow-up** on whatever is still owed.

**A thin input.** Suppose the manager sent only the draft lines, with no log, figures or chat. The context says so plainly. Each kept item becomes a question marked "unanchored: no signal in the input", there is no recognition item because nothing names what went well, pay still goes to the parking lot, and the brief ends its context with what to pull before the meeting: the action log, recent schedule changes, the team chat. A short brief is correct here; an invented signal is not.

**Old notes only.** Suppose the input were last meeting's notes and nothing newer. Every action in them is past its date with no status since, so the context says every status is unconfirmed, and the From-the-draft lines read:
- "<action>, <owner>, due <date>" -> not done: no status since the notes, so Confirm
- "<figure> up on the month before" -> context only: already discussed at that meeting, so no opener
- "<personal or family matter>" -> parking lot: private conversation

The agenda then lists each action as **Confirm: <action>** with its owner and due date, the manager's own first, and keeps the one topic the notes left undecided as the live item. A figure from the notes stays in the context as background. A personal or family matter appears only in the parking lot, by topic, with none of its details.

After this file, the reply begins with `# 1-on-1 brief: ` and the report's name, and the parking lot is its last line.
