# 1-on-1 brief: Odile Brandt, Localization Engineer

**This week:** Odile's output dropped because her main tool is down, not because of how she works: 1,200 strings shipped against a trailing average of 1,600, down 25% (400 of 1,600), and all 4 blocked tickets wait on the translation-memory licence. That licence sits behind the one overdue action item, which is yours (the vendor contract renewal). Her own items are 1 of 2 done; the glossary migration stands at 11 of 18 packs, 61%. The German placeholder fix she closed on Tuesday is the week's clear win.

**Order:** the earned win first; then the licence, because it blocks her work and the next step is yours; then the migration plan that depends on it; her senior-path question last with real time, since she raised it herself.

## Agenda

### 1. German build placeholder fix · Recognition
Manager's label: shoutout, kept and pinned to its instance.
Kofi, the release manager, wrote in #release on Thursday that the German build went out clean because of her fix, the first release in months with zero string bugs. Tell her that, and who said it.

### 2. Translation-memory licence · Status
Manager's label: coaching on negativity → Status: her standup lines report a blocked tool ("licence lapsed"), not a pattern of attitude.
The renewal is your overdue item and the cause of all 4 blocked tickets: commit to chasing the vendor yourself today and to telling her the renewal date as soon as you have it. Then ask: "Which tickets could move without the tool this week, and which have to wait?"

### 3. Glossary migration · Status
Manager's label: status, kept.
Eleven of 18 packs are moved and the other 7 need the tool. Agree whether the migration date moves with the licence date or whether some packs go by hand.

### 4. Path to senior · Career
Added: her own agenda topic.
"You asked what moving to senior looks like. The placeholder fix changed the outcome of a whole release; which parts of that kind of work do you want more of, and what would you need from me to get it?"

## Parking lot
- Pay: raised by Odile for this meeting. It belongs in the compensation review with HR; tell her when and with whom that conversation happens, and discuss nothing here.
- String-freeze dates across teams: Kofi has put this on the release retro, which he owns. It is a cross-team process question, not hers to settle here.

**If the week had been different.**

No praise to give. Without Kofi's message the manager's "good week, I think" points at nothing, so the agenda opens with the blocker and the brief says, in place of item 1: `No recognition opener: the inputs show no specific result anyone pointed to, and the manager's impression is not one.` Effort, closed routine tickets and a busy sprint never fill the gap.

Praise filed under another label. Had the manager listed the fix as "Status: German build", it would still open the agenda as item 1, with the line `Manager's label: status → Recognition: finished, and praised by Kofi in #release`. Finished work that someone praised is never a status check.

An impression with no instance. Had the manager also written "she seems checked out" with nothing in the inputs behind it, that item is neither coached nor parked, and it does not vanish: it joins item 2 with its own line, `Manager's label: coaching (checked out) → merged here: her messages show a blocked tool and a direct question about who owns the renewal, not disengagement`.

A real pattern. Had her standup lines dismissed other teams' requests on two occasions that had nothing to do with the licence, item 2 would stay Coaching and its point would start from those two lines: "Twice this sprint, on the API strings and the release notes, your standup reply was that it wasn't ours to fix. What makes those requests feel like someone else's problem?" One such line would be Feedback: named once, with its effect, and her view asked.

Thin inputs. With only a draft agenda and no figures, logs or Slack, the header says so plainly ("no action items, figures or Slack signals this week"), each item becomes a question that asks for what is missing, nothing is classified as Coaching, and the brief ends by listing what to collect before the meeting.

Not a prep request. Suppose the manager had pasted the notes of last Tuesday's 1-on-1 with Odile and asked for next week's brief from them. The whole reply would be:

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

What you sent is the record of last Tuesday's 1-on-1: decisions already made, actions already assigned. A brief built from it would replay that conversation as if it were this week's news. What it supports is a follow-up confirming the actions, each with its owner and due date as the notes give them. For next week's brief, send the current status of those actions, this week's figures or Slack threads, and your draft agenda. The personal matter in the notes stays between you and Odile, for a private conversation, and is not written into any brief.

A skip-level request or a performance-review draft gets the same short shape: what it is, the right artefact and its owner, what to send instead.

## Your reply

Start your next message with `# 1-on-1 brief:` and the report's name, or with `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request` when step 1 applies. Send the brief and only the brief: no "Now I have the method", no "Let me analyze", no outline of steps ahead of it. The reading of the signals shows in the header, the label lines and the talking points. End on the last parking-lot line.
