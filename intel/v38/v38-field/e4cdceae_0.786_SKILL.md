# Weekly 1-on-1 brief: the signals relabel the draft

A weekly 1-on-1 is the report's meeting, not a status meeting. The manager's draft labels are hypotheses; the log, figures and messages decide. The tests below surface only through the brief's own lines (the Draft count, each relabel note, the Dropped list), so the reply is the brief alone and its first characters are `# ` and the report's name.

## Tests that relabel the draft

1. **Done means no status item.** Shown done in the log or a message: drop the status question; credit for the result becomes recognition evidence.
2. **In progress with no blocker gets no agenda time**: it shows only in the context's open-actions line, never as a Follow-up or parking-lot entry.
3. **Blocked by another team means Unblock, owned by the manager.** The manager escalates or brokers with that team; the report gets at most one question, on exactly what is needed, never a progress question.
4. **Test each coaching label.** Coaching needs a pattern: two or more instances of one behaviour in the input.
   - Contradicted by praised, proactive or self-started work: drop it, naming the signal.
   - Explained by a blocker (terse or slow after repeated unanswered asks): the situation, not the person; fold it into the Unblock item with one question on the escalation route.
   - One incident, no other pattern: no coaching item; at most one question on what is still owed from it (an answer, a file, a fix), never on how to do better.
   - A real pattern: Coaching as questions on the dated instances; if the report already named it, build on that and aim at the mechanism (when to raise a change), not the trait.
5. **A label can understate.** "Quick feedback" or "low" on an incident with client or cross-team impact, or on an overload making the report a single point of failure, moves up: a proper item (the owned fix, the step against a repeat) or a manager Decision (back the report's proposal). A first slip the report owns gets questions, not a reprimand; a process talk someone else asked for goes to the parking lot.
6. **Recognition needs a named signal from this period**, a result and who credited it, quoted over the manager's vague memory; then it is item 1. Nothing named, or a result already discussed at the last 1-on-1: no opener.
7. **Sensitive topics never reach the agenda**, even when the report raised them or old notes list them. Pay, health, family or personal matters, leave, HR or conduct get no item and no talking point; the parking lot names only the category ("a personal matter", "a pay question") and the venue (private conversation, HR, compensation process), never what it concerns, not even in brackets.
8. **Decisions go where they are made.** Promotion or level calls go to calibration or the skip-level; only the report's next step stays. A career question the report raised: questions on their recent work, plus "book a dedicated career conversation if it needs more".
9. **The manager's own open items** (overdue, not started) go inside the item they bear on, else in a last item, Owed by me.
10. **Missing inputs are named, never filled.** No signals: keep the draft's items as questions tagged "(no signal yet)", name what is missing and what to pull, keep it short. Every name, project, date and figure in the brief comes from this input; an unknown date is left out, never a placeholder.
11. **Old notes as the only input** are the last meeting's record, and nothing has come in since. A result in them was recognised there: no opener. Every action in them is now due with no update: write "confirm" and its due date, never in progress, on track or "I will"; the manager's own become Owed by me, asking whether each was done. Add no item they do not hold.
12. **Figures:** each number pair the input states (period vs last or average, estimate vs actual, scoped vs delivered, x of y), once, with inputs. Change = (new - old) / old, whole percent: 11 vs 9, +22%. Two percentages or two scores differ in points: 82% vs 70%, +12 points. No rate for the action log; nothing else computed.

## The brief

- `# <Name> (<role>): 1-on-1 brief`
- **Context.** One or two sentences that read the week, then short lines: Source (what the input holds, and "last 1-on-1 notes only" or "draft only" when that is all); Figures; Open actions (status, owner); Gap (the one missing input that matters most); "Draft: N items - k relabelled, d dropped, r routed."
- **Agenda**: numbered: recognition, unblocks and decisions, the report's topics, coaching, career, owed by me. Each: bold tag and topic, "(draft: X -> Y, because <signal>)" only on a draft item you relabelled, then 1-3 bullets of one sentence each, on named signals. Coaching and career bullets are questions.
- **Dropped**: each draft item the brief removes, opening with the draft's own words in quotes, then the signal that removed it; a relabelled item is not also dropped. None: "Nothing dropped."
- **Parking lot**: category -> venue; or "Nothing for the parking lot this week."

## Why the tests hold

- Status the log already answers costs the report's meeting time; status belongs async. A status line survives only when nobody can answer it without the meeting.
- Reading a situational behaviour as a trait is the fundamental attribution error. Before coaching tone or pace, check whether the person was waiting on someone; the remedy is the manager's escalation, and feedback to the report would punish the symptom.
- One data point is an anecdote. A real pattern is coached with questions about the instances (situation, behaviour, impact), so the report reasons about it rather than defends.
- Generic praise reads as inattention. Specific recognition names the work and the person who credited it, which tells the report what to repeat.
- A printed brief travels: it is left on desks and shared on screens. Health, family, pay and HR detail stay off it; the topic is named only so it is not lost.
- Venues: compensation goes to the compensation process or HR partner; personal and health matters to a private conversation, then HR for formal leave or flexibility; promotion to calibration or the skip-level; a cross-team block to the manager's peer on that team; a process dispute with another stakeholder to its own meeting; plain progress to async.
- The manager's own overdue items are the first accountability in the room; naming them keeps the brief honest about what the report is waiting on.
- Old notes are a week stale. Every due date in them has passed or arrives at the meeting, so a status is a question to confirm, not a fact; a result already praised in them was recognised then.

## Writing the items

- Tag words: Recognition, Unblock, Decision, Follow-up, Coaching, Career, Owed by me. Write "(their topic)" only when the report raised it.
- A question is anchored when it names the thread, ticket, document or date it rests on: "On RFI-12, what would the client need from us to close it?", not "How is it going?".
- Keep the report's own words when they explain the item (their self-flag, their proposal), in a short quote.
- Timing uses only what the input implies: before the next 1-on-1, before a named deadline in the input, at the next review cycle. No invented days, durations or placeholders.
- A thin brief stays thin. Fewer, honest items beat a full five-item shape; never add a recognition, coaching, career or parking-lot line to look complete.
- An item never runs past three sentences, its bullets included.

## Delivery

Call read_reference on `references/worked-brief.md` before writing: one invented brief, then short cases that change the call. It shows form only; no fact, name, figure or project from it ever enters your brief. Then send the brief once. Do not announce it or say what you are about to do: the very first characters sent are `# ` and the report's name, and the last are the parking-lot line.
