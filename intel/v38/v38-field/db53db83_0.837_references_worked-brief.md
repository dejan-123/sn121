# One invented week, turned into a brief

Ines Halvorsen is an invented localisation editor on the translation desk of an invented publisher, with a 30-minute weekly 1-on-1. Her manager's material for the week:

- Draft agenda: (1) "Shoutout - she's been on fire lately" (the manager could not say for what); (2) "Status: glossary migration"; (3) "Coaching: she missed the Portuguese style-guide date"; (4) Ines's own topic, "What would the lead-editor role ask of me that I'm not doing yet?"; (5) "She asked about moving to a four-day week."
- Action items: Ines, migrate the legacy glossary into the new term base - done Tuesday, all 1,840 entries moved. Ines, deliver the Portuguese style guide - delivered Monday, one working day after its Friday due date. Ines, review the two new hires' first chapters - done. Manager, confirm the Q1 freelance budget - overdue.
- Figures: 18 translation reviews closed this sprint against a three-sprint average of 12; on the medical list, 2 reviews closed against an average of 6, the rest waiting for freelance cover; query tickets from the print vendor fell from 22 last month to 11 this month.
- Threads: the print vendor's project manager in #vendor-desk: "Since Ines cleaned up the term base our query tickets have halved - thank you." A senior editor: "Both new hires asked for Ines as their reviewer again." Ines to her manager on Thursday: "The Portuguese guide will be a day late - the native reviewer is off sick. You'll have it Monday."

The brief written from that week:

```
# 1-on-1 brief: Ines Halvorsen
Localisation editor, translation desk - 30 minutes

**Context**
The glossary migration closed on Tuesday with all 1,840 entries moved, and the print vendor's query tickets fell from 22 to 11, a 50% drop. Ines closed 18 reviews this sprint against a three-sprint average of 12, 50% above her usual pace, but only 2 on the medical list against an average of 6, down 67%, with the rest waiting on freelance cover. The Portuguese style guide arrived one working day late; she warned you the day before that the native reviewer was ill. You owe her the Q1 freelance budget, now overdue, and it is what the medical list is waiting for.

**Agenda**
Recognition first because an outside signal earned it; her own question next, while there is time for it; the late guide last, as a short check.

**1. Term-base cleanup - Recognition**
Was: Shoutout with no reason given. The vendor's thread supplies the reason.
- Signal: the vendor's project manager wrote that query tickets halved after her cleanup.
- Say: name the result and that the vendor noticed it unprompted.

**2. Lead-editor opening - Career**
- Signal: both new hires asked for her as their reviewer again.
- Ask: "Which parts of reviewing the new hires feel closest to a lead's job, and which feel furthest?"
- Ask: "What would you want to try before applying - running a review cycle, owning the freelance plan?"

**3. Q1 freelance budget - Status**
Was: Status: glossary migration. The migration is done and sits in the header; the open item is the budget you owe her.
- Decide: give her a date for the budget confirmation.

**4. Portuguese style guide - Feedback**
Was: Coaching. One late delivery, flagged a day ahead with a reason; nothing in the input shows a pattern.
- Ask: "What would have let the guide go out on Friday - a second native reviewer on call?"
- Say: the early warning she gave is the standard to keep.

**Parking lot**
- Four-day-week request: a working-arrangement conversation with HR; acknowledge it and book a time, nothing more here.
```

Why each call went the way it did: the opener rests on the vendor's words, not on the manager's memory; the glossary item left the agenda because done work has nothing to check; the "coaching" label fell because one late delivery is an event, not a pattern; her career question moved ahead of the feedback because she raised it and has been thinking about it; the four-day week is a working-arrangement request with its own process. Every count that came with a baseline is in the header with its change: (11 - 22) / 22 = -50%, (18 - 12) / 12 = +50%, (2 - 6) / 6 = -67% - the line of work that fell as well as the ones that improved.

## When the evidence changes

1. **No outside signal.** Had the vendor not posted and the manager still only remembered that she had "been on fire", item 1 would go. The agenda would start `No recognition opener: nothing this week names a result.` and the migration figures would stay in the header as facts.
2. **A real pattern.** Had her two previous deliverables also slipped, each without warning, the guide would be coaching: "Three of your last four dates slipped - what do the three have in common?" An open question about the pattern, not a verdict.
3. **An impression with no signal.** Had the note said "Coaching: she seems to have lost interest" while the threads showed her warning early and closing more reviews than usual, the item would not stay coaching. It would be headed `**4. Review workload - Status**`, followed by `Was: Coaching ("seems to have lost interest"). No signal in the input supports the impression; the threads show early warnings and more reviews closed than usual.`, and its bullets would ask about the work itself.
4. **A label on a problem around her.** Had the note said "Feedback: review backlog" while she had flagged that freelance cover was never approved, the item would be status, with the decision on the manager's side.
5. **Thin input.** With only the draft agenda - no figures, threads or action items - the header would list what is missing, the agenda would carry no opener, and each item would keep one open question ("Where does the lead-editor opening stand for you?") with no anchor invented.
6. **The wrong job.** Had the manager pasted the notes of last week's 1-on-1 and asked for next week's prep, the whole reply would be:

```
# Not a prep brief: these are notes from a meeting that already happened
These notes record last week's 1-on-1, so a brief built on them would only repeat it. What they need is a follow-up: a short recap to Ines with each action's owner and due date. For next week's brief, send this week's material - where each of those actions now stands, any new figures or threads, and your draft agenda.
```

Preparation for a skip-level and a performance-review draft get the same kind of reply, each naming its own artefact.

Now write the reply for the week in front of you. Its first two characters are `# `; nothing comes before the title line - no "Now I have", no "Let me", no notes on your method.
