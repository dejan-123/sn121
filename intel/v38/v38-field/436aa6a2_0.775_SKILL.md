# Weekly 1-on-1 brief

The draft is a hypothesis; the signals decide each item's class. Send only the brief: a title line `# <Report name>, <role>: 1-on-1 brief`, then **Context**, **Agenda**, **Parking lot**. Do not announce it or say what you are about to do: the very first characters sent are `# ` and the report's name.

## What earns each class

Classify every draft item and every signal it missed; the brief shows only outcomes. A class nothing in the input supports is absent.

- **Recognition**: someone named in this input credits specific work. Quote who said it about what, never a vague "did something good". No such signal, or a result discussed last meeting: no opener.
- **Unblock**: progress waits on another team, an approval, access or an unanswered request: the manager's move (escalate to that team's lead), naming the team and what waits, not a progress question. Curt messages after repeated unanswered asks, however many, belong here: one escalation-route question, no tone coaching.
- **Decision**: overload the report flagged: the manager backs a fix, often the report's own proposal, not feedback.
- **Feedback**: a "quick" or "low" label on an error or delay that reached a client, customer or operation, or on a request for a process conversation. A first slip the report owns gets questions on the fix and the missing check, not a verdict. A senior audience is not impact.
- **Follow-up**: one instance with no outside harm, or "no other pattern", that left something owed (an answer, a fix): one question on it, no coaching. Nothing owed, or work just in progress: no item.
- **Coaching**: two or more instances. Questions on those instances, aimed at the habit the report can change, building on any cause the report named.
- **Career**: a topic the report raised: one or two questions on named evidence, plus a dedicated career talk if needed. A routine check-in on the draft, not raised by the report: one question on this week's strongest evidence. A promotion or level decision goes to the parking lot (calibration or skip-level); only the report's next step stays.
- **Manager owes**: open items the log assigns to the manager, never Unblock; not the report's overdue items.
- **Dropped**: a status item on work logged DONE or handed off (praise for it feeds Recognition); a coaching label the signals contradict (say what they show). Work in progress, no blocker: one Context line with its figure.
- **Parking lot only**: pay, health, family or personal matters, flexibility for them, HR issues. No talking points, no details.

Past-meeting notes are history: each action reads "status unknown: confirm", and results discussed there are context, not Recognition.

## Context

2-4 sentences on what the figures mean. Each derived figure once, inputs shown: a count or duration against a baseline or estimate as percent change, (new - old) / old, whole percent, signed; a rate already in percent as points (new - old); another score as points and percent. No completion rates. Then one `Draft check:` line: each draft item dropped or routed, and why. Name every missing input.

## Agenda, parking lot

Order: Recognition if earned; time-sensitive Unblock and Decision; the report's own topics; Feedback, Follow-up or Coaching; Career; Manager owes. Each item: `N. **Class: topic**`; if class or weight differs from the draft, then `Draft: <their label> -> <class>, because <signal>.` Coaching and career lines are questions on a named thread, ticket or message. Parking lot: `<neutral topic> -> <venue>` (private conversation, HR, compensation, calibration, async) and nothing else, or "Nothing for the parking lot this week." Never pad it.

Never invent names, dates, durations, figures or causes, even in questions; roles for unnamed people; timing from the input or "before the next 1-on-1". A thin input keeps the draft's questions as **Status** or **Career** items marked "(no signal supplied)", with no guessed state; Context ends with what to pull first.

## Worked example

Call read_reference on `references/line-maintenance-brief.md` before writing: a draft the signals overturn, then how the call moves when the input changes. Copy its moves, never its names, days or facts. Then send the brief, once.

## Why these classes hold

- **Situation before disposition.** A curt message after repeated unanswered requests is the blocker's symptom; coaching tone while the dependency stays open misreads cause (the fundamental attribution error). The manager's lever is the escalation.
- **One is an incident, two is a pattern.** A single rough moment gets a follow-up question on the loop it left open; a repeated miss gets coaching on its mechanism (for example, raising a flag the moment the work grows), not on the person's general skill.
- **Feedback weight follows impact.** Situation, behaviour, impact: if the impact left the team, the conversation is not quick, but a first, owned mistake is met with questions on the repair and the review step that would have caught it. A related process conversation someone else asked for can be routed to its own session.
- **Load is not a skill gap.** When one person has become the queue that several teams wait on, the cure is a staffing or triage decision the manager sponsors, often the one the report already proposed, not feedback.
- **The weekly is the wrong room** for pay, health, personal circumstances and level decisions. They need privacy, policy or a committee, and a printed brief must not carry their details: the parking lot names the topic in neutral words and the venue, nothing more.
- **The manager unblocks.** A dependency on another team is fixed by the person with the authority to escalate, so the brief assigns the move to the manager. The manager's own overdue items sit beside the item they hold up, as commitments.

## Wording

- Quote two to six words of the deciding signal, with its source, so every point is anchored.
- Questions start with what, which, how or where; never "why didn't you".
- Write zero results plainly: "No coaching item this week: one instance, no pattern."
- Each `Draft:` line is where the manager sees a reclassification; keep it to one clause.

Do not announce it or say what you are about to do: the very first characters sent are `# ` and the report's name.
