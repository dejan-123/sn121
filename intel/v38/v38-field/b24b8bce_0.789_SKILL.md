# Weekly 1-on-1 prep brief

The reply is the brief itself. Do not announce it or say what you are about to do: the very first characters sent are `# ` and the title. The readings below are silent; what they decide shows only inside the brief. Open `references/bakery-planner-brief.md` before writing and follow its shape; after reading it, write no line about applying it.

## Silent readings

**1. Test each draft label against the signals**; labels move for five reasons:
- *Finished work.* A status item the log marks done is dropped as status; praise of the result is recognition evidence.
- *Work stuck on someone else* (a team, an approval) becomes **Unblock**: the manager pushes next (escalate, broker). Friction shown while chasing it is a symptom, not Feedback or Coaching: fold it in with one escalation-route question.
- *Missing, contradicted or single evidence for a trait.* **Coaching** needs two or more instances; ask about their mechanism, from the report's own words. A label the signals contradict is dropped, naming them. A single stumble whose error reached no client or other team, however senior the room, gets at most one **Follow-up** question on what it still owes.
- *Misjudged size.* A light feedback label on a slip whose error reached a client or another team is a full **Feedback** item: the fix and the missing step, as questions; a first slip the report owns is no reprimand. A process talk a senior person asked for: parking lot, with them.
- *Misjudged load.* A low label on a capacity problem that blocks other teams or drew senior attention is a high **Workload/Decision** item backing the report's own remedy.

Venues: pay, health, family, leave or flexibility go to the parking lot by topic only, no details or talking points. A promotion or level decision goes to its venue (calibration, skip-level; say if already booked), and only the report's next step stays. Career topics the report raised: 1-2 anchored questions. In-progress work with no blocker: one context line.

**2. Recognition** only from a named signal this week (who praised what, where); never a vague "did great"; else no opener.

**3. Figures.** Each pair (this period vs last or a trailing average, for each table row; estimate vs actual; scoped vs delivered) gets one change in the context, both inputs shown: (new - old) / old in whole percent; scores and rates move in points first (40 -> 50 = +10 points, +25%). No completion rates or "N of M done". Never invent a period, percentage, count, name or date.

**4. No signals:** say so, mark each question "(unanchored)", put what to pull before the meeting in the context.

**5. Only a past meeting's notes:** nothing in them is this week's news. Their figures and wins read `-> context only: discussed at that meeting, so no opener`; no Recognition item. Every action is a **Confirm:** item, status unknown, with owner and due date (manager's first), never Unblock or done. The topic left undecided is the live item.

**6. Manager still owes:** only manager-owned rows (log or notes) not marked done. A done row or a Manager's move is not owed.

## Brief shape

`# 1-on-1 brief: <Name>, <role>`, then:
- **Context**: 2-4 sentences: the week's story, derived figures, missing inputs; then `Manager still owes: <row> (<status>)` or `Manager still owes: nothing; the manager's rows are done or absent.`
- **From the draft**: one line per draft item or note topic:
  - `"<draft words>" -> not <label>: <signal>, so <Class>`
  - `"<draft words>" -> kept as <Class>, <signal>`
  - `"<draft words>" -> dropped: <reason>` or `-> parking lot: <venue>`
- **Agenda**: numbered; recognition (if warranted), unblocks and decisions, the report's topics, coaching, career. Each `**<Class>: <topic>**`, at most 3 sentences; coaching and career lines are questions naming their thread, ticket or pattern. A manager action reads `Manager's move: ...`.
- **Parking lot**: `<topic> -> <venue>`, only topics for another venue, no details; or `Nothing for the parking lot this week.`

Nothing comes after the parking lot.

## Why the venues differ

A weekly 1-on-1 holds what the two people can move this week: blockers the manager can clear, feedback on recent work, the report's own questions. Pay and level decisions run through the compensation and calibration processes; health and family matters need a private conversation that a printed brief must not carry; a process dispute raised by another senior person needs that person in the room. Routing these is respect for the topic, not avoidance.

Unblocking is the manager's job, not a status question for the report. Feedback is not the fix for a process gap: when a review step was missing, the item is the step, and any template or rule the manager owes is part of it.

## Wording

- No bare "you". Write "Manager's move:" for the manager's actions; questions for the report use the report's name or "they".
- Questions name their anchor: the message, the ticket, the log entry, the figure. A question that could be asked of anyone at any company is replaced or cut. A generic "career check-in" with no topic from the report gets one question tied to this week's strongest signal.
- Keep the draft's own words in the From-the-draft lines so the manager sees which label moved and why. Counts in these lines (asks, pings, days) come from the input, never from the example.
- One label per item. If an item is both a fix and a lesson, it is one Feedback item, not Feedback plus Coaching.
- Talking points carry facts from the input only. If the input does not give a date, a number or a name, the brief does not either.

## Length and thin inputs

Aim for 350-700 words. A thin input gives a short brief that names its gaps; never pad it with generic questions, a recognition opener or a parking-lot entry. A rich input is cut to the calls that matter: each agenda item at most 3 sentences, the context at most 4.

The reply is the brief alone: the very first characters are `# ` and nothing follows the parking lot.
