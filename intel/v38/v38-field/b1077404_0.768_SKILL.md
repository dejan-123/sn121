# Re-filing a 1-on-1 draft against the signals

Before writing, open `references/library-desk-worked-brief.md` and match its layout and length.

## Silent readings

**1. Trail.** Name each absent kind of input; never fill a gap with a guessed signal, name, date or number. An action with no recorded status is "status unknown, confirm". If the only input is an earlier meeting's notes, every action in them is in that state, and results reported there were praised then: header only, no Recognition item.

**2. Re-filing.** The class follows the evidence, not the manager's word: Recognition, Unblock, Decision, Coaching, Career, Status.
- Status on work marked done: dropped to Off the draft; praise for it is recognition evidence.
- Waiting on another team (unanswered pings, pending access): **Unblock**; the manager escalates or brokers, naming the team.
- In progress, nothing blocking: one header clause, never an item.
- Coaching needs two or more instances with no blocker behind them. Contradicted by the signals: drop it, say why. One rough moment in otherwise good work: drop the label, keeping at most a one-question Status item on its open follow-up. Sharp messages after repeated unanswered requests are a blocker symptom: one question inside the Unblock, never Coaching.
- A real pattern: **Coaching**, only questions, on the named thread; if the report named it first, start from their words and the mechanism, not the trait.
- "Quick feedback" on a mistake a client or customer saw: a full item asking about the fix and the skipped review step; a first, owned mistake gets questions.
- A label that understates a load (others queued behind one person, leaders involved): **Decision** on workload, backing the report's proposal.
- The manager's open items: on the You owe line and beside the item they affect; never in the parking lot.

**3. Routing.** To the parking lot as topic and venue only, with no talking points or details:
- pay, grade, compensation: the compensation process or HR;
- health, family, personal matters, flexibility: a private conversation (HR if needed);
- a career-path or level question the report raised: a dedicated career conversation; the agenda keeps one line, anchored to the report's request, to agree when. A draft career check-in with no request behind it: parking lot only, no agenda line;
- promotion packets and level decisions: calibration or the skip-level; the report's next step stays on the agenda;
- a process talk someone else requested: its own meeting.
Empty: "Nothing for the parking lot this week." Never pad it.

**4. Recognition test.** Open with recognition only when a named signal from this period earns it (a named person's praise, a shipped result), replacing any vague shoutout.

**5. Header arithmetic.** Counts, days, sizes: (new - old) / old, whole percent, signed: 12 vs 15 (-20%). Scores and percentages: points, a subtraction: 81% vs 77% (+4 points). A slowed stream gets its figure too. No rate over action items; open ones keep the input's own status word, none inferred. Each figure once, in the header.

## The brief

Nothing before the title or after the parking lot; 350 to 700 words; at most 3 sentences per item, questions included. Each draft item appears once: kept, dropped to Off the draft, or routed.
- `# 1-on-1 brief: <name>, <role>`, then an italic inputs line.
- **Where things stand**: 3 to 5 sentences: figures, work in flight, the missing input (thin input: what to pull). Then a **You owe:** line for the manager's open items, if any.
- **Agenda**, numbered: earned recognition; unblocks and decisions; the report's topics; coaching; the career line. Each: bold class and topic, a bracket with the draft label and the moving signal, then points. Coaching and career lines are questions naming a thread or ticket.
- **Off the draft**: dropped or header-only draft items, with the signal; omit if none.
- **Parking lot**: topic and venue; never a manager's to-do.

## Why the venues differ

A weekly 1-on-1 is for work that can move this week: an unblock only the manager can trigger, a decision, the report's own topics, feedback on something observed. Anything that needs preparation, privacy or another decision-maker gets its own room. A career path needs the level guide and time; a promotion is decided in calibration, not across a desk; pay follows the compensation cycle; health and family stay between two people and HR. Routing a topic is not dismissing it: the brief names the venue so the report hears that it is taken seriously.

An unblock is not a status check. The report has already done the chasing; the next move belongs to someone with authority over the other team's queue. Feedback is not the fix for a process gap either: when a mistake comes from an unclear step (a review nobody owned, a handover nobody confirmed), the item asks about the step, and the manager's own overdue item often sits right beside it.

A trait word in the draft (checked out, careless, slow, abrupt) is a hypothesis. Behaviour on a named ticket or message is evidence. Check the word against the trail before it reaches the page, and say in one line which signal confirmed or overturned it.

## Header figures in practice

- Round to the whole percent, half away from zero, and keep the sign.
- A rate or a score is compared in points; never take a percent change of a percentage.
- Name the baseline in the same clause as the figure.
- If one of the two numbers is missing, give the one you have and say the comparison cannot be made; do not estimate.

## Writing the items

- Talking points are tethered: each sentence names the ticket, message, document or figure it rests on. A question with no anchor in the input is marked as needing a signal.
- Coaching questions ask about the decision point, not the outcome: when did the change become visible, what made it hard to raise, what would make flagging it early easy next time.
- The manager's own commitments are written plainly as theirs ("you owe the figures; send them before the review").
- Timing uses only what the input states (a due date, end of week, before a review). Never invent periods, percentages, counts, names or dates.
- Plain, dense sentences. Three is the ceiling per item, not the target.

## Delivery

Do not announce the brief or say what you are about to do: the very first characters sent are `# 1-on-1 brief:` followed by the report's name. The last line sent is the final parking-lot entry.
