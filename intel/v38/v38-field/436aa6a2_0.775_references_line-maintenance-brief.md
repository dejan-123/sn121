# Worked example: a line-maintenance planner whose draft agenda the signals overturn

A brief is written straight out, with no notes or walk-through ahead of it: its first characters are `# ` and the report's name.

Invented case: Corvane Air, regional line-maintenance base. Every name, day and figure below is made up and belongs to this case only; none of it goes into your brief.

## The brief as sent

# Ilse Marchetti, Line Maintenance Planner: 1-on-1 brief

**Context**
Work orders closed fell to 18 against a trailing average of 24 (-25%), and all five on hold are aircraft QTV waiting on Technical Services, so the drop is a dependency, not Ilse's pace. Last week's open actions are her calibration register (8 of 20 entries moved, no blocker reported), her QTV write-ups (overdue, held by the same instructions) and the manager's sign-off authority request to Quality, overdue. The QTV drain-valve deferral runs out Saturday, and nothing in the input shows a date from Technical Services.
Draft check: the QTR status is dropped (package released Tuesday) and the shift allowance is routed (pay belongs with HR). Zero coaching items: one owned slip, no earlier instance.

**Agenda**

1. **Recognition: QTR back on the line a shift early**
Draft: "nights said something nice" -> the night shift lead's handover note: QTR "back on the line a shift early", "cleanest A-check package this year." Credit the package order that took away the wait for panel access, which is what the note praises.

2. **Unblock: Technical Services repair instructions for QTV**
Draft: Status -> Unblock, because five work orders, three deferrals and Ilse's overdue write-ups wait on Technical Services after two requests with no reply.
Manager's move: call the Technical Services lead for all three instructions, drain valve first. Ask Ilse: "Which of the three grounds QTV if it slips past Saturday, and which can wait?"

3. **Feedback: the QTS overnight package**
Draft: "quick feedback" -> Feedback, because the missing card delayed a departure by 35 minutes and the QA lead has asked for a process conversation.
She owned it in her message to the manager ("the QTS miss is mine"), so open with questions, not a verdict: "Is the rebuilt template live, and were any open packages built from the old one?" Then: "What check before release would catch a superseded card, and who should run it?"

4. **Career: her question on maintenance control**
Draft: "mentioned it at some point" -> her own submitted topic.
Ask: "Which parts of the QTV chase and the QTR re-sequencing felt closest to control-room work, and which control decisions have you not yet made?" If it needs more than this slot, book a dedicated career conversation.

5. **Manager owes: deferral sign-off authority**
The request to Quality is overdue and bears on her control-room question, since sign-off is part of that role. The input gives no date, so commit to one in the meeting.

**Parking lot**
- Shift-allowance question -> compensation conversation with HR
- How revised cards reach overnight packages -> separate session with the QA lead and Ilse.

## What would change the call

**If the slip had stayed inside the hangar.** Had the night shift caught the missing card before release, with no delay and no QA request, item 3 shrinks to a one-line **Follow-up**: one instance, owned, nothing reached the operation, so the only question is the open loop, "Is the rebuilt template live?" If the log instead showed a second slipped card in an earlier week, the item becomes Coaching, with questions on both packages aimed at how revisions reach her template, not at care in general.

**If the manager had pasted only the draft.** With no tracker, deferral list, log or chat, the brief stays near 350 words. Context names each missing input; there is no recognition opener, because nothing names the work; the QTV and QTS lines become **Status** items marked "(no signal supplied)", each one plain question with no guessed state; the shift allowance still goes to the parking lot; Context ends with what to pull: the tracker, the deferral list and the last handover log. If the paste were only last meeting's notes, every action in them reads "status unknown: confirm", the manager's own included, and a result discussed in that meeting is context, not an opener.

**If she had asked to be put forward for senior planner.** A level decision is not made in a weekly. With a calibration slot already booked, the parking lot reads "Senior planner case -> calibration (slot booked)", and the agenda keeps only her next step, say shift-lead references the action log shows as not started, as one question: "Which shift leads can speak to the QTR and QTV work, and when will you ask them?" The QTR praise becomes evidence for that case, not a promise.

Your reply after reading this file is the brief alone: no "Now I'll apply the skill", no item-by-item notes. Its first characters are `# ` and the report's name, and its last line is the parking lot.
