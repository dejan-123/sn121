## The brief

# 1-on-1 brief: Mateo Lindqvist-Ruiz, Site Reliability Engineer

## Context

The failover runbook is already done (published Tuesday), so the draft's status item drops, and Ines Varga credited Mateo's 02:10 rollback call with keeping last week's outage under 20 minutes. Handover reviews look slow on paper, 3 closed against a trailing average of 8 (−63%), but four of them are parked on the database team's sign-off after two pings: the week's theme is a blocker you can remove, not a pace problem. You owe Mateo the capacity-planning brief. He has asked about the platform architecture group, and he has raised a possible move to Lisbon for personal reasons.

## Agenda

### 1. The 02:10 rollback (Recognition)

Ines Varga named it in #incident-review on Wednesday, so the shoutout the draft could not place has a source.
- "Ines said your rollback call at 02:10 kept the outage under 20 minutes. What told you it was time to roll back rather than keep diagnosing?"

### 2. Handover reviews waiting on the database team (Blocker)

The draft calls this slowness, but four reviews are blocked on DB sign-off and Mateo has pinged twice in #sre, so the work for you is removing the block.
- "Four reviews are waiting on the DB team after two pings. Who on their side should I go to so these move this week?"
- "Is anything else holding the rest, or do they move once sign-off lands?"

### 3. My overdue capacity-planning brief (Manager follow-up)

The action log shows your item overdue; give Mateo a date before asking him for anything new.

### 4. Path to the platform architecture group (Career)

This is Mateo's own topic, and the runbook and the rollback call are his most recent evidence of wider scope.
- "When you picture the architecture group, which part of the runbook work felt closest to what they do?"
- "What would you want to try next quarter to test that path?"

## Parking lot

- Possible move to Lisbon (partner's new job): a private conversation this week, outside this agenda, and the relocation policy with HR if he wants it. No talking points here.

## Variant: the input is thin

Had the manager pasted only the draft agenda ("status on the runbook, workload, he wants to discuss his pay") with no action items, figures or Slack, the context would say exactly that: no signals, action items or figures were provided, so the brief asks rather than asserts. Every talking point becomes an open question ("Where is the runbook now, and what is left?"), a line "Before the meeting: pull the runbook ticket and his current queue" is added, there is no recognition item because nothing in the input earns one, and pay goes to the parking lot ("a dedicated pay conversation following HR's process; no talking points here"). No figure, date or achievement is written that the input does not contain.

## Variant: a first slip the report already owns

Had the draft said "quick feedback: he sent the client report before my review" and the signals shown a first-time slip that Mateo had already admitted and was fixing, with the account director asking for a process conversation and the manager's own review checklist overdue, the brief would hold one short item classed Follow-up, not Coaching: one sentence that he owned the fix, and one question, "What do you need from me while you close it out with the client?" The process conversation goes to the parking lot ("a separate conversation with the account director and you about the review gate; no talking points here"), and the overdue checklist becomes the manager's own follow-up item.

## Variant: the coaching note has a pattern behind it

Had the manager written "coach him on estimates" and the signals shown two sprints in a row over estimate (2 days became 5, then 3 became 6) with Mateo's own message that the scope grew from 9 services to 15 and he flagged it late, coaching stays on the agenda, built on the cause he named: "The scope went from 9 services to 15 before you flagged it. What would have let you raise it on day one?" It is framed as a question about raising scope changes early, and it credits that he already spotted the problem himself.

## Checks that carry over

- Every item's class comes from its signal, and a reclassification is stated in one line.
- Recognition only with a named signal; coaching only with a pattern; blockers are for the manager to remove.
- Sensitive and out-of-scope topics go to the parking lot with a destination and no talking points.
- Derived figures use two numbers the input gives, one step of arithmetic, both numbers shown.
- Missing inputs are named; nothing is invented.

Write the brief now. Its first characters are `# 1-on-1 brief:` followed by the report's name. Do not write "Now I'll prepare the brief", "Here is the brief", a list of what you will do, or any other sentence, note or horizontal rule before it: the manager asked for the brief, and the brief is the whole reply.
