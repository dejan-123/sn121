Two invented weeks, answered in full — one standard, one thin — then the
branches that change the calls. Every name, team, and figure is invented.

## Week one — Renata Iwu, Operations Analyst, Freight Division, Marlowe Logistics

The input that arrived:

- Manager's draft agenda: "Status on the carrier-onboarding tracker.
  Coaching — Renata's replies to the dispatch desk have gotten short and
  it's starting to get noticed. Recognition for something, she had a good
  week I think. Career check-in quickly at the end."
- Open items: Renata's onboarding checklist refresh — OVERDUE (due last
  Friday). Manager's division-capacity summary for Renata — IN PROGRESS.
- Sprint table: onboarding tracker 3 tickets closed vs 8 trailing 3-week
  average; 4 blocked tickets, all marked "waiting on Legal contract review".
- Signals: Tue 09:20 Renata → #ops-dispatch: "Third request on the Legal
  review — the tracker can't close without the counter-signed terms."
  Thu 15:40 dispatch lead Marta → #team-wins: "Renata caught the duplicate
  billing file before it hit invoicing — saved us an embarrassing call
  with Crossmark Freight." Wed 11:05 Renata → manager DM: "Covering Tomas's
  queue while he's out, replies will be shorter than usual this week."
- Renata's submitted topic: "Whether the senior analyst certification is
  something I should start."

### The prep brief

**Context:** Renata Iwu, Operations Analyst, Freight Division. Week is
blocked-not-slow: 3 tickets closed against an 8-ticket trailing average,
and all 4 blocked tickets sit on one upstream cause — Legal's contract
review, which is not hers to clear. Two public wins on record (billing-file
catch, dispatch coverage) and one submitted career topic.

| # | Item (as filed) | What the signals say | Call | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Status: carrier-onboarding tracker | 3 of 8-avg closed; 4 blocked, all on Legal | Status — surface the upstream block as the manager's action | sprint table |
| 2 | Coaching: short replies to dispatch | Covering Tomas's queue — short replies are a coverage artifact, not a tone pattern | Downgrade — fold into item 1's capacity question | her Wed DM |
| 3 | Recognition "for something" | Marta's public post: caught the duplicate billing file before invoicing | Recognition — named, specific | #team-wins Thu |
| 4 | Career check-in "quickly at the end" | Her own submitted topic, high salience | Career — real item, not a footnote | her submission |

**Talking points:**

- Item 1: "Legal's review is the whole bottleneck — what's the fastest way
  I can help unstick that counter-signature, escalate or a nudge from me?"
  (The sprint table shows all 4 blocked tickets on one approval.)
- Item 2: "With Tomas's queue on your desk too, is the coverage load
  workable this week or do we need to rebalance something?" (Her own DM
  names the cause; the question tests load, not tone.)
- Item 4: "You've been thinking about the senior analyst cert — what
  would make the timing right: the Crossmark save and this week's coverage
  are exactly the kind of evidence it asks for." (Her topic, her words,
  anchored to two named signals.)

**Routing notes:** No sensitive item this week. Renata's OVERDUE checklist
refresh reads as blocked by the same Legal review — blocked, not
delinquent; say so rather than letting it sit against her.

**For you (manager):** your division-capacity summary is still IN PROGRESS
— and it's the document that would answer her real question in item 2
(can this coverage load continue?). Also: the Legal escalation is yours,
not hers — three asks from a report is the point where it moves up.

**Evidence gaps:** none material — the signals cover every filed item.

## Week two — same shape, thin input

The input that arrived: manager's draft agenda only — "how's the tracker,
quick workload check, she asked about her review timeline." No signals
attached, no action items, no figures.

### The prep brief

**Context:** Renata Iwu, Operations Analyst. No signals were pulled this
week — the brief is honest about that rather than padded around it.

| # | Item (as filed) | What the input shows | Call |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Status: carrier-onboarding tracker | No figures supplied | Status — genuine question, no anchor claimed |
| 2 | Workload check-in | No signals supplied | Question — workload evidence is this week's gap |
| 3 | Review timeline | A compensation-adjacent topic she raised | Route out — name the venue |

**Talking points:**

- "Where does the tracker stand against last week's numbers — and what's
  the one thing blocking it?"
- "How's the load this week — anything I should be taking off your
  plate?"

**Routing notes:** Review timeline is a compensation and leveling
conversation — it belongs in a dedicated review meeting with the band
data in hand, not as talking points in a weekly. The correct move in the
room: acknowledge the request, book the dedicated slot.

**Evidence gaps (this week's real content):** sprint/tracker figures —
absent; action-item list — absent; any signal on workload — absent. Pull
all three before the meeting or the status and workload items are
conversations with no ground under them.

**Recognition:** absent — nothing in this week's input warrants one.

## A third shape — the input arrives as last week's notes

Sometimes the manager pastes notes from the previous 1-on-1 instead of a
draft agenda. Nothing to reclassify — the work is derivation.

Given notes like: "Discussed: onboarding tracker progress (Renata reports
60% through the Legal-wait items). Action: the manager to send the Legal
escalation draft by Nov 12. Action: Renata to draft the coverage proposal by Nov 13.
Personal: Renata mentioned a parent-care situation may need December
flexibility." — the brief becomes:

| # | Item (derived) | What the notes show | Call |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Legal escalation — YOUR action, due Nov 12 | Manager-owned, due date against the meeting date | Status — surface as the manager's item first |
| 2 | Coverage proposal — Renata's action, due Nov 13 | Report-owned, at-risk by the meeting | Status question |
| 3 | December flexibility / parent care | Sensitive | Route — support note, zero talking points |

- The sensitive item routes as a one-line note: "December flexibility was
  raised — handle as a support and coverage-planning conversation, not an
  agenda item with drafted points." Named, given its venue, done.
- Due-date math is the header's job: items due Nov 12–13 against a Nov 14
  meeting are the week's at-risk set — compute the gap, don't copy the date.
- The parent-care note connects to the coverage thread without being
  conflated with it: the flexibility need informs the coverage proposal;
  the brief links them, it doesn't merge them.

## The branches — same week, different facts

What changes each call:

- **If item 2's signal had been a pattern** (three threads of short or
  clipped replies across weeks, no coverage note): the coaching item
  stands — but still as a question: "I've noticed the dispatch replies
  getting clipped across a few threads — what's driving it?" The pattern
  earns the item; it never earns a verdict stated as fact.
- **If the sensitive item is filed** (e.g., she asked about a transfer
  to another division): it appears in routing notes only — named, given
  its correct venue (a career-path conversation, possibly skip-level),
  zero talking points drafted in this brief.
- **If the blocker were hers** (blocked tickets owned by Renata, none
  upstream): item 1 becomes a delivery question anchored to the ticket
  count — the same row, opposite owner. The table decides the question;
  the question never decides the table.
- **If the input had arrived as last week's notes instead of an agenda** —
  the contract is unchanged but the derivation inverts: the agenda table
  is built from the notes' actions and threads, every row marked
  inferred, and each open item's gap is measured against the meeting
  date. The label column reads "what the notes imply," and the
  evidence-gap rules apply double — a derived item states its inference
  instead of pretending a filed label exists.
- **If an item had been filed Low while carrying High weight** — a
  "quick question" on a dependency four other teams are waiting on: the
  filed priority is a claim like any other label. Re-tier it in the
  table, name the waiting parties, and say why the weight changed.
  A mis-weighted item is the same failure as a mis-typed one.
- **If she had already diagnosed the pattern herself** — a coaching item
  where the report's own DM names the failure and its cause: the session
  stops being a lecture. The question becomes the protocol — "what
  flags the next one early?" — anchored to her self-report, not to the
  manager's summary of it.
- **If the manager's own overdue item caused the miss** — a deliverable
  on the manager's side that would have prevented the report's flag:
  it surfaces as the manager's action in the routing notes, never as
  the report's coaching item. Same fact, opposite owner — the row is
  charged where the cause lives.

## What this shape enforces

- Every filed item gets a row — the manager's label, the signals' read,
  the call, the anchor. No item is answered before it is classified.
- The manager's label is a claim, not a column to copy — the signals'
  column is what the input actually shows.
- Every coaching or career item survives only as a question tethered to
  a named signal. No anchor, no talking point.
- Sensitive items get a venue, not a paragraph.
- The header computes: point changes, completion rates, blocked counts —
  never restates raw numbers.
- The manager's own open items surface; the brief's reader is the
  manager, and their deliverables are part of the prep.
- A thin week produces a thin brief. Padding is a worse failure than
  a short agenda.
