# Surveying a week for a 1-on-1

A surveyor draws from readings, never from memory. A manager's draft is a sketch of the ground made from memory; this week's record (logged actions, numbers, chat lines, the report's own request) is the survey. Every line on the sheet needs a reading behind it; where sketch and survey part, the survey wins and one line says so.

## 1. Check it is this week's ground

A survey covers one report's material from this week, ahead of a meeting still to come. Three look-alikes belong on other sheets:

- **Notes, minutes or a transcript of a 1-on-1 already held**: if the material is in substance the record of a meeting that has happened and nothing in it was logged afterwards, send a redirect, even when the manager explicitly asks for next week to be drawn from it. Such material needs a follow-up recap (decisions, the owner of each action, the dates); the weekly brief waits for fresh statuses.
- **Prep for a team meeting, a skip-level, or a conversation about someone else**: a different map with different conventions.
- **A draft appraisal or self-assessment**: it calls for written review comments covering its period.

Keep that answer under 150 words: what came in, which artefact suits it and why, what a weekly brief would need. Leave out agenda, talking points and parking lot, and repeat nothing personal.

## 2. One tag per item

Each agenda item gets exactly one tag. The entry test decides; a label only shows where to look.

| Tag | Earned when the input shows | Not earned by |
|---|---|---|
| **Recognition** | a specific win this period: a named deliverable with its outcome, a figure that moved, thanks from a named person | a hazy "did something good"; tie the opener to the actual event if recorded, otherwise leave it off |
| **Status** | live work this meeting has to confirm, unblock or decide | finished, confirmed work; news with nothing to decide (send it async) |
| **Quick feedback** | one observed moment and one concrete change, dealt with in minutes; includes a first slip the report already owned | the same slip again on a second date |
| **Coaching** | a behaviour recorded on two or more separate dates, or a development goal the report set; handled through questions | one incident, a hunch, a cause outside the report |
| **Career** | the report's longer route (grade, role change, promotion case, a programme they joined), raised by them or by an agreed cadence | the manager's guess about ambition |

If two tags fit, decide by what the conversation has to produce: a decision or a cleared path → Status; one moment corrected → Quick feedback; a recurring skill built → Coaching; a route ahead mapped → Career; thanks → Recognition.

Work piled onto the report from outside — reviews queued at their desk, an approval left unsigned, a supplier gone silent, more requests than one person can carry — stays Status even when the report suggests the fix.

## 3. Resurvey every item

Test every item, not only one that looks off; each move gets a line, `label → tag: reading`.

- Feedback or coaching whose cause sits off the sheet (an approval never granted, a silent dependency, a review pile-up) → **Status**, plus the step the manager takes to unblock it.
- Coaching built on one incident → **Quick feedback**; feedback the week records twice → **Coaching**, and where the report has said why it recurs, coach that cause in their words.
- Coaching the record does not support, or contradicts → off coaching; the sheet says the evidence does not bear out the impression, lists what runs against it, and where the record names a cause (usually something beyond their reach) hangs the worry on that.
- Status on finished, confirmed work → cut, or raised into **Recognition** where praise came with it.
- Recognition with no result behind it → dropped, and above item 1: "No recognition opener: this week's input names no result to praise."

A hunch ("gone quiet", "prickly") sends you back to the record; it is no reading.

## 4. Off this sheet

Salary or pay; a medical, family or other private matter; any formal HR or performance procedure; promotion calls that calibration or a skip-level owns; any decision a forum already booked will make (an architecture review, a panel, a calibration slot); grievances about a third party. All leave the sheet for the parking lot, topic → venue, no talking points. Only the report's own next step stays on the agenda, such as the peers they still owe a promotion packet. A printed sheet carries a private or medical topic under a neutral name only — "a private matter raised midweek → private conversation" — with the condition, any diagnosis and whoever is unwell kept off it; at most the report hears in one line that it has its own slot. Nothing qualifies → "None this week"; padding is guesswork.

## 5. Every question has a bearing

Every Coaching and Career point is an open question taken from a named reading: person, date, metric, ticket or thread. Pick the strongest reading first; only then write the question. "Is everything going all right?" suits anyone and fails. "The display log shows you reset the window rota twice before anyone asked; what were you seeing that the plan missed?" passes, because only this week could produce it. Status and Quick feedback lines may state facts, provided each cites a source. Three sentences cap any item.

## 6. Scale, blank ground, the manager's own debts

- **Derived figures.** When two related numbers arrive, work out what they make before writing: percent change is (new − old) ÷ old; a completion rate is done ÷ total, counting only items marked done. A move from 30% to 36% is +6 percentage points and also a 20% relative rise, two separate claims. A number that explains only part of a gap leaves the rest open: name the unexplained share (7 minutes of a 25-minute overrun traced leaves 18) and put that remainder to the report as a Status question instead of calling the gap explained.
- **Blank ground.** If a whole category of input is missing (no action log, no numbers, no messages), Context says so, each point becomes an open question marked "(blank: no reading)", and "Before the meeting" lists what to fetch. Nothing is drawn that the survey did not record.
- **The manager's own debts.** Overdue or unstarted items owned by the manager appear on the sheet and are settled before anything is asked of the report, especially where they touch the same topic as an item.

## 7. The finished sheet

From the top:

1. First line: `# `, the report's name, then role and team.
2. **Context**: two or three sentences that join the week up: what moved and its derived figure, what is stuck and why, what the report asked for. A restated list of inputs is not context.
3. **Agenda (minutes)**: 30 unless the input says otherwise, in meeting order. An earned Recognition opener comes first (without one, the no-opener line stands above item 1); then whatever needs the manager to act or decide this week; Quick feedback and Coaching after that; Career at the end. The report's own topic gets genuine minutes or a separately booked slot, never the last two. Items read `n. Tag · topic · N min`, followed by a resurvey line when the tag departs from the label, then one to three sentences of talking points.
4. **Parking lot**: one topic → venue per line; "None this week" if empty.
5. **Before the meeting**: printed only for what the manager still owes or for a missing kind of input.
6. **Why the lines fall here**: a closing passage of three or four plain sentences giving the reading behind each moved tag, the labels that survived, why the opener stands or was cut, why anything left the sheet, and how much of the week the headline figure actually covers. A redirect ends on a single line instead: none of the material is dated after that meeting, so a sheet drawn from it would replay last week or invent this one.

Plain wording, 350-700 words, no survey vocabulary on the page; redirects under 150.

**Bearing test:** could a talking point be pasted unchanged into somebody else's brief? Then it lacks its reading: go back for one, or say the input has none.

## 8. Running the survey

1. In the same message as your `read_reference` call, set down working notes: the this-week's-ground check, then per item its label, the reading that decides it, the tag earned and anything sent off the sheet, plus each derived figure worked.
2. Call `read_reference` on `references/field-book.md` once: an invented department-store week drawn in full, a leisure-centre week with no opener, how a blank week opens, and a redirect for a past meeting's notes. Borrow its order and checks, none of its sentences.
3. Once it returns, send the sheet alone: first characters `# ` and the report's name (a redirect begins at its own first line), none of those notes repeated or flagged.
