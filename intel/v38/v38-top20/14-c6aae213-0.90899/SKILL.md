# One-on-One Prompt Book

A stage manager calls a show from the prompt book, never from memory. A manager's draft is a pencilled running order; this week's record (logged actions, numbers, chat lines, the report's own request) is the book. Every cue must be written there; where pencil and book disagree, book wins and one line says so.

## 1. Confirm it is tonight's show

A book is one report's material from this week, before a meeting still in rehearsal. Three look-alikes belong to other productions:

- **Minutes, notes or a transcript of a 1-on-1 already played**: where the material is in substance the record of a meeting already given and nothing in it was logged after that curtain, redirect, even when the manager asks outright for next week to be called from it. What it calls for is a show report, a follow-up recap (who decided what, who owns each action, by when); a weekly brief waits for next week's statuses.
- **Prep for a whole-team meeting, a skip-level, or a talk about a third person**: another stage, other rules.
- **An appraisal or self-assessment draft**: it needs written review comments for its period.

Answer in fewer than 150 words: what arrived, which artefact fits it and why, what material a weekly brief needs. Skip agenda, talking points and parking lot; repeat nothing personal.

## 2. One cue type per item

One tag per agenda item. Entry tests decide; a label only says where to look.

| Tag | Earned when input shows | Not earned by |
|---|---|---|
| **Recognition** | this period's concrete win: a named deliverable and its outcome, a figure that moved, praise from someone named | a vague "something good"; cue the opener from the real event if recorded, else cut it |
| **Status** | work still in rehearsal where this meeting must settle progress, lift a blocker or decide | work already done and confirmed; an update with nothing to decide (send async) |
| **Quick feedback** | a single observed moment and one concrete adjustment, wrapped in minutes; a first slip already owned up to included | that same slip on a second date |
| **Coaching** | a behaviour logged on 2+ distinct dates, or a growth goal the report named; played as questions | a lone incident, a hunch, a cause outside the report |
| **Career** | a report's longer arc (grade, role move, promotion case, a scheme they signed up for), opened by them or on an agreed cadence | a manager's guess at ambition |

Torn between two? Name what this scene must deliver: a decision or a cleared path → Status; a single moment set right → Quick feedback; a skill that keeps coming up → Coaching; a road ahead mapped → Career; thanks → Recognition.

Load stacked on a report — a queue of reviews landing on them, approval sitting unsigned, a workshop that stopped replying, more calls than one deputy can take — holds as Status, even with the report offering a fix.

## 3. Re-block every item against the book

Test every item, not only one standing off its mark; each move gets a line, `label → tag: signal`.

- Feedback or coaching whose cause sits offstage (an approval never granted, a silent dependency, a review pile-up) → **Status**, plus the step a manager takes to unblock it.
- Coaching built on a single incident → **Quick feedback**; feedback that recurs in this week's record → **Coaching**.
- Coaching with no support in the book, or signals running against it → struck from coaching; the page says evidence does not bear out that impression, lists what contradicts it, and where the book does name a cause (usually something held fast well beyond their reach) hangs the manager's worry on that instead.
- Status on work already struck (done, confirmed) → cut, or moved up into **Recognition** where praise came with it.
- Recognition with no result in the book → dropped, and above item 1 stands: "No recognition opener: this week's input names no result to praise."

A hunch ("has gone quiet", "is being prickly") sends you back to the book; it is no cue.

## 4. Send to the wings what plays in another act

Salary or pay; a medical, family or other private matter; any formal HR or performance procedure; promotion calls that calibration or a skip-level owns; grievances about a third party. All exit to the wings: parking lot, topic → venue (pay talk, private HR conversation on leave options, calibration, skip-level), no talking points. Printed pages carry a private or medical topic under a neutral name only — "a private circumstance raised offstage → private conversation" — the ailment, any diagnosis and whoever is unwell staying off the page; at most the report hears in one line that it has its own slot. Nothing qualifies → "None this week"; padding is set dressing.

## 5. No line without a cue light

Every Coaching and Career point is an open question built on a named signal: person, date, metric, ticket or thread. Pick the strongest signal first; only then write the question. "Is everything going all right?" suits anyone and fails. "The rota shows you covered Thursday's and Saturday's get-ins alone; what made asking for a second pair of hands harder than doing it?" passes, because only this week could produce it. Status and Quick feedback lines may simply state facts, provided each cites a source. Three sentences cap any item.

## 6. Count the house, mark dark cues, check your own preset

- **Derived figures.** Where two numbers arrive, state what they make, worked before writing: (new − old) ÷ old gives percent change; done ÷ total gives a completion rate. Going from 30% to 36% means +6 percentage points, which is also a 20% relative rise; keep those apart. A number explaining some of a gap leaves the rest unsettled: name the share nobody has explained — 7 of a 25-minute overrun traced leaves 18 minutes unaccounted — and give what is unaccounted to the report as a Status question rather than calling the gap explained.
- **Dark cues.** If a whole kind of input is missing (no action log, no numbers, or no messages at all), Context says so plainly, every point becomes an open question carrying the mark "(dark: no signal)", and "Before the meeting" names what must be fetched. Never write a cue the book does not hold.
- **Your own preset.** Overdue or unstarted items a manager owns go on this page, and get dealt with before any ask reaches the report, above all where they share an item's topic.

## 7. The printed running order

Print order, top to bottom:

1. Line one: `# `, report's name, role and team.
2. **Context**: what moved (with its derived figure), what is stuck and for what reason, whatever the report raised, synthesised in two or three sentences. Never a restated list.
3. **Agenda (minutes)**: 30 unless stated, in calling order. Earned Recognition opener first (lacking one, the no-opener line sits over item 1); next, anything a manager must act on or settle this week; Quick feedback and Coaching after; Career closes. A topic the report brought gets genuine minutes, or a slot booked separately, never the closing two. Each item reads `n. Tag · topic · N min`, plus a re-block line if its tag differs from what was pencilled, and then one to three sentences of talking points.
4. **Parking lot**: each topic → its venue; "None this week" when nothing qualifies.
5. **Before the meeting**: printed only for a manager's outstanding items or a missing input type.
6. **Why it is called this way**: three or four plain sentences to close the page: each tag you moved with the signal behind it, which pencilled labels stood, why the opener plays or is cut, why anything went to the wings, and how much of the week a headline number actually covers. A redirect takes one closing line of its own instead: nothing in the material carries a date after that meeting, so a page called from it would re-run last week's statuses or invent this week's.

Length 350-700 words, plain wording, no stage language on the page. Redirects stay below 150 words.

**Cue-light test:** ask of each talking point whether it could be printed unchanged in some other report's brief. If it could, go back for its signal, or mark that the input has none.

## 8. Calling the show

1. In the same message as your `read_reference` call, set down the working notes: the tonight's-show test, then per item its pencilled label, the signal that decides it, the tag earned and anything sent to the wings, plus each derived figure worked out.
2. Call `read_reference` on `references/prompt-book.md` once. Inside: an invented archive week called in full, how a dark week opens, and a redirect. Borrow its calling order and its checks; none of its sentences.
3. Once it returns, print the page alone: first characters `# ` and the report's name (a redirect begins at its own first line), no part of those notes replayed or flagged.
