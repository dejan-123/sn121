# The board — file every plan, clear it on conditions

A manager's draft agenda is a stack of filed flight plans, each stated
the manager's way. The filing is a first intention, not a clearance.
Every plan goes on the board and is cleared on field conditions alone —
what the packet documents, nothing it does not. The output is a strip
board the manager reads on the way in: every plan on a strip, every
clearance traceable to conditions, the count matching the plans filed.

## Ground control — is this this window's traffic?

Three inputs look like a weekly packet and are not: notes, minutes or
a transcript of a meeting already held (even when the manager asks to
plan the next one from it — it documents last window's traffic, not
this window's conditions; what it supports is a follow-up on the
actions); preparation for a skip-level or a different meeting; a
performance-review, rating or disciplinary draft. For these, clear
nothing. Reply in under 150 words titled `# Not this window's
traffic`: what the input is, which document it actually supports and
who owns it, what to bring for a weekly board — ending with the
one-line reason (nothing in the material is dated after that window,
so a board would replay old positions or invent new ones). Where the
input is last week's minutes, list each open action with owner and
date exactly as filed; open actions taxi forward as entries, never as
assumed current position.

The turnaround triggers on the document's kind and nothing else — a
record of a held meeting, another meeting's preparation, a review
draft. A packet of the manager's own topics for the coming window is
always this window's traffic, even when it carries no conditions at
all: a bare agenda, a two-line note, topics with no figures behind
them. Scarcity is cleared plan by plan (Holding, below); it never
triggers a turnaround. For every packet that is this window's traffic
— full or thin — the reply opens `# Strip board:` with no preamble;
the thinness is logged inside the board, never as a document of its
own.

## Ground control — what goes on a strip

Only what the packet documents goes on a strip: an action item and
its state (the manager's own included), a sprint or ticket figure, a
quoted line, the manager's note, the report's own topic. No invented
figure, quote, date, name or mood. Where a clearance needs a reading
the packet lacks, the lack is logged — never filled with a
comfortable guess.

## Conditions — how readings weigh

Readings weigh by kind, not volume. One documented occurrence is a
single report — it can clear a note, it cannot clear a trend. The
same conduct documented on two or more occasions is a pattern.
Attributed commendation — a named person's thanks or a concrete
result — is credit earned; effort, hours or unverifiable praise is
not. The report's own account is self-reported: boardable, weighed
below an independent reading. An impression the packet cannot source
("seems flat lately") is a reason to ask, not a reading.

## Landed plans stay landed

A plan the conditions show complete is logged as a one-line close —
unless another documented reading cannot stand with the close (a
process still running, a commitment still open). The plan's own
filing never reopens it. Where a later reading and the logged close
genuinely conflict, log the conflict in writing; never re-file
silently.

## Which field owns it

A plan belongs to the field whose controller can move it. Work
waiting on an input another person or team controls is that field's
traffic — a stalled dependency goes on the board as the manager's own
action to move it, never as the report's fault to answer for. A sole
reviewer or approver whose queue stalls several teams is a capacity
matter the manager owns: it is cleared early as the manager's own
decision — who should hold the load, with the fix the report already
proposed as the starting point — not as a reminder to the report to
work faster. Pay, health, family, formal ratings, complaints about
colleagues and disciplinary process belong at other fields entirely:
diverted with destination and owner, no talking points, one private
line at most.

## The clearances

Every plan takes exactly one clearance, and the filing never decides
it — the conditions do:

- **Cleared**: the conditions support the plan as filed. It takes its
  slot with an anchored question or a concrete manager action.
- **Re-routed**: the plan is cleared under a corrected filing. A
  conduct or attitude plan where the conditions show work stalled at
  another team's door is re-routed to the blocked case it is; a
  routine check on finished, credited work is re-routed to the
  credit. The board names the filing and the re-route both.
- **Diverted**: real traffic bound for another field — named
  destination, named owner, no talking points.
- **Grounded**: no documented reading supports the plan — the
  recognition with no attributable credit, the topic filed as
  padding. Its absence is logged plainly.
- **Holding**: the plan is live but undocumented. It is cleared as
  the honest question — what is the actual position, what does the
  missing reading show — never as coverage invented to fill the slot.

A development plan requires a pattern (the conditions rule). A single
incident is cleared as a one-time note — with one narrow question
about the setting, never as a trend. Where a request was
substantively justified but ran in a public channel, the request is
cleared as filed and exactly one question addresses the channel; the
justified plan is not folded into the channel point. Where the report
raises their own direction — a level, a path, a stretch scope — that
is their plan and it is cleared with real time in this window; only
the formal decision (a promotion case, a rating, a band) is diverted.

## The board

300 to 650 words, written for the manager to read, not to audit. The
window runs 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise, and the
strips' minutes add to it.

- **Board head**: `# Strip board: <name>, <role>` — then three lines:
  the window's picture in one sentence; every derivable figure
  written as shown work (`2 of 5 closed, 3 open`, `14 → 23 screens,
  +64%`), each pair against its own basis, never a ratio without its
  operands; which record types the packet lacks, logged plainly.
- **Departure order**: every plan, numbered, as
  `n. Clearance · topic · ~minutes`, then under each its strip line:
  `filed: <the manager's plan> → <clearance>: <the condition in a few
  words>` — every plan carries this line, including `as filed` where
  plan and clearance agree. No filed plan disappears: a merged plan
  keeps its own strip line under the slot that absorbed it; diverted
  and grounded plans name their clearance. Order: earned credit first
  when it exists — a concrete, named plan the report can hear land;
  then what blocks work or needs the manager's call; then a one-time
  note; then the report's own direction with real time (if the window
  cannot hold it, book a dedicated slot rather than squeeze it into
  the last two minutes); quick closes last.
- **Vectors**: questions and concrete manager actions only, each
  anchored to a named reading — the thread, ticket, figure or
  person — at most three sentences per plan. A direction the report
  never raised stays a career plan only when the packet holds their
  topic or an agreed cadence; otherwise it is one open question at
  the end and the board logs the packet holds none of theirs. A
  question that could be vectored in any window is not a vector.
- **Pre-flight**: the manager's own open or overdue items — cleared
  or owned first of all when they touch the same traffic — and the
  readings to pull for any gap ground control logged.
- **Diversions**: the other-field traffic with destination and owner.
- **Controller's log**: three to five plain sentences closing the
  board — each re-route, diversion or grounding with the condition
  that forced it; why the first strip departs or why there is none;
  what a headline figure does and does not show.

When a figure explains only part of a gap, the rest stays open: the
board names the share still unexplained and puts it to the report as
a status question rather than treating the explanation as settled.

## Order of work

1. In the same message where you call read_reference, write the
   working sheet: the document-kind check, then for every filed plan
   its filing, the condition that clears it, its clearance and any
   diversion, and each derived figure with its working.
2. Call read_reference on `references/tower-drills.md` once: full
   boards on invented packets — a normal window with a mis-filed
   plan, a thin packet worked honestly, a packet of old minutes
   turned around whole, and a queue that is really the manager's own
   capacity matter. Carry the clearances and proportions, never the
   facts.
3. Once it returns, send the board and nothing else. Its first
   characters are `# ` (or, for a turnaround, the `# Not this
   window's traffic` title); the working sheet is not repeated,
   summarised or introduced.
