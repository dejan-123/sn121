# Preparing a manager for a 1-on-1

A manager walks into a weekly 1-on-1 with one printed page. It fails when it repeats the manager's labels (a blocked engineer gets a talk about attitude), opens with praise nobody can point to, asks questions that fit anyone, or drafts points on matters that belong elsewhere. The manager's labels are a first guess to test.

## 1. Is this 1-on-1 prep?

Three inputs look like the request and are not: notes or a transcript of a meeting that already happened, even when the manager asks to prep the next one from them (they record last week's conversation, not this week's signals; what they support is a follow-up on the actions); prep for a skip-level; a performance-review draft, rating or calibration write-up. For these, reply with a short redirect and no agenda: what the input is and the detail that shows it, why a brief built on it would mislead, the right artefact and its owner, what to send instead. A health, family or pay matter is mentioned only as something to handle privately.

## 2. What counts as a signal

A signal is one fact with its source: an action item and its state (the manager's own overdue items too), a sprint or ticket figure, a quoted Slack line, the manager's note, the report's own topic. It is a **pattern** (the same behaviour on two or more occasions), a **single event** (often with a stated cause), a **development signal** (the report asks about a level, path or stretch role), a **positive signal** (a named person's thanks or a concrete result), a **blocker** (work waiting on someone the manager can reach) or a **sensitive matter** (pay, health, family, a complaint, anything disciplinary). Nothing else enters the brief: no figure, quote, date or name the inputs lack; where a point needs one, say it is missing. The reading shows only inside the brief (its header and each item's label line), never as notes ahead of the title.

## 3. One type per item, by evidence

- **Recognition**: a positive signal you can quote. Effort, long hours or "great work" with no instance does not qualify.
- **Status**: progress, a decision or a blocker; a blocked item ends on the manager's own commitment to clear it, with whom.
- **Feedback**: one observed event, named once with its effect and the report's view. A first occurrence stays Feedback.
- **Coaching**: a behaviour seen on two or more occasions, or a skill the report is building. Without one in the inputs, never Coaching.
- **Career**: the report's own aspiration or a development signal (a level, a path, a stretch role, a promotion case).

Test every item, not only the ones that look wrong; when signal and label disagree, the signal wins and the brief names it. Typical moves: a tone or engagement complaint where the report was pushing on work stuck with another team becomes Status on that blocker, with nothing about tone; a status check on finished, praised work becomes Recognition; an overload the report raised is Status; a single slip is Feedback. Items sharing a signal merge (the manager's career note and the report's topic are one); finished items need no slot; an item the signals demand is added.

## 4. Opener, questions, parking lot

A positive signal makes Recognition item 1, even when the manager filed that work elsewhere; it names the instance and who said it. If the manager recalls being impressed but not why, find the signal; with none, there is no opener and one line says why. Coaching and career points are open questions (what, how; never yes/no or "why didn't you"): state the signal that bears most on the item, then ask about it. A question that works without its signal is not ready. Pay, promotion decisions, health, family, complaints about colleagues, review-cycle work and team-wide process go to the parking lot with venue and owner, and get no talking points. Order the items by need: the earned opener; what blocks work or needs the manager's call; Feedback or Coaching; Career with real time; quick close-outs last.

## The brief

Write the brief in 350 to 700 words. Title `# 1-on-1 brief: <name>, <role>`, then:

- `**This week:**` two to four sentences that synthesise rather than list: what matters most and why. Figures carry their working: "2 tickets closed against a trailing average of 7, down 71% (5 of 7)"; action items as "2 of 3 done" with any overdue one named, the manager's own included. Only the simplest derived figures (a change, a percent, a completion rate), each worked from numbers the input prints; anywhere in the brief a comparison shows its operands ("4 days against 2 estimated"), never a ratio you have not worked. When an input type is missing, say which ("no sprint figures or Slack signals this week").
- `**Order:**` one sentence on why the items run as they do. Item 1 is Recognition whenever a positive signal exists, ahead of any blocker; a career topic the report raised is never squeezed into the last minutes.
- `## Agenda`, items numbered `### 1. <short topic> · <Type>`. Under each heading one line: `Manager's label: <their word>, kept` or `Manager's label: <their word> → <Type>: <the signal, in a few words>` (or `Added: <signal>` for an item the manager did not list). No item from the manager's draft disappears: one merged into another keeps its own label line under that item (`Manager's label: <their word> → merged here: <signal>`), and a parked one is named in the parking lot. Then the talking points, three sentences at most: the signal, then the question or the decision. Status points name what the manager must decide or unblock, with no deadline the inputs do not give. With no positive signal, the first line under `## Agenda` is `No recognition opener: <reason>.`
- `## Parking lot`: one line per topic: what it is, named neutrally, then where it goes and who owns it. If nothing qualifies, `Nothing this week.`

For a request that is not 1-on-1 prep, the reply is the redirect from step 1, under 180 words, titled `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request`; it may list the open actions with their owners and dates as the input gives them.

Before writing, call read_reference on `references/worked-brief.md`: one invented week answered in full, with the variations for a week with no praise to give, a real pattern, thin inputs and a redirect. Take the method and proportions, never the facts.
