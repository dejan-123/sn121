# Four short cases and a worked week

The people, the lab and every number below are made up. Use them to see how the reasoning runs and how long each part is; none of it belongs in a reply.

## Case one: only a draft came

The manager wrote: "Not much from me this week. 1. Where is the hardness-tester validation? 2. How is he finding the night rota? 3. He wants to talk about his pay band. 4. Maybe something on his development." No rows, no counts, no messages. The whole reply:

## Notes on the week
First, is this prep: yes, for a meeting still to come, but a thin week: the draft arrived on its own, without rows, counts or messages.
Action rows: none were pasted.
1. "Where is the hardness-tester validation?" There is no row, no date and no message about it, so its state is unknown and it can only be asked: Status.
2. "How is he finding the night rota?" Nothing from him is on file and nothing shows requests stacking up on him, so there is no block to act on yet, only a question: Status.
3. "He wants to talk about his pay band." Pay leaves the meeting; it is parked and nothing about it is drafted.
4. "Maybe something on his development." He has not asked it; you added it. That makes it one question, placed last.
Leaves the meeting: his pay band, for a separate pay conversation.
Not in the draft: no rows, counts or messages came with it, so nothing here can be confirmed and there is nobody to quote for an opener.
Sums: none; the paste holds no numbers.

# 1-on-1 brief: Dario Falk, Test Technician
Only your draft arrived, so every point below is a question and nothing is asserted. There is no opener, because no message or result gives you anything to quote, and no figure, because none was sent. Action items: no log was pasted.

**1. The hardness-tester validation — Status**
You do not know how far it has got. "Where has the validation reached, what remains, and is anything holding it?" Listen for a date that has slipped or a wait on another team: both would be yours to pick up.

**2. The night rota — Status**
Nothing on file says whether it suits him. "What has the night rota made easier, and what has it made harder?" Listen for requests reaching him from more people than one technician can answer, which would turn this from a check-in into a block.

**3. His development — Career**
This one is yours, not his, so it is a single question through the one piece of work your draft names. "Which part of the validation work would you like more of?"

**Parking lot:**
- His pay band: a separate pay conversation with HR; you set it up and tell him when. Nothing on pay is drafted here.

**Before the meeting:** pull the action log, the validation's current state and his messages from this week, so that the next brief can state things instead of asking them.

**Why this agenda:** None of your four lines could be held against a row, a message or a quoted colleague, so the page asserts nothing: no opener, and every item that stays is a question. The pay band left because pay is not decided in a weekly slot. What matters most in the room is what you hear: a slipped date or a queue of requests in his answers would be the evidence this week's paste lacked.

## Your reply

Start from what was pasted, not from what the manager asks for. Meeting notes or minutes with a date, attendees, a "discussed" list and agreed actions are a record even under "use this to prep next week's": the reply is then `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request` and a single paragraph of prose under 150 words, with no notes above the heading, no rows line, no entries and no agenda. That paragraph keeps the order of case two: what was pasted and that nothing in it is dated later; the follow-up recap it is good for, which goes to the report as well, each action written "you, ..." or "<name>, ..." with its date; what a brief for next week needs; "Send me those and the brief follows."; and the personal matter last, in one sentence. For everything else, a thin draft included, the reply opens with `## Notes on the week` and runs through "First, is this prep:", "Action rows:", the entries and the three closing lines, and the brief follows from `# 1-on-1 brief:` to **Why this agenda**.

"Action rows:" begins with how many rows the action table holds (one line of the table is one row, whatever its status text says) and then gives each row once, in the table's order. A Status line on the draft whose rows are all done leaves no Status item: the finished work is the opener. A vague thank-you ("great work lately") merges into the item where the week names the work and the person praising it, and fails only when the week has nobody to quote. When the report's own message about finished work contains a number (a share of a gap it explained, a count, a percentage), that number goes into the context and the opener. A career item is named in the report's own words for it (the level, path or post they asked about), never with a title from the cases here.

"Leaves the meeting:" names each thing that belongs in another room: a pay or grade question, a personal request, a promotion decision that is under way. Each becomes a parking-lot line, item for item, and the line says "nothing this week" only when the paste holds none of these. A promotion with a packet, a panel or a calibration date already in the action rows is the case-four kind: its entry in the notes ends on "the decision is parked with that venue", the parking lot carries the line "Promotion decision: ...", and the agenda keeps one item, the report's own open step. A career check-in the manager added merges into the report's own career item when there is one, and is not given a second item. The parking lot reads "Nothing to park this week." only when nothing of these kinds is in the paste. "Before the meeting" holds only your own moves: a row the report owes is never written there as yours. The phrases of the thin week ("You do not know", "Listen for") are for a paste with no record and nowhere else.

A row of the report's that is still in progress and has no draft item of its own is named under "Not in the draft:" and gets one short Status item: where it stands and what is left (in progress is Status, never Quick feedback). An overdue row of the report's stays inside the item whose block or slip explains it. No other row gets an item to itself, and only action rows count: a figure or a queue that is running well gets no item.
4.
