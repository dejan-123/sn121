# Redwater Parcel Depot: four invented cases (form only)

Redwater, its staff, customers and figures are made up. The cases are here for the shape of a reply and nothing else: the reading of the draft in plain prose, the subjects outside the draft, the worked figures and the action-log line, then the brief with a title taken from the week, a short running order, a parking lot holding only what the venue test sent there, and a last paragraph on what changed. Take no name, figure, phrase, fraction or event from here; in a real reply every one of those comes from the paste. A reply's first characters are `## Your draft, read against the week`, or `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request` when the paste records a 1-on-1 already held; it never announces itself.

The reply:

## Your draft, read against the week

You sent a draft, the action log, one pair of figures and the week's messages, for a 1-on-1 still ahead.

1. "Shout-out, strong season." "Strong season" points at no piece of work, but the record has a witness. Winston Aldred wrote on Tuesday that the trial week arrived a day early with the lowest mis-sort rate any depot has sent, so that message becomes the opener.
2. "Status: next-day sortation." This is not a status question; it is stuck. Sortation fell to 3,150 cages from a usual 4,200 while the returns team held conveyor 2 every night, and Nora has already found a way round it, conveyor time at the Ashby Cross depot on Thursdays, which waits only on your approval of the trunk run. It becomes an Unblock, and her plan to go to the site committee if no night is released by Wednesday close stays hers to carry out.
3. "Feedback: sharp with the returns team." Both messages, Monday's and Wednesday's, chase the same conveyor after earlier requests got nowhere. The edge comes from the stall, not from Nora, so there is no feedback item; the conveyor sits under line 2.
4. "Coaching: the mislabelled cages." One dated occasion is on record, Thursday's delivery to Castlemere. One occasion is Quick feedback, and Nora owned it the next morning and sent the right cages. The code sheet she should have had is your overdue row, so it is named first. Marianne Deak's message about the cages also asks for a process conversation on cage-code checks; that request goes to the parking lot under her name, while the slip stays here.
5. "Status: scanner checks." Still open at 61 of 90 lanes, so it stays as one Status question about the remaining 29.
6. "She asked about depot manager." Nora raised it, so it is Career with real minutes. The directors' panel is booked, which sends the decision to the parking lot; her own write-up of the load-ratio results, not started, stays on the agenda.

Beyond the draft: Nora joined the parcel carriers' association's mentoring programme, which belongs under Career. Three rows are yours: the panel booking is done, while the cage-code sheet is overdue and the conveyor belts are in progress. Marianne Deak asked for a process conversation about cage-code checks; that talk is parked, though the slip itself stays on the agenda. Nora's two open rows already sit inside an item (the scanner checks are line 5 of the draft, the load-ratio write-up sits under Career), so neither gets a second item.

Figures: The log as pasted: (1) Harlow Pharmacies trial week, Nora, DONE; (2) scanner checks, Nora, IN PROGRESS; (3) weigh-scale recalibration, Nora, DONE; (4) load-ratio write-up, Nora, NOT STARTED; (5) cage-code and route-tag sheet, you, OVERDUE; (6) directors' panel booking, you, DONE; (7) conveyor belts, you, IN PROGRESS. DONE rows: (1), (3) and (6). Action log: 3 of 7 rows done (43%). (3,150 - 4,200) / 4,200 is a fall of 25%. Nora ties about 40% of the shortfall to scanned cages waiting for a conveyor, which leaves 60% unexplained. The scanner checks stand at 61 of 90, with 29 lanes to go.

# Nora Pemberton, Sorting-Hall Lead: a pharmacy bid won on accuracy, a conveyor she can't get a night on

**State of play.** The trial week reached Harlow Pharmacies a day early and their logistics officer calls its mis-sort rate the lowest any depot has sent. Sortation is stuck behind the returns team's nights on conveyor 2, and two of the open log rows are yours. "Strong season" had nothing behind it until Winston's message. In figures: sortation down 25% (3,150 cages against 4,200), 60% of that drop unexplained; action log: 3 of 7 rows done (43%).

**Open rows in your name.** The cage-code and route-tag sheet, overdue: clear it before the meeting, or bring a date; Nora's Friday message says the Castlemere cages were tagged from the old sheet. The conveyor belts, in progress: clear them before the meeting, or bring a date.

## Running order: the trial week, conveyor 2, the cages, the scanner checks, her next step

**1. Recognition: the Harlow Pharmacies trial week**
Winston Aldred wrote that the trial week reached every branch a day early and its mis-sort rate is the lowest any depot has sent. Read Nora those words.

**2. Unblock: nights on conveyor 2**
Your move: approve the trunk run for the Ashby Cross depot's conveyor time today and agree a fair split of nights with the returns team lead. Nora said she would take it to the site committee if no night is released by Wednesday close; back her if she does. Then ask what she thinks lies behind the unexplained 60%.

**3. Quick feedback: the mislabelled Castlemere cages**
Start with your overdue code sheet, since the cages were tagged from the old one. Nora owned the slip and sent the right cages the next morning.
- "You said on Friday you tagged from the old sheet: with the new one in hand, what check before dispatch would catch a wrong code?"

**4. Status: scanner checks**
- "61 lanes are checked; what stands between you and the last 29?"

**5. Career: depot manager and the mentoring programme**
- "The load-ratio write-up is the next step toward the post: when could the directors see a draft?"

## Parking lot
- The depot-manager decision: the directors' panel is already booked; it is settled there, not in this meeting.
- How cage codes are checked before dispatch (asked for by Marianne Deak): a meeting with Marianne and whoever runs dispatch; offer her two dates this week.

**Why the brief departs from your draft.** The opener rests on Winston's message, because a named customer praised finished work. Sortation moved from status to an Unblock and swallowed the sharp-messages line, since those messages were chasing the conveyor. "Coaching" became Quick feedback on a count of one, with your code sheet named first, and Marianne's process request and the promotion decision went to the parking lot because each needs another setting.
