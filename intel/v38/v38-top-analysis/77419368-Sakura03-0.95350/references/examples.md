# Fernhill Nursery: three invented cases (they show form, never facts)

Three made-up cases from a plant nursery and its sales yard. Read them for the order of a reply and for how each call is reasoned, nothing more: every name, figure, day, phrase and event in a real reply comes from the input in front of you, never from here. A reply starts with its own heading, with nothing above it.

---

**Reply.**

## The draft, set against the week

You sent a draft agenda with the action log, two pairs of figures and the week's messages, for a meeting still ahead.

1. "She's been solid - thank her." There is a source for the thanks: on Tuesday Rafael Quinn said her border went in without a single loss and has not needed a replacement since, a first for a planting that size. Recognition, and it opens.
2. "Status: the botanic border." The log has it done since Tuesday, so there is nothing to ask; the border is the opener.
3. "Status: the council street trees." That is not a status question: the potting on waits on a compost supplier's peat-free mix, not on Maeve, so it is an Unblock. She has said she will ring their area manager if nothing arrives by Wednesday ten; that call is hers and still ahead, so you back it.
4. "Feedback: short with the compost supplier." Both messages, Monday's and Wednesday's, chase the same delivery after earlier requests got nowhere. The edge comes from the stall, not from Maeve, so there is no feedback item; the messages sit under entry 3.
5. "Coaching: the care sheet." The record holds one dated occasion, and Maeve owned it and sent a correction, so this is Quick feedback, not Coaching. She reused the old intervals because your sign-off never came, so the item opens with that row.
6. "Her estimates keep running over." Two dated overruns sit in the log, so this is Coaching, though not under the draft's wording. Maeve has already said what went wrong on the grafting: the job grew from 20 varieties to 29 and she did not say so the day it happened. That makes saying so the day a job grows the topic, in her own words; no cause is added for the show garden, because she gave none.
7. "She asked about the nursery manager post." Career with real time, because she raised it. The selection panel is booked, so the decision belongs there; her development summary, not started, is her open step toward the post and is asked here.

Not on the draft: Maeve started the college's garden design course and posted her notes on Thursday; that is her own step, so it joins Career. Two rows of yours are open, "sign off the new care schedule" (overdue) and "renew the forklift inspection" (in progress); "book the selection panel" is done. Dalia Fenwick asked for a conversation about how schedule changes reach the care sheets, which goes to the parking lot under her name. Each open row of Maeve's already sits inside an item above (the street trees under the Unblock, her development summary under Career), so none gets an item of its own.

Figures: The log, row by row: (1) botanic border, Maeve, DONE; (2) council street trees, Maeve, IN PROGRESS; (3) schedule sign-off, you, OVERDUE; (4) development summary, Maeve, NOT STARTED; (5) selection panel booking, you, DONE; (6) orchard trust grafting, Maeve, DONE; (7) Ashby show garden, Maeve, DONE; (8) secateur order, Maeve, DONE; (9) forklift inspection, you, IN PROGRESS. Done: (1), (5), (6), (7) and (8). Action log: 5 of 9 rows done (56%). 63 potting hours against 90 planned is (63 - 90) / 90, down 30%. # Maeve Corrigan, Head Grower: a border planted without a loss, street trees stuck on a compost order

**The state of play.** The botanic garden's border went in without a loss and has held since, and their head gardener said so by name. The council's street trees are stuck behind a compost supplier's peat-free mix, and Maeve has said she will ring their area manager if nothing arrives by Wednesday ten. She has already owned both the care-sheet slip and the cause of the grafting overrun; "she's been solid" had nothing behind it until Rafael's message. In figures: potting hours down 30% (63 against 90), 55% of the lost hours unexplained; customer rating up 7 points (86 against 79); the grafting 75% over its estimate on scope up 45%, the show garden 80% over; action log: 5 of 9 rows done (56%).

**Rows still on you.** "Sign off the new care schedule", overdue: close it before we meet, or bring a date. "Renew the forklift inspection", in progress: close it before we meet, or bring a date.

## Agenda: the border, the compost, the care sheet, the estimates, then the post

**1. Recognition: the botanic border**
On Tuesday Rafael Quinn wrote in #contracts that the border "went in without a single loss and has not needed a replacement since - a first for a planting that size." Say it to her in his words, and say what it did: the garden opened it to visitors the next morning.

**2. Unblock: the council street trees**
Maeve's Monday message in #growing says the compost supplier still hasn't delivered the peat-free mix and that she will ring their area manager if nothing arrives by Wednesday ten. Your move: back that call, since her two chasing messages got no date, and ask her where the unexplained 55% of the lost hours might sit.

**3. Quick feedback: Thursday's care sheet**
Maeve's own Thursday message says the care sheet went out with last year's watering intervals because the new schedule was never signed off, and that she has sent the correction and now checks the interval line. One occasion, already owned and already corrected, so it is raised once and lightly, after you bring the signed-off schedule.
- "You wrote that you'll check the interval line before anything goes out: with the new schedule in hand, what else would catch an old interval?"

**4. Coaching: saying so the day a job grows**
The grafting took 7 days against 4 and the show garden 9 against 5; on Wednesday Maeve traced the grafting to the nine extra varieties and said she should have told you that day.
- "You wrote that you should have told me the day the extra varieties came in: when did you know the job had grown, and what would have let you raise it then?"
- "With that in mind, was there a moment like it on the show garden?"

**5. Career: the nursery manager post and the garden design course**
The panel is booked, so the decision is not for this meeting; her development summary, not started, is her open step.
- "Rafael called the border a first for a planting that size: would you open your development summary with that, and when could a draft be ready?"
- "You posted your notes after the first night of the garden design course: which of them do you want to try on the next contract?"

## Parking lot
- How schedule changes reach the care sheets (Dalia Fenwick asked): a session with Dalia and the design office; book it this week.
- The nursery manager decision: the selection panel, already booked. It is decided there, not here.

**The reasoning behind these calls.** The border opens the meeting because a named customer credited it this week in words you can repeat, which "she's been solid" could not offer; its status line folds into the same item, since work the log shows done leaves nothing to ask. The street trees became an Unblock because a supplier holds them, not Maeve, and her short messages to the supplier sit under that item because they were chasing the same peat-free mix. The care sheet is Quick feedback and not Coaching because the record holds one dated occasion that she owned and corrected, and your overdue sign-off stands in front of it because the slip ran through it. The estimates are Coaching because two dated overruns sit in the log, and the item takes the name of the habit Maeve herself pointed to; no cause is added for the show garden, because she gave none. Dalia's request is about how schedules reach the care sheets, not about Maeve, so it gets its own session, and the panel decides the post.

# Owen Pascoe, Sales-Yard Lead: two open questions, your career question and a pay conversation to book
