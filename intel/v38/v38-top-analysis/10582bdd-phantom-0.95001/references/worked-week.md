## Before you write

- Base your judgment on what landed in the paste, not on the manager's framing. When something carries a date, a roster, a list of things "discussed", or actions people agreed to, that is a record—even if it arrives labelled "use this to prep next week's". A record gets a reply that opens with `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request`, with nothing stacked above it, carrying only the redirect, the way the second brief case does: a single plain paragraph naming those notes and what they covered, free of reading lines, entries, or an agenda.
- In every other case, lead with one sentence that wears no label, follow with the entries (a single sentence apiece during a quiet week), and close with "Outside the draft:" and "Figures:". The figures tally the log before anything else (the row count, one log line for each row no matter what its status reports, every item named once following the log's sequence), then handles each number pair in turn. The page spans from `# 1-on-1 brief:` down through **Why the agenda changed**, where you state in your own words what the record shifted and the reason.
- Each agenda item must tie back to a draft line or to "Outside the draft"; no single item should swallow the whole half hour. A completed row owes nothing. When a Status line's rows are all finished, it produces no Status item—the wrapped-up work becomes the opener instead. A row still underway whose date slipped earns one brief Status item even when the draft overlooked it; no other row qualifies.
- Quiet-week phrasing ("You do not know where it stands", "Listen for") applies only when the week arrived carrying no record. A fuzzy compliment ("great work lately") gets absorbed into whichever item the record ties to the work and the person who saw it, and only falls short when no such witness turns up. Any number from the report's own note about completed work belongs in the context and the opener.
- Whatever exits the room is always parked: compensation or level, a personal ask, a promotion already underway with a room on the calendar (the final brief case). A request to talk through process after a stumble is not that sort of thing: handle it within the Quick feedback item with one agreed step. "Nothing to park this week." shows up solely when nothing leaves the room.
- The manager's actions carry no timing or deadline you invent: "tell him you will take it to their manager", not "today", "this week" or "by Friday", unless the input supplies that date.
- None of the material from the cases above (people, kitchens, desks, numbers, sentences) shows up in your reply; every fact must trace to the manager's paste.

Write the reply now. Its first characters are `## What the record shows` (for a record, `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request`): place no sentence, note, or rule ahead of that heading (no "Now I'll prepare the brief", no "Now I have the procedure", no "Let me apply this to ...", no "Here is the brief", nothing that previews or narrates your next move), and no rule line (`---`) at any point. The `# 1-on-1 brief:` heading comes straight after the reading.
