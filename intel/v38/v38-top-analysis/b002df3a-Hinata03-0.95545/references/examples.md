# Fenwick Road Kitchens: four invented cases (form only)

Fenwick Road, its staff, customers and figures are made up. The cases are here for the shape of a reply and nothing else: the reading of the draft in plain prose, the subjects outside the draft, the worked sums and the action-items line, then the brief with a title taken from the week, a short agenda, a parking lot holding only what belongs in another room, and a last paragraph on what changed. Take no name, figure, phrase, fraction or event from here; in a real reply every one of those comes from the paste. A reply's first characters are `## Draft items checked against the record`, or `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request` when the paste records a 1-on-1 already held; it never announces itself.

## Draft items checked against the record

You sent a draft, the action log, one pair of figures and the week's messages, for a 1-on-1 still ahead.

1. "Shout-out, strong season." "Strong season" points at no piece of work, but the record has a witness. Leopold Hask wrote on Tuesday that the trial service arrived a day early with the lowest return rate any kitchen has sent, so that message becomes the opener.
2. "Status: meal dispatch." This is not a status question; it is stuck. Dispatch fell to 2,880 meals from a usual 3,600 while the banqueting team held blast chiller 2 every night, and Philippa has already found a way round it, chiller time at the Holloway kitchen on Thursdays, which waits only on your approval of the courier. It becomes an Unblock, and her plan to go to the site committee if no night is released by Wednesday close stays hers to carry out.
3. "Feedback: sharp with the banqueting team." Both messages, Monday's and Wednesday's, chase the same chiller after earlier requests got nowhere. The edge comes from the stall, not from Philippa, so there is no feedback item; the chiller sits under line 2.
4. "Coaching: the mislabelled trays." One dated occasion is on record, Thursday's delivery to Marchbank. One occasion is Quick feedback, and Philippa owned it the next morning and sent the right trays. The code sheet she should have had is your overdue row, so it is named first. Bernadette Coyle's message about the trays also asks for a process conversation on tray-code checks; that request goes to the parking lot under her name, while the slip stays here.
5. "Status: fridge probe checks." The log has all 90 fridges checked and the row done since Thursday, so there is nothing to ask and no item.
6. "She asked about kitchen manager." Philippa raised it, so it is Career with real minutes. The directors' panel is booked, which sends the decision to the parking lot; her own write-up of the portion-yield results, not started, stays on the agenda.

Left off the draft: Philippa joined the contract caterers' association's mentoring programme, which belongs under Career. Three rows are yours: the panel booking is done, while the tray-code sheet is overdue and the steamer seals are in progress. Bernadette Coyle asked for a process conversation about tray-code checks; that talk is parked, though the slip itself stays on the agenda. Philippa's one open row, the portion-yield write-up, already sits inside the Career item as her next step, so it gets no Status item of its own.

The sums: The log, row by row: (1) Severn Care Homes trial service, Philippa, DONE; (2) fridge probe checks, Philippa, DONE; (3) kettle thermostat recalibration, Philippa, DONE; (4) portion-yield write-up, Philippa, NOT STARTED; (5) tray-code and route-label sheet, you, OVERDUE; (6) directors' panel booking, you, DONE; (7) steamer seals, you, IN PROGRESS. Done: (1), (2), (3) and (6). Action items: 4 of 7 done (57%). (2,880 - 3,600) / 3,600 is a fall of 20%.
