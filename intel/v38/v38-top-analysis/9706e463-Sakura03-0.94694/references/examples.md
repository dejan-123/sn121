# Millbrook Bakery: three invented cases (they show form, never facts)

Three made-up cases from a wholesale bakery and its order desk. Read them for the order of a reply and for how each call is reasoned, nothing more: every name, figure, day, phrase and event in a real reply comes from the input in front of you, never from here. A reply starts with its own heading, with nothing above it.

---

## Case A: only the draft

**Input.** For Dermot Hale, Order-Desk Lead: "Haven't had time to pull anything. 1. How far along is the tin inventory? 2. Is he coping with the Easter rush? 3. Seems distracted. 4. Wants to talk about his pay. 5. Career check-in." Nothing else.

**Reply.**

## The draft, set against the week

Only the draft arrived, for a meeting still ahead, so this is prep, but thin: no action log, no figures and no messages came with it. No draft item can be tested against a record, so the brief asks rather than asserts.

1. "How far along is the tin inventory?" Nothing sent can answer that: no copy of the inventory, no message about it and no figure on how much is written came with the draft. It stays a Status question, and the inventory itself is the record that would settle it.
2. "Is he coping with the Easter rush?" Nothing sent can answer this either: no orders figure, no message from Dermot about his load, no sign of what slipped. It stays a Status question, and the week's order book would settle it.
3. "Seems distracted." Nothing dated sits behind "seems", so it gets no slot; a moment you actually saw, with its day, would bring it back.
4. "Wants to talk about his pay." Pay needs its own conversation, so it is parked with nothing drafted.
5. "Career check-in." Dermot has not asked about a path, joined a course or started toward a promotion, so this is your topic: one closing question framed by his work.

Not on the draft: nothing else arrived, so there are no rows of yours to report and no topic from Dermot.

Figures: none can be worked; no log and no numbers came with the draft.

# Dermot Hale, Order-Desk Lead: two open questions, your career question and a pay conversation to book

**The state of play.** No action log, no figures and no messages came with the draft, so nothing can be said yet about progress, load or trend; the tin inventory and the week's order book are the records to pull before you meet. There is no opener, because nothing sent credits a specific piece of his work, and no coaching, because nothing is on record twice. The inventory and the rush stay open questions, "seems distracted" has no dated moment behind it, and pay moves to a conversation of its own.

## Agenda: the inventory, his load, then your career question

**1. Status: the tin inventory**
You have nothing on the inventory this week, so ask him directly; the inventory itself would settle it. "I haven't seen the inventory this week: how far has it got, and is any part waiting on someone?"

**2. Status: the Easter rush**
You have no numbers on the rush either; the week's order book would show them. "Without the order book in front of me I can't tell how heavy the week was: what got pushed back?"

**3. Career (your topic): where he wants to grow**
Dermot did not ask for this, so keep it to one question, asked through the work the draft names. "Between the inventory and the Easter customers, which kind of work would you like more of next year?"

**Worth pulling first.** The tin inventory, for item 1 (Dermot has it); the week's order book, for item 2 (the order desk); and a line from Dermot on anything he wants covered, asked today.

## Parking lot
- Pay (Dermot raised it): the pay cycle, through HR; book a separate conversation this week and tell him the date. Nothing on pay is drafted here.

**The reasoning behind these calls.** With nothing on record, this brief asks more than it asserts. The two status lines stay open questions because nothing sent can answer them yet, and each asks for the one thing that would: where the work stands and what is in its way. "Seems distracted" is left out because no dated moment backs it, and the question about load reaches the same worry more fairly. Pay leaves this meeting for a conversation of its own, because Dermot raised it and it deserves real time with the right people. The career check-in stays, but as yours: one question asked through the work the draft names, since he sent no topic of his own.

## The draft, set against the week

You sent a draft agenda with the action log, two pairs of figures and the week's messages, for a meeting still ahead.

1. "She's been solid - thank her." There is a source for the thanks: on Tuesday Julian Sharpe said her order arrived with every loaf risen and not a tray sent back, a first for a bake that size. Recognition, and it opens.
2. "Status: the show order." The log has it done since Tuesday, so there is nothing to ask; the order is the opener.
3. "Status: the council school rolls." That is not a status question: the baking waits on a miller's wholemeal flour, not on Ottoline, so it is an Unblock. She has said she will ring their area manager if nothing arrives by Wednesday ten; that call is hers and still ahead, so you back it.
4. "Feedback: short with the miller." Both messages, Monday's and Wednesday's, chase the same delivery after earlier requests got nowhere. The edge comes from the stall, not from Ottoline, so there is no feedback item; the messages sit under entry 3.
5. "Coaching: the mixing sheet." The record holds one dated occasion, and Ottoline owned it and sent a correction, so this is Quick feedback, not Coaching. She reused the old weights because your sign-off never came, so the item opens with that row.

# Ottoline Reeve, Bake-House Lead: a show order baked without a return, school rolls stuck on a flour order

**The state of play.** The county show's order arrived with every loaf risen and nothing sent back, and their steward said so by name. ## Agenda: the order, the flour, the mixing sheet, the estimates, then the post

**1. Recognition: the show order**
On Tuesday Julian Sharpe wrote in #orders that the order "arrived with every loaf risen and not a tray sent back - a first for a bake that size." Say it to her in his words, and say what it did: the show sold out of it the next morning.

**2. Unblock: the council school rolls**
