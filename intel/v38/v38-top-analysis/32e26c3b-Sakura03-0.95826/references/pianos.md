# Calder Pianos: three invented cases (they show form, never facts)

Three made-up cases from a piano workshop and its showroom. Read them for the order of a reply and for how each call is reasoned, nothing more: every name, figure, day, phrase and event in a real reply comes from the input in front of you, never from here. A reply starts with its own heading, with nothing above it.

---

## Case A: a week with a full record

Messages: Monday, Odile in #workshop: "The timber merchant still hasn't delivered the spruce for the soundboards. About 45% of the lost hours are that delay; the rest I can't account for yet. If nothing arrives by Wednesday ten I'll ring their area manager." Monday and Wednesday, Odile to the timber merchant: "Third time of asking for a delivery date on the spruce. The uprights can't wait another week." Tuesday, Anselm Rhee, head of keyboard at the conservatoire, in #commissions: "Odile's grand arrived in tune and stayed in tune through the whole recital - a first for an instrument moved that far. Our exams went ahead on it the next morning." Wednesday, Odile to the manager: "The harpsichord went from 20 strings to 29 when the chapel asked for the bass choir as well, after the four-day estimate was set. I should have told you the day the extra strings came in." Thursday, Odile to the manager: "The tuning notice went to customers with last year's prices because the new list wasn't signed off and I reused the old one. That's on me; I've sent the correction and I'll check the price line before anything goes out." Thursday, Odile in #workshop: "Started the guild's evening course on voicing; my notes from the first night are in the channel." Friday, showroom lead Imre Solberg: "We need a process conversation about how price changes reach the notices."

**Reply.**

## The draft, set against the week

You sent a draft agenda with the action log, two pairs of figures and the week's messages, for a meeting still ahead.

1. "She's been solid - thank her." There is a source for the thanks: on Tuesday Anselm Rhee said her grand arrived in tune and stayed in tune through the whole recital, a first for an instrument moved that far. Recognition, and it opens.
2. "Status: the conservatoire grand." The log has it delivered since Tuesday, so there is nothing to ask; the grand is the opener.
3. "Status: the school uprights." That is not a status question: the repairs wait on a timber merchant's spruce, not on Odile, so it is an Unblock. She has said she will ring their area manager if nothing arrives by Wednesday ten; that call is hers and still ahead, so your part is to back it.
4. "Feedback: short with the timber merchant." Both messages, Monday's and Wednesday's, chase the same delivery after earlier requests got nowhere. The edge comes from the stall, not from Odile, so there is no feedback item; the messages sit under entry 3.
5. "Coaching: the tuning notice." The record holds one dated occasion, and Odile owned it and sent a correction, so this is Quick feedback, not Coaching. She reused the old prices because your sign-off never came, so the item opens with that row.
6. "Her estimates keep running over." Two dated overruns sit in the log, so this is Coaching, though not under the draft's wording. Odile has already said what went wrong on the harpsichord: the job grew from 20 strings to 29 and she did not say so the day it happened. That makes saying so the day a job grows the topic, in her own words; no cause is added for the pedal lyre, because she gave none.
7. "She asked about the workshop manager post." Career with real time, because she raised it. The selection panel is booked, so the decision belongs there; her development summary, not started, is her open step toward the post and is asked here.

Not on the draft: Odile started the guild's voicing course and posted her notes on Thursday; that is her own step, so it joins Career. Your price-list sign-off is overdue, your insurance survey is in progress and your panel booking is done. Imre Solberg asked for a conversation about how price changes reach the notices, which goes to the parking lot under his name. Each open row of Odile's already sits inside an item above (the uprights under the Unblock, her development summary under Career), so none gets an item of its own.

Figures: The log, row by row: (1) conservatoire grand, Odile, DONE; (2) school uprights, Odile, IN PROGRESS; (3) price-list sign-off, you, OVERDUE; (4) development summary, Odile, NOT STARTED; (5) selection panel booking, you, DONE; (6) chapel harpsichord, Odile, DONE; (7) pedal lyre on the Marlowe grand, Odile, DONE; (8) hammer felt order, Odile, DONE; (9) insurance survey, you, IN PROGRESS. Done: (1), (5), (6), (7) and (8). Action log: 5 of 9 rows done (56%). 63 bench hours against 90 planned is (63 - 90) / 90, down 30%. Odile puts about 45% of the lost hours on the spruce, so 55% is still unexplained. The customer rating is 86 - 79 = 7 points up. The harpsichord took 7 days against 4, (7 - 4) / 4 = 75% over, on scope that went from 20 strings to 29, up 45%; the pedal lyre took 9 days against 5, (9 - 5) / 5 = 80% over. The uprights stand at 5 of 12 repaired, 7 to go.

# Odile Ferreira, Workshop Supervisor: a recital grand that held its tuning, uprights stuck on a timber order

**The state of play.** The conservatoire's grand arrived in tune and held through the recital, and their head of keyboard said so by name. The school uprights are stuck behind a timber merchant's spruce, and Odile has said she will ring their area manager if nothing arrives by Wednesday ten. She has already owned both the notice slip and the cause of the harpsichord overrun; "she's been solid" had nothing behind it until Anselm's message. In figures: bench hours down 30% (63 against 90), 55% of the lost hours unexplained; customer rating up 7 points (86 against 79); the harpsichord 75% over its estimate on scope up 45%, the pedal lyre 80% over; action log: 5 of 9 rows done (56%).

**Rows still on you.** The price-list sign-off, overdue: close it before we meet, or bring a date. The insurance survey, in progress: close it before we meet, or bring a date.

## Agenda: the grand, the spruce, the notice, the estimates, then the post

**1. Recognition: the conservatoire grand**
On Tuesday Anselm Rhee wrote in #commissions that the grand "arrived in tune and stayed in tune through the whole recital - a first for an instrument moved that far." Say it to her in his words, and say what it did: the conservatoire's exams went ahead on it the next morning.

**2. Unblock: the school uprights**
Odile's Monday message in #workshop says the timber merchant still hasn't delivered the spruce and that she will ring their area manager if nothing arrives by Wednesday ten. Your move: back that call, since her two chasing messages got no date, and ask her where the unexplained 55% of the lost hours might sit.

**3. Quick feedback: Thursday's tuning notice**
Odile's own Thursday message says the notice went out with last year's prices because the new list was never signed off, and that she has sent the correction and now checks the price line. One occasion, already owned and already corrected, so it is raised once and lightly, after you bring the signed-off list.
- "You wrote that you'll check the price line before anything goes out: with the new list in hand, what else would catch an old price?"

**4. Coaching: saying so the day a job grows**
The harpsichord took 7 days against 4 and the pedal lyre 9 against 5; on Wednesday Odile traced the harpsichord to the nine extra strings and said she should have told you that day.
- "You wrote that you should have told me the day the extra strings came in: when did you know the job had grown, and what would have let you raise it then?"
- "With that in mind, was there a moment like it on the pedal lyre?"

**5. Career: the workshop manager post and the voicing course**
The panel is booked, so the decision is not for this meeting; her development summary, not started, is her open step.
- "Anselm called the grand a first for an instrument moved that far: would you open your development summary with that, and when could a draft be ready?"
- "You posted your notes after the first night of the voicing course: which of them do you want to try on the next instrument?"

## Parking lot
- How price changes reach the notices (Imre Solberg asked): a session with Imre and the showroom desk; book it this week.
- The workshop manager decision: the selection panel, already booked. It is decided there, not here.

**The reasoning behind these calls.** The grand opens the meeting because a named customer credited it this week in words you can repeat, which "she's been solid" could not offer; its status line folds into the same item, since work the log shows delivered leaves nothing to ask. The uprights became an Unblock because a supplier holds them, not Odile, and her short messages to the merchant sit under that item because they were chasing the same spruce. The notice is Quick feedback and not Coaching because the record holds one dated occasion that she owned and corrected, and your overdue sign-off stands in front of it because the slip ran through it. The estimates are Coaching because two dated overruns sit in the log, and the item takes the name of the habit Odile herself pointed to; no cause is added for the pedal lyre, because she gave none. Imre's request is about how prices reach the notices, not about Odile, so it gets its own session, and the panel decides the post.

## Case B: only the draft

**Input.** For Bram Thackeray, Showroom Lead: "Haven't had time to pull anything. 1. How far along is the hire-piano rota? 2. Is he coping with the exam-season rush? 3. Seems distracted. 4. Wants to talk about his pay. 5. Career check-in." Nothing else.

**Reply.**

## The draft, set against the week

Only the draft arrived, for a meeting still ahead, so this is prep, but thin: no action log, no figures and no messages came with it. No draft item can be tested against a record, so the brief asks rather than asserts.

1. "How far along is the hire-piano rota?" Nothing sent can answer that: no copy of the rota, no message about it and no figure on how much is built came with the draft. It stays a Status question, and the rota itself is the record that would settle it.
2. "Is he coping with the exam-season rush?" Nothing sent can answer this either: no bookings figure, no message from Bram about his load, no sign of what slipped. It stays a Status question, and the week's bookings would settle it.
3. "Seems distracted." Nothing dated sits behind "seems", so it gets no slot; a moment you actually saw, with its day, would bring it back.
4. "Wants to talk about his pay." Pay needs its own conversation, so it is parked with nothing drafted.
5. "Career check-in." Bram has not asked about a path, joined a course or started toward a promotion, so this is your topic: one closing question framed by his work.

Not on the draft: nothing else arrived, so there are no rows of yours to report and no topic from Bram.

Figures: none can be worked; no log and no numbers came with the draft.

# Bram Thackeray, Showroom Lead: two open questions, your career question and a pay conversation to book

**The state of play.** No action log, no figures and no messages came with the draft, so nothing can be said yet about progress, load or trend; the hire-piano rota and the week's bookings are the records to pull before you meet. There is no opener, because nothing sent credits a specific piece of his work, and no coaching, because nothing is on record twice. The rota and the rush stay open questions, "seems distracted" has no dated moment behind it, and pay moves to a conversation of its own.

## Agenda: the rota, his load, then your career question

**1. Status: the hire-piano rota**
You have nothing on the rota this week, so ask him directly; the rota itself would settle it. "I haven't seen the rota this week: how far has it got, and is any part waiting on someone?"

**2. Status: the exam-season rush**
You have no numbers on the rush either; the week's bookings would show them. "Without the bookings in front of me I can't tell how heavy the week was: what got pushed back?"

**3. Career (your topic): where he wants to grow**
Bram did not ask for this, so keep it to one question, asked through the work the draft names. "Between the hire rota and the exam-season customers, which kind of work would you like more of next year?"

**Worth pulling first.** The hire-piano rota, for item 1 (Bram has it); the week's bookings, for item 2 (the front desk); and a line from Bram on anything he wants covered, asked today.

## Parking lot
- Pay (Bram raised it): the pay cycle, through HR; book a separate conversation this week and tell him the date. Nothing on pay is drafted here.

**The reasoning behind these calls.** With nothing on record, this brief asks more than it asserts. The two status lines stay open questions because nothing sent can answer them yet, and each asks for the one thing that would: where the work stands and what is in its way. "Seems distracted" is left out because no dated moment backs it, and the question about load reaches the same worry more fairly. Pay leaves this meeting for a conversation of its own, because Bram raised it and it deserves real time with the right people. The career check-in stays, but as yours: one question asked through the work the draft names, since he sent no topic of his own.

## Case C: notes from a meeting already held

**Input.** "Turn these into next week's prep", then notes headed "1-on-1 - Greta Morrow - 9 January", with those present, a "Discussed" list, and actions: manager to sign the overtime sheet by 12 January; manager to ask the supplier about felt deliveries by 11 January; Greta to finish the humidity log by 16 January; and a line about Greta's hospital appointment.

**The whole reply.**

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

**Why no brief.** This reply redirects the request: these are the notes of the 1-on-1 you and Greta already held on 9 January, with those present and the actions handed out in the room, not prep for the next one. A brief built from them would replay last week as news, so none is drafted.

**What the notes do support.** A follow-up on their actions, yours first:
- You: sign the overtime sheet, by 12 January.
- You: ask the supplier about felt deliveries, by 11 January.
- Greta: finish the humidity log, by 16 January.
The notes do not say where any of them stands, so check each one.

**What next week's brief needs.** The current state of those three actions, this week's messages and figures, and your draft agenda.

**The personal matter.** The notes mention Greta's hospital appointment. That is for a private conversation between you and her; nothing about it is drafted here.
