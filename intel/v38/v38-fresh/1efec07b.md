# The weekly 1-on-1 brief: the week's evidence sets the agenda

A draft agenda is written from memory, which keeps topics and drops their proof. Set every topic against what the week put on record and show which record changed it; a manager acts on a reason they can see and slides back from a bare new label.

## Is there a meeting to prepare?

A brief needs current material about one person before a meeting still ahead; a bare draft agenda still gets one, made of honest questions. Notes or minutes of a conversation already held (dated, with attendees and agreed actions) do not count when nothing says what has moved since, even if the manager wants them turned into next week's prep: every status in them is from that meeting, so an agenda built on them would pass old positions off as current or guess. That record needs a follow-up on what was agreed, so it gets a redirect; so do skip-level prep and review drafts. When the input is notes of a held meeting, or has nothing behind the draft agenda, first read `references/edge-weeks.md`, which holds a finished redirect and a finished thin-week brief.

## What each topic really is

Read every source once: each action line with owner and state (the manager's too), each figure beside its comparison, each message with its sender. Labels and priorities in the draft are a first impression, not a finding.

- **Recognition** needs a concrete result from this period with a name on it: a named person's thanks, a shipped piece, a measure that moved. If the manager recalls being impressed but not by what, find the event in the input and open with the speaker's own words; if it is absent, the brief has no opener and says so. Finished work that drew praise counts as recognition whatever the draft said, and gives up its status slot.
- **Unblock** is status where the work waits on something outside the report's control: an approval never granted, a team gone quiet, more reviews than one person can clear. The manager leaves committed to a move. Curt messages after ignored requests come from the block, so a draft item about that tone folds into the unblock. When one reviewer holds up several teams, spreading the load is the manager's decision, starting from the fix the report proposed.
- **Quick feedback** is one observed slip and one adjustment. A first slip the report already owned stays this size: agree the check that prevents a repeat and ask one question about the circumstances.
- **Coaching** needs the same behaviour on two separate occasions, or a goal the report chose, and runs on questions that start from their own explanation; a slip repeated in a second week or sprint is coaching even when the report owns it. An impression like "seems disengaged" that the record contradicts (early warnings, follow-ups, other work picked up while waiting) is dropped, with those signals named, and the real cause takes the slot.
- **Career** is direction: a level, a path, a bigger scope, a programme joined. A draft line saying the report asked about or wants it counts as their topic and gets real minutes linked to the week's strongest evidence. A check-in that came only from the manager becomes one open question at the end.
- **Status** is live work whose position must be found or decided; done or approved work never holds a slot.

## What leaves the weekly slot

Pay, health, family and other personal matters, HR procedures, complaints about a colleague, someone else's request for a process conversation, and promotion decisions owned by calibration or a skip-level go to the parking lot with venue and who books it, no talking points. Only the report's own next step stays: packet work stays, the promotion decision is parked. The page is printed, so personal topics appear only as "a personal matter".

## Numbers and gaps

Do the sums first and print each as its working. Change against a baseline is (new - old) / old as a whole percent: (4 - 10) / 10 = -60%; 3 days planned and 6 used is (6 - 3) / 3 = +100%. Scores, rates and percentages move in points: subtract, never divide, so 57 - 48 = +9 points. For completion, give each row its status word and count only "done"; in progress, not started and overdue are open, the manager's rows included. When a finding explains part of a gap, state the share left (100% - 18% = 82% unexplained) and ask about it on the agenda. Counts, dates and causes come from the record only. A missing input type is named once in the context and joins the prep list.

## Order and minutes

Earned recognition; then unblocks and decisions only the manager can make, the one stalling most people first; then the report's own topic with real minutes; then feedback or coaching; last, quick status and any manager-only career question. Minutes add up to exactly 30 unless the input sets another length. Manager-owned rows that are open or late head the prep list, and the agenda item on the same work names them.

## The reply

It begins with `## Evidence check`, nothing before it: a line on whether this is prep; per draft topic in order, the draft's words in quotes, **Record:** author, channel and day, **Kind:** what it earns and why (or "as drafted"), or parked, dropped, merged; **Not in the draft:** owed manager rows, blocks, routings; **Sums:** each row with its status word, the count, each pair worked; **Missing:** only if an input type never arrived.

Then `# 1-on-1 brief - <name>, <role>`: two or three context sentences (problem, cause, whose move, key figures); **Agenda (30 min)** with items `**n. <topic> - <Kind>, <m> min**`, each at most three sentences, questions included: the signal, what the manager does, plus, in coaching or career items, a question pointing at a thread, ticket, date or the report's words. Speak to the manager as "you", quote lines to say aloud, write "name the day you will", never a placeholder. Merged, parked and dropped topics get no item of their own. Then **Parking lot** (each topic with where it goes and who books it, or a line saying nothing needs parking), **Prep list** (owed rows, inputs to fetch), and **Calls explained**: three to five short, plain sentences on each change of kind and its signal, labels kept, the opener, each parked topic. The brief stays within 350-700 words. When inputs are missing, every item is a plain question, with no coaching and no recognition item of any kind.

**Redirect** for notes of a held meeting: one note under 180 words, titled `# Not a prep brief: these notes cover a meeting that has taken place`, no evidence check: what was pasted and the giveaway; why a brief would mislead; each open action as written with owner and date, the manager's first; what to send for a real brief. A personal or health matter in the notes is never named, described or asked about; the last line is "Anything personal from that meeting is for a private conversation, not this page."

## A worked week (invented: borrow the reasoning, not the facts)

## Evidence check
Prep: current rows and chat, meeting still ahead.
1. "Status: seed-bank cataloguing". **Record:** 310 of 500 accessions in; the herbarium has not released the sheets Odile has chased since the 3rd, which hold 120 of the 190 missing. **Kind:** Unblock, out of her hands.
2. "Shoutout for the plant sale?". **Record:** the director's Monday note: "Odile's schedule gave us our best sale yet." **Kind:** Recognition.
3. "Coaching: careless with labels". **Record:** one tray mislabelled Thursday, fixed by Odile that afternoon; nothing earlier. The volunteer lead wants "a proper talk about the process". **Kind:** Quick feedback; the process talk is parked.
4. "Her question about the supervisor post". **Record:** her Thursday note. **Kind:** Career, as drafted.
5. "A word about her blunt email to the herbarium". **Record:** sent Friday, after the chasing went unanswered. **Kind:** merged into 1; the edge came from the wait.
**Not in the draft:** the nozzle order, yours and overdue.
**Sums:** cataloguing in progress, sale schedule done, nozzle order (yours) overdue, so 1 of 3 are done (33%); (1,240 - 900) / 900 = +38% plants sold; 190 - 120 = 70 missing accessions the sheets do not explain; germination 64% to 71% is 71 - 64 = +7 points.

# 1-on-1 brief - Odile Ferrant, propagation technician
Cataloguing sits at 310 of 500 because the herbarium is holding its sheets, and the next push is yours. The sale moved 1,240 plants, up 38%, and the nozzle order you owe is late.

**Agenda (30 min)**
**1. The plant sale - Recognition, 3 min** Read her the director's line and the 1,240 figure.
**2. The herbarium sheets - Unblock, 9 min** Her chasing has not moved them, so the next request is yours: name the day you will see the curator. Her blunt email came from the wait, so it needs no slot. Ask what holds the other 70 accessions.
**3. The supervisor post - Career, 12 min** She raised it, and the sale schedule is her proof she can run a glasshouse. "Which part of planning the sale would you want every week?"
**4. Thursday's tray - Quick feedback, 6 min** She caught it within hours. Agree a label check before trays leave the bench, and ask what was different that day.

**Parking lot** Labelling process talk: its own meeting with the volunteer lead, booked by you.
**Prep list** Place the overdue nozzle order.
**Calls explained** The herbarium's silence made cataloguing an unblock, with the blunt email folded in. The director's note made the shoutout recognition. A single tray, put right within hours, is quick feedback, not coaching; the process talk needs its own meeting. Career stood because Odile asked.
