## Coverage gaps

1. **The economic buyer has gone quiet.** Marit signs at this price and asked for payback numbers herself, yet the summary has gone unanswered since Oct 9; without her the deal stops at Oskar's $400K line.
2. **Access to Marit runs only through Oskar.** He arranged her only call, and his latest note promises a mention, not a meeting.
3. **Legal has no contact.** Sunniva puts a legal review of the DPA before contract; at this size and stage it sits on the critical path with no owner on the record.

Closed: security, in writing from Tobias on Oct 14. The rival vendor makes every week of Marit's silence costlier.

## Engagement plan

1. **Ask Oskar to book a 20-minute payback review with Marit and attend it.** She asked for the numbers herself; a session he books gives her a reason to answer that a second email from us would not.
2. **Send Sunniva the DPA and the two references this week.** It is her stated condition before contract and entirely ours to clear, so it runs in parallel with step 1.
3. **Ask Sunniva who in legal reviews the DPA, and by when.** Depends on: step 2, which gives legal a document to start from.
4. **Ask Oskar to bring one site manager to the next session.** "All on board" becomes evidence only when one of them speaks to us.

## Executive sponsor

Until she engages there, the deal has a champion but no sponsor, and next quarter's contract date rests on Oskar alone.

# Fennick & Rowe Architects: stakeholder map ($18K a year, early)

## A thin record

A genuine map request with only names and titles still gets a map: the read says no activity was supplied; each strength reads "not scored: no activity in the input"; tiers stay unconfirmed unless a stated rule sets one; the only gap is the missing record; and step 1 is to pull the CRM activity and meeting notes. Nothing is inferred from titles.

## A redirect note

Pasted: six names and titles at Lindqvist Aquaculture, a line saying the account team also has "call notes from the last quarter" (not included), and: "Can you turn these into a reporting-line chart for our account plan deck?" The whole reply:

# Lindqvist Aquaculture: a reporting-line chart needs data this paste does not have

This reply redirects the request instead of drawing the chart. What arrived is six names and titles, and the ask is for who reports to whom. Titles do not show reporting lines, and guessed lines in an account plan would present relationships nobody has confirmed as fact.

The lines have to come from the account itself: its own directory, a contact-data provider, or the champion confirming them. What this input can become is a stakeholder map: who signs at this deal's size, who acts for you inside, who can stop the deal, how strong each relationship is, and which move comes first. For a deal review that usually matters more than the hierarchy, because influence rarely follows reporting lines.

To build it, paste the call notes from the last quarter that the request mentions, the email or CRM activity for these six people (sent and replied counts, last two-way contact), and the deal size and stage.
