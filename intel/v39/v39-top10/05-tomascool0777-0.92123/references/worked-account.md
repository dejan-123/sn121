# Worked account and three short cases

Everything below is invented. Copy the way of reasoning and the shape of the map, never the names, numbers or wording.

## The worked account

What the seller pasted, in brief:

- Account: Calloway Mills, a textile manufacturer with about 1,400 staff. Deal: $640K ARR for a fabric-defect inspection platform across four plants. Stage: late evaluation, security review "open" in the deal record.
- Ines Marlow, Director of Quality: asked for the first call through the website, sent a year of defect logs before we asked, forwarded the pilot plan to the plant leads the same day, introduced the IT security lead. Said she can sign anything up to $250K; above that it goes to the COO. 14 emails from us, 13 replies, last on Tuesday. On Monday she asked for the pilot results summary we promised; we have not sent it.
- Royce Adebayo, COO: at the kickoff in July for ten minutes ("worth a look"). Five emails from us since, no replies. Last contact from him: 74 days ago.
- Tamsin Kroll, IT Security Lead: reviewed the data-flow pack and wrote on the 3rd: "Our requirements are met, nothing further from security."
- Okello Brandt, Procurement Manager: sent the standard supplier onboarding form; we returned it.
- Ines also said: "the plant managers will want a say before Royce signs". No plant manager is named anywhere.

The map, as it should be written (a memo to the seller, no section labels after the title):

**Stakeholder Map — Calloway Mills | $640K ARR, late evaluation**

Ines Marlow is driving this deal for you and has done everything a champion does, but it is above her $250K limit, so it is Royce Adebayo's to sign, and Royce has not answered you in 74 days. Security is finished: Tamsin confirmed in writing that the requirements are met, whatever the deal record still says. Right now the next move is yours, because Ines asked on Monday for the pilot results summary and is still waiting. This is where each person stands.

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Strength (0–5) | Trajectory | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ines Marlow | Director of Quality | Champion (not the economic buyer) | 5 | Rising | Inbound request; sent a year of defect logs unasked; forwarded the pilot plan the same day; introduced security; 14 sent / 13 replies; limit $250K |
| Royce Adebayo | COO | Economic buyer | 1 | Dormant | Ten minutes at the July kickoff ("worth a look"); 5 sent / 0 replies; 74 days silent |
| Tamsin Kroll | IT Security Lead | Security gate (closed) | 3 | Complete | Data-flow pack reviewed; written "requirements are met" on the 3rd |
| Okello Brandt | Procurement Manager | Gatekeeper (process) | 2 | Transactional | Sent the standard onboarding form; we returned it |

The plant managers who "will want a say" have no names in your records, so they have no rows.

For a deal of this size, three things are missing. The person who signs has no relationship with you: $640K is above Ines's limit and Royce has been silent for 74 days. The plant managers will weigh in before he signs, and you cannot reach any of them. And you do not know what procurement needs beyond the onboarding form.

I would take these in order, since each one sets up the next:

1. Send Ines the pilot results summary today. She asked for it on Monday, it is the one thing you owe, and it is what she will take to Royce.
2. Once she has it, ask Ines to bring Royce into a 30-minute results review that she leads. Do not email Royce yourself: five messages have gone unanswered, and going around Ines would cost you the champion.
3. In the same conversation, ask Ines to name the plant managers who will want a say, and offer them a walk-through of the pilot results at their own plant.
4. Separately, ask Okello what procurement needs after the onboarding form, and by when, so nothing surprises you at signature.

As for an executive sponsor, you do not have an active one. It has to be Royce Adebayo, because only he can sign at $640K, but all he has given you is ten minutes in July. Ines is the only route to him. Make the results review in step 2 the test of her reach, and treat the deal as at risk until Royce has said something for it himself.

## Short case 1: a small deal

A $24K yearly licence for one training team of nine. The team lead, who says she approves software under $40K, came in through a webinar, booked the demo herself and has sent four use-case questions. An HR analyst joined one call to check data retention and said it "looks fine". No procurement or legal step has come up.

How the map handles it: the team lead is champion and decision-maker (strength 5, rising); the analyst is a narrow check already satisfied (tier "Compliance check (met)", strength 2, trajectory "Complete": her check is done, not fading and not unconfirmed). The paragraph on what is missing, as written: "Nothing that matters is missing at this size. The deal rests on one relationship, which is normal for a purchase inside her own limit. The analyst has already called the retention check fine, so it is done." The plan has three steps: the demo on her own questions, a one-line courtesy note to the analyst with the retention summary, the order form. It does not make the analyst's written sign-off a condition of anything, and it does not ask for introductions to the rest of the team: one buyer is enough here. It does not ask the team lead to name finance, IT or legal people. Sponsor: the team lead herself; no executive above her is needed for a purchase inside her own limit, and adding one would slow a deal that is moving.

## Short case 2: one person holds all access

A $290K deal where the only contact is a head of maintenance. He started the evaluation, sent his asset list within the hour, and writes that "our CEO wants a cost comparison"; the CEO has no name in the notes and no record. Three people at the demo were never introduced. His last email, eight days ago, is still unanswered by us.

How the map handles it: the opening paragraph says the deal is single-threaded, in those words. One table row, his; the CEO decides but has no name, so she is not a row. Under the table: "The CEO and the three people at the demo have no names in your records, so they have no rows." The first thing missing is a second thread. The plan opens with replying to his email with the cost comparison the CEO asked for and an offer of a short session he hosts for her; then asking him for the attendees' names and roles. No cold message to the CEO: we have no introduction and no contact details. Sponsor: the CEO in name, nobody in practice; say so.

## Short case 3: a request this skill does not answer

"Make me a slide with the account's reporting hierarchy, who sits under whom." Five names and titles follow; no notes, no activity.

The reply is a redirect, not a map:

**Redirect — reporting-hierarchy slide, not a stakeholder map**

You are asking for a reporting-line chart. This skill maps influence and relationship strength from what people have done; it does not draw hierarchies, and the five names and titles you sent contain no reporting lines, so any chart drawn from them would be invented. Take the reporting lines from the customer's own org chart or ask your contact at the account to confirm them, then build the slide in an org-charting tool. If what leadership needs is who decides this deal and how strong each relationship is, paste the meeting notes and CRM activity for the five contacts and you will get the map: who influences the decision, how strong each relationship is, where coverage is missing and what to do first.

## Checks before you write

1. Every tier and strength cites a count, a date or the person's own words from the input, and only figures the input gives (no invented email counts, no day counts worked out from dates).
2. A stated spending limit is compared with the deal value before anyone is called the buyer.
3. A gate confirmed in writing is marked closed, whatever an older label says.
4. Nobody mentioned without a name gets a name or a table row, even the person who signs.
5. Gaps are only those this deal, at this size and stage, actually has.
6. Anything we owe comes first in the plan; dormant executives are reached through the champion.
7. Someone silent for weeks scores 2 at most, whatever their title; a supporter whose promise led to nothing is a 4 and stalling, not a 5 and rising.
8. The sponsor paragraph says plainly when there is no active sponsor, and still names the candidate and the route.

Your reply begins with the bold title line of the map, or with the bold redirect line. After a map's title there are no bold section labels: it reads as a memo to the seller. Do not open with "Now I understand the skill", "Now I will", "Let me", "This is not a stakeholder map request", a "---" line, or any note about the request or what you are about to do.
