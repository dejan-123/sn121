# Counsel cases — four invented replies (form only)

All four accounts are made up. Every reply is the document itself —
the first characters are `# ` and the account name; nothing goes above
the title. Copy the form: the read in plain sentences, calls resting on
the record, gaps shaped by the deal, moves ordered by what unblocks.
Copy no name, figure, or fact — a real input supplies its own.

---

## Case one: a full account

Supplied for Merrivale Furnishings (contract-furniture manufacturer,
1,400 employees), a $620K order-management platform deal in late
evaluation:

- Contacts: Petra Lindh, Operations Systems Manager — brought us in,
  runs the evaluation, shared their order-flow spreadsheets unprompted,
  introduced us to the IT lead; 9 of 10 emails answered, usually same
  day; last touch 3 days ago. Gerald Voss, VP Operations — attended the
  September kickoff, said "Petra runs this for me — bring me the
  decision pack when it's ready"; 1 reply to 7 emails since; last touch
  58 days ago. Tomás Ibru, CFO — in CRM; 0 emails answered, 0 sent, no
  meetings. Kira Antol, Information Security Lead — completed the
  security review, confirmed in writing "requirements met, nothing
  further from my side"; 4 of 5 emails answered; last touch 16 days
  ago. Renata Solis, Procurement Manager — assigned in October; sent
  the vendor onboarding packet; we returned it 9 days ago; her review
  is open.
- Deal context: Petra's note — "Gerald wants this, but anything over
  $400K needs the CFO's signature and Gerald's ceiling is $400K."
  Competing vendor TradeLane still in evaluation. The ops director
  Petra reports to (unnamed) has been mentioned twice as "will want to
  see the rollout plan" — no contact, no record.

The reply:

## The read

the delegation is written — the backing is real but second-hand, and
his strength with us stays low until he answers directly. Petra's
decision pack is the route back to his attention; Tomás is the sponsor the
deal will eventually need, and he is currently nobody to us.

## Why it reads this way

The roster puts the VP on top; the counts put Petra there. Gerald is
named sponsor but dormant — September's endorsement holds, today's
relationship is Petra's. Kira's row is closed because her own writing
closed it. The ops director carries no invented name — the role is in
the input, so the row cites the role and nothing more.

## Case two: a thin record

Supplied for Calloway Signs (custom-signage shop, 60 staff), a $22K
order-tracking subscription:

- Contacts: Mara Calloway, owner-operator — replied to our inbound,
  described losing two orders to miscommunication, asked detailed
  integration questions; 4 of 5 emails answered; last touch 8 days
  ago. "It's my call — anything under thirty a year I decide myself."
  Den Hale, shop-floor lead — joined the first demo, asked one
  question, nothing since; 0 replies to 2 follow-ups.

The reply:

# Calloway Signs: signer in hand, thin around her

## The read

A $22K tool for a 60-person shop — inside the buyer's stated authority,
no process raised, early stage.

Mara Calloway, owner-operator: initiated, described real pain, asked
specific questions — driver and signer in one person, 4/5 holding.
Eight days quiet is a small-deal pause, not a pattern. Den Hale,
shop-floor lead: one demo, one question, then silence — gone quiet,
0 of 2 replies. One appearance is a touch, not a thread.

Named, never met: none. On our desk: nothing owed.

## The people

| Person | Role | Warmth | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mara Calloway | Driver + Signer | 4 — holding | Self-initiated; specific questions; "my call" under $30K; 4/5 replies |
| Den Hale | Gone quiet | 1 — dead | One demo, one question; 0/2 follow-ups |

## What's missing

Coverage fits the deal — no procurement, legal, finance, or executive
layer applies at this size and none is invented. No single-thread flag
on the decision either: the engaged signer decides herself, and a warm
signer is a deal that can close, not a thread that can snap. The
honest line to carry is thinner: the whole relationship still sits
with one person, because Den's one appearance widens nothing yet. If
Mara goes quiet for a quarter there is no second door — one question
in the plan works on that, and it is a coverage note, not a blocker.

## Next moves

1. Answer her integration questions completely — they were specific;
   a complete answer is the cheapest trust we can buy.
2. Offer the pilot framed to her own words — "stop losing orders to
   miscommunication" — around the demo she already suggested.
3. Ask who touches orders day to day — one question in her next reply;
   if Den or anyone else runs the board daily, that is the thread to
   warm.

## The sponsor

No separate sponsor is needed. Mara is driver and signer inside her
own stated authority — say so plainly and move on.

## Why it reads this way

The signer-in-hand ruling keeps the deal clean — no invented process,
no thread flag on a decision she holds herself. Den's row stays
honest: one appearance is not a relationship, so he lands "gone quiet"
rather than a second thread, and the coverage note owns what the
record actually shows — thinness, not risk.

## Between cases: when signals pull apart

Records are rarely uniform. When two signals point different ways on
one contact, the read settles them in a fixed order:

- **Recency outweighs volume.** Twenty warm replies last quarter lose
  to six quiet weeks now — post the silence, and let the older warmth
  be the question the next touch settles.
- **Initiation outweighs reply-count.** A contact who answers
  everything but starts nothing is a friend, not a driver — drivers
  spend first.
- **An owned act outweighs reported warmth.** "He told the deans he
  wants it" is a claim until the record shows the act — the person who
  reported it holds the settling question.
- **A written close outweighs seniority.** A review closed in the
  reviewer's own words is closed at any title — reopened by new
  writing, never by worry.
- **Authority outweighs the organogram.** A $400K ceiling on a $620K
  deal is the gap wherever the chart puts her.

And when two calls still both fit, say which the record cannot settle
and the one question that would — an owned ambiguity outranks a
confident wrong call.

## Case three: waking the dormant signer

Supplied for Vostra Packaging (mid-market food packager, 900 staff),
an $880K warehouse-automation deal at proposal stage:

- Contacts: Ilsa Renner, Head of Operations — brought us in two
  quarters back, runs the pilot, forwards every internal update; 8 of
  10 emails answered; last touch 4 days ago. Marcus Fane, CFO —
  signed the pilot budget last year; nothing since — 0 replies to 6
  emails in 110 days; no meetings on record. Nina Okoye, IT Security —
  review open; asked for our architecture documentation 12 days ago;
  unanswered.
- Deal context: the amount needs CFO sign-off; Ilsa's authority stops
  well short. The capex board (unnamed) approves anything above the
  CFO's own line.

The reply:

# Vostra Packaging: champion warm, signer dormant 110 days

## The read

An $880K proposal-stage deal — CFO signature required, capex board
above that, one review open and owed.

Ilsa Renner, Head of Operations: brought us in, runs the pilot,
forwards internals — driver, 4/5 heating. Marcus Fane, CFO: signed
the pilot a year ago, then 110 days of silence — gone quiet, 1/5
dead; the signature this deal needs sits with him regardless. Nina
Okoye, IT Security: checker, review open — and her request sits
unanswered on our desk for twelve days.

Named, never met: the capex board above the CFO's line — no names, no
record. On our desk: Nina's architecture documentation, owed.

## The people

| Person | Role | Warmth | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ilsa Renner | Driver | 4 — heating | Runs pilot; forwards internals; 8/10 replies |
| Marcus Fane | Signer | 1 — dead | Pilot signatory; silent 110 days; 0/6 |
| Nina Okoye | Checker | 3 — holding | Review open; docs requested 12 days ago, unanswered |
| Capex board | no record | unscored | Named as approval layer; no person, no record |

## What's missing

- **The signer is dormant.** The amount is beyond Ilsa's authority and
  Marcus — the only person who ever signed for us — has gone quiet for
  nearly four months. Route: never cold. Ilsa briefed him originally;
  the way back runs through her calendar.
- **A review we owe.** Nina asked for architecture documentation and
  it has sat twelve days — the debt on our desk gates her sign-off.
- **Single thread.** The only living line runs through Ilsa — every
  path to Marcus and to the capex board goes through her. If she
  stalls, the deal stalls.

## Next moves

1. Send Nina the architecture documentation — the debt is ours and it
   gates her review.
2. Ask Ilsa for the re-warm path to Marcus — a short brief in his
   language (payback period, downtime risk) that she can carry into
   his next staff meeting. No cold email: a note he never expected
   asks a stranger for the deal.
3. Then the introduction itself — Ilsa in the room, the brief ahead
   of it.

## The sponsor

None yet. Marcus is who the seat needs — a signature from last year
is history, not backing. The brief carried by Ilsa is the step that
would earn it; until he engages there is no sponsor to name.

## Why it reads this way

A year-old signature reads as dormancy, not sponsorship — the seat
stays honestly empty. The route respects the only living thread, the
debts settle before anything is asked of theirs, and the capex board
is a gap of one line rather than an invented name.

## Case four: a request the record cannot support

Supplied for Merrivale, mid-cycle:

- A request: "Sketch me Merrivale's internal buying structure — who
  reports into whom — so I can show leadership the org picture."

The reply:

# Merrivale Furnishings: org chart — redirect

The request asks for a reporting structure; what was supplied is a
contact list, activity counts, and notes. Titles show who people are;
nothing here shows who sits above whom — an org chart built from this
record would be invented lines on a leadership slide.

What this record does support is the map: Petra driving, Gerald's
endorsed-but-dormant backing, the CFO the deal needs and has never
met, the ops director named by role. For a real org chart, ask
Merrivale for their structure — or ask Petra directly; she maps it
better than we ever could.
