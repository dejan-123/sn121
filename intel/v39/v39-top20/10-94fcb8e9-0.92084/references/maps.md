# Maps for invented accounts (form only)

Every account, person, date and figure below is fiction, and each map is written for its own account. A real reply draws every name, number and sentence from its own record; anything lifted from this page would be invented. What transfers is the order of the page, the habit of describing what a person did before saying what they are, and the plain way doubt is shown when the record is thin or contradicts itself.

## Map A: a large deal run through a delegate

The record, in brief. Ridgeway Regional Airports (three regional airports, 2,100 staff). Deal: $860K ARR, baggage-flow analytics. Stage: commercial review; the CRM stage still says "safety review pending".
- Ilse Varga, Head of Baggage Operations: contacted us after a conference talk; sent six months of mishandled-bag data unasked; wrote the internal options note; arranged an August session with the COO. Says she can approve up to $250K herself and "anything above goes to the investment board". On 2 October: "I'll get it onto the board's November agenda"; nothing has come of it yet. Her email of last Tuesday, asking what format the board paper should take, is still unanswered on our side. CRM: 16 sent, 15 replies, last touch 3 days ago.
- Teodor Brandt, Chief Operating Officer: the August session, which our VP of Operations attended; said "this is the direction we need" and told Ilse to "keep it moving with the board". CRM: 4 sent, 1 reply, last touch 41 days ago.
- Mareike Holt, Head of Airside Safety: asked for our operating procedures; wrote on 30 October, "safety case accepted, nothing further needed from my team".
- Callum Pryor, Head of IT Infrastructure: joined one technical session without a question; three emails unanswered since.
- Anouk Lemaire, Procurement Lead: will raise no order until our supplier risk questionnaire has passed her review; we returned it on 4 November and she confirmed receipt.
- Ilse, in passing: "our Finance Director's team will need to be comfortable with how this lands in capex" and "the terminal duty managers will want to see it before rollout". No CRM record exists for either.

The reply:

# Ridgeway Regional Airports: stakeholder map

## The record, person by person

At $860K this deal is far above Ilse Varga's $250K limit, and by her account the investment board takes anything larger, so the yes has to come from a board nobody on our side has met. On the way it must also satisfy the Finance Director's team on capex treatment, IT infrastructure and procurement's supplier review.

Ilse Varga, Head of Baggage Operations, has done everything a champion does. She approached us after the conference talk, sent six months of mishandled-bag data unprompted, wrote the internal options note and brought the COO into an August session; she has answered 15 of our 16 emails, most recently 3 days ago. One thing is unproven: her 2 October offer to put the platform on the board's November agenda has produced no date and no name. Champion, reach unproven; Strong, stalled on that offer. Her question about the board paper is sitting unanswered with us.

Teodor Brandt, Chief Operating Officer, endorsed the direction at the August session ("this is the direction we need") and told Ilse to "keep it moving with the board". He has delegated, so we reach him through Ilse, and his near-silence since (1 of 4 emails answered, last touch 41 days ago) is what delegation looks like rather than a sign of cooling. Executive sponsor working through Ilse; Thin. By his own instruction the decision sits with the board.

Mareike Holt, Head of Airside Safety, asked for our operating procedures and wrote on 30 October that the safety case is accepted and her team needs nothing more. Our CRM still shows "safety review pending"; her dated, first-hand message is later, so the CRM stage is out of date and her test is passed. Evaluator, passed; Working.

Callum Pryor, Head of IT Infrastructure, sat through one technical session without asking anything and has answered none of the three emails since. That tells us nothing about his view either way: quiet, position unknown; Cold.

Anouk Lemaire, Procurement Lead, will not raise an order until our supplier risk questionnaire clears her review; we returned it on 4 November and she confirmed she has it. Gatekeeper, review open; Working on process.

Named, never met: the Finance Director's team ("will need to be comfortable with how this lands in capex") and the terminal duty managers ("will want to see it before rollout"). The board's members are not named anywhere.

We owe Ilse an answer on the board-paper format.

Everything above Ilse reaches us through her: the COO session, the board route and what little we know about finance. The account is **single-threaded** on her.

## Coverage gaps

To close, this deal needs the investment board's approval above $250K, the finance team's acceptance of the capex treatment, a view from IT infrastructure and procurement's sign-off on the supplier review.

**Structural risk: the account is single-threaded on Ilse Varga.** If she is off sick, moves role or loses the internal argument, we lose our only way to the board.

- **Investment board**: decides above $250K; no member is known to us.
- **Finance Director's team**: named, never met; its comfort with the capex treatment comes before the board and is reached through Ilse.
- **IT infrastructure**: Callum Pryor's view is unknown.
- **Terminal duty managers**: named, never met; Ilse can say who speaks for rollout.

Safety is settled and procurement's review is in progress. Nobody has said whether a contract this size goes to legal; that is a question for Ilse rather than a contact to hunt for.

## Engagement plan

1. **Ilse: reply today on the board-paper format, and in the same note ask which board meeting will take the paper, who writes it, and whether she will introduce us to that person.** This settles what we owe, widens the single thread and puts her offer to the test. It has worked when we have a meeting date and one new name; steps 3 to 5 depend on it.
2. **Anouk Lemaire: ask when her supplier review will be done and what she needs after it.** No order can be raised before then, and her timing shapes the board paper.
3. **Teodor Brandt: once Ilse has the board date, have our VP of Operations, who sat in the August session, write to him with what the board will want to see.** One reply to four emails from the account team says another email from us would not land. It has worked when he confirms he will speak for it at the board.
4. **Ilse: ask what Callum Pryor would need to see, and whether she or a colleague of his can introduce the question**, rather than a fourth email. It has worked when we know whether infrastructure is waiting on something or has not started.
5. **Ilse: an introduction to the Finance Director's team with the capex treatment prepared**, after step 1, so finance has seen the case before the board meets.

Mareike Holt's test is passed; nothing more is asked of her.

## Executive sponsor

On their side, Teodor Brandt backs the project from above and has handed it to Ilse; he does not sign, the board does. On ours, the VP of Operations who met him in August is the natural counterpart, and his note should follow Ilse's board date, not precede it.

The Head of Baggage Operations ranks above the COO here since, on the record, she does the work and he delegates it; the safety review counts as passed despite our CRM stage because its owner closed it in writing.

## Map B: a small purchase

The record, in brief. Ashcombe Museum Trust (three museums, 140 staff). Deal: $14K a year, visitor-survey analytics for an audience research team of five. Stage: early evaluation; a scoping call is booked for 12 December.
- Petra Lindholm, Head of Audience Research: asked for a demo after a sector webinar; built three dashboards in the sandbox from her own survey export; asked twice how charts export into their board report template; told us "anything under $15K comes out of my research budget". Her second export question, sent on Monday, is still waiting for our answer.
- Rafael Aguiar, Digital Systems Officer: joined the second call for the login question; confirmed by email that staff accounts work with it and it "should be fine"; nothing since.
Nobody has mentioned procurement, legal or the trustees.

The reply:

# Ashcombe Museum Trust: stakeholder map

## The record, person by person

A $14K subscription for a research team of five, early in evaluation, with a scoping call on 12 December. A purchase this size needs someone who can pay for it and confidence that it works with the trust's systems, and both are already present.

Petra Lindholm, Head of Audience Research, came to us after a webinar, built three dashboards from her own survey data and has twice asked how charts move into the board report template. She pays for tools under $15K from her own budget, and this one is $14K. Economic buyer and champion; Strong, rising. Her second export question has been waiting on us since Monday.

Rafael Aguiar, Digital Systems Officer, joined one call for the login question and confirmed in writing that staff accounts work and it "should be fine". His test is passed; "should be fine" is a cautious phrase, not a reservation to pursue. Evaluator, passed; Thin, simply because there has been little reason to talk.

Petra is our only active contact and also the person who pays, so a single relationship carries no risk here.

## Stakeholder table

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Relationship strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Petra Lindholm | Head of Audience Research | Economic buyer and champion | Strong; rising | Built three sandbox dashboards, two export questions, buys under $15K herself. |

## Coverage gaps

No gaps: the person who pays and the person who checks the systems are both engaged, nobody has raised procurement, legal or the trustees, and at $14K inside Petra's own budget there is no one else to find.

## Engagement plan

1. **Petra: answer her export question before the 12 December call**, with a sample chart in the board template. It has been waiting since Monday and it is the thing she keeps asking about.
2. **12 December scoping call: agree the start date and how the subscription is invoiced.** It has worked when she names a start date.

Rafael has confirmed what he needed to; nothing more is asked of him.

## Executive sponsor

On their side none is needed, since Petra pays from her own budget. On ours, a purchase this size does not call for an executive.

The map stays short because the record gives Petra the authority to buy and nothing in it points to anyone else.

## One contact, one route (excerpt)

When one contact is our only route to the person who decides, the opening says so by name: "Every route to the managing director runs through Felix Ambrose, who submitted the business case and is waiting on her; she has never been introduced to us, and Felix's latest email, asking whether we can support her review, is still waiting for our answer." The first step pays that debt and widens the route at once: "**Felix: reply today, offer to answer the managing director's questions on the business case, and ask whether he would bring her into a short review with him there.** It has worked when a date is set." The second asks him to name the colleagues who sat in on the demo without being introduced. Writing to the managing director behind his back would spend the only relationship we have.

## The note (names and titles, nothing else)

The record, in brief. Brookvale City Council (local government, 3,400 staff). Deal: $720K, case-management system. Stage: tender evaluation. The account executive writes: "Can you put together an org chart of the council's decision makers, with the reporting lines drawn in, for my QBR deck?" Supplied: four names with titles (Imogen Hale, Director of Housing; Desmond Achebe-Ward, Head of Digital; Lucia Ferro, Category Manager; Tomas Wren, Information Governance Lead) and the remark that "the notes are all in Salesforce".

This note is the entire reply:

# Brookvale City Council: org chart not drawn

The request is for an org chart of the council's decision makers. The paste holds four names and titles; the notes themselves are in Salesforce and did not come through.

Titles are no guide to reporting lines, and still less to who will decide a $720K purchase, so drawing lines between these four would put invented structure on a QBR slide. The council can confirm its own reporting lines if you ask.

With the notes pasted, this becomes a stakeholder map: who can approve $720K, who is pushing for us, who could hold up the order, and the next move. Please paste, for each person, the meeting notes, our emails and their replies with dates, and every remark about who approves or which budget pays.

So far the record names Imogen Hale, Desmond Achebe-Ward, Lucia Ferro and Tomas Wren, none yet with enough behind them for a role or a strength.
