# Three worked cases (all invented, for the form of a reply)

None of this is real. There is a small order, then a large deal, then a request that leaves nothing to map. Borrow the form and the order of the thinking. When you write for real, every name, number, date and sentence is taken from the record in hand, never from here.

## A small order

Record: Garrow Joinery (20 staff, one workshop). Deal: 14k a year, quoting software. Stage: trial arranged.
- Idris Farrow, Owner. Rang us after another joinery recommended the product; has sent three emails since, each with questions of his own (importing his price list, quotes on a phone on site). Told us: "anything below 20k is my call." A trial on three live quotes is arranged for the 12th.
- Minna Aldous, Bookkeeper. Sat in on the second call for one question, whether quotes export to the accounts package, got the answer and said "that will do me for the present". Not heard from since.

# Garrow Joinery: stakeholder map

14k a year, quoting software; trial arranged.

Idris Farrow is buying this himself and is the one pushing it. He rang us on another joinery's recommendation, has written three times since with questions of his own, and told us "anything below 20k is my call", which covers a 14k order. Minna Aldous had a single question, whether quotes export to the accounts package, got her answer and said "that will do me for the present", so that requirement is closed. Nothing stands in the way of this deal. What nobody has tested yet is the decision itself: Idris has asked a great deal and has not said what would make him sign.

For a 14k order from a 20-person firm, coverage is complete as it stands. We are talking to the person who signs, the only requirement anyone raised has been answered, and since the record mentions no procurement, legal or IT approval, I add none. A single contact is ordinary for an order this small and is not a gap.

Three moves, all built on the trial already arranged:

1. Set up the trial on the 12th around Idris's own questions, the price-list import and quotes on a phone on site.
2. While the trial runs, show him a quote arriving in the accounts package, so that Minna's answered question stays answered.
3. At the end of the trial, ask Idris what he still wants to see before signing and how an order under his limit is actually placed. His reply is the closing plan, and it checks the authority he described.

On an executive sponsor: it is Idris. The amount is inside his own limit and he is already pushing the purchase, so no one above him is needed.

## A large deal with gaps

Record: Morven Cold Stores (refrigerated warehousing, nine sites). Deal: 520k a year, energy management for the cold rooms. Stage: "contract review, pilot sign-off pending".
- Dilys Crewe, Head of Facilities. CRM: 19 emails sent, 17 replies. Wrote the internal paper for her leadership herself and sent it to us to check the figures; walked our engineer round two sites. 6 May: "My sign-off stops at 150k. Anything bigger is signed by the commercial director, and he will ask what it does to the maintenance budget."
- Rufus Tindall, Chief Operating Officer. Came on one site visit our account director arranged on 14 May: "this is where we need to go, Dilys has the detail." CRM: 7 emails sent, 1 reply, the last 9 weeks ago.
- Ottilie Hexham, Site Engineer. Ran the pilot; wrote on 2 June: "pilot readings accepted, nothing outstanding on my side."
- 9 June, email from Dilys asking how our cost sits against the maintenance budget. No reply from us on record.

# Morven Cold Stores: stakeholder map

520k a year, energy management for the cold rooms; stage "contract review, pilot sign-off pending".

Dilys Crewe is the reason this deal is alive: she wrote the internal paper for her leadership herself, sent it to us to check the figures and walked our engineer round two sites. She cannot approve it. In her own words her sign-off stops at 150k and anything bigger is signed by the commercial director, whom we have not met, and whose one known question, the effect on the maintenance budget, reaches us only through Dilys. The stage field still shows the pilot sign-off as pending, yet the site engineer accepted the readings in writing on 2 June, so the open matter is finance. We are also late ourselves: Dilys's 9 June email about the maintenance budget has had no reply.

Dilys refers to the commercial director (6 May) without naming him, so the commercial director has no row.

For 520k at contract review, the map has three holes. The biggest is any line to the commercial director, who signs above 150k. Next is an executive who is still engaged: Rufus spoke up once, on 14 May, and has replied to 1 of 7 emails. Last is a second way in: the commercial director and Rufus are both reachable only through Dilys, which makes the deal single-threaded. The pilot is not among the gaps, because the site engineer closed it in writing.

The moves, each one opening the next:

1. Reply to Dilys's 9 June email with our cost set against the maintenance budget. It answers the commercial director's question, and it is what we owe.
2. Ask Dilys for a meeting with the commercial director that she runs, with that comparison on the table. We send the commercial director nothing before then.
3. Ask our account director, who arranged Rufus's one site visit, to bring him to the same meeting. One more email from us would only be one more he leaves unanswered.

On an executive sponsor: the deal has none so far. The authority sits with the commercial director, who does not know us yet; Rufus backed the idea once and then went silent. Dilys is the way to both of them, not the sponsor. The meeting in step 2 is where a sponsor could appear.

## Nothing to map

Record: "Need the reporting lines at Hollin Dock Services on one slide for the pipeline meeting. Who sits under whom?" Sent with it: Emrys Thwaite, Terminal Operations Director; Yusra Kemble, Head of Procurement; Lowri Fenwick, Safety and Compliance Manager; plus the remark that visit notes and the email trail are filed in the CRM.

# Hollin Dock Services: the paste does not support a reporting chart

You asked for the reporting lines at Hollin Dock Services on one slide for the pipeline meeting. What arrived with the request is three names with their titles, Emrys Thwaite (Terminal Operations Director), Yusra Kemble (Head of Procurement) and Lowri Fenwick (Safety and Compliance Manager), and a remark that visit notes and the email trail are filed in the CRM. Neither the notes nor the trail were in the paste.

I have drawn nothing from the titles. A job title does not tell you who someone answers to, or how much say they have in a purchase: a director can sit two levels from the decision or right beside it, and the weightiest title in the room is often not the person who moves the order. Once a guessed line is on a slide, the meeting will treat it as fact.

The reporting lines themselves are best had from the people: ask any of the three who they report to, or ask Hollin Dock for its own chart. What the pipeline meeting needs is something else: who approves this deal, who has done something for us, and which conditions are still open. Paste the visit notes and the email trail for these three, with anything said about who signs, and the map will answer exactly those questions.

## Writing the reply

That was the last case. What you write next is the reply, complete, and its very first characters are `# ` followed by the account named in your record: no sentence of introduction, no line of dashes. Nothing stands above that title, and the reply never mentions these cases or a skill.
