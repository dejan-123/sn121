# Terms of the map

Before replying, read `references/water-utility-maps.md`: its maps are invented and fix only layout and proportions; all facts come from the record.

The reply starts with exactly `## Evidence on file: <account name>` (a redirect: `## Request on file: <account name>`). Nothing precedes it: no announcing sentence, rule line or word on method, files or skills. Every call rests on a fact in the paste. Quote counts only where the record has them; never rebuild sent/replied totals from meetings, and say when a grade rests on notes alone.

## Grades when the record sets no scale

Ratio = replies / messages we sent. Date from the buyer's last reply, attendance or action; a "last touch" field may log our own send.

| Grade | Ratio | Last exchange | Initiative |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.75+ | 14 days or less | starts threads, shares internal data, introduces |
| B | 0.5+ | 30 days or less | prompt answers, follows through |
| C | two-way, narrow | 31-60 days, or cooling | one topic, transactional |
| D | under 0.5, or one acknowledgment | over 60 days | we chase, they seldom answer |
| 0 | none | never | cc only, never met |

Down one step on a falling trajectory; seniority never lifts a grade; a meeting the buyer asked for beats one we set up. Never "rising" while the decision it feeds is stalled: the stall is the risk.

Tiers: **Authority** (the record gives them approval of this amount), **Champion** (acts for the deal repeatedly: initiates, shares, forwards, introduces), **Gate owner** (holds a requirement that can stop the purchase), **Influencer** (substantive input, no advocacy or authority), **Observer** (on record, nothing consequential).

## Where conduct outranks the title

**Work done beats rank held.** Whoever sets agendas, sends internal data unasked and makes introductions is the Champion. An executive who arrives late, says little or leaves follow-ups unanswered is low influence (Observer or Influencer, D or 0), however senior.

**Authority follows the amount.** A stated limit below the deal puts approval above it; whoever the record ties to that seat is Authority ("likely" if inferred), graded honestly. Reach that signer through the champion: a specific ask, material to carry (a one-page case for the level above), a brokered meeting; never cold email a dormant executive.

**A handed-down deal sets the route.** An executive who endorsed it, passed it to their own person and answers one message in several is reached through that delegate, never around them.

**Gates close in writing.** A security, procurement, legal or finance condition is a gate whatever its owner's rank. Only a written, unqualified confirmation on record clears it; quote it. A spoken or hedged OK stays open: request a written copy at the next contact already on the calendar. A form the buyer wants from us is our debt.

**No contact record, no row.** A role or name with no contact record is a gap; invent no name, title, count or date.

## Order of play

1. Debts: an unanswered buyer email, promised material unsent, a delivery nobody confirmed, a form to complete.
2. The move that most frees the stalled approval.
3. Open gates with their owners, then breadth.

Each action names a contact or gap, its signal and its dependency; plan only steps the record names (an unraised review is a question for the champion). If the approver is unreached and one person is our only way in, flag the deal single-threaded, naming them; first answer what they await from us, offering concrete help with the approver's review, then ask for the approver's name and a brokered meeting. A small deal its one contact signs alone needs no flag.

**Sponsor test.** Authority over this amount, engagement on record, a reachable path. Never a champion whose limit ends below the amount, nor one who only briefs upward. Short of all three: "Nobody qualifies yet", plus the likeliest seat, what is missing, the route. A buyer whose stated limit covers the deal needs none.

## Asks the record cannot carry

If the ask depends on facts missing from the paste (who reports to whom, a hierarchy picture, influence percentages, budgets nobody stated), or the evidence is described but not pasted, the whole reply is this note:

```
## Request on file: <account name>
### What was asked, and why it cannot be drawn
  Two sentences: the request, the fact it depends on, and that titles never show reporting lines.
### Named contacts
  | Contact | Title (as given) | Tier |
  Each named person with the title verbatim; tier "not assessable on this record".
### What a stakeholder map gives instead
  Seats from conduct, grades from counts, the approval path, gaps and an ordered plan.
### What to send
  Meeting notes, sent/replied counts per contact, confirmed reporting lines, stated limits.
```

No grades, arrows or boxes. Where the map is supportable and only an extra is not, write the map and spend one sentence on the extra.

## Situations the patterns leave open

**Pledges without movement.** A champion who promises to push and then produces nothing keeps the seat; the trajectory turns down and the grade follows.

**One bright meeting, then silence.** A senior who engaged well once and has been quiet for weeks holds a fading grade; re-entry runs through the introducer or the colleague on our side who was in the room.

**Expansion of an existing account.** Renewal history proves use, not approval of the bigger amount; enthusiastic users may hold no budget for it. Test the new amount against the stated limits from scratch.

**Several sites at once.** Every site lead is a thread with its own grade; a group known only as "the other sites" is a gap; central approval is mapped once, above them.

**A contact about to move.** The seat holds until the move; ask now for a hand-over introduction to the successor.

**Leaning toward a rival.** Questions only about a competitor's terms reveal a preference; record it and answer it through the champion.

**Possibly one person, named twice.** Keep both references apart unless the record joins them, and ask.

**Weight of a signal.** Free-text notes outrank the activity columns. A same-day answer carrying internal figures beats several one-line acknowledgments, and forwarding, internal briefings and introductions weigh most, because the buyer spends personal credibility on them.

## The page

450-750 words, sections in this order:

```
## Evidence on file: <account name>
  4-6 bullets: amount and stage, stated limits, each gate and its state, people referenced
  but never introduced, anything we owe, how thick the evidence runs.
## Stakeholder table
  Contact | Title (as given) | Tier and the conduct behind it | Grade and the counts behind it | Part in this deal
## Coverage gaps against the approval path
  Who approves above the stated limit, which named gates stay open and who holds them, who was
  referenced but never introduced, each with its fact; inside the buyer's own authority,
  "coverage fits" and the trigger that would change it.
## Engagement sequence
  Numbered: debts, then the most deal-unblocking move, then gates and breadth; the
  single-thread flag where it applies.
## Executive sponsor call
  The sponsor test applied by name, or "Nobody qualifies yet" with seat, missing evidence, route.
## Why some calls overrule the title
  One or two sentences per person whose tier or grade departs from a title or touch field,
  told through what that person did.
```

## Release audit

Strike anything that fails a line here before sending:
- each row is a named person who has a contact record;
- each count comes from the paste, none rebuilt from meetings;
- each cleared gate quotes the written confirmation;
- action one pays whatever we owe;
- dormant, unintroduced and delegating seniors are reached only via the person holding access;
- one access holder with approval beyond them means the single-thread flag;
- the reply opens on its heading, and nothing in it is absent from the input.
