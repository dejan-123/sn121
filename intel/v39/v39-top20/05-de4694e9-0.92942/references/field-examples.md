# Field examples: two invented accounts

Nothing here is real. The hotels, the packaging firm, the people and every number were made up to show how a reading turns into a map. Carry over the method and the shape of the page; a name, figure, quote or event from these pages has no place in a real reply, where every fact comes from the record the seller supplied.

### The reply

# Calloway Coast Hotels - stakeholder map for the $860K revenue-management deal

### How I read the account

An $860K deal in commercial review, with RateHarbor on the shortlist. Ines has told us how it is approved: anything over $400K goes to the investment committee, which the CFO chairs. Her paper is already with that committee for December, so the decision sits with people we have never met.

Ines Valcourt is the champion. She set up discovery, ran the three-hotel pilot, sent booking-pace data unasked and brought the Chief Commercial Officer into the pilot review: fourteen replies to sixteen emails, and one door upward already opened. Her latest note says the paper is with the committee and she will pass on any questions, so her pattern has not changed; what we are waiting on now is the committee, not her. That is 5, steady. Not rising: nothing she has done lately is bigger than what she did in August. She is not the buyer, because $860K is over her committee line.

Rafael Odum has a mixed record. In August he shared complaint data unasked and forwarded our case study to three general managers the same afternoon, which is champion behaviour. Since then: a summary asked for "for the exec team" with no word of where it went, and "I'll push where I can" on 24 October with nothing after it. Six replies to nine emails. The August acts keep him a champion; the promises with nothing behind them make him 4, cooling, untested on access. One ask settles it: who received the summary, and will he bring us into that conversation? A name and a date mean he is still working for us; a vague answer means his push stopped short of the exec team.

Leonie Marchetti is where title and record part. "This is the direction for next year" sounds like a sponsor, but one reply to five emails, and that reply an acknowledgment, is us talking: 2, dormant. The remark keeps her on the map as the executive worth testing, through Ines, who brought her in once.

Agnieszka Holm finished her review on 21 October: "Nothing outstanding from security." The record gives no counts for her, so conduct decides: she asked for one document, read it and signed off. An evaluator whose requirement is met, 3, steady, and she needs nothing more from us. The stage field still calls the assessment open; her note is newer and first-hand, so I go with it.

Desmond Achebe sent the onboarding form and the data-processing agreement on 3 November, and both are still with us. His gate is open and the next move is ours. A gatekeeper who answers within a day and has taken no side: 3, steady.

Nobody has met the CFO, who chairs the committee (Ines, 8 October), or the three general managers who received Rafael's case study. RateHarbor on the shortlist means the committee will compare us line by line, so the paper needs the pilot results in the committee's own terms.

### Coverage gaps

- **Economic buyer (the CFO, name not given).** $860K is over Ines's $400K line, so the investment committee decides and the CFO chairs it. No contact and no read on what the committee weighs.
- **The general managers.** The hotels would run the forecasts every day; three GMs saw the case study and none is named.

Legal has not come up. Whether the committee wants a contract review before December is a question for Ines, not a gap.

### Engagement plan

**Single-thread risk:** every route to the committee runs through Ines. If she is pulled away before December, nobody else speaks for us in that room.

1. **Ask Ines for a route to the CFO before the December meeting:** a 20-minute pre-read of her paper with her in the room, plus a one-page answer to any question the committee raises. Done when we hold a date. No direct note to the CFO: the paper is hers, and a message around her lands back on her desk.
2. **Return Desmond's onboarding form and data-processing agreement this week** (in parallel). They are the one item fully in our hands.
3. **Test Rafael** (in parallel): ask who received the summary and whether he will bring us into that conversation. A name and a date put him back to steady; silence tells us his push has stopped.
4. **Ask Ines whether Leonie would add a line of support to the committee paper** (after 1). A yes makes her a sponsor in the making; a hesitation keeps her an observer.
5. **Ask Rafael for the three general managers' names** (after 3) and offer them a short walkthrough of the pilot forecasts.

Agnieszka has closed her review and is asked for nothing more.

### Executive sponsor

There is no executive sponsor yet. The committee and its chair hold the decision and have never met us. Leonie has the title and one warm remark but not the record. Ines has the commitment and has opened one door upward, but sits below the committee line. The sponsor to earn is the CFO, reached through Ines's paper rather than around it, with Leonie worth testing as a second voice.

### Why the plan starts where it does

The paper is already with the committee, so the deal waits on a CFO who has never met us, and Ines is the one person who can put us in that room. The forms we owe Desmond move alongside because nothing else depends on them; Rafael's test tells us whether we have one champion or two.

## Account two: a request the record cannot carry

### What the seller pasted

"Marrowby Packaging, 2,700 staff, $1.6M warehouse-robotics service contract, negotiation stage. Rank their leadership by real power, top to bottom, for my QBR slide on Friday. Contacts: Halvard Jensen (VP Operations), Mei-Lin Okafor (Director of Engineering), Bastien Roux (Category Manager, Indirect Procurement). The call recordings are in Gong."

### The reply

# Marrowby Packaging - a power ranking cannot be read from these titles

You asked for Marrowby's leadership ranked by real power for Friday's QBR slide. What arrived is three names with titles; the call recordings are mentioned but were not pasted, so nothing in them has reached me.

Power in a purchase shows in what people do - who calls the meetings, who states the budget, whose question stops the room - and a title shows none of it. A VP of Operations may sign this contract or may never have heard of it. A ranking guessed from titles and put on a QBR slide is read as fact and becomes the account plan, so this note ranks nobody and gives no tier, score or step.

Paste the call notes and, for Halvard, Mei-Lin and Bastien, the emails we sent, the replies and the date of the last one, plus anything said about who approves at $1.6M or which reviews come before signature. From that I can map who is carrying the deal, how strong each line is, what is missing and the first move.

## Your reply

Both accounts are finished, and the record in front of you is the one to map. The seller pastes your message straight into their plan, so it is the map or the note alone: its first characters are `# ` and that account's name. Nothing sits above the title - no sentence about what you have read or are about to do, and no rule line - and that holds for a short note too.
