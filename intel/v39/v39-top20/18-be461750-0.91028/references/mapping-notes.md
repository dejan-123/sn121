# Mapping notes

Once this file is read, the tool work is finished and your next message is the map itself. Its first characters are `# Stakeholder map`; it opens with no announcement, no divider and no summary of what you are about to do.

## Reading counts honestly

- When the paste gives sends and replies, write the ratio ("answered 2 of 7"), then the last touch as stated, then who started the thread. When it gives neither, there is no ratio to write.
- A quick, friendly single reply after many sends is still a 2; tone does not repair asymmetry.
- An attendee who joined late, stayed quiet and left early has shown nothing; do not convert presence into support.
- When a contact's words promise action ("I'll take it from here", "leave it with me") and the notes show no action since, score the action, quote the words, and name the gap between them as a conflict.
- Recency on its own is not strength. A procurement contact who emailed last week about paperwork is current but neutral.
- A written closure from an evaluator ("requirements met") ends that requirement, even if the deal header still lists the review as pending. Point out the stale header.

## Gap logic by deal shape

Work out the needed roles from these questions, in order, and stop when the answers run out:
1. Who can sign at this value? If a stated ceiling is below the deal, the person above it is required.
2. Which gates have the contacts named (security questionnaire, risk review, purchase order, contract review)? Each gate needs an owner we can reach.
3. Who did a contact say must weigh in? A named team or role with no contact is a gap labelled "named in notes".
4. Does the stage make a role due now? A late-stage, large deal needs the signer and the paper owners engaged; an early, small one does not.

A small purchase inside one buyer's own authority, with no procurement or legal mentioned, usually has no structural gaps at all. Say that in one line and spend the words on the next meeting.

## Single-thread test

List every route to the economic buyer and to any approver the notes mention. If all routes pass through one contact, the account is single-threaded on that person. The first move asks that person for a warm introduction or a joint meeting, framed around something the buyer actually asked for. Our own executive can offer a peer conversation, but only through that introduction.

## Worked example (invented account)

Input sketch: a regional grocery chain, a 400K renewal-plus-expansion, mid-evaluation. Contacts: a store-systems manager who has answered 9 of 10 emails, booked every meeting and said finance "will want the payback math"; a chief information officer copied on the first email, answered 1 of 5, last touch 70 days ago; an unnamed finance lead mentioned once.

An excerpt from how the deal is wired:

> Nobody we know can sign a deal of this size: the notes give the store-systems manager no budget line, and her own remark that finance "will want the payback math" points at the finance lead, whom we have never met. She is the one driving it: she booked all three meetings and answered nine of our ten emails, so she is the champion at strength 5. The chief information officer has the rank to sponsor it, but after the opening email he answered one message in five and has been quiet for 70 days; he sits at strength 1 and the record does not support calling him a supporter. Every route to finance runs through the store-systems manager, which makes this a single-threaded deal.

Next moves, first two:

> 1. The deal is single-threaded on the store-systems manager, so ask her this week to bring the finance lead into a payback walkthrough built on the question she relayed; she has already offered that opening, so nothing has to happen first.
> 2. Once the walkthrough has a date, ask her whether the CIO should join it, instead of sending him a sixth unanswered email.

Sponsor, candidly, with the closing lines:

> Nobody on their side is carrying this deal yet. The CIO has the rank but not the record; the finance lead, once met, is the likelier person to win. The walkthrough comes first because it is the only move that puts us in front of whoever signs; if the manager will not set it up, that refusal is itself the news to act on.

## Last checks before sending

- Every name in the table appears in the paste, spelled the same way, and every behaviour sits with the person the note gives it to.
- Counts and recency are quoted, not computed: no number of days or weeks appears unless the paste wrote that number, and a ratio appears only where the paste has both numbers.
- Each dated event in the reply is the event the paste gives for that date: something we sent is not mistaken for something still owed.
- The last entry of every thread was checked, and anything waiting on us is a move.
- If the signer is someone we talk to directly, the word single-threaded does not appear.
- Every strength number has its counts or behaviour next to it.
- Every conflict the notes contain is written out with the action that settles it.
- The first move removes the current blocker and, where access runs through one person, says so.
- Nothing in the gaps comes from a checklist the deal's size does not justify.
