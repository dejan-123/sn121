# A worked map

One invented account, answered in full, then how the reply changes for other deal shapes. Copy the method and the form; every name, figure and date in a real reply comes only from its own input.

## The input, condensed

Account: Harlow County Water Authority (regional water utility, 1,400 employees). Deal: $640K ARR asset-management platform. Stage: late evaluation, procurement next quarter.

## The reply

# Harlow County Water Authority: stakeholder map

**Bottom line.** Wes is carrying this deal: he started it, feeds us data unprompted and is taking the case to the capital committee himself. The risk is above him: Priya signs over $250K and has not answered us since the proposal, and the committee that approves above $450K has never seen us. Security and procurement are down to two documents. The first move is Wes's: a working session with Priya on the committee numbers he already asked us for.

## Coverage gaps: $640K, late evaluation

- **Economic buyer unreached.** Priya must sign at this size and has never engaged; every path to her so far is Wes.
- **Committee unknown.** Nobody on the roster sits on the capital committee, and Wes is presenting without us in the room.
- **Engineering backing unclear.** Imogen's position is unknown, and committees ask the chief engineer.
- **No legal contact.** A $640K multi-year contract will go through legal review, and nobody on the roster owns it yet.
- **A competitor is in the evaluation**, so a stalled committee date costs more than time.
Security and procurement are covered and need only their stated items.

## Engagement sequence

1. **Answer Wes's Sep 30 request, still unanswered, with the committee numbers, and ask him for a joint working session with Priya on them**, so we meet her through the paper she must sign rather than a cold email. Depends on nothing; do this first.
2. **Send Ada the pen-test summary**, the last item in her Sep 4 note. Depends on nothing; run it in parallel.
3. **Send Colm the insurance certificate and point to step 2** to clear his Sep 18 waiver conditions. Depends on step 2.
4. **Ask Wes whether Imogen backs the committee paper**, and if she is undecided, offer her a short technical session on the failure data. Depends on Wes's answer; settles the conflict in her row.
5. **Agree with Wes who presents what to the committee, whether we can attend, and who handles contract review on their side**, so legal is not discovered at signature. Depends on step 1.

## Executive sponsor: Imogen, once re-engaged; Priya approves

Imogen is the sponsor to win: as Chief Engineer she is the most senior person with a stake in the outcome who has engaged with us directly, and committees ask the chief engineer. Her support is unproven (one review, then a decline), so recommend her with step 4, Wes asking where she stands and offering her a technical session, before anyone relies on her. Priya is the approver, not the sponsor: she signs above $250K and is reached through step 1. Until Imogen backs the paper, the forecast should treat the deal as champion-led.

## What drove these calls

The call that departs most from the titles is Wes above the Chief Engineer: every step forward started with him, while Imogen's praise came in one short review and has not been followed by a single substantive reply. Priya keeps Tier 1 through 71 days of silence because Wes's own words put the signature above $250K in her hands; silence lowers her strength, not her authority. The plan does not email Priya cold or give the committee members names, because the only path to either that the record supports runs through Wes. If Wes cannot get Priya into the session, that is the signal that his reach stops below the budget line, and the next move is a second sponsor rather than more champion activity.

## A single-threaded deal

Input, condensed: Corvane Seafoods (cold-chain operator), $420K, mid-evaluation. One contact in CRM: Ruth Abara, Head of Fleet Systems, 8 emails from us, 8 replies, last touch 3 days ago. She asked for the first call, sent route data unprompted and ran our demo for "three colleagues" who were never introduced. Sep 5, Ruth: "Our CFO wants the payback period before she signs." Oct 2, Ruth: "Still with the CFO, I'll chase." We have not answered her Oct 2 email. Nobody else is in CRM.

# Corvane Seafoods: stakeholder map

**Bottom line.** Everything we know about this deal comes from Ruth, and she is doing all she can: she started it, shares data unasked and answers every email. But the person who signs is a CFO we have never met, and her only question so far, the payback period, has been relayed second-hand. We also owe Ruth a reply. That reply is the place to ask for a joint session with the CFO.

## Stakeholder map

A $420K deal in mid-evaluation: by Ruth's account it needs the CFO's signature, and the stage will bring a security or procurement step that nobody has named yet.

| Contact | Title | Role in this deal | Tier | Strength | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ruth Abara | Head of Fleet Systems | Champion, sole contact | 1 | 5, steady | Asked for the first call, sent route data unprompted, ran the demo, 8/8 replies; Tier 1 in practice, and the only path to everyone else |

Referenced, not reached:
- **The CFO** (no name or contact): mentioned by Ruth on Sep 5 and Oct 2; signs the deal and wants the payback period; route: through Ruth.
- **Three demo colleagues** (unnamed): attended the demo; roles unknown; route: ask Ruth who they are.

## Coverage gaps: $420K, mid-evaluation

- **Single-threaded through Ruth.** She is the only contact and the only path to the CFO; if she moves or stalls, the deal goes dark.
- **Economic buyer unreached.** The CFO has never met us; her criteria arrive only through Ruth.
- **The evaluation team is anonymous.** Three people saw the demo and none can be mapped.

## Engagement sequence

1. **Answer Ruth's Oct 2 email with the payback analysis and ask her for a joint session with the CFO to walk through it.** Signal: the CFO's stated question, our unanswered reply. Depends on nothing; do this first.
2. **Ask Ruth for the names and roles of the three demo colleagues.** Signal: unnamed attendees. Run in parallel.
3. **In the CFO session, ask what else she needs to sign and who else reviews**, which surfaces the procurement or security step. Depends on step 1.

## Executive sponsor: none yet

No executive has engaged. The CFO is the approver and the natural sponsor at this size, but she is known only through Ruth, so she is the target, reached through step 1, not a sponsor to count on.

## What drove these calls

Ruth's excellent strength is exactly why the deal is fragile: every fact, including the CFO's question, reaches us through one person. The CFO gets no row because the input gives no name or contact for her, and the plan goes through Ruth rather than around her. If Ruth cannot get the CFO into a session within a couple of weeks, that tells us her reach stops below the signature, and the next move is to ask her who else the CFO listens to.

## A small deal

Input, condensed: Pell & Hartley Architects, $24K a year for one 9-person studio. Odette Vance, Studio Director, approves tooling up to $30K; she found us through the website, has had two calls, asked detailed questions and booked a trial. Leo Marchetti, IT Coordinator, joined one call, confirmed single sign-on works and said "fine from my side". No procurement, legal or other approver is mentioned.

# Pell & Hartley Architects: stakeholder map

**Bottom line.** Odette is buying this herself: the price sits inside her approval limit, she leads the studio that will use it, and the trial is booked. Leo's one requirement is met. The roster already fits the deal, so the work is the trial and the paperwork, not more contacts.

## Stakeholder map

A $24K purchase for one studio under the buyer's own limit needs the buyer and any sign-off she names; both are here.

| Contact | Title | Role in this deal | Tier | Strength | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Odette Vance | Studio Director | Buyer and champion | 1 | 5, rising | Approves tooling up to $30K, came inbound, two calls with detailed questions, trial booked |
| Leo Marchetti | IT Coordinator | IT sign-off | 2 | 3, steady | One call; confirmed single sign-on works ("fine from my side"); requirement closed |

## Coverage gaps: $24K, early evaluation

The roster fits this deal: Odette holds the budget and leads the team that will use it, and Leo's requirement is met. Nothing in the input raises procurement, legal or an executive approver, so none is added.

## Engagement sequence

1. **Run the booked trial with Odette's studio** and agree with her beforehand what a successful trial looks like. Signal: trial booked. Do this first.
2. **Send Odette the order form when the trial ends.** Depends on step 1; the price is inside her limit.

## Executive sponsor: Odette

At this size Odette is both sponsor and approver; no one above her needs to be involved.

## What drove these calls

A small deal inside one person's authority needs few people, so the map stays short. Adding finance, legal or an executive would create gaps the deal does not have and slow a purchase Odette can make herself. Leo's "fine from my side" is treated as closed because the requirement he owns was tested and met. If Odette mentions a second approver or the price rises above her limit, the map grows to include whoever that brings in, and not before.

## How the reply changes

- **A senior title with thin behaviour.** An executive who praised the project once and then delegated it ("keep driving this") is Tier 1 on authority with weak direct strength; the person she delegated to is the operating champion. Say both and route to the executive through the champion.
- **Conflicting signals.** Keep both in the Rationale column ("supportive in the briefing vs 1/4 replies, 57 days silent"), say which you weight and why, and make one step the action that settles it.

## Check before sending

Every tier and score cites a count or an event from the input. Every table row's Contact cell is a name the input gives; titles, teams and unnamed personas sit in the Referenced block and in the gaps. Tier follows authority and behaviour, not title. No name, contact, meeting or figure appears that the input lacks. Single-threading, when present, is the first gap and its repair the first step. Each step names a person or gap, a signal and a dependency. The reply starts with the `# <Account>: stakeholder map` line, then the bottom line, and ends with what drove the calls.
