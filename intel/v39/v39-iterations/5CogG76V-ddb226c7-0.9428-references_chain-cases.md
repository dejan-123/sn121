# Four worked replies

The accounts, people, figures and dates below are invented and appear nowhere else. They show only what a finished reply looks like. In a real reply, every fact comes from the record in front of you.

## Case 1: a large deal with several contacts

The record as pasted:

```
Account: Kestrel Valley Water (regional water utility, 1,900 staff)
Deal: €1.75M a year, network asset-monitoring analytics, utility-wide. Stage: late evaluation; the CRM stage note still reads "security review pending".

Contacts:
- Ines Marchetti, Head of Network Assets. In every session since the first workshop on 3 Feb. Sent two years of leak-incident data without being asked. Introduced us to Tomasz Wren and Aoife Brennan. 18 Apr, to us: "Our chief executive likes it, but wants to know whether this lets us retire the old telemetry contract before she takes it to the board." CRM: 14 sent, 13 replies.
- Tomasz Wren, Director of Operations. Attended a briefing on 6 Mar that our CEO set up; asked about depot-by-depot rollout. 9 Mar: "Keen to see this through evaluation." Nothing since. CRM: 5 sent, 1 reply.
- Aoife Brennan, Cyber Security Lead. Reviewed our penetration-test summary. 2 Apr: "Hosting and access model meet our standard; nothing further from me." CRM: 4 sent, 4 replies.
- Graham Oduro, Category Lead, Procurement. On one call, 8 Apr. Sent the supplier questionnaire on 10 Apr; we returned it on 14 Apr. CRM: 3 sent, 1 reply.

Context: Ines says anything over a million goes to the board. A second vendor is still shortlisted. No legal contact on record.
```

The reply:

# Stakeholder map: Kestrel Valley Water, €1.75M a year, late evaluation

## Reading the record

Ines has told us that anything over a million goes to the board. At €1.75M the signature is the board's, and the chief executive decides whether the paper reaches it. Neither has met us. A second vendor is still shortlisted, which makes drift costly. Ines carries the deal, and the security gate is already shut even though the stage note says otherwise. The first move is to put the answer to the chief executive's question in Ines's hands.

**Ines Marchetti, Head of Network Assets.** She has been in every session since the 3 Feb workshop. She sent two years of leak-incident data nobody asked for, and she made both of the introductions we have inside the utility. On 18 Apr she passed us the chief executive's question in her own words. She has answered 13 of our 14 emails and has delivered access twice, which makes her the carrier at 5, steady. She heads a function and does not sign. By conduct she is still the most important person in the record.

**Tomasz Wren, Director of Operations.** The title suggests a buyer. The record, though, shows one meeting (the 6 Mar briefing our CEO arranged), one question on depot rollout and a single warm note on 9 Mar: "Keen to see this through evaluation." The CRM shows 1 reply to 5 emails and nothing after that note. Nothing says he holds budget, so I do not hand him the pen. He is an endorser whose backing is unconfirmed, at 1, dormant.

**Aoife Brennan, Cyber Security Lead.** She answered all four of our emails and closed her review in writing on 2 Apr: "nothing further from me." The stage note that still says the security review is pending is out of date. She is a gate owner with her gate closed, at 3, steady. Her quiet since then is what a finished review looks like.

**Graham Oduro, Category Lead, Procurement.** He joined one call on 8 Apr and sent the supplier questionnaire on 10 Apr, which we returned on 14 Apr; the CRM shows 1 reply to 3 emails. The exchange is procedural, so he sits at 2, steady. Our return was the last word, so the next move on his gate is his.

Mentioned, never met: the chief executive, known only through Ines and carrying a stated condition, and the board, which comes from Ines's remark about spend over a million. Neither has a name in the record, and neither gets one here.

Owed by us: nothing. The questionnaire is back with procurement and the security review is closed.

Every route to the chief executive and the board runs through Ines: the deal is single-threaded on her.

## Stakeholder table

| Contact | Title | Role in this deal | Link 0-5, trend | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ines Marchetti | Head of Network Assets | Carrier | 5, steady | 13 replies to 14; volunteered incident data; two introductions |
| Tomasz Wren | Director of Operations | Endorser, unconfirmed | 1, dormant | 1 reply to 5; warm note 9 Mar, nothing since |
| Aoife Brennan | Cyber Security Lead | Gate owner, closed | 3, steady | 4 replies to 4; "nothing further from me", 2 Apr |
| Graham Oduro | Category Lead, Procurement | Gate owner, open | 2, steady | questionnaire sent 10 Apr, returned by us 14 Apr |

## Where the chain breaks

- **Single-threaded on Ines.** If she is pulled onto other work, nobody else in the record can take the case to the chief executive.
- **The pen has no link to us.** The chief executive and the board have never met us. Her question about retiring the old telemetry contract is the one condition anyone at the top has stated.
- **A dormant endorser.** Whether Tomasz still backs the deal is unknown after 9 Mar.
- **Contract review is unmapped.** The record names nobody for it. That is a question for Ines, not a name to guess.
- **A rival still shortlisted**, so time spent waiting is time given to them.

## Repairs

Nothing is owed, so the order starts from the chief executive's condition and works outward. Everything at the top goes through Ines.

1. **Put a one-page answer to the telemetry question in Ines's hands within the week.** Set what the old contract covers against what we would replace, using the incident data she sent, and size it to drop into a board paper. Done is Ines confirming the chief executive has it.
2. **Ask Ines to host a short session with the chief executive on that page.** This waits on step 1 and is the move that widens the thread at the top. In the same conversation, ask who prepares the board paper and who reviews contracts.
3. **Reopen Tomasz through our CEO, who arranged his only meeting with us.** A peer call on depot rollout, the subject Tomasz raised himself, will show whether his support holds. The account team sends him nothing cold.
4. **Ask Graham what follows the questionnaire and on what timetable**, once the chief executive's question has an answer, so the gate does not become the delay.

## Executive sponsor

There is no sponsor yet. The person who has to carry this to the board is the chief executive, reachable only through Ines, and step 2 is what would earn her. Tomasz was warm once and stays a candidate at most until he answers us. Our CEO is his natural counterpart, since our CEO set up the 6 Mar briefing.

**Calls that depart from the obvious.** Tomasz holds the senior title but answered one email in five, so he ranks below Ines, who does the work. Her title alone would make her a middle link; her introductions make her the backbone of the chain. The security review the stage note calls pending was closed in writing on 2 Apr.

## Case 2: a small purchase inside its buyer's limit

The record as pasted:

```
Account: Lindqvist & Moro Architects (design practice, 140 staff)
Deal: £21K a year, shared model-review tool for the nine-person visualisation studio. Stage: early evaluation; one call and a two-week trial done.

Contacts:
- Rafael Ochoa, Visualisation Studio Lead. Booked the first call through our website on 2 May. Ran the trial with his whole studio and sent four emails of file-format questions. Says he signs software spend under £30K from the studio budget. Workshop with his team booked for 21 May.
- Mei Tanaka, IT Coordinator. Joined trial setup for about fifteen minutes on 9 May, asked about single sign-on, said "that covers us." Nothing since.

No purchasing or legal step mentioned.
```

The reply:

# Stakeholder map: Lindqvist & Moro Architects, £21K a year, early evaluation

## Reading the record

This is a £21K tool for one studio of nine, and its lead has told us he signs software spend under £30K from his own budget. The pen is already in the room, and nothing in the paste puts purchasing or legal on the route. The chain is short and complete, and the only thing left to win is the 21 May workshop.

**Rafael Ochoa, Visualisation Studio Lead.** He came to us through the website on 2 May, put his whole studio on the trial and has sent four emails of file-format questions. He is pen holder and carrier in one, at 5 and warming. The trial has already put us in front of his team, and the limit he stated covers the price with room to spare.

**Mei Tanaka, IT Coordinator.** She spent about fifteen minutes at trial setup on 9 May, asked one question on single sign-on and closed it herself: "that covers us." That makes her a gate owner at 2, steady, with a narrow check she has finished. Her silence since is what a closed check looks like, not a contact cooling off.

Mentioned, never met: nobody. Owed by us: nothing the record shows.

## Stakeholder table

| Contact | Title | Role in this deal | Link 0-5, trend | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rafael Ochoa | Visualisation Studio Lead | Pen holder and carrier | 5, warming | inbound 2 May; whole-studio trial; signs under £30K |
| Mei Tanaka | IT Coordinator | Gate owner, closed | 2, steady | "that covers us", 9 May |

## Where the chain breaks

Nowhere this deal needs. One strong buyer inside his own limit is the normal shape for a one-studio purchase, so I am not looking for finance, legal or executive contacts the record gives no reason to involve.

## Repairs

Both steps hang on the workshop, because it is the next thing in the diary.

1. **Build the 21 May workshop on the file-format questions from Rafael's four emails**, each shown working on his studio's own models. Done is a team that leaves with no format question open.
2. **End the workshop by asking Rafael what he needs to approve the spend, and by what date.** He holds the pen, so this is the decision itself rather than a step toward it.

## Executive sponsor

Rafael is sponsor enough. He drives the evaluation and the price sits inside the limit he stated; on this record nobody above him needs to be involved.

**Calls that depart from the obvious.** Mei's one question is a closed check, not a second relationship to cultivate. The read would change if the spend turned out to leave the studio budget, for instance if Rafael asks for it to go through purchasing.

## Case 3: one contact, stalled at an approver we have never met

The record as pasted:

```
Account: Saltmarsh Hotels (boutique hotel group, 11 properties, 700 staff)
Deal: €290K a year, guest messaging and housekeeping dispatch, group-wide. Stage: mid-evaluation.

Notes and emails:
- 3 Mar, first call: Joel Varga, Guest Experience Manager, booked it. Described housekeeping requests missed at weekend peaks. Sent three months of request logs the next morning. Asked for a pilot review "with some of our property managers".
- 17 Mar, pilot review: Joel plus three property managers whose names we were not given. Questions on night-shift handover. Joel afterwards: "They want it. I'll take it upstairs."
- 2 Apr, Joel wrote: "Our managing director wants a payback estimate before she'll approve anything at this size. Can you help me build it?" We sent a payback model on 3 Apr.
- 22 Apr, Joel wrote: "Estimate is with the MD. Nothing back yet."
- 6 May, Joel wrote: "MD asked whether housekeeping dispatch could start at four properties and extend later. What would that do to the price?" Not yet answered by us.

CRM: Joel Varga, Guest Experience Manager, 9 sent, 8 replies, last touch 6 May. Nobody else is in the CRM; the managing director has never met us.
```

The reply:

# Stakeholder map: Saltmarsh Hotels, €290K a year, mid-evaluation

## Reading the record

At €290K across the group, approval sits with the managing director. Joel quoted her on 2 Apr as wanting a payback estimate before she approves "anything at this size." She has never met us, and the record gives her no name. The estimate has been with her since Joel's 22 Apr note, so the deal is stalled at her desk. The one live opening is her own question, relayed on 6 May and still unanswered by us, so answering it is the first move.

**Joel Varga, Guest Experience Manager.** Everything in the record runs through him. He booked the first call, sent request logs the next morning, brought three property managers to the pilot review, built the business case on our payback model and took it upstairs. He has answered 8 of our 9 emails. That makes him a carrier, and the pilot review was real access. The stall since 22 Apr caps him at 4, though: his push upward has produced a submitted estimate, not an introduction. His title is a manager's and he cannot sign at this size; his conduct is a champion's.

Mentioned, never met: the managing director, through Joel's emails of 2 Apr and 6 May, and the three property managers from 17 Mar, who were never named to us.

Owed by us: an answer to Joel's 6 May question on starting dispatch at four properties.

The deal is single-threaded on Joel; if he stalls, nothing reaches the managing director.

## Stakeholder table

| Contact | Title | Role in this deal | Link 0-5, trend | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Joel Varga | Guest Experience Manager | Carrier | 4, stalling | 8 replies to 9; built the payback case; 6 May question unanswered by us |

## Where the chain breaks

- **Single thread on Joel.** He is the only person we can reach and the only route to the approver.
- **The pen has never met us.** Both of the managing director's conditions, the payback estimate and now a phased start, reached us second-hand.
- **Momentum.** The estimate has sat with her since 22 Apr. Her 6 May question is the first sign it is being read.
- **Three pilot users without names.** They liked what they saw on 17 Mar, but we cannot reach or quote them.

## Repairs

The reply we owe is also the approver's own question, so answering it well is the fastest way to restart the approval.

1. **Answer Joel this week with a priced phased option.** Cover dispatch at four properties first, the terms for extending, and the payback model rerun for that start, written so he can forward it unchanged. Done is Joel confirming it reached the managing director.
2. **Ask Joel to set up a short call where we walk the managing director through the phased option, with him in the room.** Framed as answering her question, it does not go round him. It waits on step 1 and is the only step that gives the pen a direct link to us.
3. **Ask Joel for the names and roles of the three pilot property managers.** They would run the first four sites, and a line from them would strengthen the phased case.

## Executive sponsor

There is no sponsor yet. It has to be the managing director, who holds the approval and has met only Joel; the call in step 2 is what would earn her. Joel is the champion, not her stand-in, and nothing goes to her from us until he makes the introduction.

**Calls that depart from the obvious.** One CRM row with 8 replies looks like a healthy relationship; in fact the deal is stalled at an approver we have never met. The 6 May email reads like an update, but it carries her question, and the silence on it is ours.

## Case 4: a request the record cannot support

The record as pasted:

```
Account: Brackwater Mining (copper producer, 3,000 staff)
Deal: €1.9M a year, haul-fleet telemetry. Stage: negotiation.
Request from the account executive: "Can you turn our contacts into a reporting-line diagram of their buying group for my QBR deck?"
Supplied: Selin Arkan (General Manager, Mine Operations), Duc Pham (Head of Fleet Maintenance), Oriana Vale (Group Treasurer), Kwame Asante (Supply Chain Lead). "We have five months of call notes and CRM activity on file."
```

The reply:

# Brackwater Mining: what this paste can support

You asked for a reporting-line diagram of Brackwater's buying group for your QBR deck, and the paste gives four names with titles: Selin Arkan, Duc Pham, Oriana Vale and Kwame Asante. The five months of call notes and CRM activity are mentioned but not included, so I have titles and nothing on what anyone has done.

I have not drawn the diagram. A title does not tell me who answers to whom, and a guessed hierarchy on a leadership slide will be read as confirmed. The real lines can only come from inside Brackwater, so ask one of your contacts there whether they will share or sketch them. Ordering these four by seniority would be the same guess, so I have ranked no one.

A €1.9M deal in negotiation still turns on three questions: who signs at that figure, who carries your case internally, and which reviews stand before an order. To answer them, paste:

- the dated call notes, with who attended each;
- emails sent and received per person;
- any spending limit or approval route someone has stated;
- every open review or form, with its owner and status.

## On writing the reply

Each map above opens with its reading because everything after it depends on that reading: the table, the breaks and the repairs restate calls already argued there. Each person's paragraph starts from whatever says most about that person, whether an act, a count, a quote or a closed check, so no two read alike. Each ends with a role and a score and the signal that earned them. Counts and dates are copied from the record, never worked out. Where the record does not say who wrote last, the reply says that nothing is shown as owed. A small purchase gets a small map, and a chart request gets a note, not a diagram.
