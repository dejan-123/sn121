# Worked records: three invented accounts

Every organisation, person, date and figure below is made up. The three records show how a paste becomes a full map, a short map or a note. None of their facts belongs in a real reply, which draws only on what the seller pasted.

### The reply

# Larchmont Credit Union - stakeholder map for the $920K member-retention deal

### How I read the account

A $920K deal at the business-case stage, with Ledgerline also in the running. Mateo has told us how it is approved: anything above $500K goes to the board's technology committee, and his paper is on its December agenda. So the yes belongs to a committee nobody on our side has met, and every line we have to it runs through one person.

Mateo Brandt is the champion. He booked discovery, ran the six-week pilot, sent three years of closure records unasked and brought the COO into the August readout: 13 replies to 15 emails, and a door above him already opened once. His latest note says the paper is filed and he will pass on whatever the committee asks. That is the same kind of work as before, so his pattern holds while the decision waits above him: 5, steady. It is not rising, since nothing in it goes further than August did. He does not hold the spend: $920K is over the line he described.

Sunniva Kerr's record splits in two. In August she sent processing times unasked and forwarded our case study to four branch managers the same day - a champion's acts. Since then our summary went into the COO's staff meeting with no word of how it landed, and "leave it with me" on 22 October has nothing behind it. The CRM prints no counts for her, so none appears here; what she did sets the number. The August acts keep her a champion; the autumn words with nothing after them make her 4, cooling. One question settles it: how did the staff meeting take the summary, and would she take us into the next one?

Ruth Asante is where title and record part. A warm remark at the readout and a thank-you the next day read like sponsorship, but one reply to four emails, and that reply the thank-you, is a line that went quiet in August: 2, dormant, an early backer gone silent. The remark shapes how we approach her, not the number. Mateo brought her in once and is the way back.

Dariusz Pell followed the integration session with more questions the next morning, took our two documents and wrote on 19 October: "Risk review complete from my side." The stage field still lists the review as open; his note is newer and first-hand, so it stands. Three replies to three emails: an evaluator with the requirement met, 3, steady, and he needs nothing more from us.

Hollis Grange sat in the same session, asked twice about write access to the ledger and left early. The two unanswered emails are his, not Dariusz's. Someone whose view we do not know is not an opponent: 1, stance unknown. A third email repeats what has already failed; Dariusz, who was in the room with him, is the better way in.

Odile Fenn is in the CRM but has never been reached. Mateo says she will want the five-year cost view before the committee reads the paper, so she shapes what the committee sees without signing it: 1, never reached.

Bertrand Achterberg sent the due-diligence questionnaire on 2 November and it is still with us. His gate is open and the next move is ours. A gatekeeper who answers within a day and has taken no side: 3, steady.

The platform writes scores into the member CRM, which marketing runs, and nobody from marketing is in the record. Nobody from legal is either, and a $920K contract will need a reviewer. With Ledgerline also shortlisted, whatever reaches the committee will be read against a rival's numbers.

### Coverage gaps

- **Economic buyer: the board's technology committee (members not named).** $920K is above Mateo's $500K line. No contact, and no read on what the committee weighs beyond his paper.
- **Finance (Odile Fenn).** Named and never reached; her cost view goes in front of the committee.
- **The member CRM's owner.** The platform writes into a system marketing runs, and nobody from marketing is in the record.
- **Contract review.** Nobody from legal appears, and the contract will need a reviewer before December.

### Engagement plan

**Single-thread risk:** every line to the committee runs through Mateo. If he is pulled away before December, nobody else speaks for us in that room.

1. **Ask Mateo for two introductions in one message:** to Odile, carrying the five-year cost view she wants, and to whichever committee member he thinks should see the pilot before December. We will know it is done when one meeting has a date. Nothing goes to the committee around him; the paper is his.
2. **Return Bertrand's questionnaire this week** (in parallel). It is entirely in our hands.
3. **Ask Dariusz whether Hollis's write-access questions were settled, and offer a short call with both of them** (in parallel). Not a third email to Hollis.
4. **Test Sunniva** (in parallel): ask how the staff meeting took the summary, whether she would take us into the next one, and for the four branch managers' names. A name and a date put her back to steady; silence says her push has stopped.
5. **Ask Mateo who reviews the contract, who in marketing runs the member CRM, and whether Ruth would add a line of support to his paper** (after 1).

Dariusz has closed his own review and is asked only about Hollis.

### Executive sponsor

Nobody qualifies yet. The yes belongs to a committee that has never met us; Ruth's single warm remark was followed by silence; Mateo works hardest for us but his authority stops at $500K. The person to win is a committee member, reached by way of Mateo's paper, and Ruth is worth one test through him as a second voice.

### Why the plan starts where it does

December is fixed and the committee has never heard from us, so the introductions through Mateo come first. The questionnaire runs alongside because only we can move it; Sunniva's test shows whether the account has one champion or two.

# Ashgrove Physiotherapy - stakeholder map for the $26K scheduling tool

## Record three: an ask the record cannot answer

### The paste

"Dunmore Glassworks, 3,100 staff, $1.9M furnace-monitoring service contract, final negotiation. Rank their leadership by who really decides, top to bottom, for my forecast call on Monday. Contacts: Isak Thorvald (Plant Director, Ravensby), Celine Abara (Head of Engineering), Piers Lockhart (Commercial Director). The call recordings are in our call tool."

### The reply

# Dunmore Glassworks - who decides cannot be ranked from three titles

You asked for Dunmore's leadership ranked by who really decides, for Monday's forecast call. What came through is three names and their titles; the call recordings are mentioned but were not pasted, so nothing in them has reached me.

A title does not show who signs a $1.9M contract, who reports to whom, or whose doubt would stop it, and a ranking guessed from titles goes into the forecast as fact. So this note ranks nobody and gives no tier, score or step. The true answer lives with Dunmore: ask Isak, Celine or Piers who signs at $1.9M and who else has to agree before they do, or ask for Dunmore's own organisation chart. One answer in their words beats any order guessed from here.

Send the call notes, and for Isak, Celine and Piers our emails, their answers and when the last one came, plus anything said about approval or sign-off. From that I can map who is carrying the deal, how strong each line is, what is missing and the first move.
