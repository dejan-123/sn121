# Sample maps (invented accounts; they show layout and argument, and none of their facts may be reused)

None of the five accounts below exists. The first shows what to send back when no map is possible. The other four are complete maps: an order small enough for one signature, which gets the short map; a large deal in which the economic buyer has not been reached; a deal whose sponsor has gone quiet; a deal that depends on a single contact. A real reply draws every fact from its own record, in that record's own form.

## Sample 1: when no map is possible

Record: "Could you chart the reporting structure of Jarrow Cinemas for me, so that I can walk our directors through it? We are shortlisted for the 190k seat-booking software deal." Sent with it: Alba Quince, Estates Director; Emeric Zhou, Head of IT; Una Abbott, Procurement Lead; plus a note that the call write-ups are stored in the CRM.

# Jarrow Cinemas: no structure can be drawn from job titles alone

Your request was for the reporting structure of Jarrow Cinemas as a chart, to walk our directors through; it concerns the 190k seat-booking software purchase, where we are on the shortlist. With the request came three names and job titles: Alba Quince (Estates Director), Emeric Zhou (Head of IT) and Una Abbott (Procurement Lead), plus a note that the call write-ups are stored in the CRM.

Nothing has been drawn, for two reasons. The write-ups were mentioned but not attached, so I have no record of what anybody did. And a title says neither who a person reports to nor who signs a 190k purchase or would sponsor it, so any line I drew would be fiction, and fiction on a slide is soon repeated as fact.

Only Jarrow can supply the reporting lines, either as its own organisation chart or by one of the three answering the question.

What our directors need for a shortlisted 190k deal is something else, and a stakeholder map can give it once the write-ups are in hand:

- whose signature an order of 190k needs, and if we have any line to that person;
- which of the three has actually done something for the deal;
- which conditions are still open, and the first move.

To build it, please send:

- the call write-ups in full;
- per person, the number of messages from us, the number answered and the date of the latest reply;
- any remark, from anyone, on who approves the purchase or who else must consent.

## After the samples

No further samples follow. Before writing, find the sample whose record is shaped like yours (bare titles; one signer; a buyer out of reach; a sponsor gone quiet; a single contact) and follow its reasoning, not its facts. The reply is about the account alone; samples and skills have no place in it, and no lead-in sentence or line of dashes sits at its top. Two checks before writing. First, when the amount is inside the limit your contact states, the reply is the short map of sample 2, in which one person signs, coverage is complete, a check that its owner called satisfied is closed, and the plan runs from the next booked event to the order form. Second, look through the record for anything it explicitly marks as waiting on us (a message it says we have not answered, a document it says we have not sent): only such items stand under `We still owe:`, and they open the plan; things we merely want to find out are gaps and moves, not debts. What you write now answers the record you were given, as a map or as a note depending on that record, and it starts with `# ` and that account's name. That title has no line of dashes above it.
