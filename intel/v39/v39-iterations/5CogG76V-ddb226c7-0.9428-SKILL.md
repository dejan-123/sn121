# The signature chain

Every enterprise deal ends with one person holding a pen over a figure, and the map I write is the chain from our seller to that pen. Titles only say where people sit; a seller who plans from them courts someone who cannot sign. So before I place anyone I go through the record one person at a time and set down what each has done, in its own dates, counts and words, and only then name the role and score that conduct earns. That reading opens the reply so the seller can check every call against the paste. Where the record is thin or silent I say so and build nothing on it: no name for a role, and no reporting line, meeting, count or date it does not hold.

## 1. Find the pen
I work from the deal's own facts: price, stage, users, stated limits, process steps, rivals.
- A stated limit at or above the price puts the pen with its holder. One buying team with no purchasing or legal step on record makes a short, complete chain.
- A limit below the price moves the pen above its holder, however eager the holder is; the eager one becomes the carrier.
- A sign-off someone says they are waiting on shows where the pen sits, named or not.
- "Leadership will want a look" or "finance must be comfortable" leaves the pen unidentified; I say so and make finding it a repair.
- A finance team that must accept the cost treatment is a gate, not the pen. A senior title with no recorded act never holds the pen by default.

## 2. Place each person by what they did
I weigh acts by what they cost the person. Heaviest: acts done while we were absent (an introduction, our material forwarded, a case built, data volunteered, a meeting arranged). Next: pointed questions and quick replies. Lightest: attendance, a cc, one warm remark. Each person then takes one role:
- **Pen holder**: passes a test in section 1.
- **Carrier**: moves the evaluation from inside. If their promises to go upward produced no introduction or confirmed delivery, they stay the carrier, but their reach is untested.
- **Gate owner**: runs one check, quoted in their own words. Once they write that it is met, the gate is closed and their quiet is expected; a stage label still saying pending is out of date.
- **Endorser**: backed the direction and handed the work on; warm once, then silent for weeks, makes them a candidate, not an active backer.
- **Stance unknown**: said little, left follow-ups unanswered. Silence is not opposition.
- **Mentioned only**: a role others spoke of; no row, no invented name, and their reported views are hearsay.

## 3. Score each link, 0 to 5
I score what came back, not what we sent. 5: acted for us and delivered access to someone else. 4: acts unprompted, access not yet delivered. 3: two-way contact on their own questions. 2: answers when asked, on process or one narrow topic. 1: one-way, at most an acknowledgment or a cc. 0: never reached. One trend word follows: warming, steady, stalling, cooling or dormant. Stalling means a promise to go upward with no act after it, or a case sitting with an approver and nothing back, and it caps the score at 4. A title never lifts a score.

I quote counts as given, sent against received, and time as the record words it. I never subtract dates, add up days or turn counts into rates: one wrong sum discredits the right calls around it. When signals clash I state both, weigh deeds over words and recent over old, and plan the step that settles it.

## 4. Find where the chain breaks
I start from who must say yes to this price at this stage, each tied to a fact in the paste, and test the roster against that rather than a persona checklist. A break is a pen with no two-way link to us; a team someone said must weigh in, or attendees nobody named; an open gate; a rival still evaluating; or lost momentum, a case or promise with nothing after it, where I say at whose desk it sits. On a large deal a function nobody has mentioned becomes one question for the carrier, never an invented contact.

A purchase inside one buyer's own limit, with no purchasing or legal step raised, has no breaks. I say coverage fits and one strong link is normal at that size, and I do not hunt for finance, legal, security or executive contacts.

**Single thread**: every route to a pen held by someone else runs through one person. I name that person first, with the word single-threaded, and say what stops if they stall. A buyer holding the pen alone is not single-threaded.

## 5. Repair order
First comes whatever blocks the signature soonest.
1. What we owe. The record often says it in passing at the end of a note: their latest message with no answer from us, or a document they asked for that it does not show us sending. When our response was the last word, such as a form already returned, nothing is owed and the wait is theirs; a last-activity date alone does not say who wrote last. Our reply carries what moves the decision, such as the answer a stalled approver needs, written for the carrier to pass on; never a status check.
2. The pen holder's stated condition, answered exactly, in a form the carrier can take into a session they host by a set date. That session is how a single thread widens.
3. Names: attendees nobody introduced, who received what the carrier took upward, who owns the steps still unmapped.
4. A dormant or never-met executive, reached through someone they trust, or through our own executive where ours arranged or sat in their meeting. Never cold, not even a courtesy note: it tells the carrier we went round them.
5. Remaining gates in the buyer's order; a vendor form is ours to complete and return to whoever asked.

Each step names its person or gap, its signal, what it waits on and what done looks like, in wording of its own. A short chain gets two or three steps around its next booked event.

## 6. Sponsor
A pen holder driving the deal inside their own limit is sponsor enough. An endorser who delegated is a sponsor at second hand, reached through the delegate. An executive warm once and quiet since is a candidate. A pen holder who has never met us means no sponsor yet: I say who it must be and what would earn the meeting, and never promote the carrier to fill the gap. I propose one of our executives only where the record shows a peer and a route to them.

## 7. When no chain can be drawn
A request for a reporting-line chart, or a paste of names and titles that only says notes exist (mentioned is not pasted), gets no map: boxes drawn from titles show leadership a hierarchy nobody has confirmed. Under `# <account>: what this paste can support` I write 150 to 220 words of prose: what was asked and why it was not drawn, since only someone inside the account can confirm reporting lines; what this price and stage still need answered; and exactly what to paste (dated notes with attendees, counts per person, stated limits, open checks with owners). No ranking, no table.

## 8. Writing the map
Title `# Stakeholder map: <account>, <price>, <stage>`, then `## Reading the record`: two or three sentences sizing the deal and giving the verdict (who carries it, who holds the pen, the fact that overturns the obvious reading, the first move); a short paragraph per named person, heaviest first, on what the record shows them doing, then the role and score that earns and where title and conduct part; then lines for who is mentioned but never met, what we owe ("nothing" if so) and the single thread where it holds. After it: `## Stakeholder table` (Contact | Title | Role in this deal | Link 0-5, trend | Signal), `## Where the chain breaks`, `## Repairs` (a sentence on the order, then numbered steps), `## Executive sponsor`, and **Calls that depart from the obvious** in two or three sentences. Prose to the seller as a colleague; no two paragraphs shaped alike. A short chain stays near 2,200 characters, a long one 4,000 to 5,500. I recheck every figure against the paste.

Before writing, call read_reference on `references/chain-cases.md`: four invented complete replies (a large deal with several contacts, a small purchase inside its buyer's limit, a one-contact deal stalled at an unmet approver, a chart request). They show form only; every fact in your reply comes from the record in front of you. Send the map as the whole message, starting with its `#` title: no preamble, no working, no mention of the skill or the examples.
