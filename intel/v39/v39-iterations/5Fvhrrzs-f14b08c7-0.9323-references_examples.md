# Invented accounts, for form only

Made-up accounts and the reply each one gets. They show the order of the calls and how the page sits. A real reply takes every name, figure, count and sentence from its own input; a detail carried over from these pages would be an invention.

## Case A: a full record, large deal (container terminal)

Input, in summary. Account: Brightwater Terminals, container terminal operator, 1,800 staff. Deal: $860K ARR, yard-planning platform. Stage: final evaluation; the stage notes still read "compliance review open". A rival vendor, Quaylink, is on the shortlist.
- Radhika Solberg, Head of Terminal Planning: sent their berth-delay data without being asked; arranged the May session with the Port Director; says her own budget line stops at $300K and the investment board signs above it. In June she said she would "get us a slot at the investment board"; her two messages since say only that she is "still working on it". Last touch two days ago.
- Teun Abiola, Port Director: one session in May, arranged by Radhika and attended by our regional director; said "this is the direction for us" and told Radhika to "take it to the investment board". Since then he has sent one line of thanks and nothing else, for ten weeks.
- Mirjam Castellano, Customs Compliance Lead: asked for the audit-log specification, came back the next day with questions of her own, and wrote on 9 August, "audit requirement met; no further questions from compliance".
- Odhran Petrov, IT Security Architect: joined one technical session and asked nothing; our emails to him since then are unanswered.
- Sunniva Achebe, Procurement Lead: sent the supplier onboarding form last week and said a security sign-off must be on file before she can raise an order; we have not returned the form. She answers within a day or two; no view expressed.
- Radhika, in passing: "the Finance Director chairs the investment board" and "the shift supervisors will want a say on rollout". Neither is in the CRM.

The reply:

# Stakeholder map: Brightwater Terminals, $860K yard-planning platform

## Reading of the record

An $860K contract in final evaluation, with Quaylink also on the shortlist. Radhika Solberg's budget line stops at $300K and she says the investment board signs above it, so the decision sits with people we have not met. Procurement has its form out and wants a security sign-off on file; compliance was asked and has answered.

Radhika Solberg, Head of Terminal Planning. She sent the berth-delay data unasked and arranged the May session with the Port Director: a champion on her acts, with access proven as far as him. Since her June promise of a slot at the investment board there have been words and no date, so access to the board is untested and the trend is down: 4/5, cooling.

Teun Abiola, Port Director. The title reads like a sponsor's, and in May he sounded like one: "this is the direction for us", and he told Radhika to take it to the board. Since then one line of thanks has come back and nothing else, for ten weeks. The silence is later than the meeting and longer, so he is not a sponsor today: 1/5, dormant. He passed the decision to the board himself, so he is not the buyer either.

Mirjam Castellano, Customs Compliance Lead. She asked for the specification, followed up with questions of her own, which is engagement and not silence, and wrote on 9 August that the audit requirement is met. Our stage notes still say "compliance review open"; hers is first-hand, dated and later, so the stage line is stale. Influencer, requirement met: 3/5, steady.

Odhran Petrov, IT Security Architect. Silent in his one session, and our emails since are unanswered. Neither opposition nor approval: disengaged, stance unknown, 1/5, dormant. Procurement wants a security sign-off on file, so his silence leaves that step open.

Sunniva Achebe, Procurement Lead. Her onboarding form has been with us since last week and she answers within a day or two. A gatekeeper doing her job: 2/5, steady. The delay is ours.

Waiting on us: the onboarding form, since last week. The account is **single-threaded** on Radhika: the May session and all we know of the board came through her.

## Coverage gap analysis

A contract of this size has to pass a signer above $300K, a security sign-off, compliance and procurement.

**Structural risk, single-threaded on Radhika Solberg.** If she is away or loses heart, there is no route to the decision.

- **The signer: unmet.** The investment board signs above $300K; we know its chair by role only.
- **Security sign-off: open.** Its owner has given no view, and procurement cannot raise an order without it.
- **Rollout voice: unidentified.** The shift supervisors were mentioned and are unknown to us.

Compliance is closed, and procurement is covered once the form goes back. Whether their legal team reviews a contract of this size is a question for Radhika; nobody has mentioned it.

## Engagement plan

1. **Ask Radhika for the introduction behind her June promise.** Which board meeting will take it, who presents, and will she bring us to that person? Every route upward runs through her, so this repairs the single thread and tests her access to the board in one ask. It is done when we hold a date and a second name; steps 3 and 5 wait on it, the others do not.
2. **Return Sunniva Achebe's onboarding form today** and ask what the security sign-off needs from us. It is the one thing fully in our hands.
3. **Bring the Port Director back through someone he has met.** Our regional director sat in the May session; once Radhika confirms the board date, he writes to Teun Abiola offering what the board will need. The account team's own messages drew one line of thanks, so another one is not the way.
4. **Ask Radhika how Odhran Petrov is best approached and what he needs to see**, in place of another email. That settles whether security is waiting for something or has not started.
5. **Ask Radhika which shift supervisors to involve, and what Quaylink has shown them.**

Mirjam Castellano has closed her point and is asked for nothing more.

## Executive sponsor recommendation

Brightwater has no sponsor for this today: Teun Abiola endorsed it once and has been silent for ten weeks, and the board that signs has not met us. He is the one to win back, and our regional director is the grounded choice to do it: he sat with him in May, so a note from him continues a conversation where one from us would start cold. Its subject is the board, so it waits for step 1. Nothing goes to the Finance Director from our side before Radhika brings us in: we have no name, and a first message from a vendor's executive would land cold.

**Why the map reads this way.** The Head of Terminal Planning ranks above the Port Director because the record shows her acting and him silent. He is not counted as a sponsor and not written off: his one signal was real, it is ten weeks old, and a route back exists. Security is open, not hostile: its reviewer has said nothing either way. Compliance is treated as closed against our own stage notes because its owner said so in writing. Nobody is named for the board or the shifts because the record names nobody.

## Case B: a full record, small deal (dental group)

Input, in summary. Account: Fernhill Dental Group, 40 staff. Deal: $16K a year, patient-recall tool for a front-desk team of nine. Stage: trial, second week; a review call is booked for Friday.
- Anouk Baptiste, Practice Manager: came to us through a referral, set up the trial herself, sent two lists of questions about recall rules, asked for the price in writing; said tools under $20K are hers to approve.
- Lorcan Idowu, Lead Hygienist: joined part of one call, checked the recall intervals and said they cover what the practice does, "fine as it stands for now"; nothing from him since.
Nobody has mentioned purchasing or a contract review.

The reply:

# Stakeholder map: Fernhill Dental Group, $16K patient-recall tool

## Reading of the record

A $16K purchase for a front-desk team of nine, two weeks into a trial, with a review call booked for Friday. A deal of this shape needs the person who signs and a check that the tool fits, and the record holds both.

Anouk Baptiste, Practice Manager. She came in through a referral, set up the trial herself, sent two lists of questions about recall rules and asked for the price in writing. Tools under $20K are hers to approve and this one is $16K, so she is buyer and champion in one person: 4/5, warming.

Lorcan Idowu, Lead Hygienist. He joined part of one call, checked the recall intervals and said they cover what the practice does. One narrow check, passed: an influencer whose requirement is met, 2/5, steady. His "for now" is a caveat in his wording, not a second signal, so nothing conflicts and nothing is reopened.

Waiting on us: the written price. Because the contact is the buyer, there is no thread to someone else that could break.

## Engagement plan

1. **Send Anouk Baptiste the written price before Friday**, with the trial's end date on it. She asked for it and it is still open.

## Executive sponsor recommendation

Not needed. The buyer is the day-to-day contact and the amount is inside her own authority.

**Why the map reads this way.** Two rows and two steps are the whole map because the purchase is that small: the person running the trial can sign for it. Purchasing and legal are absent because nobody in the record has raised them. Lorcan Idowu's low score describes how little we have spoken, not a risk; his check passed.

## Case C: names and titles only, and a request for a drawing (cement producer)

Input, in summary. Account: Varnholt Cement, building-materials producer, 4,400 staff. Deal: $1.7M ARR, kiln-maintenance platform. Stage: contracting. The sales director writes: "Can you draw me the chain of command for their plant and IT leadership, boxes and arrows, for Monday's review?" Supplied: three names with titles (Berit Naidoo, Head of Plant Reliability; Caspian Rowe, IT Portfolio Manager; Edurne Kessler, Strategic Buyer) and the remark that "all the call notes are in the CRM".

The reply:

# Varnholt Cement: no chain of command can be drawn from this paste

You asked for "the chain of command for their plant and IT leadership, boxes and arrows". What arrived is three names with titles; the call notes are said to be in the CRM and were not pasted.

A title shows neither who reports to whom nor who decides, and an arrow between two boxes in a leadership review would be a guess read as fact. So nothing is drawn and nobody is ranked. Reporting lines are Varnholt's to give: their own org chart, or one of the three confirming who sits where.

What this record can support, once pasted, is a stakeholder map: who signs at $1.7M, who is working for the deal, who can stop it, and the first move. For that, paste the call notes and, for each contact, the messages sent against replies, the date of the last exchange, and anything said about approval.

## The same form on a thin record

Suppose Radhika were our only contact, everyone else known through her emails alone: she has told her director about us, written the costed note the director asked for and handed it in, and her latest email, of 3 June, says the director is still reading it and that she will chase; we have not answered it. The reading says so in its first sentences and flags the single thread. Her paragraph ends: "She has done what she said she would, and the note is with her director. Her latest email reports the wait and says she will chase it: a status report that is ours to answer, not a promise gone quiet. Telling her director about us is not yet bringing us to her, so access is untested. 4/5, steady." The table holds her one row and no other:

| Radhika Solberg | Head of Terminal Planning | Champion; access to her director untested; high | 4/5; last touch 3 June, her email; steady | Wrote and handed in the note her director asked for. The wait is the director's and the unanswered email is ours, so nothing is marked down. |

Under it: "Mentioned, no contact: her director, who asked for the costed note; the colleagues she brought to the demo. None is named in the record." Step 1 reads: "Answer Radhika's 3 June email today, and in the same reply ask to join when she takes the note to her director." Nothing goes to the director from us. Every section is still there, shorter.

## Had it been different

- Had Radhika's budget line covered the deal, she would be buyer and champion together and the board would not appear as a gap.
- Had the Port Director replied last week, the conflict about him would be gone: an active sponsor at 3/5.
- Had the record named no board and nobody with stated authority, the buyer would read "not established": the Port Director's title and his warm words would not make him the signer, and asking Radhika who signs would be step 1.
- Had Odhran never joined a session and been known only because Radhika mentioned his team in passing, he would be unclassified with no contact and not scored: a remark about a department is hearsay, and step 1 would ask her who decides there.
- Had she done everything she promised and the wait sat with her director's review of a case she had submitted, her trend would read steady, not cooling, and step 1 would ask what the director still needs to see.
- Had the dental deal been ten times the size and across every practice, Anouk's limit would put the buyer above her, and the silence about purchasing would become a question to ask.
- Had Varnholt's request come with the call notes pasted, it would get the full map, with one line pointing to their own org chart for the reporting question.

Each reply above is the whole of what was sent: it starts at its title and ends with its last line.
