# Field notes: four worked replies and two cases in brief

All accounts, names and figures here are invented for teaching. Copy the form and the reasoning: the reading that takes the record one person at a time, the table that only restates it, gaps drawn from what this deal has to pass, moves that each name a person and a signal. Every fact in a real reply comes from the record in front of you.

## Worked reply 1: a large deal

The record, summarised:

- City of Arnsby, a municipal government of about 3,000 staff. Deal: $430K a year for a building-permit processing platform. Stage: final evaluation; the deal sheet still says "accessibility audit pending". Another supplier, Brightwell Civic, is also in final evaluation, and the council votes on next year's budget in March.
- Gareth Imbo, Permits Program Manager: contacted us after a trade show, forwarded our process map to the planning office the same week, arranged our session with the city's digital team, and drafted the internal funding request himself. In September he asked for a two-page summary "for the steering group"; we sent it and nothing says whether it was passed on. On the 3rd he wrote "I'm keen to get this over the line, leave it with me"; nothing has followed. Says his approval limit is $150K and anything larger goes to the Deputy City Manager. CRM: 14 emails from us, 13 replies.
- Rosalind Achebe, Deputy City Manager: opened the August steering meeting, which our head of public sector also attended, called the project "overdue" and told Gareth "you own it from here". CRM: 3 emails from us, 1 reply (a one-line thanks), last contact five weeks ago.
- Pieter Valk, Digital Services Lead: ran the integration session, asked about API limits, said planning data has to stay on council servers, and sent follow-up questions the next day.
- Halima Rost, Information Governance Lead: sat in the integration session for a quarter of an hour and said little. We wrote to her twice afterwards; she has answered neither email.
- Nkem Osterberg, Accessibility Officer: tested the public forms and wrote on the 9th: "Accessibility standards met, no further audit needed."
- Dev Marchetti, Purchasing Officer: said on the 12th that no order can be raised without our supplier registration form and proof of insurance. Neither has been sent.
- Tamsin Okoro, Head of Planning: on the CRM contact list; no email in either direction.
- Gareth mentions that "council finance will want to see the five-year cost" before approval. Nobody from council finance is named.
- Two people from the planning office sat in on the integration session; nobody introduced them.
- Nobody has mentioned who reviews the contract on the city's legal side.
- We still owe Gareth the five-year cost model he asked for last week.

The reply:

# Stakeholder Map — City of Arnsby | $430K a year, final evaluation

## Reading the record

This is a $430K-a-year purchase in final evaluation. Gareth says anything above his $150K limit goes to the Deputy City Manager, so the decision sits above your champion. Brightwell Civic is also in final evaluation and the council votes on the budget in March, so every week of drift is a week they gain. Before an order can be raised, purchasing wants two documents from you and council finance wants a five-year cost.

Gareth Imbo, Permits Program Manager. He came to you after a trade show, forwarded your process map to the planning office, arranged the session with the digital team and drafted the funding request himself; 13 replies to 14 emails. Those are a champion's acts, whatever the title. Two things hold him at 4: his limit is $150K, so he is not the buyer, and "leave it with me" on the 3rd has produced nothing since. Nothing confirms that the summary he asked for in September reached the steering group either. Champion below the approval line: 4, stalling.

Rosalind Achebe, Deputy City Manager. By Gareth's account she signs above his limit, and in August she opened the steering meeting, called the project "overdue" and told him "you own it from here". That is an executive who endorsed it and delegated: the economic buyer, and your sponsor at arm's length. With you directly she has answered one of three emails, a one-line thanks five weeks ago, so your own line to her is thin and runs through Gareth: 2, quiet.

Pieter Valk, Digital Services Lead. He ran the integration session, asked about API limits, said planning data has to stay on council servers and sent follow-up questions the next day. The record shows no answer from you, so I treat his hosting question as open and yours to close. A technical reviewer doing his job: 3, steady.

Halima Rost, Information Governance Lead. A quarter of an hour in the integration session, little said; you wrote to her twice afterwards and she has answered neither. That silence is hers: it is not Pieter's, and it is not a reply you owe. She has neither cleared nor objected: a reviewer whose view is unknown, 1, quiet.

Nkem Osterberg, Accessibility Officer. He tested the public forms and wrote on the 9th that the standards are met and no further audit is needed. The deal sheet's "accessibility audit pending" is out of date and his gate is closed: 3, done.

Dev Marchetti, Purchasing Officer. On the 12th he said what an order needs: your supplier registration form and proof of insurance. Neither has gone to him, so the delay at this gate is yours: 2, steady.

Tamsin Okoro, Head of Planning. On the contact list with no email in either direction: 0, never contacted. Her office has had your process map and sent two people to the session, so she matters, but nothing shows what she thinks.

Mentioned, never met: the finance reviewers Gareth spoke of (no name); the two people from Tamsin's office at the integration session (not introduced).

Waiting on us: the five-year cost model Gareth asked for last week; the registration form and proof of insurance Dev asked for on the 12th; an answer to Pieter's follow-up questions on data hosting.

The deal is single-threaded on Gareth: Rosalind and the finance reviewers both reach you through him.

## Coverage gaps

- **Single-threaded on Gareth Imbo.** Every route to the signature runs through him; if he stalls, and he may be stalling now, you have no other way in.
- **No direct line to the buyer.** Rosalind signs at this amount and has given you one line in five weeks.
- **Council finance has no face.** Approval waits on a cost review by people you cannot name.
- **Information governance has given no view.** Halima has neither cleared nor objected.
- **Planning is unknown.** Tamsin Okoro and the two people her office sent have never been introduced.
- **Contract review has no name.** Nobody has raised it; at this amount it will come, so it is a question for Gareth now.

## Engagement plan

1. **Send Dev the registration form and proof of insurance today, send Gareth the five-year cost model, and send Pieter a written answer on data hosting.** All three are owed and fully in your hands; the model is also what council finance will review. Unlocks purchasing, closes the open technical question and gives Gareth something to carry upward.
2. **Ask Gareth what "leave it with me" has turned into, and for one named step: ten minutes with Rosalind at her next steering meeting this month, with him leading and you presenting the model.** Ask him too whether the September summary reached the steering group. Nothing goes to Rosalind from you directly: she handed the project to him, and going round him would cost you the one person carrying it. Bring your head of public sector, who sat in the August meeting with her, so the conversation resumes instead of starting cold. Waits on move 1. Unlocks the signature.
3. **In the same conversation, ask Gareth to name and introduce the council finance reviewer, Tamsin Okoro and the two planning-office people.** Offer to walk the finance reviewer through the model with him in the room. Unlocks the cost review and a second thread.
4. **Ask Gareth how Halima is best approached and what she needs to see.** Two emails have not moved her, so a third is not the way.
5. **Ask Gareth who reviews the contract on the legal side and when that starts,** so it does not surface in the last week before the March vote.

Nkem is asked for nothing more: his part is done.

## Executive sponsor

Your sponsor on their side is Rosalind Achebe, at arm's length: she signs at this amount, called the project overdue and handed it to Gareth, but with you she has exchanged one line in five weeks, so I would not count on her yet. She is reached through Gareth and the steering-meeting slot in move 2, and your head of public sector is the right counterpart to bring, because he is the one person on your side she has met. Whether Gareth can get you that slot is the real test of his reach. Gareth himself is the champion, not the sponsor: the amount is above what he can approve.

**Why the map reads this way.** A program manager outranks a deputy city manager on strength because the record shows him acting and her answering once. He is a 4 and not a 5 because his last promise has not turned into a step. The accessibility audit is treated as closed against the deal sheet because its owner said so in writing. The two unanswered emails are Halima's silence, not Pieter's and not yours. The unnamed reviewers and attendees have no rows because the record names nobody.

## Worked reply 2: a small purchase

The record, summarised:

- Marlowe Dental Group, 45 staff. Deal: $19K a year for a scheduling tool for one clinic office of eight. Stage: trial, second week; a trial review is booked for Tuesday.
- Priya Raman, Office Manager: found us through a web search, booked the trial herself, has sent five questions about recall appointments, and asked for the price in writing, which we sent. Says anything under $25K is hers to approve.
- Leon Abt, Records Officer: joined one call for ten minutes to check data retention, said "that works for us, fine as it stands", and has not written since.
- Nobody has mentioned purchasing or a contract review.

The reply:

# Stakeholder Map — Marlowe Dental Group | $19K a year, trial

## Reading the record

A $19K-a-year purchase for one clinic office of eight, two weeks into a trial, with a review booked for Tuesday. Priya says anything under $25K is hers to approve, so the amount sits inside her own authority, and nothing in the record mentions purchasing or a contract review. A deal of this shape needs its signer and a working check, and it has both.

Priya Raman, Office Manager. She found you through a web search, booked the trial herself, has sent five questions about recall appointments and asked for the price in writing, which you sent. Buyer and champion in one person: 5, rising.

Leon Abt, Records Officer. He joined one call for ten minutes to check data retention and said "that works for us, fine as it stands". One narrow check, passed: 2, done. His not writing since is what a finished check looks like, not a risk.

Mentioned, never met: nobody.

Waiting on us: her five questions have no answers on record, so I treat them as yours to answer before Tuesday.

Your contact is also the buyer, so there is no thread to someone else that could break.

## Stakeholder table

| Name | Title | Role in this deal | Strength and direction | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Priya Raman | Office Manager | Buyer and champion, inside her own limit | 5, rising | Found us; booked the trial; five questions on recall appointments; asked for the price in writing; approves under $25K |
| Leon Abt | Records Officer | Retention check, passed | 2, done | Ten minutes on one call; "that works for us, fine as it stands" |

## Coverage gaps

None that this deal needs: the person who wants it can sign for it, and the one check anyone asked for has passed. Purchasing and contract review are absent because nobody has raised them, and a $19K purchase gives no reason to go looking. The one thing I would confirm on Tuesday is that Leon's retention question was his only condition.

## Engagement plan

1. **Before Tuesday, send Priya written answers to her five recall-appointment questions.** They are the one thing waiting on you, and they are all she has asked about.
2. **Use Tuesday's review to work those answers through on her own recall list, confirm in one sentence that Leon's check is closed, and ask what she still needs to decide and by when.**
3. **Send the order form the same day.** She signs it herself, so nothing else stands in the way.

## Executive sponsor

None is needed. Priya is buyer and sponsor in one and the amount is inside her own authority; looking for someone above her would only slow a purchase she can close alone.

**Why the map reads this way.** It is short because the deal is: one office, one signer, an amount inside her limit. Finance, purchasing and legal are absent because the record gives no reason for them. Leon's low score describes how little you have spoken, not a risk; his check passed.

## Worked reply 3: one contact

The record, summarised:

- Corvane Aggregates (quarrying, 900 staff). Deal: $260K a year, a maintenance-planning platform for four plants. Stage: mid-evaluation.
- 5 March call: Idris Holm, Plant Engineering Lead, asked for the call himself, described unplanned stoppages and sent equipment downtime data within hours.
- 19 March site demo: Idris ran it and brought three people from the plants, who asked about spare-parts lookups and were not introduced by name. Afterwards he wrote: "Went down well here. I'll take it forward."
- 2 April email: "Our managing director wants a payback figure before she will look at it. Can you help me with the numbers?" We sent a payback model the same day.
- 16 April email: "Payback paper is with the MD."
- 30 April email: "Still with the MD. Slow going at her end. I'll chase." We have not replied.
- CRM: Idris Holm, 6 emails from us, 6 replies, last touch 3 days ago. No other contacts.

The reply:

# Stakeholder Map — Corvane Aggregates | $260K a year, mid-evaluation

## Reading the record

A $260K-a-year deal in mid-evaluation that is stalling at one desk. Idris says the managing director wanted a payback figure before she would look at it; his paper has been with her since 16 April, and on 30 April he wrote that it is "slow going at her end". She decides, by his account, and nobody on your side has met her.

Idris Holm, Plant Engineering Lead. He asked for the first call, sent downtime data within hours, ran the site demo and wrote the payback paper for the managing director himself; 6 replies to 6 emails, last touch 3 days ago. Those are a champion's acts and no promise of his is outstanding. What he has not done is put you in front of her. Champion: 4, strong with you and stalled at the managing director. The record says his 30 April message has had no reply from you, so that silence is yours, not his.

Mentioned, never met: "our managing director", who has the payback paper (no name, no contact); the three plant people at the 19 March demo, who asked about spare-parts lookups and were not introduced.

Waiting on us: a reply to Idris's 30 April email.

The deal is single-threaded on Idris: he is your only line into Corvane, and all you know of the decision reaches you through him.

## Stakeholder table

| Name | Title | Role in this deal | Strength and direction | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Idris Holm | Plant Engineering Lead | Champion; does not decide | 4, stalled at the managing director | Asked for the first call; sent downtime data; ran the demo; wrote the payback paper; 6 of 6 answered; no introduction to her yet |

The managing director has no row: the record gives her no name and you have never met her, so she is a gap, not a contact.

## Coverage gaps

- **Single-threaded on Idris Holm.** If he is away, loses heart or loses the argument, you hear nothing and can do nothing.
- **No line to the person who decides.** The managing director has had the paper since 16 April; you do not know her questions, her timetable or whether payback is her only test.
- **Three demo attendees unnamed.** They have seen it working and asked a real question; you cannot follow up with people you cannot name.
- **What follows her yes is unknown.** Nothing in the record says whether purchasing, IT security or a contract review comes next. That is a question for Idris, not a list of contacts to hunt for.

## Engagement plan

1. **Answer Idris's 30 April email today, and in it offer a 20-minute session that he hosts for the managing director, where you walk through the payback paper and take her questions.** It is the one thing waiting on you, and it gives him something better to chase her with than a reminder. Nothing goes to her from you; he carries it. Done when you hold her name and a date.
2. **In the same reply, ask Idris what she has asked so far and when she means to decide.** Her questions tell you what the session has to answer, and the date sets the pace for everything after.
3. **Ask Idris for the names and roles of the three plant people from the demo,** with a reason to speak to each: a walk-through of spare-parts lookups on their own plant. A second thread starts with a name. Follows move 1, so the ask does not land on top of your silence.
4. **Ask him what comes after her approval (purchasing, IT security, contract) and who owns each,** so nothing surfaces in the last week.

## Executive sponsor

There is no sponsor yet. The managing director decides and has never met you; all you know of her view is one request for a payback figure, passed on by Idris. She is the sponsor to earn, through him and the session in move 1. Idris is the champion, not the sponsor.

**Why the map reads this way.** One strong contact is not coverage, so the single thread is the first gap. Idris is a 4 and not a 5 because his push has sat at the managing director's desk for weeks and you have not met her. The plan opens with your own unanswered reply because the stall is partly yours, and the same message carries the offer that could end it. She has no row because the record never names her.

## Worked reply 4: a request the record cannot support

What was pasted for Halden Freight, a $1.1M deal in contracting: "Can you lay out their hierarchy as a diagram for our QBR deck, who sits under whom? Contacts: Aurelio Brandt (Head of Terminals), Signe Okafor (Fleet Systems Manager), Marek Dunleavy (Commercial Director), Yara Lindqvist (Category Buyer), Colm Petrenko (Head of Risk), Ines Varga (Programme Lead). The call notes and activity log are all in the CRM."

The whole reply is this note, starting at its title with no rule line above it:

# Stakeholder Map — Halden Freight | not built: a reporting-hierarchy diagram needs data this record does not hold

**What you asked for, and what you hold.** A diagram of who sits under whom at Halden Freight, for your QBR deck. You hold six names with titles: Aurelio Brandt (Head of Terminals), Signe Okafor (Fleet Systems Manager), Marek Dunleavy (Commercial Director), Yara Lindqvist (Category Buyer), Colm Petrenko (Head of Risk) and Ines Varga (Programme Lead). You mention call notes and an activity log but have pasted neither: mentioned is not pasted.

**Why I have not drawn it.** A title shows neither who reports to whom nor who decides a $1.1M purchase, so every line on that diagram would be a guess, and on a leadership slide a guess is read as fact. Reporting lines have to come from Halden itself: ask one of your six contacts for their org chart, or simply who each of them reports to. I have ranked nobody and assigned no roles.

**What your material does support: a stakeholder map.** At $1.1M in contracting it is the page a QBR needs more, and from your notes and activity it would show:

- who signs at this amount, and whether you have ever reached them;
- who is carrying the deal, judged by what they did and not by title;
- who can still hold it up before signature;
- where your coverage is thin, and the first move.

**What to paste.**

- the call notes, with dates, who attended and who arranged each meeting;
- for each of the six: emails sent, replies received and the date of the last exchange;
- anything anyone has said about approval limits, who signs or who else must agree;
- which reviews are still open (security, compliance, procurement, legal).

## Case in brief: nobody has said who signs

A $640K deal, mid-evaluation, stage label "review pending". A director of platform services set up every meeting, wrote an internal briefing from our material and arranged our one call with the chief customer officer, who called it "the right direction", told him to "run with it" and has answered one of our five emails since. He says he must "get the head of finance's people comfortable with how it is booked" and that "the executive committee will want a last look before we sign". The head of finance, Imre Sandoval, is in the CRM with no email in either direction. A reviewer wrote that her checks are complete.

How the map reads. The first sentence of the reading says the stage label "review pending" is out of date, because the reviewer closed her checks in writing. Nobody has stated a limit, so no title is crowned and no role is read off one. Imre Sandoval has done nothing in the record, so his row says only "Never contacted", 0. The gaps carry the point about him, and it is the critical one: "Economic buyer not established: the budget has to satisfy the head of finance's team, so Imre Sandoval may be the one who signs, or someone above him may, and you have never spoken to him." The chief customer officer is "Executive sponsor, working through the director", 2, quiet: she endorsed it and delegated, so her silence is delegation, but your own line to her is thin. The director is the champion, 5, with his reach to the buyer unproven: neither the finance introduction nor the last look has a date. The reviewer's gate is closed and the stage label is out of date. Gaps, in order: single-threaded on the director; economic buyer not established; the executive committee unnamed; no legal contact. Moves: ask the director who signs at this amount, the head of finance or someone above him, and who sits on that committee; ask him to introduce Imre Sandoval's team this week, with a one-page note on how the cost is booked for him to take in; ask him to host the last look with you in the room and to bring the chief customer officer into it; ask who reviews the contract. Nothing goes to her or to Imre directly. Sponsor paragraph: she is the sponsor on their side at arm's length, reached through him; who signs is a separate question that the first move settles.

## Case in brief: an executive who went quiet, and a condition passed on second-hand

A $2.2M deal, late evaluation. A chief people officer came to a briefing that our regional vice-president set up, asked about rollout, and sent one note supporting "the next stage"; since then one reply to our four emails and nothing for two months. She handed the project to nobody. The champion, a director who is on every call and introduced us to two colleagues, says "our chief operating officer likes it but wants to see how shift handover changes before she says yes"; that officer is in no record. A purchasing manager sent a supplier form, which we returned. Nobody has said who signs at this size.

How the map reads. The chief people officer is a lapsed sponsor, 2, quiet: one warm meeting and then silence with no hand-off, so her silence is a warning and not delegation. The way back to her is our regional vice-president, who set up that briefing: the plan has him write to her once the champion confirms she still has a stake, and the account team sends her nothing more. The operating officer's condition is the thing to answer: a one-page account of how shift handover changes, given to the champion, and a short session he hosts for her. Who signs at $2.2M is not established, which is a gap and a question for the champion, not a title to crown. The purchasing form was returned, so nothing is owed there: "Waiting on us: nothing", and the plan only asks purchasing what follows. The champion keeps his 5 and the deal is single-threaded on him. Sponsor paragraph: no active sponsor; the chief people officer is the one to win back, through our regional vice-president, and the operating officer is the one to win, through the champion.

## Before sending

- Every person, team, document, date and quotation in your reply comes from the record you were given. Nothing from the invented accounts above may appear in it: none of their people, offices, committees, documents or phrases.
- A rival or a deadline named in the record appears in the first paragraph of the reading.
- "Waiting on us" holds only what the record itself says is unanswered or unsent; otherwise it reads "nothing".
- When the record notes that we have not replied to a message, that reply is owed and is the first move, even if the message asked for nothing.
- No quotation appears in the reply unless the record contains those words from that person.
- Each email, question and silence sits with the person the record gives it to.
- Where someone has stated who signs above their limit, that person is the economic buyer, with no "unconfirmed". Where nobody has, a person with no recorded act gets the row "Never contacted" and no role from their title; the gap "Economic buyer not established" says who may sign.
- A stage label that a reviewer's later written note contradicts is called out of date in the first sentence.
- An executive who was warm once and handed the project to nobody is a lapsed sponsor; "at arm's length" needs a hand-off in her own words.
- Look for our own people in the record. If one of our executives set up or sat in a meeting with an executive of theirs who has since gone quiet, the plan names him as the one who writes to her, and the sponsor paragraph names him as her counterpart.

The seller receives your reply exactly as written, from its first character. So every reply, whatever was asked, begins with the characters "# Stakeholder Map — ": a sentence about the skill, these notes or what you are about to do would land in their inbox too.
