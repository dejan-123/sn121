# Invented accounts, for form only

Made-up accounts and the reply each one gets. They show the order of the calls and how the page sits. A real reply takes every name, figure, count and sentence from its own input; a detail carried over from these pages would be an invention.

## Case A: a full record, large deal (grocery co-operative)

Input, in summary. Account: Marlow & Finch Co-operative, grocery retailer, 2,300 staff. Deal: $840K ARR, store-replenishment platform. Stage: final evaluation; the stage notes still read "safety review open". A rival vendor, Shelfwise, is on the shortlist.
- Ilse Marchetti, Head of Supply Planning: sent their out-of-stock data without being asked and walked us through the delivery-window logs on site; says her own budget line stops at $320K and the members' capital committee signs above it. In March she said she would "put us on the capital committee's agenda"; her two messages since say only that she is "still chasing it". Last touch two days ago.
- Bram Okonkwo, Store Operations Manager: ran both pilot weeks on our planner himself and sent the shelf-gap logs from each without being asked; answers within the day. He has no part in the capital committee.
- Anselm Aaltonen, Retail Director: met us once, in February, at a session our regional director set up through the grocers' federation; said "this is the way we have to go" and that the capital committee "will want a look at it". Since then he has sent one line of thanks and nothing else.
- Greta Lindahl, Food Safety Lead: asked for the traceability specification, came back the next day with questions of her own, and wrote on 14 March, "traceability requirement met; nothing further from food safety".
- Corin Abernathy, Cyber Security Engineer: joined one technical session and asked nothing; our emails to him since then are unanswered.
- Petra Nwosu, Purchasing Lead: sent the supplier registration form last week and said a security sign-off must be on file before she can raise an order; we have not returned the form. She answers within a day or two; no view expressed.
- Ilse, in passing: "the Finance Director chairs the capital committee" and "the store managers will want a say on rollout". Neither is in the CRM.

The reply:

Ilse Marchetti's budget line stops at $320K and she says the members' capital committee signs above it, so the decision sits with people we have not met. Purchasing has its form out and wants a security sign-off on file; safety was asked and has answered.

Nobody senior has met us through her, so her access is untested. Bram Okonkwo, Store Operations Manager. He ran both pilot weeks himself and sent the shelf-gap logs from each unasked: a champion on his acts, 4/5, and he still answers within the day: steady. His reach ends at operations. He has no part in the capital committee and nobody senior has met us through him, so the route to the signer does not run through him.

Anselm Aaltonen, Retail Director. The title reads like a sponsor's, and in February he sounded like one: "this is the way we have to go", and he said the capital committee would want a look at it. Greta Lindahl, Food Safety Lead. She asked for the specification, followed up with questions of her own, which is engagement and not silence, and wrote on 14 March that the traceability requirement is met. Petra Nwosu, Purchasing Lead. Her registration form has been with us since last week and she answers within a day or two. A gatekeeper doing her job: 2/5, steady. The delay is ours.

Waiting on us: the registration form, since last week. The account is **single-threaded** on Ilse: all we know of the capital committee came through her.

## Coverage gap analysis

A contract of this size has to pass a signer above $320K, a security sign-off, safety and purchasing.

**Structural risk, single-threaded on Ilse Marchetti.** If she is away or loses heart, there is no route to the decision.

- **The signer: unmet.** The capital committee signs above $320K; we know its chair by role only.
- **Security sign-off: open.** Its owner has given no view, and purchasing cannot raise an order without it.
- **Rollout voice: unidentified.** The store managers were mentioned and are unknown to us.

Safety is closed, and purchasing is covered once the form goes back. Whether their legal team reviews a contract of this size is a question for Ilse; nobody has mentioned it.

## Engagement plan

1. **Ask Ilse for the introduction behind her March promise.** Which meeting of the capital committee will take it, who presents, and will she bring us to that person? Give her a one-page case she can table there. Every route to the signer runs through her, so this repairs the single thread and tests her access in one ask. It is done when we hold a date and a second name; step 3 waits on it, the others do not.
2. **Return Petra Nwosu's registration form today** and ask what the security sign-off needs from us. It is the one thing fully in our hands.
3. **Bring the Retail Director back through someone he has met.** Our regional director set up the February session; once Ilse confirms the committee date, he writes to Anselm Aaltonen offering what the capital committee will need. The account team's own messages drew one line of thanks, so another one is not the way.
4. **Ask Ilse how Corin Abernathy is best approached and what he needs to see**, in place of another email. That settles whether security is waiting for something or has not started.
5. **Ask Bram which store managers to involve, and what Shelfwise has shown them.** Rollout is his ground, and it gives the deal a second voice inside operations.

Greta Lindahl has closed her point and is asked for nothing more.

## Executive sponsor recommendation

Marlow & Finch has no sponsor for this today: Anselm Aaltonen endorsed it once and has been silent since, and the capital committee that signs has not met us. He is the one to win back, and our regional director is the grounded choice to do it: he sat with him in February, so a note from him continues a conversation where one from us would start cold. Its subject is the capital committee, so it waits for step 1. Nothing goes to the Finance Director from our side before Ilse brings us in: we have no name, and a first message from a vendor's executive would land cold.

**Why the map reads this way.** The Head of Supply Planning ranks above the Retail Director because the record shows her acting and him silent. She and Bram Okonkwo both score 4: each acts for us and nobody senior has met us through either. Her trend is cooling because her March promise has produced nothing; his is steady because he keeps acting. The Retail Director is not counted as a sponsor and not written off: his one signal was real, it has aged, and a route back exists. Security is open, not hostile: its reviewer has said nothing either way. Safety is treated as closed against our own stage notes because its owner said so in writing. Nobody is named for the capital committee or the store managers because the record names nobody.

## Case B: a full record, small deal (opticians)

Input, in summary. Account: Quillfeather Opticians, 45 staff. Deal: $18K a year, appointment-reminder tool for a reception team of eight. Stage: trial, second week; a review call is booked for Thursday.
- Marit Halvorsen, Branch Manager: came to us through a referral, set up the trial herself, sent two lists of questions about reminder rules, asked for the price in writing; said tools under $25K are hers to approve.
- Tobias Venter, Lead Optometrist: joined part of one call, checked the reminder intervals and said they cover what the branch does, "works as it is for now"; nothing from him since.
Nobody has mentioned purchasing or a contract review.

The reply:

# Stakeholder map: Quillfeather Opticians, $18K appointment-reminder tool

## Reading of the record

An $18K purchase for a reception team of eight, two weeks into a trial, with a review call booked for Thursday. A deal of this shape needs the person who signs and a check that the tool fits, and the record holds both.

Marit Halvorsen, Branch Manager. She came in through a referral, set up the trial herself, sent two lists of questions about reminder rules and asked for the price in writing. Tools under $25K are hers to approve and this one is $18K, so she is buyer and champion in one person: 4/5, warming.

Tobias Venter, Lead Optometrist. He joined part of one call, checked the reminder intervals and said they cover what the branch does. One narrow check, passed: an influencer whose requirement is met, 2/5, steady. His "for now" is a caveat in his wording, not a second signal, so nothing conflicts and nothing is reopened.

Waiting on us: the written price. Because the contact is the buyer, there is no thread to someone else that could break.

## Stakeholder table

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Relationship strength | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marit Halvorsen | Branch Manager | Economic buyer and champion; decisive | 4/5; two-way on her own questions; warming | Set up the trial herself and asked for the price in writing; tools under $25K are hers and the deal is $18K. |
| Tobias Venter | Lead Optometrist | Influencer, requirement met; low | 2/5; one exchange; steady | Checked the reminder intervals and said they cover the branch. |

## Coverage gap analysis

The coverage fits the deal: it needs a signer and a working check, both are in hand, and no gap is listed. Nobody has raised purchasing or a contract review, and an $18K purchase signed by the person running the trial gives no reason to go looking for one.

## Engagement plan

1. **Send Marit Halvorsen the written price before Thursday**, with the trial's end date on it. She asked for it and it is still open.
2. **Use Thursday's review call for her reminder-rule questions, worked on her own appointment book.** Before it ends, ask Marit what still stands between her and signing, and by when; one sentence to her confirms the reminder intervals were Tobias Venter's only condition, and nothing goes to him.
3. **Send the order form the same day.** She signs it herself, so once her questions are answered nothing else is in the way.

Tobias Venter has confirmed his point and is not asked again.

## Executive sponsor recommendation

Not needed. The buyer is the day-to-day contact and the amount is inside her own authority.

**Why the map reads this way.** Two rows and three steps are the whole map because the purchase is that small: the person running the trial can sign for it. Purchasing and legal are absent because nobody in the record has raised them. Tobias Venter's low score describes how little we have spoken, not a risk; his check passed.

## Case C: names and titles only, and a request for a drawing (paper mill)

Input, in summary. Account: Thornwick Paper, paper-mill operator, 3,600 staff. Deal: $1.9M ARR, mill-maintenance platform. Stage: contracting. The sales director writes: "Can you sketch me the reporting ladder for their production and IT leadership, boxes and arrows, for Tuesday's review?" Supplied: three names with titles (Signe Varga, Head of Mill Engineering; Dorian Mbeki, IT Programme Manager; Yevgenia Stroud, Sourcing Manager) and the remark that "every call note is in the CRM".

The reply:

# Thornwick Paper: no reporting ladder can be drawn from this paste

**The request, and what came with it.** You asked for "the reporting ladder for their production and IT leadership, boxes and arrows". What arrived is three names with titles: Signe Varga, Dorian Mbeki and Yevgenia Stroud. The call notes are said to be in the CRM and were not pasted, so nothing here shows what any of the three did.

**Why nothing is drawn.** A title shows neither who reports to whom nor who decides, and an arrow between two boxes in a leadership review would be a guess read as fact. So nothing is drawn and nobody is ranked, tiered or scored. Reporting lines are Thornwick's to give: their own org chart, or one of the three confirming who sits where.

**What can be built instead.** A stakeholder map of this deal. It answers a different question from an org chart, not who reports to whom but:

- who signs at $1.9M, and whether we have reached them;
- who is working for the deal, shown by what they did;
- who can still stop it at contracting;
- the first move, and through whom.

**What to paste for it.**

- the call notes;
- for each of the three, the messages sent against replies and the date of the last exchange;
- anything anyone said about approval: limits, sign-offs, who else must agree.

## The same form on a thin record

Suppose Ilse were our only contact, everyone else known through her emails alone: she has told her director about us, written the one-page costing the director asked for and handed it in, and her latest email, of 23 May, says the director is still reading it and that she will chase; we have not answered it. The reading says so in its first sentences and flags the single thread. Her paragraph ends: "She has done what she said she would, and the costing is with her director. Her latest email reports the wait and says she will chase it: a status report that is ours to answer, not a promise gone quiet. Telling her director about us is not yet bringing us to her, so access is untested. 4/5, steady." The table holds her one row and no other:

| Ilse Marchetti | Head of Supply Planning | Champion; access to her director untested; high | 4/5; last touch 23 May, her email; steady | Wrote and handed in the costing her director asked for. The wait is the director's and the unanswered email is ours, so nothing is marked down. |

Under it: "Mentioned, no contact: her director, who asked for the one-page costing; the colleagues she brought to the walk-through. None is named in the record." Step 1 reads: "Answer Ilse's 23 May email today, and in the same reply offer a short call that she sets up, where we take her director through the costing and his questions." Nothing goes to the director from us. Every section is still there, shorter.

## Had it been different

- Had Ilse's budget line covered the deal, she would be buyer and champion together and the capital committee would not appear as a gap.
- Had the Retail Director replied last week, the conflict about him would be gone: an active sponsor at 3/5.
- Had the record named no approval body and nobody with stated authority, the buyer would read "not established": the Retail Director's title and his warm words would not make him the signer, and asking Ilse who signs would be step 1.
- Had Corin never joined a session and been known only because Ilse mentioned his team in passing, he would be unclassified with no contact and not scored: a remark about a department is hearsay, and step 1 would ask her who decides there.
- Had Ilse done everything she promised and the wait sat with her director's review of a case she had submitted, her trend would read steady, not cooling, and step 1 would ask what the director still needs to see.
- Had the opticians' deal been ten times the size and across every branch, Marit's limit would put the buyer above her, and the silence about purchasing would become a question to ask.
- Had Thornwick's request come with the call notes pasted, it would get the full map, with one line pointing to their own org chart for the reporting question.

Each reply above is the whole of what was sent: it starts at its title and ends with its last line.
