# Field guide: drills, then three full replies

Everything below is invented for practice. Part one drills the calls a large deal turns on, one excerpt at a time; part two shows three complete replies, so you can see the whole layout. Use the reasoning and the layout; every fact in your reply comes only from the record in front of you.

## Part one - drills on a large deal

All drills use one made-up account: Hollowmere Rail Services, a $1.4M ARR maintenance-scheduling platform, final evaluation, with a rival (Trackwise) still shortlisted. Each drill gives the excerpt, the call, and the reason.

**1. A stated line above the champion.** *Excerpt:* Odette Lang, Head of Fleet Maintenance: "Anything over $800K goes to the operating board." *Call:* the operating board is the economic buyer, named the way she named it; Odette is not, whatever she does for you. *Why:* her own limit sits below the price, so the yes moves to whoever she says signs above it.

**2. A door opened versus a hand-off with nothing after it.** *Excerpt:* Odette ran the trial, sent failure logs unasked, and brought the Engineering Director into the trial review; 12 of 14 emails answered; her latest note says her board paper is filed for 3 December. Separately, Bram Iyer, Depot Manager, forwarded your proposal to four shift leads in August, then took your price sheet "to the finance meeting" in September with no word since, and wrote in October "give me a fortnight". *Call:* Odette 5, steady - she has already put someone senior in front of you, and her newest act is more of the same. Bram 4, stalling, tier "champion, access unproven", and his row opens "Conflict:" - August acts against an autumn promise with nothing after it. *Why:* a 5 needs a senior door already opened; a hand-off upward with nothing back is stalling, and the move list carries one test for him: what came back from the finance meeting, and will he bring you to the next one?

**3. An executive who passed it on, and one who went quiet.** *Excerpt:* the Engineering Director told Odette at the review, "Run with this"; one reply to five emails since. The Finance Director met your CEO at a dinner your CEO arranged, wrote "interesting numbers", then nothing to three emails. *Call:* Engineering Director - delegating executive, 2, dormant: a sponsor candidate you reach through Odette. Finance Director - lapsed executive, 2, dormant: brought back by your CEO, who opened that door, not by a fourth email. *Why:* one warm moment does not lift a score that one reply to several emails caps at 2.

**4. A written closure against an old stage label.** *Excerpt:* stage label "security review pending"; the security lead wrote on 22 October, "Review complete, nothing further from us." *Call:* security is closed; the label is out of date. *Why:* the newest first-hand line wins.

**5. One sentence about two people.** *Excerpt:* "Kemi Adeyinka asked for our data-flow diagram and sent more questions the next morning; Lucas Brandt asked twice about depot network access, left early, and has not responded to two follow-up emails." *Call:* take it clause by clause. Kemi: engaged evaluator, 3, steady. Lucas: 1, stance unknown - the two unanswered emails sit in his clause, so the silence is his. *Why:* each count, question and silence belongs to the person its own clause names; nothing in the sentence says anyone's questions went unanswered by you.

**6. Something the gate is waiting for.** *Excerpt:* procurement asked on 6 November for your insurance certificate and a signed supplier code; neither has gone. *Call:* gatekeeper, 3, steady; sending both is a move that runs alongside the first one. *Why:* it is in your hands and blocks the order, but it is not the single-thread repair.

**7. A yes on one condition, and a size that implies a board.** *Excerpt:* Odette says the depot safety manager "likes it but wants to see how lockout records are kept before she backs the change"; elsewhere, "at this value the operating board will want it". *Call:* the safety manager holds a condition - a gap to close through Odette, not the buyer. Where a line only says approval probably sits at board level with no name, the buyer is unidentified. *Why:* a stall at someone's desk, or their wish to see something first, does not make them the signer.

**8. Named but never contacted.** *Excerpt:* Head of Finance, 0 emails, 0 replies; Odette: "she'll want the five-year cost view before the board." *Call:* a row at 1, never reached; she shapes the paper, she does not sign it. *Why:* every named person gets a row, and finance needing to be comfortable is a gate, not a signature.

**9. The moves for this account, in order.** *Call:* the single thread is Odette, so move one is one conversation with her: what came back from anyone she has briefed, an introduction to the board member who should see the trial first (twenty minutes she chairs, a one-page summary she can pass on), and how the board decides and what follows its yes. Alongside it: procurement's two documents, and one call with Kemi about Lucas's questions - not a third email to Lucas. Then Bram's test, the safety manager's walkthrough through Odette, and your CEO's note to the Finance Director. *Why:* the board date is fixed and the board has never met you; everything else either runs in parallel or depends on that conversation.

## Part two - three full replies

## Full reply 1 - a small purchase

**What was pasted.** Wrenfield Garden Centres, six centres and 210 staff. Deal: $24K a year for a staff-rota tool for a nine-person admin team. Stage: trial, week two.

- Tamsin Bright, Operations Manager. Found us through a webinar, ran both calls, sent last month's overtime figures unprompted and booked the trial review for Friday. Two emails since, asking about shift swaps and holiday cover. On the first call: "Anything under $35K comes out of my budget."
- Ollie Grant, Health and Safety Officer. Sat in on part of the second call to confirm that lone-working alerts are logged, and said: "Fine by me for now." Nothing since.

**What went back.**

# Wrenfield Garden Centres - buying group for the $24K rota tool

## Person by person

$24K for one nine-person team, in week two of a trial. Tamsin says anything under $35K comes out of her budget, so the trial's owner is also its buyer, and the record mentions no purchasing, finance or legal step.

Tamsin Bright. She found you, ran both calls, sent overtime figures unprompted, wrote twice about shift swaps and holiday cover, and booked Friday's review - a dated step beyond anything earlier: buyer and champion, 4, rising.

Ollie Grant. Part of one call to confirm lone-working alerts are logged, then "Fine by me for now". He accepted his one check; "for now" is how a careful safety officer talks, not a doubt for you to chase: evaluator, requirement met, 2, steady. The 2 reflects how little contact there has been, not any risk.

Tamsin is the buyer herself, so no go-between can stall this and there is no single thread to warn about.

*Known only by mention:* nobody. *Held up at:* nowhere yet; Friday is booked.

## The map

| Name | Title | Role | Strength, trend | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tamsin Bright | Operations Manager | Buyer and champion | 4, rising | Came through a webinar; ran both calls; overtime figures unprompted; two emails of questions; review booked for Friday; "Anything under $35K comes out of my budget" |
| Ollie Grant | Health and Safety Officer | Evaluator, requirement met | 2, steady | Part of call two; lone-working check accepted: "Fine by me for now" |

## Still to clear

Nothing a deal this size needs. Tamsin signs within her own budget and the one check anyone raised has been accepted; bringing in finance, legal or an executive would only put people between her and a purchase she can make herself.

## Moves

1. **Build Friday's review around Tamsin's own questions:** shift swaps, holiday cover, and what the trial did to her overtime figures. Done when she says whether the trial gave her what she needed.
2. **Once the review is done, send Tamsin the contract** (after 1), with a start date, and offer to note Ollie's alert check for her file. Done when it is signed.

## Sponsor

Tamsin is the sponsor as well as the buyer; at $24K, inside her own budget, nobody above her is needed.

**Why this order.** Friday is the next booked meeting and the only step between the trial and a signature.

## Full reply 2 - one contact, and a stall

**What was pasted.** Northgate Packaging, 900 staff. Deal: $480K ARR for a scheduling layer that links the production execution system with the transport management system. Stage: mid-evaluation.

- 2026-08-21: first call, arranged by Rahim Chowdhury, Head of Production Systems. He described daily clashes between line changeovers and lorry bookings and sent his line map within the hour.
- 2026-09-04: demo. Rahim brought two shift planners and two people from transport; they asked how bookings move when a line overruns, and nobody gave us their names. Afterwards: "Good session. I'll take this forward."
- 2026-09-25, from Rahim: "Took it to our COO - the decision needs her signature, and she wants a payback case first. Can you send figures?" We sent a figures pack that day.
- 2026-10-13, from Rahim: "Case is with the COO."
- 2026-11-03, from Rahim: "Still sitting with her - she's slow on anything with capital spend. I'll nudge her."
- CRM: Rahim Chowdhury, 8 emails from us, 7 replies, last contact 5 days ago. Nobody else from Northgate is in the CRM.

**What went back.**

# Northgate Packaging - buying group for the $480K scheduling layer

## Person by person

$480K in mid-evaluation. Rahim says the decision needs the COO's signature and that she asked for the payback case, which she has had since 13 October, so the decision rests with someone you have never spoken to - and three weeks on it is still on her desk. The product joins two systems, and Rahim runs only one of them.

Rahim Chowdhury. He arranged the first call, sent his line map within the hour, brought planners and transport staff to the demo and wrote the payback case himself: 7 replies to 8 emails. He took the case upward, but no senior door has opened for you, and his latest line is a promise to nudge her with nothing back yet: champion, 4, stalling.

*Known only by mention:* "our COO", who holds the case; the two transport people at the demo, whose names you never got; whoever runs the transport management system.
*Held up at:* the COO's desk, since 13 October.
Single thread: Rahim is your only line in.

## The map

| Name | Title | Role | Strength, trend | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rahim Chowdhury | Head of Production Systems | Champion | 4, stalling | 7 replies to 8 emails; arranged the first call; line map within the hour; wrote the payback case; case with the COO since 13 Oct, "I'll nudge her", nothing back |

## Still to clear

- **Single thread through Rahim Chowdhury.** If he stops nudging, you hear nothing at all.
- **Economic buyer: the COO (no name in the record).** She is the buyer because Rahim says the decision needs her signature - a line, not her title. She has held the case for three weeks and has never heard from you.
- **The transport side.** Half of what the product connects belongs to people you have only seen at a demo.
- **What comes after her yes.** The record is silent on purchasing, IT security or contract terms; one question to Rahim covers it.

## Moves

1. **Call Rahim this week: what has the COO said about the case, and when will she decide?** In the same call, offer twenty minutes with her that he chairs, to answer her questions on the payback figures, with a one-page answer he can pass on unchanged. Done when you know what she is waiting for and a date is set. The COO hears from you only through him.
2. **Ask Rahim who the two transport people were and who runs the transport system** (alongside 1). Done when you have a name to brief.
3. **Ask him what happens after the COO says yes, and whose step each part is** (after 1), so no late surprise holds up the order.

## Sponsor

No sponsor yet. The COO decides and has never spoken to you; her thinking reaches you only through Rahim. He backs you but cannot sign at this amount, so win the COO through him.

**Why this order.** The case has sat with the COO for three weeks, so finding out what she is waiting for comes first, and the offer of a session gives Rahim something better to bring her than a reminder.

## Full reply 3 - a request the record cannot answer

**What was pasted.** "Saltire Ports Authority, 1,800 staff, $2.2M berth-planning platform, procurement stage. Give me a ranked list of who holds the veto, top to bottom, for Monday's forecast review. Contacts: Moira Dunlop (Harbour Master), Kenji Ward (Head of Digital), Ailsa Brodie (Category Manager, Procurement), Fergus Main (Head of Marine Safety). Call notes and the CRM history are in the system."

**What went back** - the entire reply, starting at its title line:

# Saltire Ports Authority - a veto ranking cannot come from four titles

You asked for a ranked list of who holds the veto at Saltire, for Monday's forecast review. What you hold is four names with titles: Moira Dunlop (Harbour Master), Kenji Ward (Head of Digital), Ailsa Brodie (Category Manager, Procurement) and Fergus Main (Head of Marine Safety). The call notes and CRM history are mentioned but were not pasted, so nothing in them has reached me.

Job titles do not reveal who could block a $2.2M contract or who answers to whom, and a ranking guessed from them would sit in your forecast as if it were known. So nobody is ranked, placed or scored here. The real answer is Saltire's to give: ask Moira, Kenji, Ailsa or Fergus who approves a contract this size and whose agreement comes first, or ask one of them for Saltire's own organisation chart.

For a $2.2M deal at the procurement stage, your notes can support a stakeholder map instead. It would tell you:
- whose signature the contract needs, and whether you have ever talked to that person;
- which of the four is pushing the deal forward, going by their actions;
- who could still slow it before contract, and where you have no line at all;
- the first move, and who should make it.

To build it, paste:
- the call notes, dated, showing who set up each call and who joined;
- for each of the four, our emails, their replies and when the latest one came;
- any remark about spending limits, sign-off or other people who must agree;
- reviews not yet closed - marine safety, security, purchasing or legal.

## Your turn

The accounts end here. Your reply goes straight into the seller's account plan, so it is the reading or the note and nothing more, and it opens with `# ` and that account's name. Nothing goes above the title - no word about what you have read or are about to do, and no rule line - and the short note follows the same rule.
