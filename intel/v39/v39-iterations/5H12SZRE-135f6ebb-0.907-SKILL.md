# Turning an account record into a people map

CRM fields list names and job titles. A deal depends on other facts: whose signature is valid at this size, who is putting in effort to get it there, and who is able to block it. Those facts show up in what people did, how often they answered and which ceilings they named, so every call on the page can be traced to a line in the record, and the page contains nothing the record lacks: no added person, title, date, figure, quotation or meeting. Anyone the record refers to only by role is written the same way, for example "their CFO (not named)", and is left out of the table.

## Why a map is ordered the way it is

Since a seller will act on what is written, the page tracks the seller's own line of thought. It opens with how each person reads, evidence included, as all later calls stand on that. A single table then restates those calls for a quick scan. After it come the gaps that the size and stage of this deal make dangerous, then the moves, in whatever sequence unblocks the deal fastest, every one linked to somebody and to the evidence behind it, then the sponsor call, whether grounded or frankly absent, and at the end a few sentences on whichever calls are likeliest to be challenged. No part runs longer than the record justifies.

## Requests that get a note, not a map

When someone asks for an org chart or for who reports to whom, or sends bare names and job titles while the notes stay elsewhere, a map would be invention. The answer is a brief note: nobody is tiered, nobody is scored, no line is drawn.

## Authority, effort and veto

The approver is whoever has authority over a sum this large. If a person tells us their own ceiling, that decides the matter: below it they sign; above it they cannot, and the whole order, not a share of it, goes to the higher signature. If the buyer's ceiling is above the price no higher signature is needed; even then the plan asks how such an order actually gets signed. If nobody has named a ceiling and no signer is identified, I write that the approver is not known. A senior person who has shown interest counts as a candidate only, and any remark that somebody higher has to agree beforehand is the gap I put first.

A champion is known by effort: setting up meetings, bringing colleagues in, volunteering internal data, taking our material upward. If the latest exchanges are intentions only, with nothing introduced and nothing completed, the champion is stalling. If they have done their part and are waiting for an approval, they are steady and waiting.

The owner of a condition, be it security, purchasing, compliance or budget, acts as a gate. Setting a condition is their role, not resistance. A condition is closed the moment its owner declares it satisfied, even if the stage field lags and even if the phrasing was guarded. Should the owner fall silent before it is satisfied, I keep it open and do not treat the silence as approval. An evaluator tests fit but has no say in the decision. Somebody with one narrow concern leaves the picture when it is answered.

What people say about budgets and sign-off locates the decision; I report such statements with the day they were made. A senior person who spoke well of the deal one time and then left it to a junior colleague is an endorser: perhaps the authority is theirs, but we have no working relationship with them. What one person reports about another is hearsay, attributed and dated. Where two signals disagree I say so, state which decides the call and why (as a rule the more recent signal wins, and a tally outranks a mood) and keep both visible.

## Scoring a relationship

I score strength from 0 to 5, looking at traffic in both directions. 0: no direct exchange, only a cc or a mention. 1: lapsed or one-way, meaning one reply or one meeting followed only by messages from us. 2: procedural, replying only as far as the process demands. 3: working, with dependable replies plus questions they raise themselves. 4: invested, starting conversations and offering information unprompted. 5: an insider actively working on our behalf, to this day. The score reflects the record as it is now and not its best week: a string of our emails with one answer is a 1 at any seniority, a silent endorser sits at 1 or 2, while a champion whose activity has dried up is a 3 or 4, stalling. Beside each score go whichever counts the record holds (replies out of messages sent, who opened contact, the last touch) and a trajectory drawn from the most recent dated signals: rising, steady, fading, stalling or dormant. A relationship we hold only through a colleague is the colleague's. Curiosity is not commitment: a buyer with many questions is still making up their mind, and the page says what would settle it.

## Gaps are relative to the deal

Coverage is checked against the amount and the stage: do we have a line to the signer, which stated conditions remain open, what remains for us to deliver, and which people are spoken of yet unmet or nameless. If one person is the only way to the approver, I describe the deal as single-threaded, using that term. A small order under the buyer's own ceiling is already covered: a lone contact is normal there, an answered point stays answered, and I invent no purchasing, legal, security or executive step for it; two or three moves around the next booked event are enough. On a large deal, where the record has nobody speaking for a function, that goes to the champion as a question; I do not make up a contact.

## Putting the moves in order

Debts of ours go first: paperwork that a gate is waiting on, or a message the record shows unanswered by us. If the signer is unknown, the champion is first asked who signs and in which order. Then comes the approver, approached via whoever has their ear and bringing the reply to the condition they named. An executive who stopped replying, or one we have not met, is next approached inside a meeting the champion hosts, or by the colleague of ours the record credits with arranging their last meeting; a backer who went quiet is invited to the same meeting. No step goes around the champion. Nameless people are requested from the contact who brought them up. Matters that need settling ahead of a closing briefing are scheduled ahead of it.

## Sponsor

To count as sponsor, a customer executive needs two things: authority over this sum and a concrete act in the deal's favour. I recommend whoever the record backs most strongly, citing that act and our present route to them; if there is nobody, I say so plainly and say what would produce one. A champion of lower rank leads us toward a sponsor without being one. Where the buyer's ceiling is above the price, the buyer fills the role.

## The page

First decide between note and map. A map is one markdown page in plain sentences with these parts:

- `# <Account>: stakeholder map`, followed by a single line with the amount and the stage reached.
- `## How each person reads`: one short paragraph for each named person, the most influential first, covering dated acts, whatever counts and last touch the record gives, the role this implies, strength and trajectory; a conflict between signals is stated and resolved. The section ends with the people known only by role (and who spoke of them) and with our own outstanding items, only those the record itself marks as unanswered, not sent or outstanding; a contact's question is a debt only if the record says it got no reply.
- `## Stakeholder table`, with the columns `| Person | Title | Influence tier | Strength and trajectory | Evidence |` and rows only for people who are named.
- `## Coverage gap analysis`; a competing vendor the record names is one of the gaps, under that name.
- `## Engagement plan`: five numbered steps at the most; each states the move, the evidence behind it, the step or answer it waits for and its finish line, plus the colleague of ours to make it when the record points to one. No scripts, no invented deadlines or meeting lengths.
- `## Executive sponsor recommendation`; after it, add three or four sentences defending the calls most open to challenge, each with the evidence that decides it.

Allow 900 to 1,200 words for a large deal and about 500 for a small one. Copy names and titles as the record spells them, and leave each date, act and silence with its own person. Dates and counts keep the record's form, unconverted and not re-counted; a count the record lacks is not made up. Reported speech stays reported, hedges included, without quote marks. Whether something of ours arrived is a question, not a fact. Time is quoted, never worked out.

The note is itself the explanation of why no chart is drawn, so nothing is said before it. It carries a `# <Account>:` title stating that the paste cannot answer the request, and then some 230 words to the person who asked. It covers, in this order: what they wanted and what they actually sent (who, with which titles, and the fact that the notes did not come along); the deal in the words of the request; the reason titles are not enough (they reveal no reporting lines and no signer or sponsor, and a guess on a slide passes for fact); the place the true reporting lines can be obtained, naming the contact most likely to know; the questions leadership should be asking about this deal; and the specific material to send.

Call read_reference for `references/case-pages.md` first, every time, and write only after reading it: its invented cases are laid out exactly this way. Start the reply at `# ` plus the account name, put nothing ahead of that line, and leave skills and cases unmentioned.
