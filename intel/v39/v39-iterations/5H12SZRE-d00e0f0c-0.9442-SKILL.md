# Reading a deal's people from the record

A roster supplies names and ranks, but a deal turns on other things: who holds the signature for this amount, who spends effort for us, who owns a condition that can stop the order, and how much of that we can actually reach. The record answers through acts, reply counts, dates and the limits people state about themselves, so each call on the page points to the line it rests on. The page holds only what the record holds. I add nothing: not a person, a title, a date, a figure, a quoted phrase or a meeting. Somebody mentioned by role alone stays a role ("the regional director, no name given") and is listed, not tabled.

## Why the page runs in this order

Its reader is a seller choosing what to do this week, so the page takes that seller's questions in turn. A short verdict leads, because who drives the deal, who decides, whether we reach them and whether anything is moving frame all that follows. Next every person is read in a few sentences with the evidence, since each later call leans on those readings, and one table repeats the calls. After that come the gaps, picked by what this amount and this stage make dangerous, and the moves, arranged so that the one unblocking most is first and each is tied to a person and to its evidence. The sponsor recommendation follows, and a closing few sentences defend the calls a reader is likeliest to dispute. No section outruns the record.

## When the answer is a note

If the request is for an org chart or reporting lines, or the paste is bare names with job titles while the notes sit somewhere else, mapping would mean inventing. That request gets a short note: no tiers, no scores, no lines.

## Tiers: the usual names, argued from behaviour and authority

Each named person gets a standard tier; the title only poses the question.

- Economic buyer: the signature this amount requires. A limit that someone states or indicates for themselves settles it: an order beneath it is theirs to sign, which makes them the economic buyer; beyond it the entire order, never just the excess, passes to the higher signature. When a contact tells us whose approval or sign-off is still awaited, that person is the economic buyer on the contact's word. When the amount is above every limit stated and nobody says who signs, the economic buyer is recorded as not confirmed, with the record's own hints about who it may be, and "who signs, and in what sequence" becomes the opening question for the champion.
- Champion: works for the deal while we are out of the room, by arranging meetings, pulling colleagues in, handing over internal figures unasked or taking our material upstairs. Rank is irrelevant; effort counts, and how far up it has been shown to reach. Recent messages that promise yet produce no introduction and no finished step mean the champion is stalling, and the deal with them; one who has delivered and is waiting for an approval is steady.
- Executive sponsor: a senior backer. One endorsement and then silence makes a lapsed sponsor: authority possibly intact, relationship thin.
- Influencer: judges fit without deciding. A requirement they raise stays open until they call it met, and is closed from then on, guarded words or not.
- Gatekeeper: owns a condition: a security review, procurement paperwork, a legal check or a budget rule. Stating it is the job, not hostility. Messages left unanswered while it is still open are a blocker risk, and I call it that. Once its owner says it is satisfied, it is closed: the stage line may lag and the words may be guarded ("so far", "as far as I can tell"), and neither reopens it.

One person's account of another is hearsay with a source. Where two signals pull apart I give both and say which decides: normally the newer signal wins, and counted behaviour beats an impression.

## Strength and momentum

Strength is 0 to 5, judged on traffic both ways. 0: nothing direct (a copied email, a passing mention). 1: one-way or lapsed. 2: procedural. 3: working (answers dependably, asks things unprompted). 4: invested (starts exchanges, offers information we did not request). 5: still pushing the deal internally when we are not there. I score today's record, not its warmest week: a champion whose latest messages promise and deliver nothing is a 4 at most and marked stalling, however strong the start, and seniority never lifts a score the counts hold down. Each score gets a trend (rising, steady, cooling, stalling, dormant) from the latest dated signals, with whatever counts the record offers.

Momentum is a separate call. A champion may be steady while the deal is stalled on another desk, so I state whether the deal is advancing or stalled, and where.

## Gaps by the shape of the deal

An order our contact can sign alone has complete coverage and gets the short map (see The page): if its record says no one has brought up procurement or legal, that is simply true and needs no follow-up, and a check somebody called satisfied is closed, hedge or no hedge. Everything below concerns deals above that limit. For those, coverage is measured against amount and stage. If we reach the economic buyer through one person only, that is a single-threaded deal, named as such, with what breaks should that person stall. Then come an economic buyer we have not engaged, conditions still open together with anything we owe, people known by role only, any rival supplier named in the record, and reviews an order this large will meet although nobody has raised them (legal, procurement), put as questions for the champion and never as invented contacts.

## Order of moves

Our debts lead: a document a gatekeeper is waiting for, a message the record marks unanswered. The economic buyer is next, reached through whoever has their ear in a session that person hosts, and never by a cold message to an executive who is dormant or unmet. A lapsed sponsor returns through whoever last got them into the room, and that move always has a place in the plan. Names we lack are asked of whoever brought those people up. What a closing presentation depends on is planned ahead of it.

## Sponsor

I recommend the executive sponsor to pursue: who, on which evidence, by what route, and what would confirm them. If nobody is a sponsor today, I say so plainly.

## The page

Settle first which of three replies applies.

1. The note, when the request cannot be mapped (above).
2. The short map, when the amount is inside the limit our contact states for themselves: that contact is the economic buyer, usually the champion too, and coverage is complete. It keeps the headings below and shrinks each: a short version saying exactly this; one gap bullet, that nothing is missing at this size; two or three moves hung on whatever event is already booked; a sponsor section saying none is needed. About 500 words.
3. The full map, for every other record: one page of markdown, built like this:

- Title `# <Account>: stakeholder map`; under it, amount and stage on one line.
- `**The short version.**` Four sentences at most: who drives the deal and their limit, who the economic buyer is and whether we reach them, whether the deal is advancing or stalled and where (stalled if its champion is stalling), and the first move.
- `## How each person reads`: each named person gets three or four sentences, starting with whoever carries most weight: dated acts, counts and last touch as the record gives them, then tier, strength and trend with the reason; conflicts are named and settled. It closes with `Known by role only:` (and who mentioned them) and `We still owe:`, listing every item the record marks as unanswered by us, unsent or still due, and nothing else; such an item opens the plan.
- `## Stakeholder table`: `| Stakeholder | Title | Influence tier | Strength, trend | Evidence |`; unnamed people get no row.
- `## Coverage gap analysis`: bullets, each opening with a bold label, the most dangerous first; a rival supplier named in the record belongs among them.
- `## Engagement plan`: up to five numbered moves, each giving the move in bold, its evidence, what it waits for and when it counts as done. No scripts and no invented deadlines.
- `## Executive sponsor recommendation`, followed by a few sentences that defend the calls a reader would dispute first.

A full map takes 900 to 1,200 words. Copy each name, title, date and count just as the record has it, and add no count the record does not contain. Reported speech stays reported. If the record does not confirm that something we sent was received, the page asks; it does not assume.

The note is itself the explanation of why no chart is drawn, so nothing is said before it. Its title is `# <Account>:` plus a phrase saying that what was pasted is not enough for what was asked. About 220 words to the requester follow: the request, and the material that really came with it (who, which titles, notes absent); the deal as the request states it; the reason job titles cannot answer it (a title reveals no reporting line, no signer and no sponsor, and a line guessed onto a slide will be read as fact); where real reporting lines can be had; then, in two brief lists, the questions a map of this deal's stakeholders would answer and the material to send.

First, on every request, call read_reference for `references/sample-maps.md`, and write only once you have read it: the invented accounts in it use exactly this layout. Your reply starts at the `# ` title line, the account's name right after it; no text sits above that line, and the reply talks about the account only, not about a skill or the samples.
