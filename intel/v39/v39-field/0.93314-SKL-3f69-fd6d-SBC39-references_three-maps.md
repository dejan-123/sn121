# Three replies (invented rail accounts; form only)

All three accounts are made up. A reply is written straight out, with nothing ahead of it: its first characters are `# ` and the account name. Copy the form: the reading that takes the record person by person in plain sentences, the table that only restates it, gaps drawn from what this deal has to pass, steps that each name a person, a signal and what they unlock, the sponsor paragraph that says what is missing, the closing reasons. Copy no fact, name, phrase or figure; in a real reply every one comes from the record supplied.

## Case one: a large deal

The record pasted for Northmere Rail (national passenger rail operator, 9,000 employees). Deal: $900K ARR, ticketing-analytics platform. Stage: late evaluation, privacy assessment pending.

- Ingrid Moyo, Chief Commercial Officer: came to a dinner our CEO hosted in March; CRM shows 5 emails from us, 1 reply; no contact for 70 days.
- Tobias Achterberg, Revenue Planning Manager: on every call since discovery; last CRM touch 3 days ago; sent station-level sales extracts without being asked; introduced us to the data architect. On 2026-10-06 he said: "Anything above $250K goes to our finance director, and she will ask how this lands in the capital plan."
- Renske Dlamini, Data Architect: reviewed the integration spec and on 2026-10-14 wrote: "The integration approach works for us."
- Paolo Estevez, Data Protection Officer: sent a passenger-data assessment form on 2026-10-12. We have not returned it yet.
- 2026-10-02 workshop: Tobias brought three colleagues from the pricing team; they asked sharp questions and were not introduced by name.
- No finance contact in CRM.

The reply:

# Northmere Rail: finance signs above $250K and has never heard from us

## What the record shows

This is a $900K deal in late evaluation. Tobias says anything above $250K goes to the finance director, so the amount puts the decision above everyone we talk to. The stage line says a privacy assessment is pending.

Ingrid Moyo, Chief Commercial Officer. The title reads like a sponsor, but the record holds one dinner our CEO hosted in March, one reply to five emails and 70 days without contact. That is an executive by position only, not a sponsor today: strength 1, dormant.

Tobias Achterberg, Revenue Planning Manager. A manager by title and the champion by behaviour: on every call, sales extracts sent without being asked, and the introduction to the data architect was his. No reply counts recorded for him, so none is written; last touch 3 days ago. Strength 5, steady. He does not decide, and he has told us who does.

Renske Dlamini, Data Architect. She reviewed the integration spec and on 2026-10-14 wrote "The integration approach works for us." No counts recorded for her, so none is written: evaluator, check cleared, strength 3, steady.

Paolo Estevez, Data Protection Officer. He sent an assessment form on 2026-10-12 and it is still with us. A gate, open and waiting on us: this is the privacy assessment the stage line calls pending. Strength 2, steady.

Named but never met: "our finance director", who by Tobias's account signs above $250K and "will ask how this lands in the capital plan". No name and no contact. Also the three pricing colleagues at the 2026-10-02 workshop, who asked sharp questions and were not introduced by name.

Ours to close: Paolo's assessment form from 2026-10-12 has not gone back to him.

## Stakeholders

| Person | Tier | Strength | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finance director (role only, not in CRM) | Decides | none, never started | Signs above $250K, per Tobias on 2026-10-06 |
| Tobias Achterberg, Revenue Planning Manager | Champion | 5, steady | On every call; unprompted sales extracts; made the architect introduction; no counts recorded |
| Ingrid Moyo, Chief Commercial Officer | Executive by position only | 1, dormant | 1 reply to 5; 70 days silent; met once at our CEO's dinner |
| Renske Dlamini, Data Architect | Evaluator, check cleared | 3, steady | "The integration approach works for us", 2026-10-14; no counts recorded |
| Paolo Estevez, Data Protection Officer | Gate, waiting on us | 2, steady | Assessment form sent 2026-10-12, not yet returned |

## Coverage gaps

- **Single-threaded on Tobias Achterberg.** Every live line runs through him or someone he introduced. If he stalls, we have no other way to the signature.
- **No line to the signer.** The finance director decides at this amount and has never heard from us; her capital-plan question is known only second-hand.
- **The commercial chief has gone quiet.** Her position cannot be counted as cover while one email in five is answered.

## Engagement plan

1. **Return Paolo's assessment form now.** It is the only blocker that waits on us, and an open gate weakens every ask after it. Unlocks the privacy assessment the stage line is waiting for.
2. **Ask Tobias to bring the finance director into a short session on the capital plan, and give him a one-page cost treatment to take in with him.** The signal is his own remark that she signs and will ask exactly that. No direct email to her: she has never met us, and going round Tobias would cost his trust. Depends on step 1, so no gate is waiting on us when she asks. Unlocks the signature.
3. **Leave Ingrid for now; once the finance session is booked, ask Tobias whether an update should reach her through him or through our CEO, who hosted the March dinner.** Four of our five emails to her went unanswered, so another from the account team will not help. Unlocks executive cover if finance hesitates.
4. **Ask Tobias for the names and roles of the three pricing colleagues from the 2026-10-02 workshop.** They have already seen the product and asked good questions, so a second thread starts with them. No guessing who they are.

## Executive sponsor

There is no sponsor yet. The finance director holds the signature and has no relationship with us; Ingrid Moyo has the title but one reply in five and 70 days of silence. The sponsor to earn is the finance director, through Tobias and the capital-plan session. Tobias is the champion, not the sponsor: the amount is above anything he can approve.

**Why the map reads this way.** Two senior titles sit below a planning manager because the record shows him acting and them absent. The plan starts with the form we owe because that is the one blocker fully in our hands, and it reaches finance through Tobias because he is the only person she would take the meeting from.

## Case two: a small deal

The record pasted for Pennard Valley Heritage Railway (volunteer-run steam line, 45 staff). Deal: $22K a year, the same analytics tool for the nine-person bookings team. Stage: second week of a trial; review call booked for Thursday.

- Marta Quist, Bookings Manager: asked for the trial herself, has logged in every day, sent four questions about seasonal fare reports. Said on the first call: "I sign for software up to $30K."
- Dev Rana, Volunteer Coordinator: joined the kickoff for a few minutes, asked whether volunteer rosters stay out of the exports, was told they do, and said "that works for me". Nothing since.

The reply:

# Pennard Valley Heritage Railway: the buyer signs for it herself, so the map stays as small as the deal

## What the record shows

A $22K subscription for one nine-person team, two weeks into a trial. Marta says she signs for software up to $30K, so the amount sits inside her own authority, and nothing in the record mentions procurement, finance or legal.

Marta Quist, Bookings Manager. She asked for the trial, logs in daily and has sent four specific questions. She decides and she champions: strength 5, warming.

Dev Rana, Volunteer Coordinator. One question at kickoff, answered, and "that works for me". An evaluator whose narrow check is satisfied; strength 2, and nothing more is needed from him unless Marta says so.

Named but never met: nobody. Ours to close: her four questions, due at Thursday's review.

## Stakeholders

| Person | Tier | Strength | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marta Quist, Bookings Manager | Decides and champions | 5, warming | Asked for the trial; daily logins; "I sign for software up to $30K" |
| Dev Rana, Volunteer Coordinator | Evaluator, check satisfied | 2, steady | One question at kickoff; "that works for me" |

## Coverage gaps

None that this deal needs. The person who wants it can sign for it. One thing to confirm on Thursday: that Dev's roster question was the only condition from his side.

## Engagement plan

1. **Thursday's review with Marta:** answer her four fare-report questions on her own trial data, then ask what she still needs to decide and by when.
2. **In the same call, confirm with Marta that Dev's roster check is closed.** It costs one sentence and removes the only loose end.
3. **Send the order form after the review.** She signs it herself, so nothing else stands in the way.

## Executive sponsor

None is needed. Marta holds the authority for this amount and is already driving the trial; looking for someone above her would slow a deal she can close alone.

**Why the map reads this way.** It is short because the deal is: one team, one signer, an amount inside her limit. No finance, legal or procurement lines appear because the record gives no reason for them. Dev is scored low without being a risk, since his one question was answered.

## Case three: a request the record cannot support

What was pasted for Calderbrook Metro (city tram operator, 2,600 employees), a $1.6M deal at the shortlist stage: "Give me a power ranking of their leadership, 1 to 5, with who holds the veto, for Friday's review slide. Contacts: Aurelio Banks (Head of Fares), Sunniva Okafor (IT Programme Lead), Dermot Hale (Commercial Director), Priya Lindqvist (Category Buyer). Call notes are in the shared drive."

The whole reply is this note, from its title line to its last sentence, with no reading and no map:

# Calderbrook Metro: the record cannot support a power ranking yet

**What was asked, and what came with it.** A 1-to-5 ranking of Calderbrook's leadership with the veto holder, for a review slide. The paste holds four names with titles and says the call notes sit in the shared drive; none of them is here.

**Why nothing is ranked.** A title shows neither who can stop a $1.6M purchase nor who is working for it. A ranking built from four titles would be a guess, and once it is on a leadership slide it becomes the account plan. So there is no ranking, tier or score in this reply.

**What to paste.** The call notes, and for each contact the emails we sent, the replies we got and the date of the last one, plus anything anyone said about who signs at this amount. From that the map shows who is carrying the deal, how strong each line is, where coverage is missing and the first move, which answers the question behind the slide better than a ranking would.

## Writing the reply

The examples end here. The next message is the reply itself and nothing else. It starts with `# ` and the account name from the record in front of you: no sentence before it, no "Now I'll", no mention of the skill or of these examples, no `---` line above the title. That holds for the short note of case three as well.
