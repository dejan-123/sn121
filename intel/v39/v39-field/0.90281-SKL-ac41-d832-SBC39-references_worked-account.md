# One invented account, mapped, then changed one fact at a time

Everything here is invented: a water utility, its people and its numbers. It is here to show the reasoning. A real input has its own people, and a call made below holds for your input only if your input carries the same signal.

## The input (summarised)

Halvorsen Water Authority, a regional water utility with about 1,400 staff. Deal: $640K a year for a network asset-monitoring platform. CRM stage: late evaluation, "contract review pending"; the utility wants a decision before its capital plan closes in March.

- **Ines Marlow, Director of Network Assets.** Requested the first meeting. Sent three years of leak-repair data before we asked. Forwarded our proposal to three colleagues the same day and introduced us to Ruth. Said her own budget covers tools up to $250K and anything larger is signed by the finance director. On 10-29 wrote: "I really want this, leave it with me." Nothing followed. 9 emails out, 8 replies; last contact 6 days ago.
- **Gareth Pennock, Finance Director.** Copied on the proposal email in July and silent ever since: he has neither met us nor answered or passed anything on. 94 days.
- **Ruth Achebe, GIS Analyst.** Asked detailed questions about the asset data model, wrote the integration requirement herself, replies the same day. 6 out, 6 replies; 4 days ago.
- **Colm Draycott, Head of Cyber Resilience.** Joined the technical call 15 minutes late and left early; two emails since, no reply. The utility's security assessment form is with him and still open. 61 days.
- **Moira Quaid, Deputy Chief Executive.** Opened our executive briefing on 09-12 and said the platform "belongs in next year's capital plan". Then nothing: 5 emails out, 1 reply (a thank-you on 09-14). 49 days.
- **Tomasz Weller, Procurement Lead.** Sent the supplier onboarding checklist; says a PO needs the security form closed and finance approval. Prompt, neutral, no view on the product.
- **Hollis Grant, Contracts Counsel.** Wrote on 10-02: "Your standard terms are acceptable; no redlines from us."
- Ines on 10-20: "Our operations director will want to see the field rollout plan." No operations contact exists.

## The reply

```
# Stakeholder map: Halvorsen Water Authority - network asset monitoring, $640K a year

Late evaluation with a March capital-plan deadline. The deal needs the finance director's signature, and he has not engaged in 94 days; the security form with Colm is the other gate before Tomasz can raise a PO. Our only route upward runs through Ines, whose last promise has had no follow-through.

## Stakeholder table: an engaged champion, a buyer who has never met us

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Strength (1-5, trend) | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ines Marlow | Director of Network Assets | Champion | 4, steady | Asked for the first meeting, sent leak data unprompted, forwarded the proposal to three colleagues, introduced Ruth; 8 replies to 9 emails. Not the buyer: her limit is $250K against a $640K deal. "Leave it with me" on 10-29 has no action behind it yet, so her reach upward is unproven. |
| Gareth Pennock | Finance Director | Economic buyer | 1, dormant | Named by Ines as the signer above $250K. Copied once in July; no reply or meeting in 94 days. Authority is clear, relationship is absent. |
| Ruth Achebe | GIS Analyst | Influencer | 5, warming | Junior title, but she wrote the integration requirement and answers within the day (6 of 6). Title overruled upward: her sign-off on the data model is needed. |
| Colm Draycott | Head of Cyber Resilience | Gatekeeper | 1, cooling | Senior title, but late to his one call, left early, no reply to two emails in 61 days. Title overruled downward: he is not driving the deal, yet the open security form makes him a gate. |
| Moira Quaid | Deputy Chief Executive | Unconfirmed (provisional sponsor) | 2, dormant | Conflict: opened the briefing and placed the platform in "next year's capital plan", then 1 reply to 5 emails and 49 days of silence. Her words are strong, her recent behaviour is not; the call stays provisional until step 3 tests it. |
| Tomasz Weller | Procurement Lead | Gatekeeper | 3, steady | Owns the PO step and stated its two conditions: security form closed, finance approval. Prompt but neutral. |
| Hollis Grant | Contracts Counsel | Influencer (closed) | 3, steady | Accepted standard terms on 10-02. The CRM's "contract review pending" is out of date. |

## Coverage gaps: the signer and the operations director

1. The finance director signs at this size and has never met us; he is the gap that decides the deal, and the way to him is through Ines (step 2), not a cold email.
2. Ines expects the operations director to want the field rollout plan, and nobody in that role is in the record. A utility-wide rollout changes how field crews work, so this approver is real; ask Ines for the introduction once the plan exists.
3. Moira is the only executive who has spoken for the platform, and seven weeks of silence leave her support unconfirmed (step 3).

Legal is not a gap (Hollis accepted our terms on 10-02), and the technical side is covered by Ruth.

## Engagement plan: security form, then the finance director

1. Ask Ruth to work through the security form with us this week and take Colm a nearly finished version. Tomasz named that form as one of his two PO conditions, it has sat with Colm for 61 days, and Ruth answers the same day, so she is the fastest way to close it.
2. Ask Ines to set up a 30-minute budget review with the finance director, where we walk him through the leak-cost figures her own data produced. Her "leave it with me" of 10-29 needs a concrete next act, and the signature waits on this meeting.
3. In the same conversation, ask Ines whether the platform is still in the capital-plan draft and whether Moira would open the finance review. A yes confirms Moira as sponsor; a no hands sponsorship to the finance director.
4. Once the budget review is booked, send Ines the field rollout plan for the operations director and ask her to make that introduction.
5. Correct the CRM: the contract review closed on 10-02.

## Executive sponsor: Moira, if she still holds the capital plan

Align with Moira Quaid if step 3 confirms she still holds the platform in the capital plan: she is the only executive who has said so, and she outranks the signer. Until then the relationship is one thank-you email old, so pair her with our COO only after Ines confirms. If she has stepped back, the finance director becomes the sponsor as well as the buyer, and our CFO should ask for the step 2 meeting through Ines.

**Why the map reads this way.** Two titles were overruled by the record: Ruth's junior title hides the person writing the integration requirement, and Colm's senior one hides a contact who has gone silent while holding the security form. The CRM's "contract review pending" is contradicted by Hollis's written acceptance, so legal is neither a gap nor a step. The plan opens on the security form because it is the one PO condition that can move this week, and the finance director comes next because the signature waits on him.
```

## What to notice

- Each tier call carries its signal, and each override says which way it went: Ruth up, Colm down.
- Ines is a strong champion and still not the buyer, because her stated limit is below the deal.
- Moira's conflict is named in the row, and step 3 exists to settle it.
- The CRM label said legal was pending; the record says it is done, so it is not a gap and gets no step.
- The operations director is a gap, not a table row: nobody named them, and a row reading "operations director (unnamed)" would put a person in the table that the input does not contain.

## The same account, one fact changed

**1. A small purchase.** Had Ines been buying a $30K pilot for her own team inside her $250K limit, she is both champion and economic buyer: $30K sits well inside her limit, so nobody above her has to sign, and her table row says so in those words. The gap analysis shrinks to the people who will use the tool. Finance, procurement, legal and the operations director are not added, because nothing in the deal needs them; and had Ruth said the data model "works for us", the step is to get that in writing, not to look for Ruth's manager. There is no single-thread flag either: Ines can sign alone, so there is nobody behind her to reach, and Ruth's brief review is a sign-off to confirm, not a second thread to build. The plan ends at a dated pilot decision with Ines.

**2. Only one thread.** Had Ines been the only contact in the CRM, with the finance director only mentioned in her emails, the map opens with: "**Single-thread risk:** every route to the finance director, who signs this deal, runs through Ines, and he has never been introduced." The table then has one row, Ines; the finance director appears only under Coverage gaps, because the input never gives him a contact of his own. Step 1 becomes her warm introduction, offering to join her budget review. Emailing the finance director cold is ruled out: it goes around the only person working for us.

**3. A gate already closed.** Had Colm returned the security form signed last week, it would not appear in the plan at all, even though the CRM still says "security pending". The plan would open on the finance director's budget review, because that is now the only thing between this stage and a signature; asking anyone to "confirm" a sign-off that is already in writing is not a step.

**4. The conflict resolved.** Had Moira forwarded the proposal to finance last week with "please prioritise", the newest signal is an action, not a statement. She becomes a confirmed sponsor, and step 3 disappears.

**5. A request the input cannot support.** Asked for a diagram of the reporting lines to show leadership, with only names and titles pasted, the reply is short:

```
# This input cannot support a reporting-line chart

You asked for a diagram of the reporting lines. The input lists names and titles but no reporting relationships, and a chart built from titles would be a guess presented as fact. What I can build is a stakeholder map: influence tier, relationship strength, gaps and a plan. With titles alone every tier would be Unconfirmed. Paste the meeting notes and CRM activity (dates, who wrote to whom, replies), and any reporting lines you know, and I will map it.
```

Now write the reply for the account in front of you. Its first line is the `#` title; no sentence such as "Now I'll apply this" comes before it.
