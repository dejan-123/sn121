Carrowmere Water, a regional water utility with about 1,400 staff. Deal: $640K ARR for an asset-maintenance analytics platform. The CRM stage field reads "Technical evaluation". A rival vendor ran a free pilot at the utility last spring.

## The reply

# Carrowmere Water - stakeholder map for the $640K asset-analytics deal

### How I read the account

Ines has done almost everything that moves this deal: she ran the pilot, handed over failure data nobody requested, opened the door to operations, and told us exactly how approval works. Thirteen replies to fourteen emails, with initiative from her side, is a 5. Her own words also set her limit: anything over $250K goes to a committee the CFO chairs, so at $640K she can recommend but not approve. Her 28 October export question is waiting on us - the quiet in that thread is ours.

Gordon is the conflict. His title and his remark about winter planning suggest a natural sponsor, but his record is one calendar acceptance against five emails and nothing since September, which caps him at 2. I lean toward evaluator, not sponsor: he reacted to a demonstration and has not acted since. The test is cheap - ask Ines whether Gordon will co-sign the capital paper. A yes makes him a sponsor in the making; a vague answer means interested, not invested.

Hannah is listed but has never been reached: no emails either way, only a badge scan. She stays in the table because the empty row is the point - the control room would live with the platform's alerts every day, and nobody from it has seen the pilot results.

The CFO is referenced, never introduced. Ines named the role and the committee; we have no name, no contact and no read on what the CFO weighs. That makes the CFO both the economic buyer and our largest gap, and because Ines is our only route to the committee, the deal is single-threaded through her.

Two lines outside the contacts change the plan. The rival's free pilot means the committee will set our price against an option it already tried at no cost, so the paper has to carry the difference in results. And the stage field still says technical evaluation, but the notes show the pilot finished on 20 October and the approval paper under way; I go with the notes. Nothing in the record raises procurement or legal.

### Stakeholder map

| Name | Title | Tier | Strength and direction | Evidence and reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ines Valbuena | Asset Strategy Manager | Champion | 5, rising | 13 of 14 replies; ran the pilot; failure logs unprompted; introduced operations; drafting the capital paper, but below the $250K committee line |
| Gordon Achterberg | Director of Operations | Evaluator (sponsor untested) | 2, cooling | 1 reply to 5 emails, a calendar acceptance; warm remark on 12 Sep, silent since - title says sponsor, record says observer |
| Hannah Reiss | Control Room Manager | Unengaged | 1, never reached | Badge scan only; 0 emails either way; her team would use the alerts daily |

### Coverage gaps

- **Economic buyer (CFO, unnamed).** By Ines's own threshold, the $640K decision sits with the committee the CFO chairs. No contact, no signal of priorities.
- **Daily users.** The control room has no voice in the evaluation; Hannah is a name without a relationship.

### Engagement plan

**Single-thread risk:** every path to the capital committee runs through Ines. If she is pulled onto other work before March, we have no other voice in the room.

1. **Answer Ines's export question and ask for the route to the CFO in the same reply.** Send the pilot data in the shape her paper needs, offer to draft its financial appendix, and ask whether a 20-minute pre-read with the CFO's finance partner would help her case. No direct email to the CFO: it would bypass the person writing our paper.
2. **Build the appendix around the rival's pilot** (in parallel). Put our pilot's failure-prediction results beside what the free pilot delivered, so the committee sees why a paid option beats one it already tried.
3. **Test Gordon through Ines** (in parallel). Ask whether he will co-sign the paper; if he will, give him a one-page crew-scheduling note, since that is the topic he raised.
4. **Ask Ines to introduce Hannah** (after 1). Offer the control room a short walkthrough of the alerts from the pilot, so the paper can cite the people who would use them.
5. **Ask Ines which purchasing route follows a committee yes** (after 1), and work back from the March date.

### Executive sponsor

There is no executive sponsor yet. The CFO holds the decision but has given no signal; Gordon has seniority but not the record; Ines has the commitment but sits below the approval threshold. The person we need is the CFO, reached through Ines's paper rather than around it, with Gordon worth testing as a second voice if he co-signs.

### Why the plan starts where it does

The deal is not short of interest; it is waiting on our unanswered question to the one person writing the approval paper. Answering it while asking for the CFO route restores momentum with our only thread and starts widening it in the same move.
