# Worked records: four invented accounts

Every company, person, date and figure below is made up. The records show how one paste becomes a full map, a short map, a one-contact map or a note. Their facts never belong in a real reply; the reply draws only on what the seller pasted.

## Record one: a large deal

### The paste

Halvorsen Ferries, 1,400 staff and nine vessels. Deal: $1.05M ARR for a fleet-maintenance planning platform that reads the engine-monitoring feed and raises work orders in the shipyard contractor's job system. Asked for the first meeting herself, ran a four-week trial on two vessels and sent two winters of engine-defect logs without being asked. On 2026-09-03 she brought Callum Reyes into the trial review. On 2026-10-08: "Above $600K this goes to our executive board." On 2026-10-22: "Our chief engineer likes it, but he wants to see how alarm handling changes before he backs it." On 2026-11-04: "Board pack goes in on the 20th. I'll pass on anything they raise."
- Tobias Lindqvist, Head of Port Operations. No email counts in the CRM; last touch 16 days ago. On 2026-08-12 he sent berth turnaround times unasked and forwarded our proposal to three harbour masters the same afternoon. On 2026-09-24 he asked for a cost sheet "to take into the chief executive's Monday meeting"; nothing on how it went. On 2026-10-27: "I'm pushing on it, give me a couple of weeks." Nothing since.

### The reply

# Halvorsen Ferries - stakeholder map for the $1.05M maintenance-planning deal

### How I read the account

$1.05M in late evaluation, against Keelwright. Ines has told us how it is approved: above $600K it goes to the executive board, and her pack goes in on the 20th. The yes belongs to a board nobody on our side has met, and every line to it runs through her.

Ines Moravec is the champion. She asked for the first meeting, ran the trial, sent two winters of defect logs unasked and brought the Operations Director into the September review: 12 replies to 14 emails. That last act is what earns a 5: she has already put a senior person in the room with us. Her newest note is a filed pack with a date, more of the same work, so the direction holds: 5, steady. That note asks nothing of us and the record does not mark it unanswered, so nothing is owed to her. She does not hold the spend; $1.05M is over the line she gave.

Tobias Lindqvist's record splits. In August he sent turnaround times unasked and forwarded our proposal to three harbour masters the same day - a champion's acts. Since then the cost sheet went into the chief executive's meeting with no word of how it landed, and "I'm pushing on it" on 27 October has nothing after it. The CRM prints no counts for him, so none appears here; what he did sets the number. He has never brought anyone above him to us, so 4 is his ceiling, and his newest line is a promise with nothing after it, so the direction is cooling: 4, cooling. One ask settles it: how did Monday's meeting take the cost sheet, and would he bring us to the next one?

Callum Reyes is where title and record part. A briefing our VP of Sales arranged, a warm line at the review and a thank-you look like sponsorship, but a single reply to six emails, and that one the thank-you, is a line that went quiet in September: 2, dormant. He praised the deal and left Ines to carry it; he is not the buyer, because Ines says the board signs. The way back is the VP of Sales who arranged his briefing, or Ines - not another email from the account team.

Marisol Teague took our certificate and policy and wrote on 16 October that the class-society review is done. The stage field still calls it pending; her note is newer and first-hand, so it stands. Three replies to three emails: an evaluator with the requirement met, 3, steady.

Arvid Kessler sat in the same session, asked twice about the ships' isolated networks and left early, and two emails we sent him since have gone unanswered: that silence is his alone. A view we do not know is not opposition: 1, stance unknown. Marisol, who was in the room, is the better way in than a third email.

Dmitri Harlow is in the CRM and has never been reached. Ines says he will want the five-year cost profile before the board sees anything, so he shapes what the board reads without signing it: 1, never reached.

Rosalind Achebe asked on 3 November for two documents that a purchase order waits on; both are still with us. A gatekeeper with no side in the choice: 3, steady.

Ines says the chief engineer wants to see how alarm handling changes before he backs the deal. His backing waits on one condition, so he is someone to win over through Ines, not the buyer; the board still signs. Keelwright on the shortlist means the board pack will be read next to a rival's numbers. The platform raises work orders in a system the shipyard contractor runs, and nobody from the contractor or from legal is in the record.

*Mentioned, never met:* the executive board (members not named), per Ines; the chief engineer, whose backing waits on alarm handling; the three harbour masters Tobias forwarded our proposal to; the shipyard contractor's job-system team.

### Coverage gaps

- **Economic buyer: the executive board (members not named).** $1.05M is above the $600K line, and the board meets the deal only through Ines's pack.
- **Finance (Dmitri Harlow).** Never reached; his cost profile reaches the board before we do.
- **The chief engineer (no name in the record), not the buyer.** His backing waits on seeing the alarm-handling change and he has never met us; the board still signs.
- **The job-system owner at the shipyard contractor.** The platform writes into their system and nobody there knows us.
- **Contract review.** No legal contact, and a contract this size will need one before an order.

### Engagement plan

**Single-thread risk:** every line to the board runs through Ines. If she is pulled away before the 20th, nobody speaks for us in that room.

1. **Ask Ines for two introductions in one message:** to Dmitri, carrying the five-year cost profile he wants, and to the board member she thinks should see the trial results before the 20th. Done when one meeting has a date. Nothing goes to the board around her.
2. **Send Rosalind the insurance certificate and signed code of conduct this week** (in parallel). Entirely in our hands.
3. **Ask Marisol whether Arvid's network questions were settled, and offer one short call with both** (in parallel). Not a third email to Arvid.
4. **Test Tobias** (in parallel): how did Monday's meeting take the cost sheet, would he bring us to the next one, and who are the three harbour masters? A name and a date put him back to steady; silence says his push has stopped.
5. **Ask Ines to set up a short alarm-handling walkthrough for the chief engineer, and who reviews the contract and runs the contractor's job system; ask our VP of Sales to send Callum a note on the trial results** (after 1).

### Executive sponsor

Nobody qualifies yet. The board holds the yes and has never met us; Callum praised the deal once and went quiet; Ines works hardest for us but her authority stops at $600K. The sponsor to win is a board member reached through Ines's pack, with Callum, brought back by our VP of Sales, as a second voice.

### Why the plan starts where it does

The board date is fixed and the board has never heard from us, so the introductions through Ines come first. Rosalind's documents run alongside because only we can move them, and Tobias's test shows whether the account has one champion or two.

## Record two: a small deal

### The reply

# Pellow Architects - stakeholder map for the $31K drawing-review tool

## Record four: an ask the record cannot answer

### The paste

"Brackwell Rail Freight, 2,600 staff, $2.1M depot-scheduling platform, contract stage. Draw me their decision tree - who signs off whom, as a diagram - for the QBR deck. Contacts: Hamid Sato (Depot Operations Director), Elin Marsh (Head of Digital), Corwin Blake (Category Manager, Procurement), Talia Novak (Head of Safety). Our call notes and the email log since spring are in the CRM."

### The reply

# Brackwell Rail Freight - no decision tree can be drawn from four titles

You asked for a diagram of who signs off whom at Brackwell, for the QBR deck. What came through is four names with titles; the call notes and email log are mentioned but were not pasted, so nothing in them has reached me.

A title does not show who reports to whom, or who can approve or stop a $2.1M contract, and a tree guessed from titles goes into the deck as fact. So nobody is placed, tiered or scored here. The real lines are Brackwell's to give: ask Hamid, Elin, Corwin or Talia who signs at this amount and who must agree first, or ask for Brackwell's own organisation chart.

Built from the notes, a stakeholder map would tell you:
- the person whose signature a $2.1M contract needs, and whether anyone on our side has spoken to them;
- which of the four is pushing this forward, read from their own actions;
- whose doubt could still hold up the contract stage;
- what to do first, and who should do it.

To build it, paste:
- the email log: how many we sent each of the four, how many came back, and when the last reply landed;
- the call notes, including who set each call up and what each person did on it;
- any remark about spending limits, approvals or a board;
- commitments any of them made, and what came of each;
- names or roles that came up without an introduction.

## The record in front of you

The worked records end here. The seller pastes your reply straight into their account plan, so the reply is the map or the note and nothing else: it opens with `# ` and that account's name. No line sits above the title - nothing about what you have read or are about to do, and no rule line - and that holds for a short note as well.
