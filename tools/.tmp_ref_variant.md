## Signal caveats

All surviving postings were voluntary, so posted-heavy rows skew aspirational and offers carry the read. Inventory leans on hearsay and a one-stage-down posting, hence Low. Trade Controls has no offer line, so the table records I=0 and the prose names the gap; internal offers use a different twelve-month clock than the nine-month evidence window.

**Why it reads this way.** The unfiltered table would flatter Engineering through a Series-E range, a contractor rate and a Munich point; all are out. The declined 93K confirms the floor. Terminal Operations trails because its signed offers clear above midpoint - inside the band's ceiling, but the market is paying above its centre, and that is what a trail looks like; for the priority roles, Engineering holds while Inventory needs more evidence before guiding a salary talk.

## Redirect pattern - no external evidence

Input digest:

- Cinderbrook Fabrication, a Series A industrial-components company of about 45 staff in Dayton, Ohio. The operations lead writes: "Annual review time - roster attached. What does competitive pay look like for our people? Our plant manager thinks the machinists are underpaid and wants the numbers. Also, while you're in there, can you collapse our two junior technician levels into one? The board wants a market analysis by Thursday." Pasted roster only: two Senior Machinists at $118K and $124K; two Machinists at $96K and $99K; three Production Technicians at $52K, $54K and $55K. No external evidence has been classified at all.

Expected reply:

# Compensation Benchmark - Cinderbrook Fabrication | not built: no classified external evidence

**What the record shows.** The roster is small but legible - and it speaks in spreads and levels, never in a named person's number: Senior Machinists at $118K-$124K, Machinists at $96K-$99K, Production Technicians at $52K-$55K. Each family holds a workable spread - the two senior machinists sit about $23K-28K above the mid-level machinists, and the two mid-levels are close enough that $96K-99K reads as one cluster rather than two out-of-line salaries. What the roster cannot say is whether any of those numbers is competitive, because nothing outside the company has been compared to them.

**Why there is no benchmark.** A verdict needs outside evidence that survives the checks - points with a source, a date, a geography, a company stage and a pay basis - and none has been classified here. Quoting market figures from memory or pulling numbers out of the air would dress a guess up as a finding, which is worse than no table at all. The plant manager's question is fair; the record just cannot answer it yet.

**What resolves it.** Classify an external evidence table first: postings, offers and surveys for these roles, in this metro or a declared remote market, from companies at a near enough stage, inside a stated recency window. Once that exists, each family gets a verdict, a confidence tier and an honest count of what was excluded.

**What belongs to a different deliverable.** Collapsing the two junior technician levels into one is a band-structure decision - it can start from the roster and your leveling philosophy today, but it is a different piece of work from a market benchmark and it does not need external data to begin. It stays a separate deliverable.

## Cases in brief

### The currency you cannot cross

Brasserie Nord, a Series C brewery group anchored to Lyon, pays in EUR. The evidence table holds a posted range for the same role, same stage, inside the window - quoted in CHF for the Zurich market. It is excluded on two checks at once (metro and basis): even setting geography aside, no exchange rate is supplied, so any EUR figure derived from it would be invented - approximate or otherwise. The evidence columns count it; the verdict never sees it. The flip side matters too: a team that works and is paid in Zurich is not deferred for being foreign - it is benchmarked in CHF against Zurich points, and its own currency carries the read.

### The boundary pair

A stated window of "points before March 2026 are out of window" admits a posted range dated 2026-03 and excludes an offer dated 2026-02 - same role, same table, one month apart. The March point joins the pool; the February point is counted in the evidence columns as excluded on recency. Boundary months are the window working as written, not a reason for doubt.

### The contractor rate

A role family has exactly two points: one in-window posted range and a contractor day rate. The day rate is excluded on pay basis - never annualised, never averaged - leaving a pool of one voluntary posting. The verdict is Low confidence, not because the filter failed but because the honest pool is thin. A thin pool read plainly beats a thick-looking pool built on a rate that was never a salary. The same basis check removes every non-base lump - a retention grant reported as one total, an equity award folded into a figure, a signing bonus with no breakdown named. It is the comparability that fails, never the label.

### The gap that stays a gap

A role family appears in the snapshot with no offer history - "no recent offers" written plainly - and the evidence table skips it entirely. The read says so: no internal offers on record, no external points survived, and the role joins the deferred section. Nobody invents an offer, back-fills a figure, or borrows confidence from a neighbouring seat. An honest gap in the benchmark is a finding; a filled one is a fabrication.

### The title that maps and the level that doesn't

Two points arrive for the same family: a "Facilities Engineer" whose scope note maps the seat to the plant-engineering role at our level, and a posted range for a "Plant Engineering Manager". The first counts - the note does the mapping, and the variant title is allowed in. The second is out on the role check alone: a manager is not the senior-IC seat whatever else it gets right, and a near title is not the seat. Read the seat, not the string.

### The unanchored role inside an anchored company

A company anchors its headquarters roles to Minneapolis but lists its sales-engineering seat as remote, no metro anchoring. A Minneapolis posted range survives only for the anchored seats; the remote seat's pool is the remote and national points. The same evidence row is in for one role and out for the other - the anchoring rule belongs to the role, not the company, and the evidence columns count it per role accordingly. If the snapshot says only "some roles are remote" and does not name them, every family without a remote note stays metro-anchored; remote-US, national and other-metro rows are out for those families.

### The point that fails twice

A Toronto, Series-D posting for a Raleigh-anchored Series-B role is one excluded point with two failed checks: `1 excluded (metro, stage)`, never `2 excluded` and never secondary. Count points in X; list failed checks in the reasons.

### The near-midpoint cluster

A band midpoint is 100K. One accepted offer at 102K and one primary posted midpoint at 103K are both above it, but only slightly: the verdict is at market. Change the posted midpoint to 104K or higher and the same two-point pattern can support a trail call. Two accepted offers at 101K and 102K are different: accepted offers carry revealed preference, so their consistent side wins even inside the band.
