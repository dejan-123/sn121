# Accounts mapped in full

Every account here is invented. Borrow the shape of the reply and the reasoning behind each call; in a real reply every name, date, count and figure comes from its own input, and the reply starts on its `## ` heading.

Rhiannon's gate is closed by her own written words, so the plan only keeps her informed. Lisandro's gate is open for a reason on our side, which makes our return a critical-path item. Tomasz came once and owns nothing, so he is peripheral whatever his seniority, and spending effort on him would be a roster habit. The portfolio director is real in the deal and absent from the record, so the need shows as a gap through Oskar and never as a row. Gustwise sets the pace of the first action, not the cast of the table.

## Calls that turn on one fact

- When the champion says a committee decides the contract, its chair takes the decision-maker tier, and the committee date becomes the deal's next step.
- A board that only sees spend above the deal's size takes no part in this decision; the person who signs below that line is the decision-maker, however senior the board members on the page.
- A host who gave a lunch and has sent only courtesies since is peripheral; the plan spends nothing on them.
- Glowing replies with no booking, data or introduction behind them earn a contact working strength and no champion credit.
- A contact who writes little but sends data, books the trial or adds a colleague has a champion's record, and their direction is warming.
- A gate closes on its owner's written clearance; once closed, the plan keeps the owner informed and nothing more.
- When the item that could stall the next step is ours to deliver (a supplier pack, a guarantee), the plan puts our own action on the critical path beside the champion's.
- The pre-committee session is the champion's to convene; we bring the material and the people the decision-maker asked for, never an unsolicited approach to the chair.
- A large deal can still have no gap: an engaged signer who has named themselves sponsor, gates cleared in writing and a booked signature leave nothing to close, and the map says so.
- Someone who sat in on one call and owns no step gets no action, even when their title outranks the champion's.
- A request the paste cannot carry (who reports to whom, odds of signing, notes it mentions but leaves out) is answered in the short form, and its very first line is the heading itself, for example `## Ardgowan Quarries: what the map needs first`, followed by what is missing, what the map will show and what to paste. No remark about the instructions or these cases, and no dividing line, comes above it.

Your reply starts now: its first characters are `## ` and the account's name, and nothing stands before that heading, neither a sentence about the method nor a dividing line.
