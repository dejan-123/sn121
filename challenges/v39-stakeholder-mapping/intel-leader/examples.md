# Invented accounts, for form only

Made-up accounts and the reply each one gets. They show the order of the calls and how the page sits. A real reply takes every name, figure and sentence from its own input; a detail carried over from these pages would be an invention.

## Case A: a full record, large deal (water utility)

Input, in summary. Account: Tarnwick Water, regional water utility, 2,900 staff. Deal: $940K ARR, inspection-scheduling platform for the pumping network. Stage: final evaluation; the stage notes still read "data review open". A rival vendor, Gridmark, is on the shortlist.
- Marisol Etxeberria, Head of Network Maintenance: on each call since the March site visit; sent their pump-failure log without being asked; wrote the internal options paper; arranged the June session with the COO; says her own sign-off stops at $350K. In May she said she would "put it on the capital panel's agenda"; no date or name has followed. CRM: 19 sent, 17 replies, latest reply two days ago.
- Cormac Adeyinka, Chief Operating Officer: one session in June, arranged by Marisol and attended by our regional director; said "this is where we have to go" and told Marisol to "take it to the capital panel". CRM since then: 7 sent, no replies, 52 days.
- Liesl Okonjo, Data Governance Manager: asked for the retention specification; wrote on 3 July, "retention requirement met; nothing further needed from data governance".
- Gethin Raman, OT Security Lead: joined one technical session and asked nothing; has left four emails unanswered.
- Yusra Halloran, Contracts Officer, Purchasing: sent the supplier registration pack eleven days ago; we have not returned it. She answers within two days; no view expressed.
- Marisol, in passing: "the Finance Director chairs the capital panel" and "the depot supervisors will want a say on rollout". Neither is in the CRM.

The reply:

## Coverage gap analysis

A contract of this size has to pass an approver above $350K, security, the data sign-off and purchasing.

**Structural risk, single-threaded on Marisol Etxeberria.** If she is away or loses heart, we have no route to the decision.

- **Budget approver: unmet.** The capital panel decides, and we know its chair by role only.
- **Security clearance: open.** The one reviewer has given no view either way.
- **Rollout voice: unidentified.** The depot supervisors were mentioned and are unknown to us.

The data sign-off is closed, and purchasing is covered once the pack goes back. Whether a contract of this size also goes to their legal team is a question for Marisol, since nobody has mentioned it.

## Engagement plan

1. **Ask Marisol for the introduction behind her May promise.** Which panel meeting will take it, who presents, and will she bring us to that person? Every upward route runs through her, so this repairs the single thread and tests her access in one ask. It is done when we hold a date and a second name, and steps 3 and 5 wait on it.
2. **Return Yusra Halloran's registration pack today**, and ask what follows it. It has sat with us for eleven days, and it is the one thing fully in our hands.
3. **Bring the COO back through someone he has met.** Our regional director sat in the June session; once Marisol confirms the panel date, he writes to Cormac Adeyinka offering what the panel will need. Seven messages from the account team went unanswered, so an eighth from us is not the way. A reply makes him an active sponsor again; silence confirms the lapse.
4. **Ask Marisol how Gethin Raman is best approached and what he needs to see**, in place of a fifth email. That settles whether security is waiting for something or has simply not started.
5. **Ask Marisol which depot supervisors to involve, and what Gridmark has shown them.** It follows step 1, because she is the one who raised them.

Liesl Okonjo has closed her point and is asked for nothing more.

## Executive sponsor recommendation

Our regional director is the grounded choice: he has met Cormac Adeyinka, so a note from him continues a conversation where one from us would start cold. Its subject is the panel, so it waits for step 1. No approach to the Finance Director is recommended yet: we lack the name and any sign that the panel's chair wants to hear from a vendor.

**Why the map reads this way.** The Head of Network Maintenance ranks above the Chief Operating Officer because the record shows her acting and him silent. He is called lapsed, not lost, because his one signal was real and a route back exists. The data review is treated as closed against our own stage notes because its owner said so in writing. Nobody is named for the panel or the depots because the record names nobody.

## Case B: a full record, small deal (veterinary group)

- Ossian Pryce, Head Nurse: checked the on-call rules during the trial and wrote "this covers our on-call pattern, good enough for me at this point"; has not joined a call.
Nobody has mentioned purchasing or a contract review.

One narrow check, passed: an influencer whose requirement is met, 2/5 and steady. His "at this point" is a caveat in his wording, not a second signal, so nothing conflicts.

Waiting on us: the written price Henrike asked for.

Because the contact is the buyer, there is no thread to someone else that could break.

## Engagement plan

1. **Send Henrike Abara the written price before Thursday**, with the trial's end date on it. She asked for it and it is still open.

## Executive sponsor recommendation

Not needed. The buyer is the day-to-day contact and the amount is inside her own authority.

**Why the map reads this way.** Two rows and two steps are the whole map because the purchase is that small: the person running the trial can sign for it. Purchasing and legal are absent from the page because nobody in the record has raised them. Ossian Pryce's low score describes how little we have spoken, not a risk; his check passed.

## Case C: names and titles only, and a request for a drawing (food manufacturer)

Input, in summary. Account: Pendrell Foods, food manufacturer, 3,900 staff. Deal: $1.3M ARR, production-planning platform. Stage: contracting. The sales director writes: "Can you sketch the hierarchy of their operations and IT leadership, boxes and lines, for my deck on Tuesday?" Supplied: three names with titles (Aurea Whitlock, Head of Plant Systems; Bram Oyelaran, IT Programme Manager; Dilys Karimi, Senior Buyer) and the remark that "the call notes are all in the CRM".

The reply, which is this note and nothing more:

# Pendrell Foods: no hierarchy can be drawn from this paste

You asked for "the hierarchy of their operations and IT leadership, boxes and lines". What arrived is three names with titles; the call notes are said to be in the CRM and were not pasted.

A title shows neither who reports to whom nor who decides, and a line between two boxes on a leadership deck would be a guess read as fact. So nothing is drawn and nobody is ranked. The reporting lines are Pendrell's to give: ask them for their own org chart.

What this record can support, once pasted, is a stakeholder map: who signs at $1.3M, who is working for the deal, who can stop it, and the first move. For that, paste the call notes and, for each contact, messages sent against replies, the date of the last one, and anything said about approval.

## Had it been different

- Had Marisol's limit covered the deal, she would be buyer and champion together and the panel would not appear as a gap.
- Had the COO replied last week, the conflict about him would be gone: active sponsor at 3/5.
- Had the record shown no one with stated authority and no panel, the buyer would read "not established", and asking Marisol who signs would be step 1.
- Had the veterinary deal been ten times the size and across every clinic, Henrike's limit would put the buyer above her, and the silence about purchasing would become a question to ask.
- Had Pendrell's request come with the call notes pasted, it would get the full map, with one line pointing to their own org chart for the reporting question.
- Had Marisol been our only contact, with everyone else known through her emails alone, the table would hold her one row, the others would sit under the reading unnamed, and every section would still be there, shorter.
- Had an email of hers been waiting on us, answering it would ride in step 1 with the request for the introduction, named with its date, and her trend would not be marked down for our silence.

Each reply above is the whole of what was sent: it starts at its title and ends with its last line.
