# Three replies in two passes (invented accounts; form only)

All three cases are made up. A reply opens with the passes and nothing ahead
of them: first characters `## Pass one`, or `# ` for the redirect note. Copy
the form — facts collected before any call, every row and gap and step tied
to a cited signal, the small-deal answer kept small. Copy no fact, name,
or figure; a real input supplies its own.

## Case one: a full account picture

What the account team supplied for Meridian Air (regional carrier, 1,400
employees), an expansion deal — $290K to add the maintenance-planning module
to the existing crew-scheduling platform:

- Contacts: Imogen Falk, Director of Crew Operations — our daily user since
  rollout; proposed the expansion herself in July; shares roster and delay
  figures unprompted; 9 replies to our 10 emails, same-day; last touch 3
  days ago. Casper Lindqvist, VP Operations — owns the combined operations
  budget; attended the expansion kickoff, asked about rollout phasing and
  downtime risk; 4 replies to our 8 emails; last touch 12 days ago.
  Djordje Anciaux, Maintenance Systems Manager — ran the technical review,
  asked about integration with their fleet-data feed, confirmed it fits;
  5 replies to our 5 emails. Petra Onyema, Procurement Officer — joined
  once, sent a pricing-schedule request on the 9th; we have not yet sent it.
- Note from the kickoff: Casper said "the CIO will want to see the platform
  roadmap before we extend it" — no CIO contact exists and none has met us.
- Deal context: renewal of the base platform lands in the same quarter;
  expansion is priced to close inside it.

The reply:

## Pass one — the signals

Supplied: a contact list, activity counts and meeting notes for a $290K
expansion deal at an existing customer — this is a stakeholder read of a
live deal, mid-evaluation.

- Imogen Falk, Director of Crew Operations: proposed the expansion herself
  in July, shares internal roster and delay figures without being asked,
  answers 9 of 10 emails same-day — last touch 3 days ago.
- Casper Lindqvist, VP Operations: attended the kickoff, asked about
  phasing and downtime risk, holds the combined operations budget. Half
  our emails answered — 4 of 8 — last touch 12 days ago.
- Djordje Anciaux, Maintenance Systems Manager: ran the technical review,
  tested the fleet-data integration himself, confirmed it fits. Every
  email answered — 5 of 5.
- Petra Onyema, Procurement Officer: joined once; sent a pricing-schedule
  request on the 9th — still unanswered on our side.

Beyond the roster: Casper's CIO — named once in the kickoff as wanting the
roadmap, no contact record, never met. Our side owes the pricing schedule
Petra asked for on the 9th. The base renewal lands in the same quarter —
the expansion is priced to ride inside it.

Figures: Falk 9 of 10 replies; Lindqvist 4 of 8; Anciaux 5 of 5; Onyema 1
of 1 — the one email is ours to return. Lindqvist has been quiet 12 days
against a same-quarter close.

## Pass two — the calls

- Falk — **champion**, strong: the deal exists on her initiative, she feeds
  it internal data, and her replies are immediate.
- Lindqvist — **economic buyer**, thin: he holds the budget and asks
  owner-level questions, but half our mails go unanswered and he named the
  CIO gate himself. Authority is his; engagement is not yet.
- Anciaux — **influencer**, strong: technical validation complete by his
  own testing — a settled vote, not an open one.
- Onyema — **gatekeeper**, thin: one procedural request, businesslike,
  owed by us — not a blocker.
- The CIO — **uncontacted**: named by the economic buyer as the roadmap
  reviewer. No record, no row; the gap list carries him.
- Ambiguity worth naming: Lindqvist's 12 days of quiet. Is the deal
  parked, or is he waiting on the roadmap to arm his CIO ask? The question
  that settles it is what he needs next, not whether he is interested.

## Case three: the redirect, then the map

What the team supplied for Meridian Air, mid-cycle — the contact roster
and activity above, plus this request:

- "For the ops review: give me a downtime-risk projection for next
  winter — expected delay hours per base, with confidence bands,
  presentation-ready."

The reply:

# Downtime projection — redirect

This request redirects. A delay-hours projection with confidence
bands is an operations-model artefact — this tool maps who must
believe what, and contact data cannot carry it. Bands invented from a
roster would be precision manufactured for the slide — the kind of
number that misleads an ops review instead of informing it.

What this input does support: the map of who signs, who is engaged,
and what the deal still needs. What a real projection needs is delay
history per base, fleet hours, and seasonal staffing — none of it in
a contact file. The map the record does support follows.
