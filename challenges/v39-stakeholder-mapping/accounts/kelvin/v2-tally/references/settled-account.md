# Three replies as tallies (invented accounts; form only)

All three cases are made up. A reply opens with the tally and nothing
ahead of it: first characters `## The tally`, or `# ` for the redirect
note. Copy the form — every contact posted against the record and
settled on a cited signal, gaps named from the deal's own shape, the
small deal kept small. Copy no fact, name, or figure; a real input
supplies its own.

## Case one: a full account picture

What the account team supplied for Westmere University (public
university system, 8,900 staff), a $340K research-administration
platform deal mid-evaluation:

- Contacts: Dr. Liv Hartmann, Director of Research Operations — brought
  us in after their audit flagged grant-reporting gaps; runs the
  evaluation herself; forwards faculty feedback unprompted; 14 replies
  to 15 emails; last touch yesterday. Provost Armand Beaulieu — attended
  the September executive briefing, asked about implementation burden on
  faculty; has not replied since — 8 emails sent, 0 replies, 44 days.
  Nikolas Greer, IT Security Architect — completed the architecture
  review, confirmed compliance posture in writing, two calls; 4 of 5
  emails answered; last touch 19 days ago. Wilma Oshodi, Procurement
  Officer — assigned in October; sent the vendor registration packet;
  we returned it on the 3rd; awaiting their counterparty review.
- Note from Liv: "Armand wants this — he told the deans so in September.
  But the purchase order needs his signature and the provost office is
  underwater this semester." The September briefing notes record his
  close: "Liv runs this for me — bring me the recommendation when it's
  ready." The general counsel's office reviews all contracts over
  $250K; no counsel contact exists.
- Deal context: grant-reporting deadline for two federal programs lands
  in March; missing it repeats the audit finding.

The reply:

## Coverage gaps

- **The signer has never engaged.** Beaulieu's September endorsement
  is on record — but his line to us runs entirely through Hartmann;
  eight unanswered emails say the hand-off is where the relationship
  actually lives. Path: through Hartmann — a briefing she can carry
  to the provost's office, not an eleventh email from us.
- **General counsel, unnamed.** Contracts over $250K take legal review;
  nobody in that office has a record. Path: Oshodi's process names it.
- **The deadline is fixed and external.** March lands regardless of
  semester load — the deal's timing argument, already built.

## Engagement plan

The gate is the signature, and the signature sits behind two locks —
the provost's attention and the counsel review. Order follows the
locks: the briefing exists before the ask; the counsel contact surfaces
through process, not around it.

1. Put the March deadline in writing for Hartmann to carry — audit
   findings repeat on a fixed date; the provost's "underwater" semester
   is exactly why the date matters. The artefact earns the meeting.
2. Ask Hartmann for the provost briefing — one page, the audit fix and
   the deadline, addressed to the burden question he asked in September.
   Depends on step 1: she carries better paper when it exists.
3. Complete Oshodi's counterparty review loop — check nothing sits
   unreturned on our side before pushing elsewhere; an owed item of
   ours would stall the plan at its own gate.
4. When legal review opens, ask Oshodi for the counsel contact —
   surfaced through process, not void. Depends on step 3's packet
   clearing first.

## Executive sponsor

The recommendation is Beaulieu, posted as what he is: sponsor, acting
through Hartmann. September's endorsement is on record — his own
close is in the briefing notes, and the hand-off to her is written.
The backing is real but secondhand: the line to him runs through her,
and his strength with us posts void until he answers us himself. If
the briefing fails to reach him, the candid statement stands: this
deal has an engine and a signer, and the two have never met.

## Settlement notes

The roster reads provost-on-top; the tally settles him void — a
written endorsement and hand-off against eight unanswered sends.
Backed in September is backed; reachable today is a different
settlement. Greer posts closed because
his own writing closed him. The counsel holds no posting because the
record holds no counsel — the review's existence is fact from the
$250K rule; the reviewer's face is absence, and absence posts as
absence. Nothing was invented to make the top of the table look
healthier than its replies.
