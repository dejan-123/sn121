# Field memos — worked examples of the form

The four accounts below are invented. Each shows the memo itself:
bold title line first, no section labels after it, second person
throughout — the way you'd brief the seller across a desk. Copy the
shape and the judgment, never the names or figures; a real input
brings its own.

---

## Memo one: a full account

Supplied for Merrivale Furnishings (contract-furniture manufacturer,
1,400 staff), a $620K order-management platform in late evaluation:

- Petra Lindh, Operations Systems Manager — brought us in, runs the
  evaluation, shared order-flow spreadsheets unasked, introduced the
  IT lead; 9 of 10 answered, usually same-day; last touch 3 days ago.
- Gerald Voss, VP Operations — September kickoff: "Petra runs this
  for me — bring me the decision pack when it's ready"; 1 of 7
  answered since; last touch 58 days ago.
- Tomás Ibru, CFO — in CRM only; no mail, no meetings.
- Kira Antol, Information Security Lead — review completed,
  "requirements met, nothing further" in writing; 4 of 5 answered;
  16 days ago.
- Renata Solis, Procurement — assigned in October; onboarding packet
  sent, we returned it 9 days ago; her review open.
- Deal context: anything over Gerald's $400K ceiling needs the CFO.
  TradeLane still in evaluation. Petra's unnamed ops director "will
  want to see the rollout plan" — mentioned twice, never met.

The memo:

**Stakeholder Map — Merrivale Furnishings | $620K, late evaluation**

This deal closes through Petra, and it stalls the day she does. She
brought you in, runs the evaluation, and shared internal data nobody
requested — every live line runs through her. The real question is
the signature: $620K sits above Gerald's $400K ceiling, so it needs
Tomás Ibru, a CFO you have never met. Gerald endorsed you in
September and handed the work to Petra — real backing once, but
second-hand now: a candidate to re-win, not a sponsor in hand. With
TradeLane still evaluating, every quiet week costs you. The map:

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Strength (0–5) | Trajectory | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Petra Lindh | Ops Systems Mgr | Driver | 5 | rising | Started the contact; runs evaluation; shared internals unasked; 9/10 same-day |
| Gerald Voss | VP Operations | Candidate sponsor | 2 | dormant | Endorsed Sept., delegated to Petra in writing; 1/7 in 58 days |
| Tomás Ibru | CFO | Signer needed | 0 | never met | Required above $400K; no record |
| Kira Antol | InfoSec Lead | Gate — closed | 4 | stable | "Requirements met" in her own words |
| Renata Solis | Procurement | Gate — open | 3 | stable | Packet returned; her review running |

Petra's ops director is mentioned by role only, twice — no name, no
record, no row.

The coverage is honest but thin. You owe nobody anything — packet
returned, security closed — yet every path out of this account goes
through one person: single-threaded on Petra. The signer has never
engaged, the ops director is a stated stakeholder you cannot reach
directly, and there is no second door if Petra goes quiet.

Order matters here because Petra's capital is finite — spend her
asks where they move the deal:

1. Build the decision pack Gerald commissioned — the rollout plan,
   the order-flow audit, the signature requirement. His own request
   is the vehicle, and it opens the CFO door in the same move.
2. Have Petra put the rollout plan in front of her director — her
   relationship, and the plan already exists once step one lands.
3. Close Solis's onboarding review — it is open and it gates the PO.
4. When the pack is ready, ask Gerald through Petra for the Tomás
   introduction. Never a cold note to him — an unexpected email asks
   a stranger for the deal and tells Petra you walked past her.

No active sponsor today. Gerald is the candidate — endorsed, then
delegated, now quiet; the pack he commissioned is the route back to
his attention. Tomás is who the seat eventually needs, and he is
nobody to you yet: his engagement is a pull-list item Petra's pack
earns. A sponsor-grade meeting, when the pack lands, is the moment
to send one of your own executives to meet his.

## Memo two: a thin record

Supplied for Calloway Signs (custom-signage shop, 60 staff), a $22K
order-tracking subscription:

- Mara Calloway, owner-operator — replied to inbound, described
  losing two orders to miscommunication, asked detailed integration
  questions; 4 of 5 answered; last touch 8 days ago. "It's my call —
  anything under thirty a year I decide myself."
- Den Hale, shop-floor lead — first demo, one question, nothing
  since; 0 of 2 follow-ups.

The memo:

**Stakeholder Map — Calloway Signs | $22K, early stage**

A small deal inside the buyer's own stated ceiling — Mara decides
under $30K and said so herself. She initiated, named real pain, and
asked specific integration questions. Eight days quiet is a pause,
not a pattern. The honest caveat: she is still your only line —
Den's one appearance widens nothing yet.

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Strength (0–5) | Trajectory | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mara Calloway | Owner-operator | Driver + signer | 4 | stable | Self-initiated; named pain; detailed questions; 4/5 replies |
| Den Hale | Shop-floor lead | Touched only | 1 | cooling | One demo, one question; 0/2 follow-ups |

No roles were mentioned without names.

Nothing is missing for a deal this size — no procurement, legal, or
executive layer applies, and inventing one would be noise. What the
record does show is concentration: the whole relationship rests on
Mara, and there is no second thread if she goes quiet for a quarter.
That is a coverage note, not a blocker.

Cheap moves first — she asked, so answer:

1. Answer her integration questions completely — a thorough reply is
   the cheapest trust on offer.
2. Offer the pilot framed in her own words — "stop losing orders to
   miscommunication" — around the demo she already suggested.
3. In the same conversation, ask who touches orders day to day; if
   Den or anyone else runs the board, that is the thread to warm.

No separate sponsor needed: Mara is driver and signer inside her own
stated authority — say so plainly and close.

## Memo three: the dormant signer

Supplied for Vostra Packaging (food packager, 900 staff), an $880K
warehouse-automation proposal:

- Ilsa Renner, Head of Operations — brought us in two quarters back,
  runs the pilot, forwards internal updates; 8 of 10 answered; last
  touch 4 days ago.
- Marcus Fane, CFO — signed last year's pilot budget; nothing since —
  0 of 6 answered in 110 days; no meetings on record.
- Nina Okoye, IT Security — review open; asked for our architecture
  documentation 12 days ago; still unanswered.
- Deal context: the amount needs CFO sign-off, beyond Ilsa's line.
  An unnamed capex board approves above the CFO's own limit.

The memo:

**Stakeholder Map — Vostra Packaging | $880K, proposal**

Ilsa is carrying this deal alone — she runs your pilot and forwards
everything internal. Two things block it: the signature sits with
Marcus Fane, a CFO whose only act was last year's pilot signature
followed by 110 days of silence, and your side owes Nina Okoye her
architecture documentation, sitting twelve days unanswered. Marcus
endorsed nothing — a signature from last year is history, not backing.

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Strength (0–5) | Trajectory | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ilsa Renner | Head of Ops | Driver | 4 | rising | Brought us in; runs pilot; forwards internals; 8/10 |
| Marcus Fane | CFO | Signer needed | 1 | dormant | Pilot signature a year ago; 0/6 in 110 days |
| Nina Okoye | IT Security | Gate — open | 3 | cooling | Docs requested 12 days ago; our reply owed |

The capex board is named as an approval layer only — no person, no
record, no row.

Your debts come first because they gate the reviewer: Nina's
documentation is owed, and her sign-off waits on it. Beyond that the
coverage is single-threaded — Ilsa is the only living route to
Marcus and to the board above him.

1. Send Nina the architecture documentation — your debt, her gate.
2. Ask Ilsa for the re-warm path to Marcus: a short brief in his
   language — payback period, downtime risk — that she carries into
   his next staff meeting. No cold email; a note he never expected
   asks a stranger for the deal.
3. Then the introduction itself — Ilsa in the room, the brief ahead
   of it, once she agrees the moment is right.

No active sponsor. Marcus is the candidate — a year-old signature,
110 days quiet; Ilsa's brief is the step that earns the room. Until
he engages there is honestly no one to name.

## Memo four: a request the record cannot support

Supplied for Merrivale, mid-cycle:

- The ask: "Sketch Merrivale's internal buying structure — who
  reports into whom — for a leadership slide."

The reply:

**Redirect — org chart for Merrivale Furnishings**

What you asked for is a reporting structure, and what this record
holds is a contact list, activity counts, and meeting notes. Titles
say who people are; nothing here says who sits above whom — an org
chart built from it would be invented lines on your slide.

What the record does support is the stakeholder picture: Petra
driving, Gerald's endorsed-then-delegated backing as a candidate to
re-win, the CFO the deal needs and has never met, the ops director
named by role. For the real org chart, ask Merrivale for their
structure document — or ask Petra, who maps it better than any
record we hold. For a stakeholder map, paste the contact list and
activity notes and the memo writes itself.
