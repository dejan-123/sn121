# Account memos: complete examples and boundaries

These fictional accounts show how to ground every call in evidence. Nothing in an example — names, amounts, events, phrasing — is material for another account. An open action here does not imply the same action is open elsewhere. Read each supplied record on its own terms.

## 1. A dormant signer inside an otherwise healthy account

### Supplied record

Tallmere Ferry Group is evaluating a crew-scheduling platform at $310,000 over two years and is late in procurement. Approvals above $200,000 require Managing Director countersignature under the group's own spending policy.

- Bram Havelock, Fleet Operations Manager, championed the trial across two routes, supplied rota data unprompted and organized the operations review. The export shows 17 messages sent and 15 replies, typically same day. His latest note says he is "still chasing the MD's diary" for the sign-off meeting. We answered yesterday.
- Corinna Voss, Managing Director, attended the February kickoff, called the platform "the right direction for the fleet," and has not written since — nine weeks. Four messages to her drew one acknowledgment. Bram alone has her calendar access.
- Tessa Lindt, Crewing Officer, completed the union-rule compatibility review in writing on October 30 and has asked nothing further.
- Ruben Ash, Harbourmaster (Safety), raised berth-conflict questions during the trial. We sent the berth-protocol annex last Friday; no response is yet recorded.

No competitor is named. The procurement calendar lists a contract decision "this quarter" without a date.

### Reply

**Stakeholder Map — Tallmere Ferry Group | $310K/2yr, late procurement**

At $310,000 the platform sits above the group's $200,000 line, so Corinna Voss's countersignature is mandatory — and she has been silent for nine weeks. Everything else in this account is healthy; the signer is the problem.

Bram Havelock is your champion. He drove the two-route trial, volunteered rota data and organized the operations review — fifteen replies to seventeen messages, same-day. His latest note says he is still chasing the MD's diary, and you answered yesterday, so no debt sits with you.

Corinna Voss attended the February kickoff and called the platform "the right direction," then went quiet: one acknowledgment to four messages across nine weeks. That is a dormant signer — earlier verbal backing, no current exchange — and Bram is the only route to her calendar.

Tessa Lindt closed the union-rule review in writing on October 30; her silence since is completion, not cooling. Ruben Ash's berth questions are an open exchange — the annex went out Friday and awaits his response.

Nobody else is identified; the MD's office is the only access gap that matters.

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Strength | Trajectory | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bram Havelock | Fleet Operations Manager | Champion | 5 | steady | Trial led; 15 replies to 17; chasing MD diary |
| Corinna Voss | Managing Director | Signer — dormant | 1 | dormant | Verbal backing Feb; 1 reply to 4; nine weeks quiet |
| Tessa Lindt | Crewing Officer | Evaluator — check complete | 3 | steady | Union-rule review closed in writing 30 Oct |
| Ruben Ash | Harbourmaster | Evaluator | 3 | steady | Berth questions open; annex sent Friday |

The MD's office is the only role mentioned without a working contact. Coverage is otherwise sound — two evaluators are engaged or closed — but approval above $200,000 physically cannot happen without Corinna's signature.

1. Give Bram what Corinna needs to sign: a one-page summary of trial results, union clearance and the berth-protocol position he can put in front of her. Ask him to confirm the sign-off meeting date rather than asking for her details.
2. In parallel, confirm Ruben received the berth-protocol annex and whether it settles his questions — the exchange is open, not overdue.
3. Keep Tessa informed only if the schedule shifts; her review is closed and needs no courtesy step.

No active sponsor is established — Bram is the champion, and Corinna is the signer whose earlier backing has gone dormant. Re-engaging her runs through Bram alone; do not approach the MD directly. Your own executive belongs in the room only once Bram confirms she will meet.

## 2. A small purchase whose buyer is already engaged

### Supplied record

Hartwell Bakery, a seven-person wholesale bakery, is weighing a $4,900-per-year order-intake kiosk. The owner, Petra Lindqvist, approves equipment subscriptions up to $8,000 herself. She contacted us after a trade fair, sent a week of order logs and booked a counter demo for Thursday. Her open question is whether the kiosk handles split payments. The record holds no message counts.

Dawit Kassa, the part-time Accountant, reviewed the sales-export format and wrote, "This export reconciles with our ledger." No later request, condition or objection follows, and he has not written since. Petra intends to decide after the demo. No purchasing process, tender or legal review appears anywhere in the record.

### Reply

**Stakeholder Map — Hartwell Bakery | $4.9K/yr, counter demo booked**

This purchase sits inside the owner's own authority — Petra Lindqvist approves up to $8,000 and the kiosk costs $4,900, so you already hold the decision-maker.

Petra initiated contact at a trade fair, sent a week of order logs and booked Thursday's counter demo — engaged buyer behaviour, improving as the evaluation advances. No exchange counts are recorded. Her split-payment question should shape the demo.

Dawit Kassa's requirement is finished: he confirmed the export reconciles in writing, attached no condition, and his quiet since is completion — a narrow, transactional contact.

Nobody else is identified and nothing is overdue; the only open item is the split-payment question.

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Strength | Trajectory | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Petra Lindqvist | Owner | Buyer; champion | 4 | improving | Sent order logs; booked demo; authority covers price |
| Dawit Kassa | Accountant | Evaluator — check complete | 2 | steady | Export confirmed reconciled; no new condition |

Coverage fits a purchase this size. Petra decides alone — an executive introduction, committee or second thread would be invented ceremony, and Dawit's finished check earns no follow-up.

1. Build the demo around the order logs Petra sent; verify split-payment handling end to end before Thursday.
2. Use the counter demo to answer her question and agree fit. If she wants to proceed, send subscription terms and an install date.

No separate executive sponsor is needed — Petra is both the engaged buyer and the authorized approver. Say so plainly and close; do not hunt above her.

## 3. Reported interest is different from access, and delegated backing needs renewal

### Supplied record

Quarrygate Climbing Centres is considering a route-setting analytics subscription across its four gyms. There is no final quote; scope and gym coverage are still being discussed. The company's approval process has not been supplied.

- Imogen Vell, Head Routesetter, ran a pilot analysis on two walls, sent last season's injury log unprompted and drafted an internal note comparing us with manual tracking. She answers regularly, though the export shows no totals. Her latest note reads: "I sent the comparison to the ops director. No word back yet." The ops director has never been introduced and is known only by title.
- Marcus Oyelaran, Regional Manager, attended the pilot review, said the analytics "could change how we set," and asked Imogen to run the evaluation. Our following six messages drew one brief acknowledgment; nothing further for six weeks.
- Petra Goundry, Safety Officer, cleared the data-handling protocol for member records. Her note explicitly excludes incident footage pending a separate retention review. She replied to all five of our technical questions. That review has not been completed.
- Tomas Weir, Office Administrator, supplied the invoicing requirements and accepted our amended vendor form. We returned the completed insurance certificate last Wednesday; no confirmation or further request is recorded.

The ops director asked for the comparison but has expressed nothing directly. Nobody has identified who approves subscriptions, and no meeting with the director is scheduled.

### Reply

**Stakeholder Map — Quarrygate Climbing Centres | scope open, pilot stage**

This remains a scoping discussion — no final quote, no established approval path — so naming a signer or inventing a threshold would be premature.

Imogen Vell is your champion: she ran the two-wall pilot, volunteered the injury log and wrote the comparison note — genuine advocacy, though her reach upward is untested. Her report that the ops director requested it establishes a request and a delivery, not endorsement and not a relationship you can use directly.

Marcus Oyelaran voiced support at the pilot review and delegated the evaluation to Imogen — but his record since is one acknowledgment to six messages and six weeks of quiet. He is a sponsor candidate whose backing needs renewal, not an active sponsor, and Imogen is the route back to him.

Petra Goundry is an engaged evaluator — five replies to five technical questions — whose clearance covers member records alone; the incident-footage retention review is a real open condition. Tomas Weir accepted the vendor form and now holds your returned insurance certificate — it awaits his confirmation, so you owe nothing unless he asks.

The ops director appears only as a title: unreached, unnamed, and not yet established as the buyer.

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Strength | Trajectory | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Imogen Vell | Head Routesetter | Champion | 4 | steady | Pilot run; comparison written; regular replies |
| Marcus Oyelaran | Regional Manager | Executive supporter — candidate | 2 | dormant | Delegated; 1 reply to 6; six weeks quiet |
| Petra Goundry | Safety Officer | Evaluator | 3 | steady | 5 replies to 5; records cleared, footage review open |
| Tomas Weir | Office Administrator | Process owner | 3 | steady | Vendor form accepted; certificate under review |

The ops director is the only role raised without a name, and every route to leadership runs through Imogen — a single thread where it matters most. The approval process, the director's name and Marcus's present backing are all unknown; do not infer a board sign-off from the company's four gyms.

1. Ask Imogen what response the director needs to the comparison and who approves a subscription of this size. Hand her a concise, forwardable answer and ask her to arrange a discussion if the director wants one — never approach the director yourself.
2. In parallel, fix the retention-review scope with Petra; keep the cleared records protocol closed while the footage condition resolves.
3. Through Imogen, invite Marcus to revisit what the pilot established and what stays open — renew his support against the route-setting benefit he originally named.
4. Ask Tomas whether the returned certificate suffices; the task is confirming status, not resending supplied work.

No active sponsor is established. Marcus is the candidate because he backed you once, but that backing needs renewal through Imogen. The director is a decision participant to qualify, not a supporter, and Imogen's champion work fills neither executive gap. No escalation from your side is justified until she secures a counterpart and a purpose.

## 4. An active customer sponsor and a separate commercial approver

### Supplied record

Caldera Springs Resort has chosen a water-quality monitoring service, conditional on an upcoming capital-committee decision. The proposed contract is $118,000. Dr. Amara Silven, Guest Experience Director, defended the service to the resort's investment panel and secured a dedicated maintenance budget line for next season. She reviews dashboards with our team every other week. She cannot commit the capital herself.

Felix Brandt, Facilities Lead, runs the installation plan and connected us with the grants officer. He sent the final sensor count yesterday and asked us to confirm the contract covers every unit. We have not answered.

Ruth Kessler, Grants Officer, verified the service qualifies under the resort's maintenance-capital category if the committee approves. She is explicit that the capital committee decides funding. Its members are unnamed and its session date is unknown.

Our operations director joined Amara's earlier investment-panel presentation. No reviewer has requested a data audit, insurance pack or further compliance check.

### Reply

**Stakeholder Map — Caldera Springs Resort | $118K, selected pending capital vote**

The service is selected subject to the capital-committee decision — genuine progress, but selection is not permission to spend.

Amara Silven is an active sponsor: she defended the proposal to the investment panel, won a dedicated budget line and still reviews dashboards with you every other week. She holds no capital authority — her influence is advocacy, not approval.

Felix Brandt is the working champion: he runs the installation plan, connected the grants officer and sent the final sensor count. His question — whether the contract covers every unit — sits with you, so the immediate delay is yours, not his.

Ruth Kessler confirmed the category works if the committee approves — a conditional evaluator, not a decision-maker and not evidence of advocacy.

The capital committee appears only as a body; its members and session date are unknown.

| Contact | Title | Influence tier | Strength | Trajectory | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amara Silven | Guest Experience Director | Active sponsor | 4 | steady | Panel advocacy; budget line won; fortnightly reviews |
| Felix Brandt | Facilities Lead | Champion; delivery lead | 4 | steady | Plan coordinated; introduction made; sensor count sent |
| Ruth Kessler | Grants Officer | Evaluator | 3 | steady | Category confirmed conditionally; committee decides |

The committee is the only role raised without named people. Sponsor and champion are both established — the material gap is the committee's process, timing and access, not executive interest.

1. Confirm the contract's coverage to Felix against his final sensor count — settle exclusions before the price enters the funding discussion.
2. Ask Amara and Felix to learn the committee's date and required material; route the scoped proposal through them and agree who presents.
3. Offer your operations director's support for that presentation if Amara wants it — the earlier joint session supplies a credible route; do not manufacture an executive meeting merely because the contract is large.

Amara is already sponsoring the purchase while the capital committee controls funding — keep those facts separate. Your operations director can reinforce her existing route when asked; nothing here justifies reaching beyond it.

## 5. A reporting diagram needs a reporting source

### Supplied record

Marlwick Botanical Trust requests a diagram of management reporting lines. It provides the names and job titles of a horticultural lead, a visitor-services manager and an estate accountant, with no reporting relationships given. It notes that meeting minutes exist but does not supply them.

### Reply

**Redirect — Marlwick Botanical Trust | reporting-line diagram**

You asked for a diagram of management reporting lines. The names and titles provided do not establish who reports to whom — connecting them would turn assumptions into an organizational chart.

That diagram needs an authoritative source: an approved org chart or reporting-line register from the trust, including any split between formal management and project responsibilities.

A stakeholder map answers a different question — who influences the purchase, how established each relationship is, where access is missing and what to do next. For that, supply the deal context and the actual meeting and correspondence records; knowing minutes exist is not enough to assess the contacts.

## How to carry the examples into another account

Borrow their evidence discipline, not their sentences or verdicts. Caldera Springs contains active sponsorship; Tallmere holds a dormant signer; Quarrygate holds earlier backing that needs renewal; Hartwell needs no separate sponsor. Different outcomes, because the records differ.

Completed checks differ too. Hartwell's export confirmation is finished. Tallmere's union review is closed while a berth question stays open. Quarrygate explicitly leaves a footage review open. "The reviewer is quiet" is never a substitute for reading what was accepted.

Paperwork follows the same rule: Quarrygate's certificate is already returned, so the seller must not be told to supply it again — the customer may still be reviewing it. Never carry a debt, an approval body or an introduction from one worked account into another.
