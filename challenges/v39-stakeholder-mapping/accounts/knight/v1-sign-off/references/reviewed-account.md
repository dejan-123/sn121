# Three replies as sign-offs (invented accounts; form only)

All three cases are made up. A reply opens with the sign-off sheet and
nothing ahead of it: first characters `## Sign-off`, or `# ` for the
redirect note. Copy the form — every contact cleared, held, or rerouted
on a cited signal, gaps named from the deal's own shape, candour
preferred over inflation. Copy no fact, name, or figure; a real input
supplies its own.

## Case one: a full account picture

What the account team supplied for Hollowmead Water Authority
(municipal utility, ~2,700 staff), a $680K asset-management platform
deal, formal procurement — invitation to tender ran in spring, we are
on the shortlist:

- Contacts: Orla Beddows, Asset Strategy Manager — authored the tender's
  technical annex; meets inside procurement rules; answers process
  questions in writing; 9 replies to 10 emails; last touch 5 days ago.
  Declan Muirhead, Director of Engineering — chaired the tender panel;
  publicly neutral in meetings, but asked our team two detailed
  off-agenda questions about legacy SCADA integration; 3 replies to 6
  emails; last touch 17 days ago. Cllr. Patricia Ekwueme — chair of the
  council's infrastructure committee; named in the tender as the
  approving body; has never met us. Ferdinand Ross, Procurement
  Officer — runs the process formally; all communication routed
  through him; 4 of 4 process mails answered same-week.
- Note from Orla, in the official Q&A channel: "The committee decides
  on the technical score plus the community-benefit statement — the
  engineering director writes the score recommendation." The community-
  benefit statement deadline is in three weeks; no committee contact
  is permitted outside formal channels.
- Deal context: two shortlisted vendors; the incumbent's ten-year
  contract is the thing being replaced.

The reply:

## Sign-off

Supplied: a contact roster, activity counts and notes for a $680K

She authored the technical annex our bid is scored against and answers

Figures: Beddows 9 of 10; Muirhead 3 of 6 with two off-agenda
questions; Ekwueme 0 of 0 — and the rules say that is correct.
Community-benefit deadline in three weeks.

## Coverage gaps

- **The approver cannot be met — by design.** Ekwueme's committee
  decides on paper; the coverage problem is not access but what the
  packet argues. Path: the community-benefit statement itself, which
  is the only message the channel carries.
- **The incumbent is the invisible contact.** Ten years of installed
  habit is a stakeholder nobody can email — countered in the technical
  score, not in outreach.
- **Single formal channel.** Every word to this account goes through
  Ross's process — flagged as a structural fact, not a complaint.

## Engagement plan

1. Write the community-benefit statement as a scored artefact, not a
   brochure — the deadline is three weeks and it is the only page the
   approver ever reads. It carries the unmet-committee gap.
2. Answer Muirhead's two off-agenda questions in the formal Q&A —
   his real diligence, answered where the rules allow it, building the
   technical score he must write.
3. Keep every channel rule exactly — the fastest way to lose a formal
   tender is an informal approach; compliance is the access strategy.
4. Through Ross, confirm the committee timetable — the date the packet
   must land by, asked in the channel that exists.

## Executive sponsor

No sponsor exists and none can be courted — said plainly. The committee
approves a score, not a relationship; the closest thing to a sponsor
this deal can build is Muirhead's recommendation, earned through
answered questions inside the rules. The candid line stands: in a
formal tender, sponsorship is a score, and the score is being written
now.

## Reviewer's note

The roster reads as four contacts; the sign-off reads as two writers
and two channels. Ekwueme is held, not cleared — a named approver with
a forbidden channel is a gap, stated as one. Ross clears controller,
not blocker, because his mail works. The incumbent was never reviewed:
ten years of habit holds no record and takes no row — it lives in the
gaps where unmet influence belongs.

## Case three: the redirect

What the team supplied for Hollowmead Water, mid-tender:

- A request: "Arrange a site tour of the water works for our exec
  team and draft the invitation letter to the utility's board —
  relationship-building ahead of the award decision."

The reply:

# Site tour and invitation — redirect

This request redirects, and the record itself gives the reason. A
site tour for our executives and an invitation to their board are
relationship artefacts — different work from a stakeholder map — and
in this account they are worse than off-scope: the tender's formal
channel forbids contact outside the procurement process, so a
relationship-building letter to the board would breach the rules the
score depends on.

What this input does support: the map — Beddows firm, Muirhead
building inside the rules, Ekwueme unmet by rule — which already says
the only relationship this deal can build is a written score. What a
real post-award engagement needs: the award itself. The plan's step 1,
the community-benefit statement, reaches the same board members
through the only door that is open — that is the tour that counts.
