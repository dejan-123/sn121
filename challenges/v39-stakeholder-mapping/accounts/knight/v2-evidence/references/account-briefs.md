# Account briefs: complete examples and boundaries

These fictional records illustrate explanations grounded in evidence. The subjects, amounts, events and language of an example are not material for another account. In particular, an open action here does not imply the same action is open elsewhere. Read the supplied record anew.

## 1. Approval by a committee, with an active sponsor who cannot sign alone

### Supplied record

Arden Ceramic Cooperative wants a kiln-maintenance planning service, quoted at $86,000 annually. It is in final evaluation. Five elected trustees must approve purchases above $60,000 by majority vote; no individual can approve this amount.

- Mira Chen, Workshop Coordinator, organized the trial across three studios, shared maintenance logs unprompted and introduced us to both the treasurer and the membership chair. The record shows 14 messages sent and 12 replies, normally within one business day. Her latest message asks whether the quote includes weekend training. We have not answered it.
- Ellis Ward, Membership Chair, told Mira to manage the trial. Since then Ellis has booked the trustee presentation, secured attendance by four trustees and offered to introduce the service at that meeting. Two of our three messages received replies; the latest reply was yesterday.
- Jules Okeke, Treasurer, said the operating-expense treatment is acceptable. Their latest note says this is an accounting view, not purchase approval; the trustees must still vote. No correspondence totals are supplied.
- Pia Moreno, Studio Safety Lead, accepted the trial's maintenance-alert procedure. She then asked whether the proposed production service also includes a separate incident-reporting function. The account team has not answered that new question. There is no note reopening the accepted procedure.

No competitor, purchasing department or legal-review requirement is mentioned. The committee presentation is scheduled for the following Thursday. The quote has been supplied; a training timetable has not.

### Reply

# Arden Ceramic Cooperative — an active sponsor, with the trustee vote still ahead

## Account read

The $86,000 annual purchase requires a majority trustee vote because it exceeds the cooperative's $60,000 threshold. Neither the project lead nor the treasurer can authorize it alone.

Mira Chen is your champion. She organized the trial, volunteered maintenance records and brought two decision participants into the conversation. Twelve replies to fourteen messages, normally within a business day, support a strong, steady relationship. Her latest training question is waiting on you; it is not evidence that she has become less responsive.

Ellis Ward is an active executive supporter. Although Ellis assigned the trial to Mira, the subsequent actions matter: a trustee presentation is booked, attendance has been secured and Ellis will introduce the proposal. Delegation here coexists with continuing sponsorship. Two replies to three messages support a working relationship, improving as access becomes concrete.

Jules Okeke has cleared the accounting treatment, not approved the purchase. The note explicitly reserves that decision to the trustees. This is a useful evaluator relationship with no response counts recorded.

Pia Moreno accepted the maintenance-alert procedure. The separate question about incident reporting is still yours to answer; it does not undo that acceptance. Her recorded engagement supports a working relationship, but there is no response history from which to claim increasing warmth.

The other trustees are not individually identified. You owe Mira an answer on weekend training and Pia an answer about incident reporting.

## Relationship map

| Contact | Deal role | Strength and direction | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mira Chen | Champion | 5, steady | Trial organized; two introductions; 12 replies to 14 messages |
| Ellis Ward | Active sponsor; committee member | 3, improving | Presentation and attendance secured; 2 replies to 3 messages |
| Jules Okeke | Accounting evaluator | 3, steady | Expense treatment accepted; trustee approval still required |
| Pia Moreno | Safety evaluator | 3, steady | Procedure accepted; separate feature question open |

## Coverage and next steps

You have several live relationships, so this is not an account dependent on Mira alone. The remaining access gap is the unidentified trustees and their questions. Their votes are necessary; an accounting clearance is not a substitute. The record establishes no separate procurement or legal task.

1. Answer Mira's training question and supply the proposed timetable before the presentation. Make clear what the quote includes and price any option separately rather than assuming inclusion.
2. In parallel, answer Pia's incident-reporting question from the service specification. Preserve the accepted maintenance procedure and describe any additional requirement separately.
3. Ask Ellis to confirm which trustees will attend and collect their questions before Thursday. Use the meeting Ellis has already arranged rather than requesting another round of introductions.
4. Present the proposal against those questions and ask the committee to record its decision. Jules's accounting note can support the discussion but cannot replace the vote.

## Sponsorship

Ellis is actively sponsoring the proposal through access and advocacy. Mira remains the working champion; the trustees collectively hold approval. No need for one of our executives is established by this record, so the planned committee presentation is the appropriate next forum.

The distinction that matters is between backing and authority: Ellis can advocate, Jules can clear treatment, and only the committee can approve this amount.

## 2. A small purchase whose buyer is already engaged

### Supplied record

Cedar Lantern Repair is considering a $6,800 annual appointment-booking tool for a four-person repair shop. Owner Iona Bell can authorize software subscriptions up to $10,000 herself. She contacted us, supplied two anonymized booking records and arranged a walkthrough for Monday. Her open question is how existing bookings would be imported. The record gives no message counts or response-time statistics.

Tariq Moss, the part-time Bookkeeper, checked the export of daily takings and said, "That export is sufficient for our books." There is no later request, condition or accounting objection. Tariq has not written since. The owner will make the purchase decision after the walkthrough. There is no purchasing department, tender process or legal review in the record.

### Reply

# Cedar Lantern Repair — the buyer can decide after Monday's walkthrough

## Account read

The $6,800 subscription is within Iona Bell's stated $10,000 authority. You already have the person who can make this decision.

Iona initiated contact, supplied booking records and arranged Monday's walkthrough. Those are useful actions, supporting an engaged buyer relationship at 4, improving as the evaluation advances. There are no message counts or response-time figures to quote. Her import question should shape the walkthrough.

Tariq Moss's accounting requirement is satisfied: he accepted the daily-takings export and added no condition. His subsequent silence does not reopen it. This is a limited, transactional relationship at 2, steady, with no evidence of advocacy or opposition.

No unidentified stakeholder or overdue reply is recorded. The import question remains to be answered.

## Relationship map

| Contact | Deal role | Strength and direction | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iona Bell | Buyer and evaluation lead | 4, improving | Supplied records; booked walkthrough; authority covers price |
| Tariq Moss | Accounting evaluator; check complete | 2, steady | Export explicitly accepted; no new condition |

## Coverage and next steps

Coverage fits this purchase. Iona can decide without an executive introduction or additional buying committee; relying on the owner is normal here.

1. Prepare the import demonstration using Iona's supplied records, confirming any limitations before Monday.
2. Use the booked walkthrough to answer the import question and agree whether the tool fits. If Iona wants to proceed, supply the subscription terms and agree a start date.

## Sponsorship

No separate executive sponsor is needed. Iona is both the engaged buyer and the person authorized to approve this purchase. Tariq's completed check needs no further sign-off.

## 3. Reported interest is different from access, and delegated backing needs renewal

### Supplied record

North Quay Costume Archive is assessing a collection-photography service. There is no final quote; options and capacity are still being discussed. Its governance and spending thresholds have not been supplied.

- Leona Price, Collection Manager, organized a sample shoot, sent a catalog of fragile items and wrote an internal comparison of providers. She replies regularly, but the export contains no totals. Her latest note says: "The museum director asked to see the comparison. I delivered it. I haven't had a decision." The museum director has never been introduced and is identified only by role.
- Arun Dey, Head of Programs, attended the initial sample shoot and said the service could improve the lending program. He asked Leona to coordinate the evaluation. Our subsequent five messages received one short acknowledgment; there has been no further engagement for seven weeks.
- Celia Roth, Conservator, approved handling the proposed fabrics under the trial procedure. Her note explicitly excludes metal accessories pending a separate abrasion test. She has answered four of our four technical messages. That test has not been performed.
- Noah Kemp, Registrar, supplied the loan-record requirements and accepted our revised inventory template. The team sent Noah the completed supplier registration form last Tuesday. No acceptance or additional request has been recorded.

The director has requested the comparison but has not expressed support in any direct communication. Nobody has identified who can authorize a purchase. There is no confirmed deadline or meeting with the director.

### Reply

# North Quay Costume Archive — the evaluation has support, but approval and access are unresolved

## Account read

This is an options discussion without a final price or established approval path. It would be premature to name a signer or invent a spending threshold.

Leona Price is your working champion: she organized the sample shoot, shared collection information and wrote an internal comparison. That is substantial advocacy, supporting strength 4, steady, although executive access is still untested. There are no reply totals to quote. Her report that the director requested the comparison establishes a request and delivery, not endorsement or a relationship with the director.

Arun Dey expressed support at the sample shoot and delegated coordination to Leona. The later record is much weaker: one acknowledgment to five messages and seven weeks without further engagement. He is a potential sponsor to re-engage, not an active sponsor today; the relationship is 2, dormant. Leona is the route back to the work Arun asked her to manage.

Celia Roth is an engaged evaluator, with four replies to four technical messages. Her approval covers fabrics only. The metal-accessory test remains a real condition, so the relationship is 3, steady and the review is partially complete.

Noah Kemp accepted the inventory template. The supplier registration form has already been sent and now awaits a response; you do not owe another copy unless Noah requests one. The exchanges support a working process relationship at 3, steady.

The director remains unreached and unnamed. No person is established as the final buyer.

## Relationship map

| Contact | Deal role | Strength and direction | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leona Price | Champion | 4, steady | Sample shoot arranged; internal comparison written; access untested |
| Arun Dey | Executive supporter; sponsor candidate | 2, dormant | Earlier support and delegation; 1 reply to 5 messages; seven weeks quiet |
| Celia Roth | Conservator; evaluator | 3, steady | 4 replies to 4 messages; fabrics approved, accessory test outstanding |
| Noah Kemp | Registrar; process owner | 3, steady | Template accepted; registration form returned, response pending |

## Coverage and next steps

The technical work has more than one live contact, but access to leadership is single-threaded through Leona. The director's name, the approval process and Arun's current willingness to back the project are unknown. Do not infer a finance committee from the institution's size.

1. Ask Leona what response the director needs to the comparison and who authorizes this type of purchase. Offer a concise answer she can forward and ask her to arrange a discussion if the director wants one. Do not send the director an unsolicited message.
2. In parallel, agree the abrasion-test scope with Celia. Keep the accepted fabric procedure separate so that resolving the remaining condition does not restart completed work.
3. Through Leona, invite Arun to review what the sample shoot established and what remains unresolved. Renew his backing against the lending-program benefit he raised, rather than assuming the old endorsement still provides it.
4. Ask Noah whether the returned registration form is sufficient when agreeing the administrative next step. The task is to establish review status, not to resend work already supplied.

## Sponsorship

No active executive sponsor is established. Arun is a candidate because he previously supported the work, but his backing needs renewal through Leona. The director is an unreached decision participant to qualify, with neither advocacy nor spending authority established. Leona's active champion work does not fill either executive gap.

Hold off on executive escalation from our side until Leona secures a counterpart and a useful purpose. The plan separates three different unknowns: current backing, access and final authority.

## 4. An active customer sponsor and a separate commercial approver

### Supplied record

Palewood Observatory has selected a specialist instrument-calibration service, subject to an upcoming annual allocation decision. The proposed fee is $145,000. Dr. Sora Malik, Science Director, has defended the service at the research council and obtained a reserved equipment-maintenance slot. She meets with us every fortnight. She cannot commit the allocation.

Bram Ellis, Operations Lead, coordinates the service plan and introduced us to the finance administrator. He sent the final equipment list yesterday and asked us to confirm that the stated fee covers all units. We have not answered.

Anika Vale, Finance Administrator, checked the service classification and confirmed it can be charged to the maintenance allocation if approved. She explicitly says the allocation committee makes the funding decision. The committee members are not named and its meeting date is unknown.

Our technical director attended Sora's previous research-council presentation. No reviewer has requested a security questionnaire, contract pack or additional compliance check.

### Reply

# Palewood Observatory — sponsorship is active; funding remains a committee decision

## Account read

The $145,000 service is selected subject to the annual allocation decision. Selection is progress, but it is not permission to spend.

Sora Malik is an active sponsor: she defended the proposal and secured a maintenance slot, and the fortnightly meetings establish continuing engagement. That supports a strong relationship at 4, steady. She does not hold allocation authority.

Bram Ellis is the working champion. He coordinates the plan, made an introduction and supplied the equipment list. His latest fee-coverage question is waiting on you, so the immediate delay is yours. This supports strength 4, steady; the record gives no response totals.

Anika Vale is a finance evaluator, not the buyer. Her classification confirmation is conditional on the committee's funding decision. Her correspondence supports a working relationship at 3, steady, without evidence of advocacy.

The allocation committee remains unnamed and its meeting date is unknown. You owe Bram an answer about the quoted scope.

## Relationship map

| Contact | Deal role | Strength and direction | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sora Malik | Active sponsor | 4, steady | Council advocacy, reserved slot and continuing meetings |
| Bram Ellis | Champion and delivery lead | 4, steady | Plan coordinated; introduction made; equipment list supplied |
| Anika Vale | Finance evaluator | 3, steady | Classification accepted conditionally; committee controls funding |

## Coverage and next steps

The sponsor and working relationships are established. The material gap is the allocation committee's process, timing and access, not a lack of executive interest.

1. Confirm the fee's scope to Bram against the final equipment list. Resolve any exclusions before using the price in the funding discussion.
2. Ask Sora and Bram to identify the committee's decision date and required material. Supply the scoped proposal through them and agree who will present it.
3. Offer our technical director's support for that presentation if Sora wants it. The earlier joint presentation provides a credible relationship and purpose; do not create an additional executive meeting merely because the fee is substantial.

## Sponsorship

Sora is already sponsoring the purchase, while the allocation committee controls funding. Our technical director can support her existing presentation route if requested. Neither Anika's classification check nor Sora's advocacy should be described as a completed funding approval.

## 5. A reporting diagram needs a reporting source

### Supplied record

Marlwick Botanical Trust asks for a diagram of management reporting lines. It supplies the names and job titles of a horticultural lead, visitor-services manager and estate accountant, without reporting relationships. It says meeting notes exist but does not provide them.

### Reply

# Marlwick Botanical Trust — reporting-line diagram

The names and titles do not establish who reports to whom. Drawing arrows from them would turn assumptions into an organizational chart.

An approved organization chart or a reporting-line register from the trust would support the diagram. Please supply that source, including any distinction between formal management and project responsibilities.

A stakeholder map would answer a different question: who influences the purchase, how established each relationship is, where access is missing and what engagement should happen next. For that, provide the deal context and the actual meeting and correspondence records. Knowing that notes exist is not enough to assess the contacts.

## How to carry the examples into another account

Use their evidence discipline, not their sentences or decisions. Arden and Palewood contain active sponsorship; North Quay contains earlier backing that needs renewal. Cedar needs no separate sponsor. Those are different outcomes because the records differ.

Completed checks also differ. Cedar's accounting requirement is done. Arden's accepted procedure coexists with a separate question. North Quay explicitly leaves an accessory test open. "The reviewer is quiet" is not a substitute for reading what was accepted.

The same applies to paperwork: North Quay's form has been returned, so the seller must not be instructed to supply it again. The customer may still be reviewing it. Never transfer the debt, approval body or introduction from one worked account into another.
