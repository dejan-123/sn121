# Three replies as decision logs (invented accounts; form only)

All three cases are made up. A reply opens with the log and nothing ahead
of it: first characters `## Decisions`, or `# ` for the redirect note. Copy
the form — every contact logged to tier and strength on a cited signal,
gaps named from the deal's own shape, the small deal kept small. Copy no
fact, name, or figure; a real input supplies its own.

## Case one: a full account picture

What the account team supplied for Aldervane Group (a 9-firm accounting
consortium, ~2,300 staff), a $420K practice-management platform deal at
contract stage:

- Contacts: Solveig Renner, Managing Partner of the largest member firm —
  ran our first demo personally, brought two other firms' partners to the
  second, sends internal utilisation data unprompted; 10 replies to 11
  emails; last touch 2 days ago. Harland Mbeki, Consortium Operations
  Director — coordinates across member firms; attends every call, answers
  reliably but briefly; 8 replies to 9 emails; last touch 6 days ago.
  Quentin Vo, Chair of the Partner Council — met once at the kickoff five
  weeks ago, asked about firm-level data separation, nothing since.
  Brigitte Asham, Procurement Lead — sent the consortium's standard vendor
  terms on the 4th; we returned markups on the 7th; she acknowledged and
  said legal review follows.
- Note from Harland: "Each member firm signs separately — Solveig's is the
  anchor; the others watch her." The consortium's external counsel reviews
  contracts jointly, but no counsel name or contact exists.
- Deal context: two smaller member firms are already evaluating a cheaper
  alternative for their practices; Solveig's signature sets the precedent.

The reply:

## Decisions

Supplied: contacts, activity counts and notes for a $420K platform deal
across a 9-firm consortium — a stakeholder read of a live, late-stage deal.

D1. Solveig Renner, Managing Partner (largest firm) — driver, live:
initiated the demo, imported two partner firms, shares utilisation data
unprompted; 10 of 11 answered; touch 2 days ago.
D2. Harland Mbeki, Consortium Operations Director — evaluator, live:
every call, 8 of 9 answered, coordinates the firms — the deal's router,
not its signer.
D3. Quentin Vo, Partner Council Chair — quiet: one kickoff five weeks
ago, one governance question, nothing since. The record cannot say if he
is satisfied or gone — the question that settles it is whether his
data-separation concern was answered.
D4. Brigitte Asham, Procurement Lead — control point, open: sent standard
terms the 4th, we returned markups the 7th, legal review next on her
word — process moving, neutral.
D5. Member-firm partners — no record: Solveig brought two to a demo;
unnamed, unlogged — the gap list carries them.
D6. External counsel — no record: named as reviewing jointly, no name or
contact — the gap list carries it.

Off the record: two member firms are evaluating a cheaper alternative —
competitive pressure inside the consortium itself. Solveig's signature is
the precedent the others watch.

Figures: Renner 10 of 11; Mbeki 8 of 9; Vo 0 of 0 since the kickoff —
thirty-five days quiet. Two firms are shopping elsewhere while nine wait
on one signature.

# Aldervane Group — $420K consortium deal: anchor driver, two firms shopping

## Stakeholder table

| Name | Role | Tier | Strength | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solveig Renner | Managing Partner, anchor firm | Driver | Live | Ran first demo; brought 2 partner firms; shares utilisation data; 10/11 replies |
| Harland Mbeki | Consortium Operations Dir | Evaluator | Live | Attends every call; routes across firms; 8/9 replies |
| Quentin Vo | Partner Council Chair | Quiet | Absent | One kickoff 5 weeks ago; data-separation question; silent since |
| Brigitte Asham | Procurement Lead | Control point | Faint | Terms sent 4th; markups returned 7th; legal review pending |

## Coverage gaps

- **Two member-firm partners, unnamed.** Solveig brought them to a demo;
  they hold no record. In a consortium each firm signs separately — the
  unmet partners are uncovered signatories. Path: through Solveig, who
  already recruits them.
- **External counsel, unnamed.** Joint legal review follows Asham's
  process; no name or channel exists. Path: Asham, when markups return.
- **Vo's open question.** His data-separation concern from the kickoff
  was never answered in the record — a quiet chair holding an
  unresolved governance question.
- **Single anchor.** The deal's logic is precedent: if the two shopping
  firms defect before Solveig signs, the anchor case weakens. Not a
  persona gap — a sequencing one.

## Engagement plan

1. Give Solveig the answer to Vo's data-separation question — a firm-level
   isolation brief she can carry to the council. It closes his open
   concern and arms her next recruitment round.
2. Return Asham's markups with a named contact for the counsel review —
   keeps the procurement track moving and surfaces the unnamed counsel.
3. Ask Solveig to bring the two evaluating firms to a working session —
   the competitive threat lives in the consortium; the driver who
   recruits is the counter.
4. Re-engage Vo directly once — one short note answering his five-week-old
   question with the isolation brief attached; a quiet chair who asked
   a real question deserves the answer, not a nurture stream.

## Executive sponsor

Solveig Renner, and the record argues it without strain: she initiated,
she recruits, she shares internals unprompted, and her firm anchors the
signature others watch. The candid caveat is scope — she sponsors her
firm's signature, not the consortium's. The consortium-level sponsor the
deal ultimately wants is Vo's council, which is why steps 1 and 4 exist.

## Log notes

The titles read Vo-on-top; the calls read Renner-on-top — ten replies
against his silence. Asham is a control point mid-process, not a blocker;
her review is moving. The two demo partners and the counsel hold no rows
because they hold no record — naming them in the table would score people
the deal has never touched.

## Case two: faint input

What the team supplied for Odense & Vale (two-partner tax advisory, 60
staff), a $22K workflow deal:

- One contact: Marisol Thwaite, Partner — joined a webinar in September,
  replied to our follow-up, asked two detailed pricing questions, then
  booked a call for next Tuesday.
- CRM shows our five emails sent, two replies — both hers.
- Note: "Other partner is Jens Odense — semi-retired, no email on file."

The reply:

## Decisions

Supplied: one contact and sparse history for a $22K workflow deal at a
two-partner advisory — faint: one active thread, one silent partner.

D1. Marisol Thwaite, Partner — driver-forming, active: webinar to
reply to booked call in her own moves; 2 of 5 answered; both replies
carried real questions.
D2. Jens Odense, Partner — unmet: semi-retired, no email on file,
named only in the account note — a co-signer with no channel.

Off the record: at $22K between two partners the approval chain is the
two of them — nothing else is owed and nothing else is missing.

Figures: two partners, one with a channel, one without.

# Odense & Vale — $22K workflow deal: one partner engaged, one unreachable

## Stakeholder table

| Name | Role | Tier | Strength | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marisol Thwaite | Partner | Driver (forming) | Active | Self-moved webinar→reply→call; pricing questions specific; call booked Tuesday |
| Jens Odense | Partner | Unmet | Absent | Semi-retired; no email on file; co-owner by name only |

## Coverage gaps

- **Half the signature unreachable.** In a two-partner firm, an
  unreachable partner is half the decision — flagged plainly. Path:
  Thwaite, on Tuesday's call.
- No procurement, legal, or executive layer exists at this size — said
  plainly; inventing one would pad a $22K deal into a fiction.

## Engagement plan

1. Run Tuesday's call on her pricing questions — they were specific;
   answer them specifically.
2. On that call, ask how the two partners decide — the Odense question,
   asked as process not pressure.
3. If a co-sign is needed, offer the short partner-brief for Thwaite to
   hand over — two pages, no meeting demanded of a semi-retired man.

## Executive sponsor

Thwaite, by elimination and by evidence: the only engaged partner, moving
under her own power. Whether a sponsor is needed at $22K is itself the
honest question — this deal wants a decision, not a sponsor program.

## Log notes

Two replies against five sends reads faint until the replies' content is
weighed — both carried buying questions. Odense is logged unmet
because that is what he is; at this size that is a process note, not a
crisis. Nothing was invented to fill the roster.

## Case three: the redirect

What the team supplied for Aldervane Group, mid-cycle:

- A request: "Draft the outreach email sequence for the five member
  firms we haven't touched — three touches each, pain-led, ready to
  send."

The reply:

# Outreach sequence — redirect

This request redirects. The map reads relationships; a ready-to-send
outreach sequence is a different artefact — copy, not coverage. Worse,
this data shows why sending it would misfire: the two member firms
already evaluating an alternative are inside Solveig Renner's own
consortium, and a absent pain-led sequence around the driver who is
recruiting them burns the warmest path the deal owns.

What this input does support: the stakeholder map itself — tiers,
strengths, the gap list naming which firms are unmet and the brokered
route to each. What a real input for outreach needs: a clear target list
the account team chose with eyes on the politics, plus what each target
already heard. The plan's step 3 — Solveig bringing the two wavering
firms to a working session — reaches the same people through a better
door.
