# Three replies as dispositions (invented accounts; form only)

All three cases are made up. A reply opens with the dispositions and
nothing ahead of it: first characters `## Dispositions`, or `# ` for the
redirect note. Copy the form — every contact fired to a tier on a cited
trigger, gaps named from the deal's own shape, an opponent named plainly
where the record shows one. Copy no fact, name, or figure; a real input
supplies its own.

## Case one: a full account picture

What the account team supplied for Vardo Quays (container port operator,
~1,900 staff), a $560K safety-compliance platform deal, late evaluation:

- Contacts: Marisol Tenn, Head of Terminal Safety — ran the incident-
  reporting overhaul that surfaced the need; owns the evaluation; sends
  incident statistics unprompted; arranged our site walk-through; 11
  replies to 12 emails; last touch 4 days ago. Edmond Varga, Terminal
  Operations Director — controls the operating budget; came to the
  September briefing, said "the incumbent's renewal is also on my desk";
  2 replies to 6 emails; last touch 33 days ago. Pietr Maughan, Shift
  Superintendents' rep — attended the walk-through, told our engineer
  "the crews won't log into another system for anything," has declined
  two follow-up sessions; 1 reply to 5 emails. Zainab Ko, Procurement
  Manager — issued the vendor questionnaire in October; we returned it
  complete on the 28th; she confirmed receipt, evaluation committee
  meets mid-month.
- Note from Marisol: "Edmond decides. But if the superintendents tell
  the crews it doesn't work, it doesn't matter what I sign."
- Deal context: incumbent vendor's renewal lands same quarter; the port's
  regulator fined them twice this year on reporting gaps.

The reply:

## Dispositions

Supplied: a contact roster, activity counts and notes for a $560K
compliance deal at a port operator — a stakeholder read of a late
evaluation with an incumbent in the room.

Marisol Tenn, Head of Terminal Safety: fires mobilizer, high — she
created the evaluation, feeds it incident data unprompted, arranged the
site visit, answers 11 of 12. The deal is her mandate.

Edmond Varga, Terminal Operations Director: fires budget-owner, none —
he named the incumbent's renewal on his desk himself, then went quiet:
2 of 6, thirty-three days. Budget authority is his; engagement is not.

Pietr Maughan, Shift Superintendents' rep: fires unresponsive — "crews
won't log into another system," two declined sessions, 1 of 5. The
record cannot call him a blocker by intent; by effect, on his own
words, he is the adoption risk — void of any signal he supports us.

Zainab Ko, Procurement Manager: fires checkpoint, faint — questionnaire
exchanged, committee date named; process moving, neutral.

Unlisted: the crews Maughan speaks for — real, unnamed, unmet; the gap
list carries them. The incumbent — named by the buyer, the rival in the
room. The regulator — external pressure, two fines this year, the
deadline the deal can lean on.

Figures: Tenn 11 of 12; Varga 2 of 6 in thirty-three days; Maughan 1 of
5 with two refusals. The deal's carrier is high, its signer is none,
and its floor is hostile.

## Coverage gaps

- **The floor is unmet.** The crews who must log in have never been
  touched; their only voice is a rep who says they won't. Marisol's own
  note names the risk — adoption, not signature, kills this deal.
  Path: through Tenn's safety work, where the crews' pain already lives.
- **The buyer is carrying the incumbent.** Varga's desk holds two
  options and his engagement is faint — the rival is not a gap we can
  meet, it is the comparison we must arm Tenn to win.
- **Authority chain is short and known** — port ops decide at director
  level; no board or counsel is implied at this size, and none is
  invented.

## Engagement plan

1. Arm Tenn's case with the regulator pressure — the two fines this year
   against the incumbent's two renewals; the dossier Varga cannot wave
   off because it is already on his desk's problem list.
2. Propose the crew-floor session through Tenn, not Maughan — a shift
   supervisor's objection is answered by crews, not by selling at him;
   Tenn's safety mandate is the door he opened.
3. Ask Tenn for Varga's real question — thirty-three days of quiet on
   an either/or decision means the question he is weighing was never
   asked back. The artefact: renewal-vs-change risk, one page.
4. Track Ko's committee — procedural, dated; one check-in, not a chase.

## Executive sponsor

Edmond Varga is the recommendation by seat, not by evidence — he owns
the budget and is the deal's deciding signature. The record says he is
none and holding the rival's offer; the candid read is that this deal
currently has a mobilizer, a gate, and no sponsor. Steps 1 and 3 exist
to convert him; until they land, nobody in this account sponsors
anything but the status quo.

## Trigger notes

The titles read Varga over Tenn; the triggers fire the reverse — her
11 of 12 and owned mandate against his 33 days. Maughan fires
unresponsive, not blocker: the record shows refusal, and the map says
what it shows, nothing harsher and nothing softer. The crews fired no
trigger because they hold no record — they are the gap that decides
whether anything signed is ever used.

## Case three: the redirect

What the team supplied for Vardo Quays, mid-cycle:

- A request: "Score every account in our port-sector territory against
  this framework and rank them by readiness — one table, top to
  bottom."

The reply:

# Territory ranking — redirect

This request redirects. A territory ranking is a portfolio artefact —
forty accounts side by side — and this tool maps the inside of one
account: its contacts, tiers, gaps, and next moves. This input carries
one account's worth of signals; stretching it into a sector table would
rank accounts on contacts the other thirty-nine never supplied.

What this input does support: this account's map — Tenn high, Varga
none, Maughan refusing — which is the readiness read for Vardo Quays
itself. What a real ranking needs is the same contact-level input for
each territory account: rosters, activity, recent notes. Send the
sector list with that data attached and the ranking is honest work —
one map per row, then the comparison the table wants.
