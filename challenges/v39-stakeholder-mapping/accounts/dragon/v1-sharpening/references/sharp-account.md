# Three replies sharpened (invented accounts; form only)

All three cases are made up. A reply opens with the sharpening and
nothing ahead of it: first characters `## Sharpening`, or `# ` for the
redirect note. Copy the form — every contact sharpened to the call the
record answers, gaps named from the deal's own shape, the candid
sentence preferred over the inflated one. Copy no fact, name, or
figure; a real input supplies its own.

## Case one: a full account picture

What the account team supplied for Caprivi Hotels (regional hotel group,
19 properties, ~3,300 staff), a $480K workforce-scheduling platform deal
mid-evaluation, six months after Caprivi absorbed a smaller chain:

- Contacts: Beatrix Ohlendorf, Group Ops Director — ran the RFP, attends
  every call, shares occupancy and staffing data unprompted; arranged
  the intro to the absorbed chain's ops lead; 13 replies to 14 emails;
  last touch 3 days ago. Massimo Ferro, Group COO — joined one call
  early in the process, asked about multi-property rollout cost, then
  went quiet; 2 replies to 8 emails; last touch 51 days ago. Greta
  Lindqvist-Nair, Ops Lead of the acquired chain — met once at Beatrix's
  introduction, sent three detailed emails about the absorbed group's
  scheduling pain, asked to be included in the pilot; last touch 16
  days ago. Holger Steinfeld, Head of Procurement — sent the vendor
  onboarding forms on the 12th; we returned them on the 14th; he
  confirmed receipt and named "committee review next."
- Note from Beatrix: "The merger doubled my property count but not my
  budget authority — anything above $300K goes to Massimo and the
  owners' rep." No owners' representative contact exists; the absorbed
  chain's former owner sits on the owners' board, unnamed.
- Deal context: the absorbed chain still runs the legacy scheduler;
  consolidation savings is the stated business case.

The reply:

## Sharpening

Supplied: a contact roster, activity counts and notes for a $480K
scheduling deal at a merged hotel group — a stakeholder read of a
mid-evaluation deal in a two-world account.

"Title says Beatrix Ohlendorf, Group Ops Director." The record answers:
she ran the RFP, attends everything, shares data unprompted, and made
the intro across the merger line herself — so the contact becomes
pusher, hot. Cannot answer: her authority ends at $300K, half the
deal — the signer question stays open below.

"Title says Massimo Ferro, Group COO." The record answers: one early
call, one cost question, then fifty-one days of silence on 2 of 8
replies — so the contact becomes decision-maker, dead. The title asks
"our sponsor"; the record cannot answer that — the question that
settles it is whether the owners' rep beside him even knows our name.

"Title says Greta Lindqvist-Nair, Ops Lead, acquired chain." The record
answers: three detailed emails, a self-requested pilot seat, pain she
volunteered — so the contact becomes assessor, warm, and the
deal's second thread by her own asking.

"Title says Holger Steinfeld, Head of Procurement." The record answers:
forms sent, ours returned, "committee review next" — so the contact
becomes control-point, cool, process moving; not a blocker, a pipeline.

Unasked: the owners' representative — named in Beatrix's own sentence,
above her authority, holding no record. The absorbed chain's former
owner — on the owners' board, unnamed. Both sharpen to the gap list.

Figures: Ohlendorf 13 of 14; Ferro 2 of 8 in fifty-one days; her
authority $300K against a $480K ask — the gap between them is the
deal's real shape.

## Coverage gaps

- **The signing pair is unmet.** Above $300K the deal needs Ferro plus
  an owners' representative nobody has a record of — two signatories,
  one dead and one unnamed. Path: through Ohlendorf, whose own sentence
  named the gate.
- **The merged half has one voice.** Lindqvist-Nair asked to be in the
  pilot; she is the entire second world of this account — an
  opportunity disguised as coverage.
- **Former owner on the board, unnamed.** Merger politics live there;
  noted as absence, not assumed as support.

## Engagement plan

1. Build the consolidation-savings brief for Ohlendorf to carry upward —
   the merged estate's own numbers, answering the cost question Ferro
   asked before he went quiet. The artefact earns the meeting.
2. Put Lindqvist-Nair in the pilot as she asked — her chain's pain is
   the business case's proof; a self-raised thread costs nothing to
   accept and everything to ignore.
3. Ask Ohlendorf how the owners' rep is best approached — the unnamed
   signer, surfaced through the pusher rather than discovered dead.
4. Track Steinfeld's committee review — the open procedural thread; one
   check-in, not a chase.

## Executive sponsor

No sponsor exists yet — the candid read. Ferro holds the authority and
has not engaged in fifty-one days; Ohlendorf holds the energy and tops
out at $300K. The sponsor this deal needs is Ferro or the owners' rep,
reachable through her — steps 1 and 3 exist to make someone qualify.

## Why these calls

The roster reads Ferro first; the record reads him fourth. Ohlendorf's
13 of 14 and owned introductions sharpen to pusher; his silence
sharpens to dead, whatever "COO" suggests. Lindqvist-Nair sharpens to
the deal's second thread — a self-raised one, which is the rarest kind.
The owners' rep was unasked because the record cannot answer anything
about him yet; that is what gaps are for.
