# Three replies as marginalia (invented accounts; form only)

All three cases are made up. A reply opens with the annotations and
nothing ahead of it: first characters `## Marginalia`, or `# ` for the
redirect note. Copy the form — every contact annotated against the
record and settled on a cited signal, gaps named from the deal's own
shape, candour preferred over inflation. Copy no fact, name, or
figure; a real input supplies its own.

## Case one: a full account picture

What the account team supplied for Cresswell Mutual (regional insurance
brokerage, ~1,500 staff), a $390K document-workflow platform deal —
a win-back: they churned from a lighter product two years ago over a
failed migration:

- Contacts: Harriet Onyekwe, Head of Broker Operations — reached out to
  us in July to revisit; led the churn post-mortem internally and says
  so openly; shares migration-defect lists unprompted; 12 replies to 13
  emails; last touch 3 days ago. Lionel Danvers, Chief Operating
  Officer — signed the original cancellation two years ago; attended
  the September re-introduction call, asked one question ("what is
  different this time"), nothing since; 1 reply to 7 emails; last touch
  48 days ago. Pranav Mehta, IT Integration Lead — new since the churn;
  ran the technical review, asked hard migration questions, confirmed
  the API surface is sufficient; 6 of 7 answered; last touch 11 days
  ago. Bronislava Kertesz, Contracts & Procurement — sent the security
  schedule and indemnity clause review on the 2nd; our counsel returned
  comments on the 6th; she acknowledged, said committee reviews
  monthly.
- Note from Harriet: "Lionel signed the cancel order. He will not sign
  the comeback order until he can say what is different — and he does
  not take my word for it." No compliance or legal contact exists; the
  indemnity review goes through her committee.
- Deal context: their broker-facing teams still run the manual
  workaround built after the migration failed; two productivity audits
  flag it.

## Coverage gaps

- **The signer is the person who cancelled.** Danvers's gate is
  stated — "what is different" — and unanswered in the record for 48
  days. Path: through Onyekwe, who led the post-mortem and can carry
  the answer; never a void approach to the man who remembers.
- **The committee is unnamed.** Monthly, reviewing; no member holds a
  record. Path: Kertesz's process names it.
- **Migration confidence is the real coverage.** Mehta's sign-off
  answers the technical half; the operational half — proof the new
  onboarding will not repeat 2024 — has no artefact in the record yet.
- **Backer carries a reputational risk.** Onyekwe's name is attached
  to bringing us back; her success and the deal's success are the same
  bet — noted, not exploited.

## Engagement plan

1. Build the "what is different" dossier for Onyekwe to carry — the
   post-mortem items mapped to the new platform's answers, migration
   plan included. Danvers asked one question in September; it is still
   the only question.
2. Fold Mehta's confirmation into the dossier — the technical half of
   the answer, signed by the person they hired to distrust us.
3. Keep Kertesz's review loop moving — return every item same-week;
   the monthly committee punishes a slow side.
4. After the dossier lands, ask for the exec session Danvers's
   question deserves — him, Onyekwe, the answer in the room.

## Executive sponsor

Lionel Danvers is the recommendation by seat and the worry by record —
authority his, warmth absent, and his signature carries the memory the
deal must overcome. The candid read: no functioning sponsor exists
yet; the deal has a backer who believes and a signer who remembers.
Converting him is the plan's spine; until step 4, that is stated, not
rounded up.

## Editor's note

The roster reads COO-first; the margin writes him void — his gate is
named in his own words. Onyekwe's annotation is the rarest kind:
backer with a past to repay, which makes her both the asset and the
exposure. Nothing was invented to soften the win-back's shape — the
record's own note says the word for it.

# Win/loss analysis — redirect

This request redirects. A retrospective on a closed loss is a
different artefact from a live stakeholder map: one explains a dead
deal, the other advances a live one. The record does confirm the
shape of the loss — a failed migration, Danvers's signature on the
cancel — but a costing and causes study for the board needs data this
input does not carry: the 2024 meeting record, the migration defect
history, the commercial terms then and now.

What this input does support: the live map — Onyekwe solid, Danvers
void and asking one question, the committee unnamed — which is the
forward-looking half of the same truth. What a real win/loss needs is
the dead deal's own file; send that and the analysis is honest work.
Until then, the map's "what is different" dossier is the actionable
half of the same answer.
