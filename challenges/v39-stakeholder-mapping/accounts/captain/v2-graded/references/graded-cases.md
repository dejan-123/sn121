# Graded cases — one full brief, then the fragments

The account below is invented; the form is what transfers — every
name, number, title and date in a real reply comes from the record
in front of you, never from here. After the full brief, a fragment
library: short signal-to-call pairs grouped by the question they
answer, covering the situations a record throws at you.

## The full brief: a delegated executive, a never-met signer

Supplied for Aldermoor Estates, mid stage:

- Deal: $510K facilities-management platform. Stage: evaluation.
- Yusuf Kamara, Head of Facilities Systems: 9 sends, 8 replies
  same-day; runs the evaluation; introduced us to the building-ops
  lead; shared their compliance-requirements document unprompted.
- Diana Whitlock, COO: endorsed in March ("Yusuf runs this for me —
  bring me the decision pack when it's ready"); 1 reply to 6 emails
  in 45 days.
- Hector Vail, CFO: required for sign-off above $300K; never met.
- Lena Marsh, IT Security: cleared in writing, her own words.
- Owen Trask, Procurement: packet exchanged, his review open.
- Mentioned not met: the estates director Diana reports to — "will
  want to see the rollout plan."

# Aldermoor Estates: champion engaged, signer never met, CFO untouched

**Who's carrying it?**

Yusuf Kamara carries it — eight same-day replies, the building-ops
introduction, internals shared unasked. Diana Whitlock endorsed the
direction in March and handed it to him: she delegated, she did not
depart — the authority is hers, the relationship is his, and her way
back is the decision pack she herself commissioned.

**Who can sign this?**

Hector Vail — $510K sits above Diana's $300K ceiling, so the CFO
signs, and we have never met him.

**How does each line stand?**

| Person | What they are here | Warmth | How sure | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yusuf Kamara | Champion — runs the evaluation | 5 — warming | solid | Shared internals unasked; made the ops introduction; 8/9 replies same-day |
| Diana Whitlock | Endorsed — delegated to Yusuf | 2 — gone cold | solid | Endorsed March, "Yusuf runs this for me"; 1/6 replies in 45 days |
| Hector Vail | The one who signs | — | solid | CFO; required above $300K; no record |
| Lena Marsh | IT security review | 4 — steady | solid | Cleared in writing, her own words |
| Owen Trask | Procurement | 3 — steady | solid | Packet exchanged; his review open |

Mentioned but never reached: the estates director Diana reports to —
"will want to see the rollout plan"; no contact, no record. Nothing
sits unanswered on our side — packet returned, security review closed.

**What's missing for this deal?**

- **The signer has never engaged.** The order needs Hector — who has
  no record. Route: through Yusuf's decision pack, which Diana
  already commissioned — the pack earns the room.
- **The estates director is a stated stakeholder with no contact.**
  Diana named the dependency; a rollout-plan review is the door.
- **The deal is single-threaded.** Every live line runs through
  Yusuf — Diana's endorsement is real but the working relationship
  is all his. If he stalls, the deal stalls.

**What frees it next?**

1. Build the decision pack Diana asked for — audit of the
   maintenance-backlog pain, the rollout plan, the signature
   requirement. Her own commission is the vehicle; it answers her
   March ask and opens the CFO door in one move.
2. Ask Yusuf to put the rollout plan in front of his director — he
   owns that relationship; the plan already exists in step 1.
3. Deliver Owen's onboarding review — his is open and it gates any
   PO; confirm nothing sits unreturned on our side first.
4. When the pack lands, ask Diana — through Yusuf — for the CFO
   introduction. Not a cold email to Hector: a note he never
   expected asks a stranger for the deal, and it tells Yusuf we
   walked past him.

**Who sponsors it?**

No active sponsor today. Diana Whitlock is the candidate — a lapsed
sponsor: the endorsement is on record, but it is second-hand now
that Yusuf owns the work. His decision pack is the route back to
her attention. Hector is the sponsor the deal will eventually need,
and he is currently nobody to us. On our side: if Diana re-engages,
a peer path could open — none exists today.

**What might we be wrong about?**

The roster puts Diana's title above Yusuf's; the counts reverse it —
that call is solid. Lena's check is closed because her own writing
closed it. The real open grade is whether Diana still intends to
appear when the pack lands — the pack itself tests it.

## The small deal, whole

Supplied for Pemberton Cycle Co., early stage:

- Deal: $18K scheduling rollout for one workshop. Stage: two calls
  done.
- Ruth Pemberton, General Manager: replied to 4 of 5, asked specific
  questions about onboarding time, suggested the trial date herself.
  Stated: "anything under $25K lands on my desk."
- Cole Ashby, workshop lead: attended one trial, asked about the
  roster view, no follow-ups since.

# Pemberton Cycle: signer in hand, thin around her

**Who's carrying it?**

Ruth Pemberton — she answered four of five, asked real questions,
and picked the trial date herself. Cole Ashby saw the trial and
went quiet.

**Who can sign this?**

Ruth — she stated anything under $25K lands on her desk, and the
deal is $18K. Champion and signer in one chair.

**How does each line stand?**

| Person | What they are here | Warmth | How sure | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ruth Pemberton | Champion + the one who signs | 4 — steady | solid | Self-initiated; specific questions; "my desk" under $25K; 4/5 replies |
| Cole Ashby | Workshop lead — went quiet | 1 — gone cold | probably | One trial, one question; 0/2 follow-ups |

**What's missing for this deal?**

Nothing that blocks this deal's shape. Ruth's stated authority
covers the amount; no procurement, legal or executive layer is
raised, and none is invented. Cole's silence is noted rather than
worked — he holds no seat on the decision. Not single-threaded:
there is no decision held elsewhere for a thread to snap on.

**What frees it next?**

1. Answer her onboarding questions completely — they were
   specific; a complete answer is the cheapest trust we can buy.
2. Offer the trial framed to her own words — "stop losing weekend
   bookings" — around the date she already suggested.
3. Ask who touches the rota day to day — one question in her next
   reply; if Cole or anyone else runs it daily, that is the thread
   to warm.

**Who sponsors it?**

Ruth. She is buyer and champion inside her own stated authority —
a small deal gets no separate sponsor and invents none. Nothing
for one of ours to do: no peer to meet, and an $18K deal does not
need an executive.

**What might we be wrong about?**

Cole's probably-grade stands open and cheap — the question in
move 3 settles it without spending a move on him. Everything else
is solid.

## The fragment library

Each fragment: the signal on top, the call underneath, the grade
where it isn't obvious. Grouped by the question they serve.

### Who's carrying it?

- **7/9 replies; set up the pilot; forwards internals unasked** →
  champion. Owned acts, not warmth, make the call.
- **Answers everything, starts nothing, three months on** → a
  friend, not a champion — champions spend first.
- **"He'll take it to the board," three weeks, no introduction** →
  untested on access. One concrete test: ask who it reached; if it
  went nowhere, plan around him rather than through him.
- **Champion went quiet after the budget meeting** → the silence is
  a fact, the reason is not. Write "gone quiet — reason unknown,"
  not a story about why.

### Who can sign this?

- **"Anything under $25K lands on my desk," deal is $18K** → she
  signs it herself — champion and signer in one chair. No executive
  gap, no procurement layer invented; the read says so plainly.
- **"The CFO signs anything above $300K," deal is $510K, CFO never
  met** → he signs; a row with `—` for warmth and the record for a
  signal. The gap section names him as the missing line.
- **VP signed last year's $90K pilot; nothing since** → the seat is
  still his on record — a past signature proves the authority, not
  the warmth. "gone cold," and the plan routes back through the
  champion, never direct.

### How does each line stand?

- **Warm meeting notes, silent inbox** → both go in the row: acts
  over words, recent over old. Grade probably, name the settling
  action — the next scheduled touch is the test.
- **"Satisfied for now," in the reviewer's own writing** → cleared.
  A closed check is a resting relationship — she is done, not gone
  quiet — and no move re-confirms what nothing doubts.
- **Procurement asked for the vendor packet; we haven't sent it** →
  our debt, stated first among the gaps and first among the moves.
  A gate can hold an order; it cannot choose you — report what it
  demanded, nothing more.
- **"The ops director will want to see the rollout plan"** → a
  sentence under the table, never a row. Rows need real names.
- **No reply in 58 days, seven sends** → gone cold. The way back is
  whoever introduced you — a cold note to a dormant signer asks a
  stranger to say yes and can cost the champion you routed around.
- **Avoidance on record — "we're pausing vendor conversations"** →
  stated plainly, facts only, no invented hostility in the telling.

### What's missing for this deal?

- **$18K deal, signer in hand, one quiet colleague** → coverage
  fits: say so. Not single-threaded — no decision sits elsewhere
  for a thread to snap on. The quiet colleague is a note, not a
  gap.
- **$510K deal, every live line through one champion** →
  single-threaded, first among the gaps — the endorser's warmth is
  real but the working route is all hers.
- **Amount above the signer's line, no route to the holder** → the
  signature gap, named plainly with the route that exists.
- **Open security review, open procurement review** → each listed;
  a named competitor still in evaluation is urgency context, not a
  missing person.
- **Executive endorsement, work handed down** → lapsed sponsor:
  the endorser is the candidate, the delegate the working owner —
  the roster row reads "endorsed — delegated to <name>."

### What frees it next?

- **We owe documents, they owe nothing** → the debt leads. Nothing
  is asked of them while our side is open.
- **Dormant signer, live champion** → reach through the champion —
  a one-page brief in the executive's own language she can carry
  upward. The cold email is never the move; a document they never
  answered still routes through her.
- **Reviewer stopped answering** → the colleague beside them, not a
  third unanswered email into the same inbox.
- **Unnamed attendees at the demo** → ask the champion for names —
  widening starts with knowing who was in the room.
- **A probably-grade standing open** → its settling action becomes
  a move, stated as a test not a task.
- **Two moves both look first** → whatever unblocks the most ranks
  higher; say what the top move waits on.

### Who sponsors it?

- **Executive endorsed, then handed the work down** → no active
  sponsor; name her the candidate, give the route through the
  delegate. Driving the work is not authority over the amount —
  the champion is never the candidate.
- **Signer never met, deal above the champion's line** → the seat
  needs earning: say who it takes and the step that would earn it.
  The friendliest title is never promoted into the seat.
- **$18K deal, buyer inside her own authority** → she is the
  sponsor; nobody above is needed and none is named.
- **Should ours go in?** — only when the record shows a peer on
  their side and a path to the meeting — an executive of ours
  already in the record is the natural route. Otherwise say what
  is missing: buyer not established, deal too small, or a peer who
  has not asked.

### What might you be wrong about?

- **Two signals pulling apart, no settling evidence** → the
  conflict is the finding; the question that resolves it is the
  close.
- **A stage label a newer note disagrees with** → post the note,
  flag the stale label — never average them into a stage the
  record never said.

### When the ask isn't a map

- **"Sketch me their org structure" on a contact record** → one
  note under ~180 words at `# <Account>: ...`: what was asked
  against what arrived; what the artefact needs (reporting lines
  live in their directory, not our emails); what this record can
  build instead, said as categories — no name, title or count
  appears; and the precise paste that would let the map exist.
  Neither wrong input produces a map.

## What the grades mean

- **Solid** — the signal is in hand and dated: the counts, the
  written close, the stated limit. Solid can still be wrong in the
  world; it is never wrong about the record.
- **Probably** — evidence leans and something could settle it; the
  call stands, marked, with the settling action beside it.
- **Can't tell yet** — the record holds nothing to grade; written
  as an unknown with the question that resolves it, never smoothed
  into a guess.

When two signals pull apart, settle them in a fixed order: recency
outweighs volume; initiation outweighs reply-count; an owned act
outweighs reported warmth; a written close outweighs seniority;
authority outweighs the organogram. And when two calls still both
fit, say which the record cannot settle and the one question that
would — an owned ambiguity outranks a confident wrong call.
