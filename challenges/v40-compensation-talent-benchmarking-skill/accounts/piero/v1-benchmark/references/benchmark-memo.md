# From the sheet to the memo

The memo goes to an HR lead who will be questioned on it: roughly 500 to 750 words about role families only. Its facts are the sheet's and no others - the paragraphs are the Say lines joined into prose, and every figure comes from the sheet. The classification machinery stays on the sheet; the memo argues and files - and each family's paragraph also carries its reads: the premium where own offers exist (their position, never a derived percentage), the falsifier as a clause (kind, metro, stage, level - no price invented; any bound it names is a printed figure - the band's edge or a counted price, nothing between them), the spread where both kinds count. Every point the memo mentions carries its ID in brackets the first time, beside the name a reader would know it by. Verdicts, counts, anchors and tiers are copied from the sheet unchanged.

Layout, in order:

- `# <Company>: ` and the title line read off the sheet's verdicts - no numbers, no tiers, and any divergence named in it.
- An opening paragraph: the metro and stage the bands are held to, the first month in, what the window shuts out by ID and title, and which rows are not prices at all, by ID - or, when none fail on kind, that is said: "none in this pool". The basis check is always said: each conversion with its sum, or that every figure was already annual base and nothing needed converting. The tier key is stated once, early: High - two or more counted accepted offers; Medium - one counted offer, or counted ranges that agree; Low - a single counted range; Deferred - nothing counted.
- Two to four paragraphs, no headings, from the firmest call to the weakest. Paragraphs carry only what the table cannot: edge-case reasoning, the divergence, hearsay and posting labels, the zone's deciding signal, the P1/P2 clause - the figures, counts and IDs live in the table and are not restated in prose. The falsifier is named once as the method - the evidence that would flip or settle, kind/metro/stage/level - and appears in a family's paragraph only where it earns a clause. Families decided by the same kind of evidence share a paragraph. Every conversion shows its sum and direction. A volunteered range is named volunteered where it comes up; a point from another metro says how that metro pays, not this one; a decline's reason or a candidate's word is named hearsay where it comes up and is never set beside a gap. Internal offers appear only as corroboration and the named zone signal - where they sit against the band and the external anchor - never inside a verdict's arithmetic, no derived percentage beside a gap. An open P1 or P2 role earns a clause where it raises what hangs on the call.
- `## <Company>'s benchmark`: a table of `Family and level | Verdict | Gap, worked | Evidence funnel | Confidence`, one row per family in snapshot order. The gap is written as its sum, `(midpoint - anchor) / anchor`, with the anchor's IDs. The funnel is the family's funnel from the sheet - retrieved, survived, partial, dropped, all four counted even at zero, each with its IDs and the reason beside the partial and dropped ones. Confidence is the tier plus its deciding reason in a few words. Under the table, one line opening `Pool:` gives the four totals.
- `## Deferred verdicts for <Company>`, always present: each deferred family, what came close by ID, and the evidence that would settle it - kind, metro, stage, level. With none deferred, that is said, then the verdict resting on least and what would firm it.
- `## Signal caveats on <Company>'s pool`: two to four sentences on the pool as a whole, each carrying something not already said - which families rest on accepted offers and which on posted ranges alone; the voluntary-versus-mandated split among the ranges that bear on a verdict; an unnamed remote split where the input leaves one open; source concentration; and the single piece of evidence that would most move the weakest call. Nothing here restates a point the paragraphs already made.
- A last line answering the loudest figure not counted - the figure on the sheet's "Loudest" line, with its ID, its figure and the check it fails; it says nothing about what the figure would have done had it passed.

No advice on what to pay. The memo starts at its `# ` title and says nothing about sheets, examples or instructions.

## Dockline

# Dockline Freight Systems: Dispatch Operations runs above market, Carrier Sales lags, Route Optimization is competitive and Warehouse Data is deferred

Dockline is held to Kansas City and Series B. September 2025 is the first month in - the boundary month itself counts, keeping a Senior Route Optimization range from that month (V-03) and shutting out an $88,500 Dispatch Operations offer from August (V-07). Four rows are not prices: an hourly contractor rate for a dispatch contractor (V-16), a Route Optimization range given only as on-target pay with no split (V-17), an undated survey median with no method behind it (V-19) and an agency's word on an analyst's salary (V-20).

Route Optimization and Dispatch Operations rest on signed offers, revealed preference. Two Kansas City companies at Series B hired senior route engineers at $153,000 (V-01) and $150,500 (V-02) - the second under the title Optimization Engineer II, which the paste's scope note maps to this seat. Their $151,750 average leaves the $148,000 midpoint 2.5% under: competitive, and close, with a P1 role open. The law-posted range (V-03) centres on $153,000 and agrees; a Chicago range (V-04) prices another metro and was volunteered besides, and Dockline's own hire at $152,000 - inside the band, on the same line as the external anchor - plus a decline where the candidate cited a rival offer about 15% higher (hearsay, and the call does not lean on it) point the same direction. The posted centre runs 0.8% over the cleared offers, a small advertisement premium; one further Kansas City hire on the same line would seal the reading - and with the P1 seat open, this is the family where a slow pipeline shows first. Dispatch Operations is the mirror. Two Kansas City offers anchor it: $90,000 (V-08) and $88,000 base (V-18), the second converted - $97,000 total less its stated $9,000 bonus. Their $89,000 average leaves the midpoint 3.4% over: above-market, close, the zone's above-market side where the anchor's reading stands. A Series A range (V-05) and Dockline's own hires at $89,000 and $91,000 - inside the band under its midpoint - sit with that reading; a third in-metro offer nearer the band's midpoint would pull it back inside the line.

Carrier Sales stands on one binding advertisement. A law-posted Kansas City range (V-09) centres on $79,000, leaving the $76,000 midpoint 3.8% under: the lagging zone, and with no offers of Dockline's own on record to test it the anchor's reading stands - lagging, close, and Low, since a single posted range is aspirational and caps the call. Two further ranges centre at the same $79,000 - one marked voluntarily posted (V-10), one volunteered under the line printed above the table (V-11) - and a slash-titled Carrier Sales / Collections Lead range (V-15) may belong to either job; all three lean the same way without binding - but they back the lone range, which is what lets it carry even a Low call. Any in-metro offer, or a second law-posted range that agrees, firms or flips it; a volunteered advertisement never could.

Warehouse Data is deferred while its P2 role is open. A remote offer at $104,000 (V-12) leans high - the snapshot leaves two roles unanchored without saying which, so it is partial, not out - and a Series A offer at $95,000 (V-13) leans low, as an earlier round usually pays. They pull opposite ways and neither binds at this round in Kansas City; counted alone the remote offer would anchor the family on the high side, so naming the open split is what settles this.

## Deferred verdicts for Dockline

Warehouse Data, Analyst. The remote offer (V-12) came closest from above and the Series A offer (V-13) from below; neither binds at Series B in Kansas City. An accepted offer for a warehouse data analyst at a Kansas City Series B company would settle it, or two postings there that agree - and with the P2 role open, that is the evidence to ask for first. Whether the family's own seat is one of the two unanchored remote roles would also decide how V-12 and V-14 read - the cheapest settlement is naming that split.

## Signal caveats on Dockline's pool

Route Optimization and Dispatch Operations rest on accepted offers, revealed preference; Carrier Sales stands on one posted range, aspirational, and is capped for it. The over-table line volunteered every range silent on its posting, so only the ranges naming a disclosure law bind. The snapshot names two roles remote without saying which, so every family was read as anchored and the remote points as partials - if Warehouse Data is one of those seats, V-12's offer binds and the deferred family firms at once. Every near-line gap here is read on a 3% convention; a wider convention would relabel Dispatch Operations and Carrier Sales, though the direction of each - band under the cleared prices, band over the cleared prices - is not in doubt. The single piece of evidence that would most move the weakest call is that same identification.

Somebody will quote $186,000 for a route engineer. That is the top of a Chicago range (V-04), volunteered besides - it prices Chicago, not Kansas City.
