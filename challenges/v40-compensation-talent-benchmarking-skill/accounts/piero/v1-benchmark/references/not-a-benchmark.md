# When there is nothing to benchmark

The reply to a non-benchmark ask is a short note and nothing else. It starts at `# <Company>: nothing to benchmark yet`, then three short paragraphs of plain sentences, under 180 words together: what was asked and what came with it; why the pay verdict is deferred - the word "deferred" is used; what to send so the market question can be answered, in the company's own metro, stage and job titles, and where the other asks belong - each named as its own piece of work, with its owner and the inputs it starts from.

No bullets, bold, tables or figures. No invented market numbers, no person's name or salary from an internal roster, no list of what cannot be done, no closing offer, nothing about skills or instructions. If part of the request is a benchmark, that part is benchmarked and the note says in a line where the rest goes. Fernhollow is invented.

## Fernhollow

# Fernhollow Gardens: nothing to benchmark yet

The team asked whether growers are underpaid, what the market pays a head grower, and whether to merge the two junior technician levels before spring hiring. What arrived was the staff roster - names, titles and salaries for forty-one people - and no outside evidence.

A salary roster is a survivors' list - a band set too low empties quietly and what remains looks about right - so the verdict on pay is deferred: no market call can rest on it, and no family here can be called competitive, lagging or above-market. The roster does show its own aggregates: senior growers cluster around $61,000-$66,000, technicians $44,000-$49,000. The merger is a structure decision, not a market one.

To answer the pay question, send dated outside rows - accepted offers or posted ranges with metro, company stage and base pay - plus the band midpoints by family and level, the evidence window, and Fernhollow's own recent offers. The merger is separate work for whoever owns levelling, starting from role scopes and the ladder, alongside the market read.
