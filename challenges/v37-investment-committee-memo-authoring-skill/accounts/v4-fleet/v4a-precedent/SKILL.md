# Precedent memo — settle the record, price the base, commit the call

The artifacts arrive with verdicts printed on them; the record exists
because printed verdicts are claims, not the decision. Settle the
record before a word of memo is drafted: each row's standing, the
count of open blocking rows, the base figure, both range products —
then the call follows. Nothing enters the memo that the artifacts do
not state; a missing input reads `[to confirm: …]`, never invented.

## Settle 1 — label versus evidence

Blocking weight belongs only to rows whose filed label means
clear-before-deal (Must-Address, Critical, Deal-Breaker, Blocker,
High). A row filed below that tier never blocks, however loudly it
reads — the diligence team weighed its severity when they filed it;
it takes a condition or a price note and a flag where the tier looks
light. It is never promoted.

For every row write two statuses, side by side: **status as
labelled** — the column's own word, quoted verbatim — and **status
on the evidence** — what the artifacts actually show:

| Labelled | Evidence shows | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| `Resolved` | another artifact or the row's own text keeps it live — appeal undecided, signature absent, re-bid running | open, flag quoting the source |
| `Resolved` | only its own wording still describes the exposure | settled, questioned — the proving document becomes a condition |
| blank / `In-Progress` / `Unresolved` | — | open |
| verified, uncontradicted | — | settled |

A row that looks under-tiered is flagged, not re-tiered; a label
contradicted by evidence is reported in the table and the
recommendation ("labelled resolved; the record shows X; treated as
open").

## Settle 2 — the base that transfers

Rebuild the base the buyer owns at close, as a bridge: reported
figure, then each deduction with its source, then the diligence base.
Strip one-time revenue, revenue that cannot transfer (grants,
licences, contracts ending on change of control), revenue already
lost — the narrative's own post-loss run-rate where given. Revenue
merely at risk — a re-bid, a customer near expiry — stays in the
base and marks the downside. Add-backs hold only where plainly
one-time; the corrected figure stands beside the seller's. Negative
EBITDA on the transferable base → EV/EBITDA is meaningless; carry
EV/Revenue.

## Settle 3 — the comp screen

Every comparable is named **in** or **out**, one line each: a
control-premium transaction beside trading peers; NM on the metric
in use; an outlier whose gap to its nearest neighbour exceeds the
spread of all the rest — no other ground. Where the test fails the
comp stays in and its gap becomes a mark against the included
median (middle value, or mean of the central pair): position the
target in words on growth, margin, concentration, retention.

## Settle 4 — the range and the ask

Two products written out — low multiple × base, high multiple ×
base, $M to one decimal; quantified debt-like items off each end.
The range is the valuation's last line. The ask reads as one implied
multiple on the restated base — bridged on both bases where they
differ — placed under/within/above the range with its gap in $M and
percent. Compute only what the call, the range or a condition needs.

## Settle 5 — the call

- `proceed` — zero open blocking rows. Where the record holds no
  blocking-tier row at all, the memo states plainly *no finding
  here is a deal-breaker* — a committee reading only manageable
  rows expects that sentence. Every open non-blocking row carries
  a named condition.
- `request more data` — requires at least one open blocking row
  clearable by a document a party can produce: consent, closure
  letter, executed renewal, clearance, audited figure. Deferral is
  executed, not vague: name the record, its issuer, its deadline
  relative to signing, and the call it unlocks — 'upon the
  executed consent, proceed at a ceiling of $X; upon refusal,
  re-price or walk'. Open rows below the blocking tier never
  downgrade a proceed — however many documents they need, their
  asks map inside proceed's conditions, not the verdict.
- `pass` — an open blocking row fails all three: unresolved;
  removes what the valuation prices at a size no range absorbs; no
  document or action before signing clears it. A right lapsing on
  a date no party can move — a patent term ending, a statutory
  licence — fails the third when the thesis prices what that
  right protects: no record exists that extends it. A cure removes
  the exposure; a filing, opinion or status report only describes
  it — documents about a risk never satisfy the third test.
  Pending approvals, a re-bid on a counterparty's calendar, a
  filed appeal — a party can still move them: askable, not
  incurable. A refusal arms only when unconditional and on record:
  a counterparty that 'indicates' it 'may not' continue, or hangs
  its exit on an 'if' the buyer's status hasn't triggered, has
  armed nothing. A leaving counterparty is incurable only where
  the artifacts show no substitute exists — a replaceable role (a
  sponsor bank, a processor, a landlord) stays askable. A
  requirement some third party must fill — a sponsor, a
  guarantor, a licensor of record — bars nothing while a
  qualifying substitute can fill the seat; one provider declining
  is not the class closed. A cure's length is not its
  incurability either — a filing that runs months still ends in a
  document. What no consent papers over is a term or rule barring
  the transfer itself — grant terms the buyer cannot hold, a
  licence ending on change of control; where the artifacts state
  the bar, the bar is the record. A frightening row failing any
  one test is a near miss — it takes a condition, never the
  decline.

An ask above the range maps in the recommendation as a hard
ceiling: *proceed, at a price inside $X–$Y* — never a decline.

## Settle 6 — the conditions

One per open or questioned row, in row order: the instrument, the
issuing party, the timing mark — before signing, at close,
post-close — only dates the artifacts give, and the consequence if
it fails: walk, re-price, or hold the escrow. Manageable rows take
covenants, holdbacks or a 100-day item, not conditions precedent.

## The memo — the record rendered

```
# Committee memo — <target>
**Recommendation: <verdict>** — <deciding rows, ask vs range>
**Record notes** (≤6 bullets): open rows, contradicted labels,
unreconciled figures, missing inputs.
```

1. `## 1. Deal thesis (≤120 words)` — what is bought, why on the
   priced base, the primary risk in its row's words.
2. `## 2. Risk record` — every row in input order: # | finding |
   tier filed | status as labelled | status on evidence | deciding
   fact ≤25 words. Beneath: `Blocking rows open: <n>` (or `none —
   no deal-breaker on the record`) plus ≤2 flag lines.
3. `## 3. Defended valuation (≤260 words + comp table)` — base
   bridge, comp screen, two products, range last, ask position.
4. `## 4. Recommendation (≤120 words)` — verdict first, deciding
   rows, ceiling where ask > range, then `Record check:` — primary
   risk = first open blocking row, price inside range, every figure
   identical across sections.
5. `## 5. Conditions precedent` — one per open or questioned row,
   ≤40 words, instrument + deadline + consequence.

Cut prose before rows, products, the verdict or conditions — a
long memo loses its tail. The record renders title-first; the five
headings follow in order.

Call read_reference for `references/precedent-cases.md` once
before drafting — the four verdict shapes on invented deals.
Follow the form; carry none of its facts.
