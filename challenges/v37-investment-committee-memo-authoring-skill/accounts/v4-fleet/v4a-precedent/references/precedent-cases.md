# Precedent cases — invented deals, form only

## Case A — request more data, executed (the deferral shape)

File: a field-service SaaS, $18.6M transferable base. One open
blocking row — a change-of-control consent from a platform vendor
covering 41% of deployments — plus two Manageable rows open (a
pending SOC 2 recertification, a key-engineer retention gap).
Ask $71M against a $60.4–$74.2M defended range.

Settle 1: the consent row carries blocking weight and stands
open — the vendor acknowledged the request but nothing is
executed. Settle 5: an executed consent would close it before
signature → request more data, executed: the record, the issuer,
the deadline, and the call it unlocks are all named.

> **Request more data.** One record decides the file: the executed
> change-of-control consent from the platform vendor, before
> signing. Upon it, the committee proceeds at a ceiling of
> $74.2M; upon refusal, the deal re-prices off the affected 41%
> or walks. The Manageable rows never held the call — they stand
> as conditions 2 and 3 inside the closing path: SOC 2
> recertification at or before close ($1.5M escrow if missed),
> retention agreements with the two named engineers by close
> (re-price if unsigned).

Deferral is a commitment with a named dependency, not a shrug:
every request carries the record, its issuer, its deadline, and
the verdict it unlocks.

## Case B — request more data (the unasked-fact shape)

File: a payments processor, $9.2M base. Blocking row open: the
sponsor bank 'has indicated it will not continue the relationship
if the acquirer is not a regulated entity'. Ask $44M inside a
$41.0–$50.8M range.

Settle 5: the exit hangs on an 'if' the buyer's status has not
answered — a position, not a refusal. The seat is also refillable:
a sponsor bank is a replaceable role. But the call itself turns on
the unasked fact → request more data, naming it.

> **Request more data.** Two answers set the call: whether the
> acquirer entity is or can be regulated as the sponsor requires,
> and whether a successor sponsor bank will commit on equivalent
> terms. Neither is in the file. If the first returns yes, the row
> closes by executed consent → conditions precedent. If the second
> returns a commitment, the seat refills → proceed. A refusal that
> is unconditional and on record, with no substitute, is what would
> turn this to pass — that record does not exist.

## Case C — pass (the incurable-break shape)

File: a licensing-revenue company, $12.1M base of which $4.0M is
federal grant revenue. The grant terms state the award is not
transferable to a for-profit acquirer; the buyer is for-profit.

Settle 2: strip the $4.0M — it cannot transfer; base reads $8.1M.
Settle 5, first test: the bar sits in the grant's own terms, not
in a missing signature. No consent exists against a rule; asking
for one is asking the rule to change. Unresolved, thesis-sized,
no closing event → pass.

> **Pass.** The transfer bar is in the governing terms; no document
> on this timeline converts federal research funding into
> acquirable revenue, and the remaining $8.1M base prices below
> what the thesis needs.

The compound-row rule: a file that pairs a documentable question
(a consent's scope, a framework's coverage) with a stated bar is
settled part by part — the consent is askable, the bar is not,
and the bar decides the call. A document can settle what a
counterparty *permits*; it cannot change what the buyer is
*allowed to own*. Where the stripped base already prices below
the thesis, deferring for the consents buys nothing — pass.

## Case D — proceed (the clean-file shape)

File: a vertical SaaS, $22.8M base. Every row filed Manageable or
Informational — three rows open: pending state approvals, a
tribunal claim mid-briefing, an NRR decline awaiting cohort
evidence. Ask $80M against a $59.9–$66.4M defended range.

Settle 1: the filing column governs — no row carries blocking
weight, whatever the wording threatens. Settle 5, second branch:
zero open blocking rows → proceed. The open records do not wait
outside; they enter as numbered conditions.

> **Proceed, with conditions, at a price inside $59.9–$66.4M.** No
> finding here is a deal-breaker: the file's highest tier is
> Manageable, so approval is the call and the open rows are its
> terms. The $80M ask prices above the defended top — the ceiling
> is $66.4M, not a pass.

The named failure this case prevents: re-tiering a filed
Manageable row because its wording sounds severe — pending
regulatory approvals and a tribunal claim are conditions, priced
in the range or held in escrow, never gates.
