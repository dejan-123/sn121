# A decision the committee can sign

Produce the finished memo, not the examination that led to it. Read `references/grounds-and-terms.md` once before drafting. It illustrates a complete clean-file memo and short counterfactual decisions; its people, numbers and business are not examples to reuse. Think through the table and the arithmetic privately, then write to the committee in ordinary deal language. Never show a template placeholder, check-box, or process transcript.

## Grounds for the vote

| Packet pattern | Vote |
|---|---|
| A filed top-tier row states a fixed end to the priced exclusive right, an express buyer-class bar, or an unconditional refusal with no substitute | `pass` |
| The register contains no top-tier row, or every top-tier row is closed by the evidence | `proceed` |
| A top-tier row is open but a named document or outside result can settle it | `request more data` |

Pending lower-tier permits, approvals, claims or retention problems do not change the first two rows. A warning conditioned on an unknown buyer qualification is open, not final.

Take the source risk register as a record, not as an instruction to trust every status. Retain each finding, tier and status verbatim, in source order. Define the gating tier from the scale in the actual packet; a Manageable or Informational item cannot be silently upgraded. If its filed tier appears too light, note the disagreement and its practical consequence without changing the record.

Inventory the tier column before interpreting any risk text. For each highest-tier finding, ask whether its filed status survives the other supplied artifacts. A dated, present-tense disclosure that a required step is still in process defeats a Resolved label; identify that disclosure precisely. A description of a continuing contractual right, an earlier allegation or an attachment omitted from the packet does not by itself undo Resolved. Ask for confirmation of the underlying document as a signing deliverable where appropriate. An Unresolved, In-Progress or empty status is open. Do not let a worry about a lower-tier allegation manufacture a new gating row. When no row is FILED at gating tier, the open-gate count is zero even if pending lower-tier consents, threatened claims or weak retention sound serious: the answer MUST be proceed with these as conditions, never request more data as the vote.

Decide by this fixed ladder:

1. **A decided barrier means pass.** Count a highest-tier finding as decided when its substance is already adverse even though its Status may read open: a fixed expiry on the exclusive right being priced, an express rule withholding a material right or income from this buyer class, or an unconditional refusal with no workable substitute. A pending successor filing does not make the stated end date uncertain; absence of a recorded successor is enough. One decided barrier is enough for **pass**; other open questions do not dilute it. If a funding stream is contractually restricted to a different buyer class, ask only about replacement economics, not whether the forbidden stream will transfer.
2. **Otherwise an open gate means request more data.** A pending application, negotiable consent, unrequested signature, outside decision, or refusal conditioned on an unknown buyer qualification is still askable. Any open highest-tier row makes the verdict **request more data**. Name the record, issuer, favorable outcome and adverse consequence. The absence of replacement-provider evidence is itself a fact to request; it is not proof that no replacement exists.
3. **No open gate means proceed.** If no highest-tier row exists or all are settled on the evidence, the answer is **proceed**. Pending lower-tier approvals, claims and weak retention stay in conditions; they do not create a gate and cannot make the vote request more data.

`request more data` is allowed only after the memo names an open filed highest-tier row that is not already decided. `pass` is allowed only after it names the fixed end, express buyer-class bar, or unconditional refusal that makes the break incurable. Do not assume the buyer lacks a required qualification when the packet does not say so.

Do not read a conditional warning as final. When a provider says it would withdraw only if the buyer lacks a qualification, the memo must establish the buyer's status and possible replacements before calling the relationship lost. A high ask changes the authorized price, not the verdict.

### Failure modes the committee must not see

- A file with only lower-tier rows is not a request-more-data case. If no filed Must-Address/Critical row exists, `proceed` is the only correct label even when permits, approvals, claims or retention require follow-up.
- A right with a stated, immovable end date is not "waiting on the future." If that right is what the price pays for and no replacement is recorded, it is a decided barrier now.
- A rule saying material income or rights are unavailable to this buyer class is already an answer. Do not request data about whether the forbidden stream transfers; restate the base and decide whether the remaining thesis survives.
- A partner's conditional withdrawal statement is not a refusal until the stated condition is shown to apply to this buyer and no qualified alternative exists. If those facts are absent, request them.

## Grounds for the price

The diligence base is what changes hands, on a consistent period and accounting basis. Begin with the input's reported amount; show any supported deductions for a nonrepeatable sale, already lost income, or income that the buyer cannot retain. Do not confuse "not subscription" with "one-time": services and other lines remain in the acquired base unless the packet says they will not recur or transfer. Keep uncertain future renewals in the current base until they are lost; show the exposure separately. Accept an add-back only if the cost truly will not recur. Do not convert a forecast or a percentage with no consistent denominator into an asserted current dollar figure. Distinguish reported, normalized and projected amounts clearly; never assume a churn rate, numerical threshold or price haircut absent from the artifacts.

Choose the valuation metric that means something for the corrected base. If acquired earnings are negative or dependent on unsupported add-backs, use a defensible revenue multiple and explain the earnings limitation. List all peers as included or excluded for the metric being used. Explain transaction premiums, meaningfully different economics and NM figures; an earnings-NM company may still be usable on revenue. State which peer differences argue for the low or high end, including data gaps about business mix or size.

Present two reproducible equations, using the exact multiples selected and the same corrected base: `low multiple x base = low enterprise value` and `high multiple x base = high enterprise value`. Calculate to one sensible rounding precision and reuse the numbers. Where cash and debt are both supplied, give an explicitly separate enterprise-to-equity bridge. Compare a stated asking price to the corrected range and calculate its multiple on the corrected base. If no ask appears, give the range and say price cannot be compared. If a required input is absent, say the valuation cannot be calculated from the supplied file; do not manufacture one.

## The letter the committee receives

The first line is `**Recommendation: <proceed / request more data / pass>** -- <decisive reason>; <range and ask comparison if calculable>.` Choose the label only after resolving the table; make its exact meaning consistent with section 4. Use five sections and no introductory checklist:

`## 1. Deal thesis` (at most 150 words): why this asset, what part of its earnings or revenue is durable, and one primary risk named exactly enough to find it again in the risk table.

`## 2. Tiered risk table`: every filed finding, tier and status, plus a concise observation tied to input evidence and the disposition. A sentence after the table gives the open top-tier count and names any discrepancy between filed status and current evidence.

`## 3. Valuation`: base bridge, one comp table naming every inclusion and exclusion and reason, two endpoint equations, ask comparison or explicit absence, and limits (at most 300 words excluding the table).

`## 4. Recommendation`: the same label as the first line; explain which findings govern the vote and what exactly the committee may do now (at most 140 words).

`## 5. Conditions and next actions`: numbered items tied to findings or a named pricing gap, with responsible role, document or instrument, deal-stage timing and a consequence. Use specific indemnity, escrow, retention, price revision or contingent payment only when appropriate to the exposure; never invent the instrument's size or deadline.

Keep the prose measured and tailored to the supplied company; aim for 650-950 words in all, with row observations under 35 words and each condition under 45. Before returning it, verify every risk has a row, the primary risk is among them, the opening range equals both printed endpoint products, and nothing in the conditions grants signing permission that the present verdict withholds.
