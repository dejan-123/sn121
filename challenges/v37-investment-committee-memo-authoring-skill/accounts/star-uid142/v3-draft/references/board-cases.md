# Trigger exhibits — invented deals, form only

One full board, then the three remaining verdict shapes. Every fact
is invented; the form is the lesson.

## Exhibit A — a full memo (request more data)

# Committee memo — Corvale Medical Logistics
**Recommendation: request more data** — row 1 (cold-chain licence
renewal, Must-Address, In-Progress) and row 3 (hospital GPO contract
consent, Must-Address, Unresolved) stay armed; $52M ask maps inside
the $48.2–$58.6M range.
**Board state**: two armed blocking triggers; row 5 filed Resolved
while the narrative says the audit corrective plan is unfinished;
no debt schedule provided.

## 1. Deal thesis (≤120 words)
Corvale routes temperature-controlled pharmaceuticals to regional
hospitals: $21.3M LTM revenue, 91% contracted, 55% gross margin,
EBITDA $2.6M. The thesis prices the contracted hospital base; two
armed triggers stand between the board and proceed — the licence
renewal mid-review and the unsigned GPO consent covering 34% of
revenue. Primary risk: row 1's pending licence.

## 2. Trigger table
| # | Finding | Tier | Status | State | Deciding fact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold-chain licence renewal | Must-Address | In-Progress | armed | Regulator holds the application; decision due |
| 2 | Fleet age raises spoilage exposure | Manageable | Unresolved | pending | Replacement cost quantified at $1.1M |
| 3 | GPO contract assignment consent | Must-Address | Unresolved | armed | 34% of revenue; consent not executed |
| 4 | Dispatch software single-maintainer | Manageable | Unresolved | pending | Key-person dependency; hire plan drafted |
| 5 | Prior-year FDA audit corrective plan | Manageable | Resolved | armed | Narrative: corrective plan unfinished |

Blocking rows armed: 2. Flag: row 5's Resolved does not hold —
the corrective plan is unfinished; it arms as questioned.

## 3. Defended valuation (≤260 words + comp table)
Base $21.3M LTM revenue; the GPO contract is at risk, not lost — it
maps in full and names the downside. EBITDA positive; the comp set
prices EV/Revenue, which leads.

| Comparable | EV/Rev | Growth | In/Out | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MediRoute Inc. | 2.6x | 15% | In | Trading peer |
| ColdChain Partners | 2.2x | 9% | In | Trading peer |
| PharmaJet (acquired) | 3.8x | 21% | Out | Control premium at close |
| TempLogistics | 2.9x | 19% | In | Trading peer |

Included median 2.6x. Corvale grows near the median (13% vs 15%) on
a thinner margin (55% vs 58%) — net a step under: 2.3x–2.8x.
2.3x × $21.3M = $49.0M; 2.8x × $21.3M = $59.6M. Range: $48.2–$58.6M
net of the $1.1M fleet reserve the narrative quantifies. The $52M
ask is 2.4x the base — inside the range.

## 4. Verdict, verified (≤120 words)
**Request more data.** Rows 1 and 3 arm and each clears on a
document: the renewed licence, the executed consent. Row 5 arms as
questioned — the audit plan is unfinished. The ask maps inside the
defended range; price is not the obstacle. Board check: primary risk =
row 1, first blocking trigger; range rebuilt above; every figure
identical across sections.

## 5. Dispositions and next steps
1. Row 1: renewed cold-chain licence before signing — failing it,
   walk; the licence is the operating premise.
2. Row 3: executed GPO consent before signing; without it, re-price
   excluding that revenue.
3. Row 5: regulator's closure letter on the corrective plan before
   close; an open finding re-prices.
4. Row 2: $1.1M fleet reserve escrowed at close.
5. Row 4: second-maintainer hire within 90 days post-close.

## Exhibit B — the pass mapping (a date nobody moves)

Rows: Must-Address `Unresolved` — the platform's central algorithm
sits inside a university licence expiring by statute in 6 months,
and the licence's own clause bars assignment on change of control;
a Manageable churn row beside it. All three tests fail: armed; the
licence is what the base prices; no document this deal can produce
extends a statutory term against a transfer bar. §4 maps:

> **Pass.** Row 1's licence ends on a date no party can move and its
> clause bars the transfer itself — the clearing record cannot exist
> on this deal's timeline. No escrow, indemnity or price repairs it.
> The file reopens if the clause or the term changes. Board check: …

The churn row is the near miss — alarming, curable: a retention
disposition and a price mark, never the decline.

## Exhibit C — the proceed mapping (no blocking trigger on the board)

Rows: Manageable `Unresolved` (documentation backlog), Manageable
`In-Progress` (data-centre migration), Informational `Unresolved`
(NRR 95%). Not one blocking trigger on the board. §4 maps:

> **Proceed, with dispositions.** No finding here is a deal-breaker:
> the board holds no blocking-tier row, and every armed row is
> curable or priced. The documentation backlog takes a named
> technical-writer plan (disposition 1); the migration maps as a
> pre-close deliverable (disposition 2); NRR prices inside the
> range. Ask $15.6M sits inside the $14.2–$17.9M range. Board check:
> blocking rows armed: none; …

Stating the absence of deal-breakers is required — a board holding
only manageable rows must be said aloud to hold none.

## Exhibit D — proceed carrying a ceiling

Rows: every blocking trigger cleared on file documents; ask $64M

> above the range; the deal clears only at or under $56.1M. Board check: …

Above range is a price disposition, never the decline.

## Exhibit E — the conditional refusal (near miss, not pass)

Rows: Must-Address `Unresolved` — the target's payment rail
"has indicated it may not renew if the buyer lacks the required
charter"; plus routine open consents. §4 maps:

> **Request more data.** Row 1 maps a position, not a refusal: the
> exit hangs on the buyer's charter status — a fact the board never
> settles — and the rail seat is fillable by any chartered provider.
> The asks: the buyer's status, and a successor rail's commitment.
> The remaining consents are ordinary documents. Nothing maps
> incurable; the file declines only when no chartered provider will
> take the seat. Board check: …

The trap: 'indicated it may not renew' reads like a refusal but
arms nothing final — the condition's trigger is an unasked fact.

## Exhibit F — the statutory clock (this one IS the pass)

Rows: Must-Address `In-Progress` — the product's core process sits
under a patent expiring by statute in 13 months; rival continuation
filings may dominate the successor landscape. Beside it:
Must-Address `Unresolved` — a lapsed safety certification,
curable by re-inspection. §4 maps:

> **Pass.** Row 1's right lapses on a date no party can move — no
> filing, consent or indemnity extends a statutory term, and the
> successor landscape is already contested. The thesis prices the
> protection that right affords; it is gone whether or not the deal
> signs. Time is not a cure: an expiry months out is as incurable
> as one tomorrow. Row 2 is curable — but a deal does not proceed
> to fix the fixable while the premise lapses. The file reopens
> only if the statute or the landscape changes. Board check: …

Contrast with Case E: there the seat could be refilled — a
provider exists. Here the thing itself expires — nothing replaces
a lapsed right.

## The remapping table and the instruments on the board

Seller or narrative mark → the board's remapping:

| They mark | The board remaps |
|---|---|
| "gross revenue $14.7M" | "net revenue $8.9M — the figure the comps price" |
| "adjusted EBITDA +$3.1M" | "EBITDA -$1.1M as mapped; non-plain add-backs rejected" |
| "94% recurring" | a composition mark, not a remapping ground |
| "pipeline growing 40%" | unmapped — growth priced is growth delivered |
| "ask $64M" | "ask $64M = implied multiple on the mapped base" |

Instruments the board disposes with: condition precedent, closing
deliverable, indemnity, escrow or holdback sized to a quantified
exposure, contingent consideration, retention or non-compete, price
reduction to a defended ceiling, long-stop date only where the
artifacts name a date.
