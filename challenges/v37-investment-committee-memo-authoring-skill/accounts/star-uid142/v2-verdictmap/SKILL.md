# The verdict map — what armed, what cleared

Three artifacts arrive with verdicts already printed on them:
statuses filed, bases struck, multiples quoted. The board exists
because printed verdicts are not the decision — they are claims
about it. Each finding is tested against its trigger, the trigger's
state maps beside the filed label, and the memo reports the board.
Filed words are never rewritten: the row keeps its tier and status
as given, and the map records any disagreement next to them — a
flag in view, never a silent edit.

Every figure maps from the inputs or is worked from them in view.
What the inputs do not contain maps as a named gap —
`[to confirm: …]` — never assumed in the seller's favour.

## Board position — what is being decided

Acquire <target> at what price. State the ask — or map its absence
as a finding, because a price nobody stated is itself a trigger.

## The board — trigger states (a closed set)

Each row maps exactly one state beside its filed tier. No fifth
state exists; `proceed`, `request more data` and `pass` are
verdicts, never trigger states.

| State | Arms when |
|---|---|
| `triggered` | a row filed at the gating tier (Must-Address/Critical) is open — filed unresolved, or `Resolved` contradicted by another artifact or the row's own words (a re-bid live, a review deferred not closed, a consent never documented, a claim the row calls pending). The filed tier decides; a Manageable row that looks deal-critical maps pending with the reason, never re-tiered. The deciding fact is quoted. |
| `pending` | a named record would settle it — the disposition is written, and under proceed the cell carries it, so no armed row is left unowned |
| `clear` | the record confirms the filing — including a `Resolved` beside an exposure that persists by nature (a consent the licence needs, a cancellation right): the label holds, the proving document maps to a closing deliverable |
| `void` | informational or outside the decision; no disposition spent |

Every row maps — none dropped, none merged; rows armed by one root
trigger keep separate entries and cross-reference ("armed by row
N"). A trigger the record arms but the table never printed earns
its own row, grounded in the artifacts' words. An analyst note
flagging a contradiction maps as evidence, never smoothed.

## Reading 1 — the base trigger

The seller's figure fires false wherever it counts revenue that
completed and will not recur, revenue that does not transfer —
grants a for-profit cannot hold, terminable contracts, departed
customers — or a gross figure where the industry's metric is net.
The base that maps is the restated one; growth maps on the same
restated basis. EBITDA maps as a viable trigger only where it is
positive on the economics that transfer and the comps are
profitable; elsewhere it maps void and EV/Revenue carries the
range. Add-backs map only where genuinely non-recurring — a
rejected one is never subtracted twice. Growth contingent on
unsigned deals — prospects in study, unconverted pilots — maps as
a claim: realized growth is the priced rate. Each figure maps
once and is reused unchanged.

## Reading 2 — the comp triggers

Each comp maps include or exclude with its reason: a control or
sponsor premium beside trading peers; growth or margin so far
from the target it prices another business; an NM multiple on the
metric in use. A limitation the comp set's own note declares maps
into the memo rather than being priced around. The target maps
against the included median in words with a direction — size,
margin, growth.

## Reading 3 — the price trigger

Two products only: an included comp's own multiple × base =
value, written in $M to one decimal. The range is those two
products, the valuation's last line. The ask maps as one implied
multiple on the priced base — once per base where the base restates
(gross-to-net, blended-to-recurring): the two readings are the
comparison, not new arithmetic. These are the map's only
calculations.
Where net debt or cash is material, the bridge maps in one line —
EV less net debt. No ask stated → the range maps as bid guidance.

## Reading 4 — the verdict

The verdict maps `proceed`, `request more data` or `pass`, names
the deciding triggers and why the other two verdicts do not map.

- `proceed` — no gating trigger is armed. A pending manageable
  trigger never blocks: its named disposition maps among the
  triggers for closing. Declining to fire on a clean map is as
  false as firing over an armed trigger.
- `request more data` — each armed gating trigger clears with a
  named record: a consent, a closure letter, an executed renewal,
  a clearance, an audited figure — and the party that issues it.
- `pass` — an armed gating trigger the map already reads against
  the deal: a counterparty on record refusing; a term or rule that
  bars the transfer itself — the asset cannot legally move to the
  buyer, and revenue that may not survive post-close is a base
  question, never a bar; or two or more armed gating
  triggers whose only clearing sits outside the deal's control —
  sovereign determinations (a court, an agency, an accreditation
  body), refusing counterparties, findings no record can settle.
  A signature nobody has requested — a licensor's consent, a
  customer's renewal — is requestable, not outside-held; a
  permission still pending — an application, an approval not yet
  granted — is requestable too, however slow it looks: a record
  that merely sits on another party's calendar is requestable, not
  impossible. A bar is a rule already on the
  books forbidding the transfer, not a decision still pending.
  Only an armed gating trigger carries the map to pass; a pending
  manageable trigger always takes its disposition. A pass names
  what would rearm the map in the deal's favour.

An ask above the range maps as a price condition, never by itself
a pass.

## The triggers for closing

One condition per pending or armed row — one-to-one, numbered,
citing its row. Each names the instrument, the record, the party
that issues it and the timing (before LOI, before signing, at
close, post-close):

- a closing deliverable for a document the deciding party issues;
- a specific indemnity or escrow for a bounded liability;
- contingent consideration for revenue whose survival waits on an
  event the seller cannot settle before close;
- a price reduction for a quantified, unfunded need;
- retention or non-compete for people the value walks out with.

Timing maps from those four marks — never an invented period,
percentage, escrow size or earnout. A trigger never predicts what
the document will say. A clean map still prints this section —
"no armed triggers" is a reading, not an omission.

## Output contract — the read

First line: the verdict word alone, the deciding trigger, the ask
or its absence — the reading the committee gets before anything
is cut. Then the five sections, in this order:

1. `## 1. Governing terms` — ≤150 words: what is being decided,
   why the restated base justifies it, the deciding trigger named
   in its row's words.
2. `## 2. Verdict map` — every finding in input order: #, finding,
   tier and status as filed, map cell ≤40 words stating the
   deciding fact. `Count: <n triggered / n pending / n clear / n
   void>` beneath, re-counting to every row.
3. `## 3. Price supported` — ≤300 words plus comp table: the
   restated base, include/exclude readings, directional
   adjustments, the ask's implied multiple, the two products, the
   range last.
4. `## 4. Verdict` — ≤140 words: the verdict as first words, the
   deciding triggers, why the other two do not map.
5. `## 5. Triggers for closing` — one bullet per pending or armed
   row, ≤45 words each, instrument named.

Cut narration before cutting rows, arithmetic, the verdict or the
triggers — the tail is what a long memo loses. Every reading is
non-interchangeable: a line that could map another finding gets
the deciding words instead.
