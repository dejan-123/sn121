# Board notes — a senior member's annotations

Annotations a senior committee member leaves on verdict maps before
the vote. They explain what the board is for — and where maps go
wrong when they are drawn carelessly.

## On printed verdicts

The input arrives covered in verdicts — Resolved, In-Progress,
Informational — and none of them is yours. The board exists to
separate what the analyst concluded from what the record shows.
A map that reproduces the filing has done photocopying, not
diligence. But a map that changes a label without showing the
contradiction has done something worse: it has hidden the
disagreement the committee most needs to see.

## On armed versus pending

The discipline that matters most is the tier line. An armed trigger
on a manageable row is a loud fact, not a gating one — the map
flags it pending with its reason, and the memo carries its
disposition. Escalating it to a block manufactures a crisis the
analyst did not file; ignoring it manufactures confidence the
record does not support. The flagged middle is the honest mark.

## On what a clean map looks like

A clean map is not an empty one. It still shows every row carried,
the base restated, the range computed — the committee trusts the
verdict because it watched the machine run. A proceed over a map
with armed triggers is not boldness; it is a reading error with a
recommendation attached. And the mirrored failure — a map that
arms triggers to justify caution — teaches the committee to
discount the next armed trigger, when it might be real.

## On price

Price is the only trigger that never decides alone. An ask above
the range changes the negotiation, not the verdict — the map
records the gap, names the price condition, and keeps the call on
the record. A pass that traces back to arithmetic alone is a pass
that was never about the deal.

## On what survives a vote

The maps that hold up in the room share a shape: every row
accounted for, every disagreement visible, two products and one
division in the price section, a verdict that names what would
change it. The ones that fail share a different shape: a dropped
row, a re-tiered label with no bridge, a downside figure composed
rather than found, a verdict whose blocker a phone call could
clear.
