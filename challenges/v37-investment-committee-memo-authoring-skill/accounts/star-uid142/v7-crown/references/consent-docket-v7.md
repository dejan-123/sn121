# Worked memo - invented example

Calder Point Foods, a contract sauce manufacturer. The register files two Critical rows: an environmental discharge claim marked Resolved, and a key-recipe license marked Resolved. Reported EBITDA 7.9M with a 1.1M insurance recovery inside it that recurs annually nowhere. Debt 9.5M and cash 1.3M are supplied. Ask 64M. Retained peers quote 8-10x EBITDA.

## The docket check

Discharge claim: the only evidence is counsel's note that "settlement terms are substantially agreed" - no executed deed anywhere in the packet. Under the proof rule this row is **open**, not resolved.

Recipe license: the narrative says consent of the founder-licensor is required on a change of control, and no signed consent appears in the record. Also **open** - the cure is a specific named instrument.

Open gating rows: 2.

**Recommendation: request more data** - two Critical closures lack their instruments (executed discharge settlement; licensor's written change-of-control consent); EV 54-64M, equity 46-56M.

## 1. Deal thesis

Calder Point manufactures branded sauces for retailers on multi-year contracts. The sustainable base is roughly 6.8M EBITDA once the non-recurring insurance recovery is removed. The decisive exposures are the two Critical rows above - each has a known cure but neither is evidenced yet.

## 2. Tiered risk table

| Finding | Filed tier | Filed status | Standing | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Environmental discharge claim | Critical | Resolved | Open - no executed settlement deed evidenced | Deliverable before signing |
| Recipe license change-of-control consent | Critical | Resolved | Open - written consent not in record | Deliverable before signing |
| Retailer concentration, top account 41% | Manageable | Open | Multi-year contract runs to term | Placement and covenant |
| Export paperwork backlog | Informational | Open | Administrative | 100-day plan |

Open gating rows: 2.

## 3. Valuation

Base: 7.9M reported EBITDA less 1.1M one-time recovery = 6.8M.

Comps: two contract manufacturers at 8.0x and 8.6x retained; a branded producer at 10.1x retained as an upper anchor for mix; an imported-goods reseller at 6.2x dropped - different asset intensity.

Placement: mid-pack margins but heavier retailer dependence - 8.0x low, 9.4x high.

8.0 x 6.8M = 54.4M enterprise value
9.4 x 6.8M = 63.9M enterprise value

Equity bridge (debt and cash supplied): net debt 9.5M - 1.3M = 8.2M. Equity value 54.4 - 8.2 = 46.2M low; 63.9 - 8.2 = 55.7M high. The 64M ask implies 9.4x the corrected base and 55.8M equity - at the very top of the defended range, so price discipline rests entirely on holding 9.4x justified.

## 4. Recommendation

Request more data. Signing waits on two named instruments: the executed discharge settlement and the licensor's written change-of-control consent. The price ceiling of roughly 46-56M equity is set now. If either instrument fails - terms rejected, consent refused - the deal reprices or ends.

## 5. Conditions and next actions

1. Discharge claim - outside counsel obtains the executed settlement deed before signing; an adverse outcome ends the deal.
2. License consent - deal lead obtains the licensor's written consent before signing; refusal ends the deal.
3. Retailer dependence - commercial lead secures the top account's renewal-intent letter; absence moves placement to the range bottom.
4. Export backlog - operations lead schedules clearance inside the 100-day plan.

---

## Alternate ending - the instruments arrive

If both the deed and the consent are produced in the packet: open gating rows fall to 0 and the verdict becomes `proceed` with the same valuation - the retailer concentration stays a covenant, not a gate.

## Alternate ending - refusal on the record

If the licensor's refusal is unconditional and recorded - "will not consent to any transfer" - no buyer act cures it and the verdict becomes `pass`.
