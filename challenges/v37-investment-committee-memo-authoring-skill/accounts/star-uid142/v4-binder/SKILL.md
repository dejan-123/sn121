# The closing binder — five tabs, then the summary

A deal does not close on arguments; it closes on documents. Build
the memo the way the data room gets built: tab by tab, each item
received, outstanding, or waived — and the resolution writes
itself off what the binder still lacks. The memo the committee
reads is the binder's cover summary, and it is the deliverable.

Complete the tabs before writing the summary; the resolution on
line one is the resolution the tabs already support.

## Tab A — the row ledger

Filed as clear-before-deal (Must-Address, Critical, Deal-Breaker,
Blocker, High — name the ledger's own label set): these items
gate the close. Filed below that tier: conditions and price items
at worst — however loudly a lighter row reads, it never gates;
where its facts argue it was filed too low, flag the filing —
never re-file it.

Each gating item is **received**, **outstanding**, or **waived**:

- **Received** — `Resolved` holds where the artifacts corroborate
  it or where the exposure persists by nature (a consent the deal
  always needs, a right the contract always held). The row's own
  wording never undoes its own status; the proving paper goes on
  the checklist.
- **Outstanding** — `Resolved` is marked outstanding where another
  artifact shows the closing paper does not exist: a renewal now
  out to bid, a review deferred, an application filed and
  ungranted, a consent required and nowhere shown. `Unresolved`,
  `In-Progress`, blank — outstanding.

Count the outstanding gating items in words under the ledger.
That count is the spine of the resolution.

A ledger filing nothing in the gating tier has a count of zero
by construction — the binder resolves proceed, and the summary
says it aloud: nothing on this ledger is a deal-breaker. Items
that merely read heavy — pending claims with injunctive
potential, regulator applications awaiting decisions, churn
metrics below par — hold their filed tier: flagged, priced into
the checklist, never promoted into the count. The flag travels
with the row; the label stays; the count reads only what was
filed.

## Tab B — the number proof

The seller's totals are received as filed but repriced before they
support a number. Rebuild the base deduction by deduction:

- out: one-time revenue (a single project, a bulk order);
- out: revenue the binder cannot deliver — grants, licences,
  contracts ending on a change of control;
- out: revenue already gone — the narrative's own post-loss
  run-rate;
- held, flagged: revenue merely at risk — renewals pending,
  customers who may leave — it stays priced and moves the terms;
- out: projections that need pilots or prospects to convert.

Add-backs stand only when plainly non-recurring and evidenced —
an annual "one-off" is a cost. Where the transferable earnings
base is negative, no earnings multiple can be received — carry
revenue and say why. The proof appears in the memo: reported
figure, each deduction, the corrected base.

## Tab C — the peer file

Each comparable is filed **kept** or **returned** with its reason:
a control-premium transaction filed among trading prices; NM on
the multiple in use; an outlier further from its neighbour than
the rest are from each other. A comp that fails its return test
stays — its distance becomes placement.

Position the target on growth, margin and retention among the
kept set; a weakness the kept peers lack pushes low. Where the
artifacts flag the set as not like-for-like, record the confidence
cost in a sentence.

The range is two products the committee can recompute, decimals
split: `3.7x × $20.0M = 3 × $20.0M + 0.7 × $20.0M = $60.0M +
$14.0M = $74.0M`. The peer file prices the enterprise; the buyer
pays that less net debt — the bridge takes one line, or is marked
to confirm. Then the ask is restated as an implied multiple of
the corrected base, with the gap in money and percent; no ask
filed, and the range is the guidance.

## Tab D — the resolution

- **proceed** — the outstanding gating count is zero. The
  resolution says it in words: nothing on this ledger is a
  deal-breaker. Outstanding lighter items ride on the checklist;
  an ask above range becomes a ceiling item — the first entry —
  never a rejection.
- **proceed, signature-gated** — one gating item outstanding, and
  it is a document a named counterparty can simply sign (a
  consent, an executed amendment, an agreement): proceed with it
  as a condition precedent — unsigned, no signing; refused, the
  resolution converts to pass.
- **request more data** — gating items outstanding whose closure
  is an outcome, not a signature: a ruling, an investigation, an
  audit result, a reversal of a position the counterparty has
  already stated. The request is a delivery instruction: the
  record, its issuer, its deadline, and which way each answer
  moves the resolution.
- **pass** — an item the binder can never hold: an unconditional
  refusal on record; a term or rule on the books barring the
  transfer itself, including a material share of the priced base —
  and a funding stream, licence or grant whose own terms prohibit
  transfer to this class of buyer bars that share the same way, no
  instrument signs over a term that defines the buyer out; a
  priced exclusive right — the patent, licence or franchise the
  asset is built on — with a dated expiry inside the hold, where
  rival filings already stand over the successor landscape, is a
  right that ends on a schedule no party can move and no closing
  paper extends.
  Everything short is outstanding, not impossible: an unrequested
  signature, a pending decision on someone else's desk, revenue
  at risk, an ask above range — none of these is a pass.

## Tab E — the closing checklist

Each numbered item cites its row and names the instrument that
fits: a signable document → condition precedent (no paper, no
signing); a bounded liability → specific indemnity, escrowed where
the artifacts size it; revenue hostage to an outside event →
contingent consideration; a quantified gap the plan omits → price
reduction; people the value walks out with → retention and
non-compete; lighter items → covenant, holdback or 100-day item.
Every entry carries its finding, the evidence that satisfies it,
its timing, and the consequence if it fails. Nothing sized, dated
or named that the artifacts did not give. Data requests go last.

## The cover summary — the memo itself

Only the finished memo — no binder, no tabs, no cover page, no
title line. The first characters are the verdict: `**Recommendation:
<resolution>** — <the item that decides it>; <supported range vs
the ask>`. The binder vocabulary stays on the desk; the memo reads
as a memo.

1. `## 1. Thesis` (≤150 words) — the case for the asset, the
   primary risk — a row the ledger must carry.
2. `## 2. Risk table` — every row: finding | tier filed | status
   labelled | standing on the evidence | consequence (gates the
   close / moves the price / terms / post-close); the outstanding
   gating count in words beneath.
3. `## 3. Valuation` (≤300 words + tables) — the number proof,
   the peer file marked kept/returned with reasons, positioning,
   both range products, the debt bridge, implied ask and gap.
4. `## 4. Recommendation` (≤140 words) — the resolution, the tab
   that produced it, what would change it. Equals line one.
5. `## 5. Conditions` — the checklist, numbered, ≤45 words each.

Written as the cover note on a binder a senior deal professional
would sign: flowing, specific, every claim pinned to its tab.
No sentence frame used twice; complete inside the bounds.
