# Binder — Larkspur Home-Care Group, built and summarised

The pack on **Larkspur Home-Care Group**, a domiciliary care
provider running ~340 carers across three regions, came in for a
decision. Below: the binder as built, then the cover summary as
delivered.

## The pack (compressed)

Row ledger as filed:

| Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Care regulator inspection rated the southern branch "requires improvement"; re-inspection window opens in 5 months | Must-Address | Resolved |
| Local-authority framework contract (38% of revenue) goes to re-tender in 9 months; incumbent status noted | Must-Address | Unresolved |
| Care-worker mileage reimbursement below statutory minimum in two regions; payroll correction budgeted | Manageable | In-Progress |
| Background-check renewals not tracked for 14% of carers | Informational | Unresolved |

Narrative: LTM revenue $27.8M including a $1.9M one-time retro
payment from the local authority covering two prior years. EBITDA
$2.9M. Cash $0.6M; debt $4.2M. Growth 8% on framework-contract
expansion. The commissioner relationship for the largest contract
runs through the founder, who plans to exit at close. Asking
price $34.0M.

Peer file: CareFirst Group 8.1x EV/EBITDA (11% growth) ·
HomeBridge Care 6.7x (6%) · ElderLine Services 9.4x (15%) ·
MediHome 13.0x (22%) — a take-private · NightOwl Care NM
(pre-profit).

## Tab A — the row ledger

Gating tier = Must-Address. Item 1 is filed `Resolved` and marked
**outstanding**: the rating has not been lifted — the re-inspection
that would lift it has not happened. A scheduled examination is
not a passed one. Item 2 is `Unresolved` — **outstanding**; the
re-tender's outcome is a decision the authority must reach, not a
paper anyone can sign. Item 3 sits below the gating tier — terms.
Item 4 is filed Informational, but untracked safeguarding checks
on 14% of carers reads above its label: **flagged as an apparent
mis-filing** — the flag travels into the memo; the label stays.

Outstanding gating items: **two**.

## Tab B — the number proof

$27.8M reported, less the $1.9M one-time retro payment — two prior
years' revenue booked once — = **$25.9M** the binder delivers.
EBITDA $2.9M clean. The framework contract's 38% is at risk, not
gone: held in the base, flagged to the resolution.

## Tab C — the peer file

MediHome is **returned** — a control-premium take-private filed
among trading prices. NightOwl is **returned** on the earnings
metric (NM). Kept: 6.7x–9.4x. Larkspur's contract concentration
and flagged compliance items put it below mid: 7.0x–8.0x.

- 7.0x × $2.9M = 7 × $2.9M = **$20.3M**
- 8.0x × $2.9M = 8 × $2.9M = **$23.2M**

Enterprise $20.3M–$23.2M, less net debt $3.6M → equity
**$16.7M–$19.6M**. The $34.0M ask implies 11.7x the corrected base
— above the range and every kept peer; the price is a checklist
item, not the resolution.

## Tab D — the resolution

Two gating items outstanding, and both close on outcomes, not
signatures: the regulator's re-inspection report, and the
re-tender outcome (or interim confirmation of Larkspur's position
in the process). **Request more data** is the resolution — and it
is an instruction, not a stall.

## Tab E + cover summary — the memo as delivered

**Recommendation: request more data** — the inspection rating and
the 38%-of-revenue re-tender are outstanding on the evidence;
supported equity range $16.7M–$19.6M against a $34.0M ask.

## 1. Thesis
Larkspur runs a real domiciliary-care book — recurring local-
authority revenue, established commissioner relationships — and
the acquisition case is that book at a high-single-digit multiple.
The primary risk is revenue continuity: 38% of it re-tenders in
nine months while a branch rating, which commissioners weigh,
sits "requires improvement" on the evidence. Those two items
frame the vote.

## 2. Risk table

| Finding | Tier filed | Status labelled | Standing on evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Branch inspection rating | Must-Address | Resolved | **Outstanding** — re-inspection not yet held | Gates the resolution |
| Framework re-tender (38% of revenue) | Must-Address | Unresolved | Outstanding — outcome pending | Gates the resolution |
| Mileage reimbursement shortfall | Manageable | In-Progress | As filed — correction budgeted | Terms |
| Untracked background checks (14%) | Informational | Unresolved | Flagged — reads above its label; flag travels, label stays | Terms |

Two gating items stand outstanding. Item 1's label is a claim the
memo declines to carry: a re-inspection scheduled is not a rating
restored.

## 3. Valuation
The base the binder delivers is $25.9M — reported $27.8M less the
$1.9M one-time retro payment — with clean EBITDA of $2.9M. The
framework contract's 38% stays in the base; its exposure is priced
through the checklist, not stripped. Of five peers, MediHome is
returned as a control premium and NightOwl on the earnings
metric. The kept set runs 6.7x–9.4x EV/EBITDA; concentration and
compliance flags place Larkspur below mid: 7.0x–8.0x.

- 7.0x × $2.9M = **$20.3M**
- 8.0x × $2.9M = **$23.2M**

Enterprise $20.3M–$23.2M, less net debt of $3.6M → equity
**$16.7M–$19.6M**. The $34.0M ask implies 11.7x — above the range
and every kept peer; in a complete binder it becomes a ceiling
item, and it never becomes the resolution.

## 4. Recommendation
**Request more data.** Two gating items are outstanding and both
close on named outcomes: the regulator's re-inspection report
(scheduled within five months) and the framework re-tender
result — or, in the interim, written confirmation of Larkspur's
standing in the process. A clean inspection report and a retained
contract settle both items and support proceed at no more than
$19.6M of equity; a lost contract converts the resolution to pass.
Neither outcome is assumed today.

## 5. Conditions
1. The regulator's re-inspection report for the southern branch,
   before signing — an unchanged or worsened rating re-opens the
   resolution.
2. Written confirmation of Larkspur's position in the re-tender
   process, before signing; the final award, before close — a
   lost award converts the resolution to pass.
3. Contingent consideration on the framework contract: the 38%
   of revenue it carries is paid for as it survives the re-tender.
4. Founder retention and non-compete covering the commissioner
   relationship through the re-tender period.
5. Evidence the mileage correction has run through payroll, plus
   a specific indemnity for any look-back liability, sized only
   on figures the artifacts give.
6. A background-check completion report covering the 14% of
   carers, post-close — the Informational label stays, and the
   flag says why the paper is still required.

## The same binder, other outcomes

**If the inspection had come back "good" and the re-tender
awarded** — outstanding count zero: **proceed**, said plainly —
nothing on this ledger is a deal-breaker — with equity capped at
$19.6M and the compliance flag carried as a post-close item.

**If the authority had already awarded the contract to a
competitor** — the outcome is on record: 38% of the priced book
is gone and no checklist entry restores it. **Pass** — the base
the binder was asked to deliver no longer exists; a new pack on
a smaller Larkspur is a new matter.

**If the only outstanding item were a signature** — say the
framework contract already awarded but requiring a countersigned
novation the authority can simply execute: **proceed, gated** —
the novation is a condition precedent (no signature, no signing;
refusal converts to pass), not a request for data.

## Binders that come back unsigned

1. **A scheduled exam treated as a passed one.** `Resolved` on a
   rating not yet lifted is the pack's most common false label;
   the memo must say why the committee cannot rely on it.
2. **A stall dressed as a request.** "Further diligence required"
   names nothing; a real request carries the record, its issuer,
   its deadline, and the answer that changes the resolution.
3. **Arithmetic the committee cannot recompute.** "Approximately
   $20–23M" with no products shown asserts a range instead of
   proving one — every product is split and written out.
4. **The small row promoted.** The background-check gap is real
   and flagged — and it still does not gate the close. Flags
   travel; labels stay; the tier system is the committee's, not
   the memo's.

## A second binder — the expiring crown

Different pack: **Corvidan Map Works**, a nautical-chart licensor
whose entire revenue line rode on one exclusive hydrographic
licence. The licence carried a dated expiry inside the hold
period — and the artifacts showed two rival publishers already
filed for the successor rights.

The ledger was small; the resolution was not. The crown right
ends on a schedule no party can move: no consent paper extends an
expiry date, no indemnity recreates exclusivity the state grants
by grant. With rival filings already standing over the successor
landscape, the binder held nothing that could deliver the priced
asset past its date. **Pass** was the resolution — not on price,
not on the lighter rows, but on the one item no checklist can
hold: time itself.

The contrast the desk kept: a renewal *application pending* is an
outcome to request — the paper exists, someone issues it. An
expiry already written into the grant is not a pending anything;
it is the end of the thing being bought. The first asks for a
document; the second has no document to ask for.
