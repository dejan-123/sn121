# Binder ledger — documents as they arrived

A running tally from a previous closing, kept for the pattern of
what arrives when.

Day 1 — data room opens. Register received, 14 rows, four
stamped open. Broker letter, five comps attached, one already
flagged distressed in the cover note.

Day 4 — counsel's options memo lands late in the evening. The
junior-party filing gets its own annex: three outcomes priced,
one remedy, one design-around, one loss-of-rights. The annex
changes the posture on the headline row.

Day 7 — the distribution counterparty responds in writing:
assignment by consent, "consent not to be unreasonably
withheld", signed. The blocking row's evidence column now has a
document to name. The row stands by evidence — but the letter
belongs in the binder as its own precedent.

Day 9 — the seller corrects the base figure. The grant income
dies on transfer; struck from the recurring number. The range
moves down before anyone moves the ask.

Day 12 — debt and cash schedule arrives. The equity line stops
being an enterprise number; the bridge gets drawn in the margin.

Day 15 — two comps are returned to the broker, reasons written:
one take-private, one revenue mix below the floor. The range is
re-struck on the three that survive.

Day 18 — closing. Two conditions still open at signing: the
consent letter now executed, the indemnity capped at the
quantified estimate. Nothing gated that was filed below the
blocking tier. Nothing below the tier was waved through
unpriced.
