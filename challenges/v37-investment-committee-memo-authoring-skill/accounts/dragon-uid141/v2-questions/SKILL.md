# Question-first — the memo reports the examination

The artifacts arrive pre-labeled: statuses filed, bases chosen,
multiples quoted. A memo that inherits those labels has examined
nothing. Each element takes the stand as the question it raises, is
answered from the record, and the memo reports what stayed open.
The input's own labels are never rewritten: a row keeps the tier and
status the analyst filed, and the answer states the disagreement
beside the filed word — never a quiet re-tier, never a repair.

Every figure answers from the inputs or is worked from them in
view. What the inputs do not contain answers as a named gap —
`[to confirm: …]` — never assumed in the seller's favour.

## Q0 — what is the committee being asked?

Acquire <target> on what terms. State the asking price — or state
that none was given and carry that absence as a question the memo
surfaces, never a number to invent.

## Q1 — does each status survive its own record?

Each finding answers "does the record confirm the filed label?".
One standing per row — no fifth exists, and `proceed`, `request
more data`, `pass` are memo answers, never row standings:

| Standing | The answer when |
|---|---|
| `blocks` | an unanswered question filed at the gating tier (Must-Address/Critical): filed unresolved, or `Resolved` whose own evidence disagrees — a re-bid still live, a review deferred not closed, a consent the text never documents, a claim the row calls pending. The filed tier decides; a Manageable row that looks deal-critical is flagged open with the reason, never re-tiered. The deciding datum is quoted. |
| `open` | a named record or step answers it; the answer is written in the cell, and under proceed it stays visible — no question reads ignored |
| `settled` | the record confirms the filed label — including a `Resolved` beside an exposure that persists by nature (a consent the licence always needs, a right to cancel): the label stands, the proving document becomes a closing ask |
| `moot` | informational or outside the decision; no ask spent |

Every finding raises its question — none dropped, none merged;
rows sharing one root cause keep separate lines and cross-reference
("same question as row N"). A question the record raises but the
table never asked earns its own row, grounded in the artifacts'
words. An analyst note flagging a contradiction is the question
asked openly, never smoothed.

## Q2 — what does the base answer to?

Three questions before any multiple is quoted. What of this recurs?
— strip the completed one-time project. What of this transfers? —
exclude revenue a for-profit acquirer cannot keep: non-transferable
grants, terminable contracts, customers already gone, and restate
the run-rate net of them. Which figure does this industry's metric
use? — where the standard is net revenue, the gross figure is the
seller's framing, not the base. Growth restates on the restated
base. Where EBITDA is negative on the economics that transfer, or
every comp is pre-profitability, "is EV/EBITDA meaningful?" answers
no — EV/Revenue carries the valuation. Add-backs answer whether
they are genuinely one-time; evergreen costs in costume answer no —
a rejected add-back is never subtracted twice. Growth contingent on
unsigned deals — prospects in study, unconverted pilots — answers
"claim": priced growth is realized growth. Each figure is computed
once and reused unchanged.

## Q3 — why does each comp belong?

Every comparable answers "why do you belong in this set?" — named
included or excluded with its reason: a control or sponsor premium
beside trading peers; growth or margin so far from the target it
prices another business; an NM multiple on the metric in use. A
limitation the comp set's own note declares — mix undisclosed, one
viable multiple — is carried into the answer, not priced around.
The target is set against the included median in words with a
direction: size, margin, growth.

## Q4 — what does the range answer?

Two products only: an included comp's own multiple × base = value,
written in $M to one decimal. The range is those two products,
the valuation's last line. The ask answers as one implied multiple
on the priced base — once per base where the base restates
(gross-to-net, blended-to-recurring): the two answers are the
comparison, not new arithmetic. These are the memo's only
calculations. Where net debt or cash is material, the bridge is
named — EV less net debt. No ask stated → the range is bid guidance.

## Q5 — what is the answer?

The recommendation answers `proceed`, `request more data` or
`pass`, names the deciding questions and why the other two answers
do not fit.

- `proceed` — every gating question answered. An open manageable
  question never vetoes: its named answer posts among the asks.
  Declining to answer a clean set is as wrong as proceeding over a
  blocking question.
- `request more data` — each blocking question has a named answer
  a document can give: a consent, a closure letter, an executed
  renewal, a clearance, an audited figure — and the party that
  produces it.
- `pass` — a blocking question the record already answered against
  the deal: a counterparty on record refusing; a term or rule that
  bars the transfer itself — the asset cannot legally move to the
  buyer; revenue that may not survive post-close is a base
  question, never a bar; or two or more blocking
  questions whose only answers sit with parties outside the deal's
  control — sovereign determinations (a court, an agency, an
  accreditation body), refusing counterparties, findings no record
  can settle. A signature nobody has asked for yet — a licensor's
  consent, a customer's renewal — is askable, not outside-held; a
  permission still pending — an application, an approval not yet
  granted — is askable too, however slow it looks: a record that
  merely sits on another party's calendar is a question, not an
  impossibility. A bar is a rule already on the books
  forbidding the transfer, not a decision still pending. Only a
  blocking question carries the answer to pass; an open
  manageable question always takes its named ask. A pass names
  what would reopen the question.

An ask above the range answers as a price condition, never by
itself a pass.

## The asks — conditions

One ask per open or questioned row — one-to-one, numbered, citing
its row. Each names the instrument, the record, the answering
party and the timing (before LOI, before signing, at close,
post-close):

- a closing deliverable for a document the deciding party can give;
- a specific indemnity or escrow for a bounded liability;
- contingent consideration for revenue whose survival waits on an
  event the seller cannot answer before close;
- a price reduction for a quantified, unfunded need;
- retention or non-compete for people the answer walks out with.

Timing answers from those four marks — never an invented period,
percentage, escrow size or earnout. An ask never predicts what the
document will say. Zero asks is a complete answer — the section
reports "no open questions," it does not vanish.

## Output contract — the read

First line: the answer word alone, the deciding question, the ask
or its absence — the line the committee reads before anything is
cut. Then the five sections, in this order:

1. `## 1. Question before committee` — ≤150 words: what is being
   asked, why the restated base justifies it, the deciding question
   named in its row's words.
2. `## 2. Sharpened findings` — every finding in input order: #,
   finding, tier and status as filed, answer cell ≤40 words stating
   the deciding fact. `Count: <n blocks / n open / n settled / n
   moot>` beneath, re-counting to every row.
3. `## 3. Valuation` — ≤300 words plus comp table: the base's
   three answers, include/exclude reasons, directional adjustments,
   the ask's implied multiple, the two products, the range last.
4. `## 4. Recommendation` — ≤140 words: the answer as first
   words, the deciding questions, why the other two do not fit.
5. `## 5. Asks` — one bullet per open or questioned row, ≤45
   words each, instrument named.

Cut narration before cutting rows, arithmetic, the answer or the
asks — the tail is what a long memo loses. Every answer is
non-interchangeable: a sentence that could answer another finding
gets the deciding words instead.

the memo in that shape; references/examination-notes.md and
references/counsel-marginalia.md carry the precedent answers and
the reviewer's questions — examine with them too.
