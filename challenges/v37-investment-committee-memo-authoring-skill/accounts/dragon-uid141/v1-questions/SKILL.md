# First Question — the memo answers the committee's questions

The artifacts arrive pre-labeled: statuses filed, bases chosen,
multiples quoted. A memo that inherits those labels has answered
nothing. This skill converts each element into the question it
raises, answers it from the record, and reports what stays open —
an unanswered gating question is what a committee must see before
it votes.

## The questioning pass

**Pass 1 — Frame the question.** Write what the committee is being
asked: acquire <target> on what terms. State the asking price — or
state that none was given and treat that absence as a question the
process has left open, never as a number to invent.

**Pass 2 — Sharpen each finding.** A filed status is not a status —
it is the question "does the record back this label?" A Must-Address
marked Resolved asks: where is the document? If the record shows no
consent letter, no auditor opinion, no signed renewal, no closure —
only a deferral, an expectation, a conversation — the answer is that
the question remains open, and the memo says so with the deciding
absence named. A Manageable that the record confirms stays Manageable
— sharpening tests the label, it does not hunt for upgrades. An
appended analyst note flagging a contradiction is the question asked
out loud; carry it, do not paraphrase it away. And an under-filed row
— a standards breach filed as routine, a churn signal filed as
informational — gets its real question named: "why is this not a
higher tier?"

**Pass 3 — Interrogate the base.** Before any multiple is quoted the
revenue base answers three questions. What of this recurs? — strip
the completed one-time project. What of this transfers? — exclude
revenue a for-profit acquirer cannot keep: non-transferable grants,
terminable contracts, customers already gone, and restate the run-rate
net of them. Which figure does this industry's metric use? — where
the standard is net revenue, the gross figure is the seller's framing,
not the base. Growth is restated on the restated base. Where EBITDA
is negative on the economics that transfer, or every comp is
pre-profitability, the question "is EV/EBITDA meaningful?" answers
no — EV/Revenue carries the valuation. Add-backs are asked whether
they are genuinely one-time; evergreen costs in costume are answered
"no" — a rejected add-back is never subtracted twice. Growth
contingent on unsigned deals — prospects in study, unconverted
pilots — answers "claim": priced growth is realized growth.

**Pass 4 — Cross-examine the comps.** Each comp answers "why do you
belong in this range?" Excluded with the reason written down:
growth/margin profile outliers; acquisitions closed at a control or
strategic premium — a banker note flagging it is the confession;
comps whose revenue mix cannot map to the target absent a stated
adjustment. Where the comp set's own note declares a limitation —
mix undisclosed, only one multiple viable — the limitation is quoted
into the memo rather than priced around. Then the arithmetic answers
in the open: stated base times stated multiple, low comp to high
comp. The ask answers its own question — "what multiple are you on
the restated base?" — the implied multiple beside the range.

**Pass 5 — The headline answer.** The answer has three states —
proceed, request more data, pass — and it is what the questioning
produced, not a tone. An unanswered must-address question means
proceed is off the table — always; a proceed written over an open
must-address row contradicts the table and fails the memo. Then ask
who holds the answer. Where open items each have a named document
that would settle them — a consent letter, a clearance, a signed
renewal, a resolving opinion — the answer is request more data: the
memo asks for the records and each open row carries the one that
would close it. Outside-held is a narrow count: only a sovereign
determination — a court ruling, agency verdict, accreditation
decision — a party on record refusing, or a finding no record can
settle (an expired patent cannot be re-papered). A signature nobody
has asked for yet — a licensor's consent, a customer's renewal — is
askable, not outside-held. Two or more open must-address items
outside-held answers pass: stacking strangers' verdicts
is a hope. The same answer holds where the open
question is structural — the core asset does not transfer, the
revenue base does not survive close — or the ask cannot reach the
range. Where no must-address question is open, proceed is a
complete and honest answer — manufacturing a blocking question to
sound diligent is the symmetric failure, and items below
must-address are hygiene named in the read, never dressed up as
gating asks. The thesis names one primary risk — the
question whose answer decides the deal — and that risk is present
in the findings table; a thesis risk absent from the table
contradicts the memo.

**Pass 6 — The remaining asks.** Each condition is a question with a
named closer: the finding it addresses, the exact record that would
answer it, the party who must produce it, and the deadline it
precedes. Quantified exposures get their arithmetic asked and
answered in dollars — set against the price, not gestured at.

## Answer vocabulary — row standings

- `blocks` — the question is unanswered and gating; the clean call
  is dead.
- `open` — a named record or step would answer it; the ask is
  written — under proceed the cell carries it, so no question reads
  ignored.
- `settled` — the record answers the filed label; carried as filed.
- `moot` — informational or outside the decision; no ask spent.
  No fifth label exists.

## Every finding raises its question

No input row is dropped or merged. Rows sharing one root question
keep separate lines and cross-reference ("same question as row N").
An answer that could be swapped under another finding is a template
answer — replace it with the deciding words from this input.

## The honest open set

"Every gating question is answered" is a complete deliverable when
the record supports it. A memo that invents a blocking question is
as wrong as one that hides an open one.

## When the record cannot answer

Missing ask, missing status column, two authorities in direct
conflict — the memo names the unanswered question and either proceeds
on what the record does answer or makes the question itself the
condition. Where two cited authorities disagree, the memo reports
the disagreement and asks for the resolving opinion — it never picks
the convenient reading. A specific the input does not give is written
as a bracketed `[to confirm: …]`, a named gap, never a guess.

## Questions a senior reviewer asks the memo

- **Which question kills the deal?** — the primary risk is the
  finding whose answer reprices or vetoes, not the largest number.
- **What is the unprinted answer?** — the implied ask multiple, the
  run-rate after departed revenue, the downside in dollars: compute
  the deciding figure and show the work.
- **Which way does it move?** — every restated base answers with its
  direction on the economics.
- **Whose framing is this?** — a multiple quoted on a flattering
  base gets the basis named and the restatement in the same breath.
- **Can this ask be answered?** — a condition no party can deliver
  is not a condition; it is the harder call wearing a costume.

## How the questioning fails

- Labels carried forward unasked — the filed Status column treated as
  testimony instead of a claim; upstream files run optimistic and
  this pass exists because they do.
- A comp average applied with no cross-examination of who belongs.
- Two currencies of truth in one answer — gross against net comps,
  full LTM against a recurring range.
- Asks that delegate ("advisers to consider") rather than close.
- A headline answer the questions cannot carry — clean proceed over
  a blocking item, or a pass where a signature ends the matter.

## Writing it down

Memo shape — the five required sections, in this order:

1. `**The question before the committee**` — the decision and the ask
   (or its absence), then two to four sentences of argument closing
   on the named primary risk, verbatim as it stands in the table.
2. `**The findings, sharpened**` — the table: `# | Finding | The
   question it raises | The record's answer | Standing`. Every row.
   Then `Still open: <n blocking / n open>` reconciling to it.
3. `**What the number answers**` — the base restated, each comp's
   in-or-out in a clause, the multiple, `base × multiple = range`,
   the ask's implied multiple beside it.
4. `**The answer**` — proceed, request more data, or pass in one
   sentence plus the deciding question; never contradicts the table.
5. `**The remaining asks**` — numbered: finding, record, party,
   timing; the character follows the answer. Last line: two sentences
   the committee can repeat.

Budget: the harness cuts an overlong memo at the tail — the answer
and the asks are the tail, so nothing may run long enough to starve
them. Hold every stated length bound the scenario gives; where none
is stated, the argument is four sentences, rows a line each, the
number six lines, the answer two sentences, each ask a line. Cut
narration before cutting rows.
