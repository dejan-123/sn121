# Worked example — the question-first memo

The user pastes three diligence artifacts and asks for the IC memo.
This file shows the full shape: the committee's question, the
sharpened findings table with its open-count line, the number worked
out loud, and asks that name record, party, and timing. Follow this
shape on every input.

---

## The input (abridged for illustration)

Acquisition of Vetridge Animal Health, a veterinary diagnostics
laboratory network. Risk table (Finding | Tier | Status):

- Central lab's accreditation under the national proficiency program
  is under review after a failed PT event; a remedial plan was
  accepted last month | Must-Address | Resolved
- Two reference opinions on whether the lab's data license permits
  use of accumulated test records in a for-profit acquirer's analytics
  product reach opposite conclusions | Must-Address | Unresolved
- Four of nine branch labs run on month-to-month premises leases |
  Manageable | Unresolved
- Client concentration: largest clinic group is 22% of test volume,
  contract evergreen with 90-day termination notice | Manageable |
  Resolved

Financial narrative: LTM revenue $19.4M, growth 21% YoY. Of this,
$1.6M was a completed instrument-validation project for a pharma
sponsor — finished, not recurring. Recurring diagnostics revenue
$17.8M, growth on that base 14%. Gross margin 58%. EBITDA $2.4M.
Seller asks $84M enterprise value.

Comparables (EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, LTM growth, gross margin):
LabCore Partners 4.4x/26x/24%/61% · VetScan Holdings 3.9x/22x/18%/57%
· PathLine Diagnostics 3.1x/16x/11%/52% · AnimalDx Cloud 5.2x/31x/
29%/63% · NovaVet Labs (acquired 15 months ago at a premium flagged
in the broker note) 6.7x/NM/41%/66%.

---

## The memo

**The question before the committee** — Acquire Vetridge for $84M.

The argument — Vetridge's recurring diagnostics base is durable:
58% gross margin on a sticky clinic channel, and the lab network's
sample-logistics moat is real. The thesis survives at the right
price. The primary risk is the central lab's accreditation standing —
if the PT review closes badly, the revenue engine itself stops
billing, and the filed "Resolved" is a remediation plan, not an
outcome.

**The findings, sharpened**

| # | Finding | The question it raises | The record's answer | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Central lab accreditation under review; remedial plan accepted | Has the review actually closed? | **open** — filed Resolved, but the record shows an accepted plan, not a closed review; an accepted plan is a schedule, not a verdict — the question stays open until the program issues clearance | Must-Address |
| 2 | Two reference opinions conflict on whether the data license survives acquisition | Does the analytics upside legally transfer? | **blocks** — genuinely unresolved: the record holds two contrary opinions and the memo does not pick the convenient one; the answer is a named condition, not a coin flip | Must-Address |
| 3 | Four of nine branch labs on month-to-month leases | Can the network be dismembered by landlords? | **open** — exposure bounded (4 of 9 sites) but real; the ask is named: written lease positions on each site | Manageable |
| 4 | Largest client 22% of volume, evergreen with 90-day notice | Is concentration resolved or merely unpriced? | **settled** — the record shows an evergreen contract properly filed; the 90-day notice is the standing risk of the format, carried into the open asks as renewal intent | Manageable |

**Still open:** 1 blocking / 2 open

**What the number answers**

What of the revenue recurs? The $1.6M validation project is
complete — the base that prices is $17.8M recurring at 14% growth,
not the $19.4M headline at 21%.

Which comps belong? NovaVet is excluded — the broker note flags a
premium-priced acquisition, and 6.7x paid for control is a
transaction price, not a market multiple. The surviving set runs
3.1x–5.2x, median 4.15x (LabCore, VetScan, PathLine, AnimalDx).
Vetridge's 14% growth sits below the set's middle but its margin is
at the middle — no further adjustment claimed.

Arithmetic out loud: 3.1x–5.2x × $17.8M ≈ $55M–$93M; the defensible
band is the median neighborhood, ~$66M–$80M. The ask's implied
multiple: $84M ÷ $17.8M = 4.7x — inside the outer range but above
the median band, and it would read as 4.3x only on the seller's
larger, less honest base. EBITDA check: $2.4M at 16x–26x gives
$38M–$62M — consistent only at the low end, the direction the
14%-growth answer argues.

**The answer** — Request more data: two must-address questions stand
open — the accreditation review that is only scheduled, and the
data-license question holding two contrary opinions — and each
answers to a named record. The ask sits above the median band on
the restated base, so the request carries a repriced proposal.

**The remaining asks**

1. Written clearance or current-status letter from the proficiency
   program on the PT review, obtained by the seller before signing —
   the accepted plan is not the answer.
2. A definitive opinion on the data-license question from counsel
   acceptable to the buyer, commissioned before definitive documents;
   if it resolves against transferability, the analytics value leaves
   the thesis and the price moves with it.
3. Written lease positions on all four month-to-month branch sites —
   term offers or named relocation plans — before close.
4. The largest client's renewal intent confirmed in writing; a
   90-day-notice contract carrying 22% of volume prices as 22% at
   risk until then.
5. [to confirm: PT-event history before the failed event — the
   artifacts show one failure and no priors.]

The read — A good lab network with two questions still open and
a price struck on the bigger, wrong base. Answer the accreditation
and license questions before signing; price the $17.8M that recurs.

## When nothing blocks — the answered shape

Had the PT review closed in writing and the license question carried
one clean opinion, every section is still present — each just
thinner:

**The question before the committee** — Acquire Vetridge for $84M.
The argument runs two sentences instead of four: a durable
diagnostics base on a sticky clinic channel, and nothing open that
gates the file.

**The findings, sharpened** — same four rows, never dropped. Rows 1
and 2 stand `settled`; rows 3 and 4 stand `open`, and each open
cell carries its named step — "lease positions asked," "renewal
intent asked" — so no row reads ignored under a proceed.

**Still open:** 0 blocking / 2 open

**What the number answers** — same restated base, same exclusions,
same arithmetic: ~$55M–$93M outer, ~$66M–$80M band. The $84M ask
implies 4.7x — just over the band, which is a price negotiation,
not a gate.

**The answer** — Proceed at the justified band: no must-address
question is open, and the memo prices to ~$80M with the ask's
premium named rather than absorbed.

**The remaining asks** — None gating. Standing items: the four
lease positions papered and the client's renewal intent confirmed
in writing — routine hygiene before close, in one line each.

The questioning runs identically — restraint changes the answer
and the asks, never the sharpness of the passes.

---

## When the answer is no — the pass shape

If the license question could not be conditioned — say the data
license is unambiguously personal to the seller entity and dies at
close — the analytics upside is gone and the base restates down with
it; if the ask still prices that upside, the answer is pass,
the findings table still shows every row, and the
asks section offers nothing it cannot deliver. A blocking question
no signature can answer is a verdict, not a negotiation.
