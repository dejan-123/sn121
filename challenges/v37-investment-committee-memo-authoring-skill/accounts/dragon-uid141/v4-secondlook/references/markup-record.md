# Markup record — a file the second reader corrected

The pack on **Pembroke Modular Housing** — a manufacturer and
installer of modular buildings — arrived believing itself clean.
It was not. Below: the markup, then the memo as issued.

## What the first draft believed (compressed)

Risk table as filed:

| Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusive dealer agreement covering three counties (28% of unit placements) requires the dealer principal's written consent on change of control; principal has indicated willingness, consent letter not yet executed | Must-Address | Resolved |
| Two crane-injury claims at customer sites; insurer notified, coverage position pending | Manageable | In-Progress |
| Factory lease has 14 months remaining; no renewal option on file | Informational | Unresolved |

Narrative: LTM revenue $44.6M including a $5.2M one-time emergency
shelter contract for a state agency. EBITDA $3.9M. Cash $0.9M;
debt $9.4M. Growth 16% on dealer-channel placements. Asking price
$58.0M.

Peers: BoxBuild Systems 7.4x EV/EBITDA (18% growth) · ModularSpace
Co 6.2x (11%) · PreFab Holdings 8.8x (24%) · ShelterCorp 12.1x
(31%) — acquired in a take-private · TinyStructures NM (pre-profit).

## The margin notes

**Rows.** Blocking tier = Must-Address. Row 1 is filed `Resolved`,
but its own text says the consent letter is "not yet executed" —
struck, the row stands **open**. Its closure is a signature the
dealer principal can simply give; that distinction runs the call.
Rows 2 and 3 sit below the blocking tier — flagged, never promoted.
Tally: one blocking row open.

**Number.** $44.6M less the $5.2M one-time emergency order = $39.4M
of transferable revenue. EBITDA $3.9M clean. The dealer channel's
28% is exposed, not gone — it stays in and moves the terms.

**Peers.** ShelterCorp is struck — a control-premium take-private,
not a trading price. TinyStructures is struck on the earnings
metric (NM). Kept 6.2x–8.8x; Pembroke's dealer concentration and
thin cash put it just below mid: 6.5x–7.5x.

- 6.5x × $3.9M = 6 × $3.9M + 0.5 × $3.9M = $23.4M + $1.95M = **$25.4M**
- 7.5x × $3.9M = 7 × $3.9M + 0.5 × $3.9M = $27.3M + $1.95M = **$29.3M**

Enterprise $25.4M–$29.3M, less net debt $8.5M → equity
**$16.9M–$20.8M**. The $58.0M ask implies 14.9x — struck to the
ceiling.

**Call.** One open blocking row, closed by a signature:
**proceed, gated on the dealer's written consent** — plus the
price ceiling.

## The memo as issued

**Recommendation: proceed** — conditional on the dealer principal's
executed change-of-control consent before signing; supported equity
range $16.9M–$20.8M against a $58.0M ask.

## 1. Thesis
Pembroke earns on repeat dealer-channel placements of modular
buildings at defensible margins; the acquisition case is that
channel at a mid-single-digit multiple. The primary risk is the
dealer agreement itself: 28% of placements ride on one consent the
file shows indicated but not executed. That signature — and the
ask's distance from the range — frames the offer.

## 2. Risk table

| Finding | Tier filed | Status labelled | Standing on evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dealer change-of-control consent (28% of placements) | Must-Address | Resolved | **Open** — letter unexecuted; signature-type closure | Gates the signing |
| Crane-injury claims | Manageable | In-Progress | As filed — coverage position pending | Terms |
| Factory lease expiry | Informational | Unresolved | As filed — no renewal option | Post-close |

One blocking row stands open. Row 1 keeps its filed label; the memo
states plainly that the label is a claim until the letter exists.

## 3. Valuation
The transferable base is $39.4M — reported $44.6M less the $5.2M
one-time emergency order — with clean EBITDA of $3.9M. The dealer
channel's 28% stays in the base: exposed revenue is priced through
terms, not stripped. Of five comps, ShelterCorp is struck as a
control premium and TinyStructures on the earnings metric. The
kept set runs 6.2x–8.8x EV/EBITDA; concentration pushes Pembroke
below mid: 6.5x–7.5x.

- 6.5x × $3.9M = $23.4M + $1.95M = **$25.4M**
- 7.5x × $3.9M = $27.3M + $1.95M = **$29.3M**

Enterprise $25.4M–$29.3M, less net debt of $8.5M → equity
**$16.9M–$20.8M**. The $58.0M ask implies 14.9x the corrected base
— nearly double the range top; price is the first term to move.

## 4. Recommendation
**Proceed**, gated on a signature. The single open blocking item is
the dealer's written change-of-control consent — a document the
counterparty can simply execute, already indicated. It becomes a
condition precedent: no executed letter, no signing; a refusal
turns the call to pass. Everything else on the table is terms.
The price is struck to the range: equity proceeds at no more than
$20.8M; the $58.0M ask is not defensible on the corrected base.

## 5. Conditions
1. Dealer principal's executed change-of-control consent letter,
   before signing — no letter, no signing; written refusal
   converts this memo to pass.
2. Price: equity at no more than $20.8M (the range top); the
   premium the ask carries is struck, not deferred.
3. Contingent consideration on dealer-covered placements: the 28%
   is paid for as it continues post-close.
4. Insurer's coverage position on the crane claims, before close;
   a specific indemnity for any exclusion, sized only on figures
   the artifacts give.
5. Factory lease renewal terms or a costed relocation plan,
   post-close within the lease term.

## Other markups — what changes the call

**If row 1's closure needed an outcome, not a signature** — the
dealer had already replied "we are reviewing whether to continue"
or the row needed a regulator's ruling: the closure is a decision
another party must reach, not a formality. The call is
**request more data**: the dealer's written position or the
ruling is the named record, and §5 asks for it instead of gating
on it. A counterparty that has stated a position — however hedged
— is a reversal to win, not a signature to collect.

**If the dealer had refused in writing, unconditionally** — the
consent cannot exist: 28% of placements have no legal path to the
buyer. **Pass** — a term on the books bars the transfer of a
material share of the priced base.

## Markups that get a second reader removed

1. **Upholding a label against the record.** `Resolved` on an
   unexecuted consent is a claim, not a closure — and proceeding
   over it is the failure the rubric is built to catch.
2. **Referring a signature.** Calling an executable consent
   "more data needed" stalls a deal that only needs a pen;
   signature-type closures gate the signing, they do not stall
   the vote.
3. **Inventing the counterparty's mind.** "The dealer will
   consent" is a fabricated specific; "indicated willingness"
   is what the file says, and the memo quotes that and no more.

## A second markup — the conditioned refusal

Different file: **Vantage Routing Systems**, a route-planning
service whose carrier integrations ran through one connectivity
partner. The partner had stated in writing it "will not continue
the integration if the acquirer is a logistics operator" — a
position taken, but conditioned on the buyer's identity, not on
the deal's existence.

The first draft read it as a refusal and struck the call to pass.
The margin corrected it: a refusal conditioned on the buyer's
attributes is a position in play — the file still held a counter
(the partner's own revenue share from Vantage argued for staying),
alternate integrators existed in the pack's own comp notes, and
the statement's condition named a structure question, not an
absolute bar. The call upheld: **request more data** — the
partner's formal written position, alternate-integration
feasibility, and counsel's options analysis, each named with
issuer and deadline.

The contrast the margin kept: had the partner written "we
terminate on any change of control, regardless of acquirer" —
unconditional, addressed to every buyer — that is a refusal on
record, and the strike to pass would have stood. The pen reads
the condition before it reads the refusal; a bounded regulatory
exposure beside it (a quantified fine, a named suspension window)
was a liability to indemnify and time, never a second refusal.

## A third markup — the clean file that scared the first reader

Different pack: **Halden Optics**, a precision-lens coater. The
first draft's call was a deferral — "several exposures warrant
resolution first". The table as filed:

| Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Product-liability claim in discovery, counsel estimates bounded exposure | Manageable | Unresolved |
| Two export licences pending routine annual renewal | Manageable | In-Progress |
| Customer-satisfaction score below peer median | Informational | Resolved |
| Founder's employment agreement expires mid-hold, renewal unsigned | Manageable | Unresolved |

The margin counted the table: zero rows in the blocking tier.
The scary rows were scary *facts*, not gating *filings* — a
claim in discovery is a claims-history number, a pending
renewal is a calendar, a weak score is a comp position, an
unsigned renewal is a retention item. The first reader had
promoted them into a gate the table never filed — and had
invented a fourth verdict, "defer", that does not exist.

The call upheld: **proceed** — written plainly, nothing on this
table is a deal-breaker — with the claim carried as a bounded
indemnity, the renewals as named closing documents, and the
score priced into the range's low end. The referral pen stayed
in the desk: it exists only for blocking rows, and the tally
read zero.

## A fourth markup — the opinion that could not help

Different pack: **Corvid Instruments**, whose premium rested on
a measurement-method patent expiring in eleven months, two rivals
already filed on the successor claims. The first draft requested
"patent counsel's freedom-to-operate opinion and a competitive-
response roadmap" — the deferral read diligent.

The margin struck it: opinion shopping beside a decided fact.
The term ends on the date whether the successors win or lose;
the premium was for exclusivity, and exclusivity has a shelf
date printed on it. No document extends a term; no opinion
unfiles a rival's continuation. Beside it sat an open supplier
certification — genuinely askable — and it did not matter: a
referral cannot cure a file the calendar already declined.

The call upheld: **pass**. The askable row was annotated as
moot — priced or waived, it could not gate a decision the
record had already made.
