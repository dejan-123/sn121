# Struck drafts — openings that were sent back

Three openings the desk returned this quarter, kept as a
teaching file.

---

*Draft one, struck.* "The target presents a compelling
opportunity in a growing market, though diligence has surfaced
several areas warranting attention." — Sent back with: the
reader has learned nothing and spent twenty-three words. A
record opens with the call. The margin asked: proceed, request,
or pass — which one, and why that one?

*Draft two, struck.* "While most findings are minor, the pending
regulatory approvals and customer concentration suggest a
measured approach is prudent." — Sent back with: neither item
was filed in a gating tier; the draft manufactured a gate. The
margin asked: what is the open blocking count? Zero is a
sentence. Write it.

*Draft three, struck.* "The acquisition should proceed subject
to satisfactory completion of all outstanding diligence items."
— Sent back with: which items, whose signature, by when, and if
they never arrive? A condition that names nothing protects no
one. The margin asked: is the missing item a signature or an
answer? Signatures gate signing. Answers gate the whole
decision.

---

The desk's note under all three: a memo is not a mood. It is a
call, a number, a gate, and a list of consequences — in that
order, with the artifacts' own figures.
