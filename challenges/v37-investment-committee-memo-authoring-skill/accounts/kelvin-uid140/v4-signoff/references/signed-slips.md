# Signed slips — a survey written out

The submission on **Ashcombe Pet Nutrition** — a premium pet-food
manufacturer that also co-packs for two national brands — came in
for a decision. Below: the survey, and the memo as issued.

## The submission (compressed)

Schedule of exposures:

| Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Sole-source animal-protein supplier (31% of input volume) gave written notice it will not renew and will not supply any acquirer of Ashcombe; no alternate supplier qualified | Must-Address | Resolved |
| Co-pack agreement with national brand (22% of revenue) requires counterparty consent on change of control; consent not yet requested | Must-Address | In-Progress |
| Elevated environmental salmonella positives in Q1; third-party audit scheduled | Manageable | In-Progress |

Narrative: LTM revenue $33.4M; EBITDA $4.4M includes a $0.6M
one-time insurance recovery. Cash $1.2M; debt $6.8M. Growth 11%.
The protein supplier's notice is described as "final"; management
notes qualification of an alternate supplier would take 12–18
months by its own estimate. Asking price $41.0M.

Market table: TailWag Nutrition 8.2x EV/EBITDA (14% growth) ·
KibbleWorks 6.9x (9%) · PurePaw Foods 9.6x (19%) · MegaBrand Pet
13.4x (24%) — a take-private · FreshPet Co-packer NM (pre-profit).

## The survey

**Schedule.** Blocking band = Must-Address. Row 1's `Resolved` label
is voided by its own content and the narrative together: a refusal
letter exists, it is described as final — the exposure is not
resolved, it is *decided against the deal*, which is worse than
open. Row 2 (`In-Progress`) is open — but askable: a consent that
was never requested. Row 3 stays Manageable — flagged, never
promoted.

**Book.** $33.4M revenue transfers; EBITDA corrects to $3.8M once
the non-recurring insurance recovery is removed.

**Market.** MegaBrand Pet is out — a control premium, not a trading
price. FreshPet is out on the earnings multiple (NM) though it
still bounds the revenue cross-check. Kept: 6.9x–9.6x. Ashcombe's
concentration and supplier exposure put it below the middle:
7.0x–8.0x.

- 7.0x × $3.8M = 7 × $3.8M = **$26.6M**
- 8.0x × $3.8M = 8 × $3.8M = **$30.4M**

Enterprise $26.6M–$30.4M; net debt $5.6M off → equity **$20.9M–
$24.8M**. The $41.0M ask implies 10.8x the corrected base — richer
than every kept peer — but price is not what declines this risk.

**Decision.** The priced book cannot transfer: the supplier of
31% of input has refused on record and unconditionally, and no
alternate is qualified inside the deal's own timeline. No
endorsement bridges a base that leaves before the premium is
earned. **Pass** is signed. The consent on row 2 is never
collected — it is moot, and the memo says so.

## The memo as issued

**Recommendation: pass** — the sole-source supplier of 31% of
input has refused any acquirer on record; supported equity range
$20.9M–$24.8M against a $41.0M ask.

## 1. Thesis
Ashcombe makes a credible premium pet food with real co-pack
income; the acquisition case was manufacturing cash flow at a
mid-market multiple. The primary exposure is input security: the
single protein source covering 31% of volume has terminated the
relationship in writing and excluded any acquirer. That fact
decides the decision before price is discussed.

## 2. Risk table

| Exposure | Tier filed | Status labelled | Status on evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protein supplier refusal (31% of input) | Must-Address | Resolved | **Decided against the deal** — written refusal on record | Blocks the decision |
| Co-pack change-of-control consent | Must-Address | In-Progress | Open — askable, never requested | Moot under the decision |
| Salmonella environmental positives | Manageable | In-Progress | As filed — audit scheduled | Post-close |

One blocking exposure stands decided; a second stands open but
untested. Row 1 keeps its filed label; the memo states plainly why
the committee cannot rely on it.

## 3. Valuation
The coverable base is $33.4M of revenue and $3.8M of EBITDA — the
$0.6M insurance recovery is one-time and comes out. Of five comps,
MegaBrand Pet is excluded as a control premium and FreshPet is
excluded on the earnings metric (NM). The kept set runs
6.9x–9.6x EV/EBITDA; supplier concentration and co-pack churn risk
place Ashcombe below the middle: 7.0x–8.0x.

- 7.0x × $3.8M = **$26.6M**
- 8.0x × $3.8M = **$30.4M**

Enterprise $26.6M–$30.4M, less net debt of $5.6M → equity
**$20.9M–$24.8M**. The $41.0M ask implies 10.8x — outside the
range and every kept peer — but the valuation is advisory only:
the decision rests on the schedule, not the price.

## 4. Recommendation
**Pass.** The priced asset cannot transfer: the supplier carrying
31% of input volume has refused the acquirer on record and
unconditionally, and management's own estimate puts requalification
beyond the deal's timeline. No closing instrument exists for an
exposure that is already decided; a price cut does not replace the
ingredient the product is made of. The open co-pack consent is
moot — it would be collected only if the file reopened.

## 5. Conditions
1. What would reopen the file: a signed supply agreement from a
   qualified protein supplier of comparable volume, or a written
   withdrawal of the supplier's refusal — either returning the
   memo to survey.
2. The salmonella audit report, if a renewed pack is ever tabled,
   post-close with a holdback sized on the remediation quote.
3. The co-pack counterparty's written consent, before signing, in
   any restructured transaction.

## Other slips — same desk, different submissions

**If the supplier letter had read "reviewing the relationship
post-close, renewal under discussion"** — hedged, conditional, not
a refusal: row 1 is open-and-askable, not decided. The decision is
**request more data**: the supplier's written position and the
requalification timeline are the two records that settle it.
§4: *"Request more data — the protein relationship is open but
askable; a hedged letter is not a refusal. The consent and the
supplier's written position settle both rows."*

**If the supplier had already re-signed and the consent was in
hand** — the blocking count is zero: **proceed**, said plainly —
nothing on the schedule is a deal-breaker — with equity capped at
$24.8M and the salmonella audit carried as a post-close holdback.

## The slip that gets referred back

Three survey errors this desk has seen:

1. **Reading a refusal as a negotiation.** "Final" written notice
   is a decision, not a phase; memos that schedule a supplier
   meeting invent a fact the artifacts contradict.
2. **Reading a phase as a refusal.** A pending application, an
   unrequested consent, a review on someone else's calendar —
   all askable. Declining an askable exposure declines a risk the
   survey never tested.
3. **Letting the price do the verdict's work.** The $41M ask is
   outside the range; in a file that signs, that becomes a
   ceiling endorsement. In this file it is a footnote — the
   schedule declined before the market mattered.

## A second slip — the walk-clause file

Different submission: **Morrison Freight Corridors**, a regional
carrier whose book ran through one retail logistics contract
carrying 46% of routed volume — with a documented
termination-for-convenience, exercisable on 60 days' notice, no
cause needed. Beside it, the priced routing software was licensed,
not owned.

The survey read the schedule two ways. A consent or a waiver can
bridge a *conditional* right — one the counterparty exercises on
an event. A termination for convenience is different in kind: the
counterparty needs nothing but the notice period, and the priced
book — nearly half of it — exists at their election alone. Paired
with a software asset the file could not show owned, the survey
found no instrument that bridges a base able to leave at will.
**Declined.**

The line the slip held: a contractual right *unexercised* is not
the same as a consent *unrequested*. The first is a sword already
hung over the book; the second is paperwork. And the smaller
exposures — a bounded claims estimate, a pending audit — were
ordinary endorsements, never the reason the risk failed.

## A third slip — the expiring cover

Different submission: **Beacon Maritime Charts**, a niche data
house whose entire premium rested on an exclusive hydrographic
licence — the asset that kept competitors out. The licence ran
to a fixed date inside the hold period, and the filings showed
two successors already lodged to claim the territory at expiry.

The schedule also carried open rows — a lapsed safety
certification, an interim treasurer — the kind of exposures a
junior survey refers for documents. The desk declined to refer:
an endorsement can bridge a document; it cannot extend a licence
whose term is fixed while successors queue at the door. The open
rows were noted as moot — priced or waived, they could not gate
a risk the calendar had already declined. **Declined.**

The line the slip held: when the priced protection dies on a
date no party can move, open items beside it are not referrals —
they are arithmetic on a risk that no longer exists. Referral
asks "what settles this?"; this file's question was "what is
left after the date?" — and the answer was a commodity book at
a protection price.

A junior's draft had referred the file for "counsel's opinion on
the succession landscape and a workaround roadmap". Returned:
an opinion does not move a term date, and a roadmap does not
restore an exclusivity that lapses. Facts already decided —
the date, the filings, the stated term — are not askable;
askable means the fact is still unknown. The junior's error was
treating "who wins the succession" as the open question; it is
beside the point — the exclusivity ends on the date whether
the successors win or lose, and the premium was for the
exclusivity.
