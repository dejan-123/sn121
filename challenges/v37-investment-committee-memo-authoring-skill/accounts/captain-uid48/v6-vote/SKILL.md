# Vote after the record is settled

The committee is asked for a decision on the deal in this packet, not a catalogue of things that might happen. Use the following order before writing any memo prose. Read `references/merchant-vote.md` once; it shows a full clean-file vote, then the differing conclusions when a top-tier question is curable or already decided against the deal. The invented example never supplies facts for the user's transaction.

## 1. Fix the voting population

Look at the tier *column* before interpreting any row. Count only findings filed at a tier whose label means "must be cleared before signing" - Must-Address, Critical, or a tier the source itself defines as deal-blocking. **The highest label present is not automatically a gate.** A register containing only Manageable, Moderate, Low or Informational-style rows has zero voting gates even when every row is pending: the committee's present call is **proceed**, with each open row assigned a condition or price protection. This does not excuse the work: name each unfinished lower-tier item in the table and assign a price protection or closing action. If a lower-tier item appears under-classified, say so next to the original tier without changing it or adding it to the blocking count. A threat to future growth does not retroactively change the tier the source assigned.

For each filed top-tier row, preserve its original status in the table and test it against independent evidence elsewhere in the packet. A current statement that the required closing act is still undone defeats a label of Resolved; quote or identify the conflicting evidence beside that label. A historical description of a right, allegation or risk in the row itself is not proof that a later closure failed - a rival's earlier filing, a contractual cancellation right, or a consent the license always required is the exposure the resolution addressed, not evidence it failed; the confirming instrument becomes a closing deliverable. Missing proof alone calls for a document request, not an automatic status reversal. Unresolved, In-Progress and empty remain open. Then write the count as a numeral under the table: `N open gating rows` counts only rows whose tier is literally the highest tier present AND whose status is open or contradicted. Do this before drafting the verdict - and if N is 0 the recommendation must be `proceed`; no lower-tier row may appear as the deciding finding.

## 2. Determine what the committee may do now

With no open filed gate, **proceed**, with named conditions for lower-tier exposures and a price ceiling if necessary. With an open filed gate that a specific document, decision or action can resolve, **request more data**. State the record, its issuer, the favorable outcome needed before signing and the adverse outcome that would cause the committee to abandon or reprice. "Proceed subject to getting it later" is not today's permission to sign. A third party that has not been asked, a pending application and an expected hearing are unresolved questions, not established impossibilities.

If the packet itself has already decided a material gate negatively and no act can restore the priced asset for this buyer, **pass**. This includes a recorded unconditional refusal with no qualified alternative, an express restriction barring what this buyer would own, or an essential exclusive right ending on a date the parties cannot move when no successor can be secured on the record. A later letter *about* the restriction does not undo the restriction. Make this decision before considering unrelated open questions. Test every adverse statement for a condition word - `if`, `unless`, `provided`, `would`. "Will not continue *if* the buyer lacks a qualification" is a question about this buyer, not a refusal: request the answer (does this buyer qualify, or can a qualified substitute take the role). Only a refusal with no condition, where the packet shows no substitute, is a decided break. A price above the range is an offer to revise, not an incurable risk.

The opening verdict and section 4 must be identical. Do not let an open Manageable row become the reason for an RMD verdict when the highest-tier count is zero; such rows belong in the terms under proceed.

## 3. Set one price, on one basis

Bridge the narrative's reported amount to the acquired base: subtract only stated one-time income, already departed income and amounts that cannot belong to this buyer. Contracted revenue merely exposed to renewal stays in the base; address it in placement or consideration. Revenue from services does not vanish just because a different share is recurring. A forecast cannot replace realized revenue. Test each EBITDA add-back for recurrence, and use EV/Revenue rather than unusable negative or unsupported EBITDA.

Every peer named in the packet gets an in/out decision for the selected metric. A control acquisition is not an ordinary trading price, and NM earnings do not invalidate a quoted revenue peer. Explain exceptional growth, margins, mix and size against the target without fabricating a numerical adjustment. Pick a low and high *explicit* multiple justified by the retained peers. Write exactly two products, rounded at the end:

`low chosen multiple x corrected base = low enterprise value`
`high chosen multiple x corrected base = high enterprise value`

The printed EV range MUST equal those two products, including in the opening line and section 4. Do not blend it with a second metric or silently apply a separate haircut. A second metric can be a labeled cross-check only, never a substitute pair of endpoints. Price-to-equity follows separately only if debt and cash are supplied. If an asking price exists, divide it by the same base and compare with the derived range. With no ask, say so; do not supply a guessed one. Never invent future retention rates, holdback sizes, deadlines or thresholds to make a condition sound specific.

## 4. Write the decision document

First line: `**Recommendation: <proceed | request more data | pass>** -- <deciding finding>; <defended EV range and ask if known>.` Use only the filled-in one-word-or-three-word recommendation, not all options. No title or checklist above it. Then exactly five numbered sections:

1. `## 1. Deal thesis`: what is acquired, the sustainable base, one primary risk also present in section 2 (up to 150 words).
2. `## 2. Tiered risk table`: every row in source order, with finding, filed tier, filed status, evidence-based standing, and consequence. Under it give the number of open filed gating rows in a sentence.
3. `## 3. Valuation`: the base bridge, all comp inclusions and exclusions named with reasons, target placement, two exact EV products and the ask comparison (up to 300 words plus table).
4. `## 4. Recommendation`: repeat the first line's verdict, identify what may happen now and what evidence would change it; separate deal permission from price permission (up to 140 words).
5. `## 5. Conditions and next actions`: concise numbered items with finding, responsible role, required paper or instrument, timing relative to signing/close and consequence if unmet. An unquantified exposure cannot be assigned an invented dollar holdback.

Write a completed professional memo, not a working sheet: no bracket placeholders, invented figures, repeated self-check lines or visible deliberation. Under 900 words if possible. Before responding, check the filed-tier count, every range multiplication, every copied input status, the primary risk's appearance in the table, and first-line/section-4 consistency. If a figure cannot be verified from supplied inputs, say what is missing rather than manufacturing it.
