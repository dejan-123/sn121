# Settle the record, then write the decision

A diligence packet is assembled by different hands. The risk table labels and tiers, the narrative describes what happened, the comp sheet supplies prices. The memo fails when it trusts one slice where the slices disagree. Work the seven rules below in order; the verdict, the range and the conditions fall out of them. Read `references/harbour-ledger.md` once - it holds one complete worked memo plus two short variant endings on different facts. The example is invented and supplies no facts for the user's transaction.

## 1. Count only the tier that blocks signing

Read the tier column before reading any row. A row blocks the vote only when its filed tier means "clear before signing" - Must-Address, Critical, or a tier the source itself defines as blocking. The highest label present is not automatically a gate. A register holding only Manageable, Moderate, Low or Informational rows has zero gating rows even if all are pending - pending, In-Progress or outstanding items on a non-blocking tier are conditions, never gates; the call is then proceed, with each open row priced or conditioned. If a lower-tier row looks under-classified, flag it beside the original tier without moving it into the blocking count. A risk to future growth does not change the tier the source assigned.

## 2. A status label is a claim, not a closure

For each blocking-tier row, keep its filed status in the table and test it against the rest of the packet. A current statement that the closing act is still undone defeats a Resolved label; quote the conflict beside the row. A history recited inside the row - a rival's earlier filing, a standing cancellation right, a consent the license always needed - is the exposure the resolution answered, not proof it failed; name the confirming instrument as a closing deliverable. **Where the packet neither proves nor contradicts a filed closure, the label stands for the count and the confirming instrument becomes a named pre-signing condition** - absent evidence is a deliverable, not an open gate. Unresolved, In-Progress and empty stay open. Then write the count as a numeral: `N open gating rows` - blocking tier AND open or contradicted. If N is 0 the recommendation must be proceed, with every outstanding instrument listed as a condition precedent in section 5, and no lower-tier row may appear as the deciding finding.

## 3. The open gate picks the verdict

- Zero open gates: **proceed**. Lower-tier exposures get conditions, price protection or monitoring, never the headline.
- Open gate with a defined, checkable closing event - a signature, a consent, a filing, a hearing, an audit: **request more data**. Name the instrument, its issuer, the favorable outcome needed and the adverse outcome that ends or reprices the deal. When that single instrument is the only thing between the packet and signing, the memo may say approval is conditioned on it arriving before signature; the printed verdict is still `request more data` or `proceed`, matching whether the gap sits on a blocking-tier row.
- Gate the packet already decided against the deal and no buyer act restores it: **pass**. An unconditional recorded refusal, an express bar on what this buyer would own, or a terminal exclusive right with no successor on the record.

Test every adverse sentence for a condition word - `if`, `unless`, `provided`, `would`. "Will not continue if the buyer lacks a qualification" is a question about this buyer: request the answer - does the buyer qualify, or can a qualified substitute take the role. Never assume the buyer fails a stated condition; that is asking, not refusing. A price above the supported range is a negotiation point, not a gate.

## 4. Value what the buyer actually acquires

Rebuild the base from the narrative, not the seller's headline. Subtract stated one-time income, revenue already departed, volume reported gross that is pass-through, and add-backs that recur. Contracted revenue merely exposed to renewal stays in; a large single customer or a terminable channel earns explicit treatment - say how much of the base depends on it and price or condition that dependence rather than silently deleting it. Negative or unsupported earnings mean EV/Revenue, with the reason stated.

Every named comp gets an in/out decision on the chosen metric, with the rule stated - control deals are not trading prices, NM rows cannot feed an earnings multiple. Position the target on growth, margin, retention and scale. Choose a low and a high multiple and print exactly two products:

`low multiple x corrected base = low enterprise value`
`high multiple x corrected base = high enterprise value`

The printed range equals those products in the first line, section 3 and section 4. **When the packet supplies debt and cash, the EV-to-equity bridge is required, not optional:** enterprise value minus stated debt plus stated cash, arithmetic shown - the memo's fairness conclusion belongs to the equity figure, and omitting the bridge leaves the ask untested. Divide any asking price by the same base, compare it to the range, and state the gap; no ask in the packet means no invented ask. Never invent retention rates, holdback sizes, deadlines or thresholds.

## 5. A condition is a closing event

Each condition names the finding it answers, the paper or instrument that closes it, the responsible party, the timing relative to signing, and the consequence if it fails - walk-away, price reset or sized protection from figures the packet actually gives. Regulatory responses awaiting an outcome, unsigned consents, unassigned IP and pending licenses each have a named deliverable; write that name, not "further diligence".

## 6. Write the finished memo only

First line: `**Recommendation: <proceed | request more data | pass>** - <deciding finding>; <defended EV range, and equity range when debt and cash are given>.` One verdict word, no title above it. Then exactly five numbered sections:

1. `## 1. Deal thesis` - what is bought, the durable base, the primary risk that also appears in the table (under 150 words).
2. `## 2. Tiered risk table` - every source row in order: finding, filed tier, filed status, standing on the evidence, consequence. The open-gate count follows under it.
3. `## 3. Valuation` - base bridge, named comp decisions, placement, the two products, the equity bridge when supplied, ask comparison (under 300 words plus table).
4. `## 4. Recommendation` - repeats the first line's verdict exactly; what happens now, what changes it; separate permission to sign from permission on price (under 140 words).
5. `## 5. Conditions and next actions` - numbered items: finding, owner role, required paper, timing, consequence.

Finished professional prose, under 900 words - no placeholders, no invented figures, no visible working. Before answering, verify the gate count, both products, every copied status, the primary risk's row, and that line one and section 4 agree.
