# Underwrite the file, not the story

A submission arrives with the broker's framing already applied - tiers assigned, closures asserted, a price floated. The underwriter's craft is to re-rate the file on its own evidence before writing terms. Six disciplines, in order, produce the verdict, the price and the conditions. `references/quayline-file.md` holds a complete worked file plus two alternate declinations; every name and figure in it is invented.

## 1. What is actually excluded from signing

In underwriting terms, an exclusion applies only to a filed blocking tier - Must-Address, Critical or whatever the register itself marks as pre-signing. The most severe-sounding label on the page is not an exclusion unless the filing says so. A file whose findings are all Manageable or Informational carries no exclusions - pending or In-Progress lower-tier items are subjectivities, never exclusions: the answer is terms, not a decline, and each residual item gets a warranty or a loading rather than a refusal. A finding that feels under-tiered is footnoted beside its filed tier, never promoted silently.

## 2. Re-rate every claimed closure

For each blocking-tier finding, the filed status stays in the table while the evidence is re-read. A packet statement that the closing act is still outstanding voids a Resolved claim - cite the conflict. An exposure narrated inside the row - a competitor's prior filing, a subsisting cancellation right, a consent obligation that pre-dates the deal - is the risk the closure answered; where the closure holds on the record, that exposure becomes a subjectivity to satisfy at signing, not a reopened exclusion. **Where the file neither proves nor contradicts a claimed closure, the rating stands and the missing instrument is written as a subjectivity precedent** - absent evidence is a paper requirement, not a reopened exclusion. Open statuses stay open.

Write the tally: `N open gating rows`. Zero produces terms (proceed). A tally of curable items produces a request for the curing paper (request more data). An incurable tally produces a decline (pass).

## 3. Write the decision

- **proceed** - no open exclusions; everything residual carries a condition, a loading or a monitoring instruction.
- **request more data** - open exclusions each cured by a named, checkable item of paper: the executed deed, the signed consent, the regulator's outcome letter, the granted license. Where the only outstanding item is a specific signature or consent required before signing, terms may be quoted conditional on it - the printed verdict remains one of the three words and matches whether a blocking row stands open.
- **pass** - the file itself closes the gate for this applicant: an unconditional refusal on record, an express bar on the asset being priced, an exclusive right terminating with no successor evidenced.

Words like `if`, `unless`, `provided`, `would` convert a refusal into a questionnaire: "declines to continue if the applicant lacks a licence" asks whether the applicant holds one or a qualified substitute exists - it is never read as a refusal, and the applicant is never assumed to fail. An asking price above the rated range is a premium negotiation, not an exclusion.

## 4. Rate the base that transfers

The rated base excludes stated one-off income, revenue from customers already gone, gross pass-through volume and recurring spend disguised as add-backs. Revenue continuing under a cancellation right or concentrated in one account remains rated, with the dependence quantified and carried into the terms - exclusion it is not, ignoring it is equally wrong. Negative or unsupportable earnings switch the rating basis to revenue, with the reason noted.

Every quoted comparable earns an in or out by a stated rule - trading rows matching the metric, control deals and NM rows out, reasons given. Position the submission against what is kept on growth, margin, retention and scale. Two products, printed exactly:

`low multiple x corrected base = low enterprise value`
`high multiple x corrected base = high enterprise value`

That pair is the range wherever the memo repeats it. **Supplied debt and cash figures make the equity calculation obligatory** - EV less net debt, shown - because the fairness of any asking price is judged on equity, and an equity-less memo leaves the ask untested. Restate the ask on the corrected base and report the gap; absent an ask, none is supplied. No invented rates, deadlines, holdbacks or thresholds - an unquantified item is flagged as missing, not filled.

## 5. Subjectivities are named paper

Each condition precedent names the finding, the exact document that satisfies it, who produces it, when relative to signing, and the remedy on failure - withdrawal, re-rating, or a sized protection taken only from file figures. Pending regulatory outcomes, unsigned consents, unassigned IP and pending licences each name their deliverable.

## 6. The output is the issued decision

First line: `**Recommendation: <proceed | request more data | pass>** - <deciding finding>; <defended EV range, and equity range where debt and cash are supplied>.` Then exactly five numbered sections:

1. `## 1. Deal thesis` - the asset underwritten, the durable base, the lead exposure also in the table (under 150 words).
2. `## 2. Tiered risk table` - every filed row in order: finding, filed tier, filed status, standing after re-rating, consequence; exclusion count beneath.
3. `## 3. Valuation` - base bridge, comparable decisions, positioning, the two products, equity bridge when supplied, ask comparison (under 300 words plus table).
4. `## 4. Recommendation` - the first-line verdict repeated; what binds now, what re-rates it; signing authority kept distinct from price authority (under 140 words).
5. `## 5. Conditions and next actions` - numbered: finding, owner, instrument, timing, consequence.

Issued prose under 900 words - no placeholders, no invented particulars, no deliberation left on the page. Before issue, verify the exclusion tally, both products, every copied status, the lead exposure's presence and the first line matching section 4.
