# The decision record — what the board settled, what it resolved

Boards do not write memos; they settle records and resolve on
them. This skill produces that record: the matter before the
board, the facts settled, the papers outstanding, the money
section, the resolution, the standing instructions — bound into
the five sections the committee asked for. Draft nothing before
the record is settled; the resolution at the top of the minute is
the resolution the record already carries.

## Settling the record — the entries that matter

**Blocking entries.** Items filed as clear-before-deal
(Must-Address, Critical, Deal-Breaker, Blocker, High — the filing's
own labels, named once) carry blocking weight. Items filed lighter
never do, however loudly they read; where an item's substance
argues it was misfiled, the record notes the suspected mis-filing —
the record annotates, it never refiles.

**Each blocking entry is settled or outstanding:**

- `Resolved` **settles** where the other artifacts corroborate it
  or where the exposure persists by nature — a consent such deals
  always need, a right the contract always held. An entry's own
  wording never unsettles its own status; the proving paper goes
  to the instructions.
- `Resolved` is entered **outstanding** where the record shows
  the closing act undone: a renewal now bid competitively, a
  review deferred not decided, an application filed and
  ungranted, a consent required and nowhere shown.
- `Unresolved`, `In-Progress`, blank — **outstanding**.

The record states the outstanding blocking count in plain words
beneath the table.

A file entering nothing in the blocking tier has a count of zero
by construction — the board resolves proceed, and the record says
it in words: nothing on this file is a deal-breaker. Entries that
merely read heavy — pending claims with injunctive potential,
regulator applications awaiting decisions, churn metrics below
par — hold their filed tier: annotated, priced into the
instructions, never promoted into the count. The annotation
travels with the entry; the label stays; the count reads only
what was filed.

## The money section — entries repriced, not repeated

The seller's figures enter the record corrected:

- one-time revenue — removed;
- revenue the buyer cannot hold (grants, licences, contracts
  ending on change of control) — removed;
- revenue already gone — removed at the narrative's own run-rate;
- revenue merely exposed (renewals pending, customers who may
  leave) — retained, and flagged to the instructions;
- projections needing pilots or prospects — removed;
- add-backs — stand only when plainly non-recurring and evidenced;
  recurring "one-offs" are costs.

Where transferable earnings are negative, earnings multiples are
entered as not meaningful — the record prices on revenue and says
why. The bridge is entered in full: reported, each deduction, the
corrected base.

**The peer entries.** Each comp is entered **admitted** or
**excluded** with reason: control-premium transactions among
trading prices; NM on the metric in use; outliers further from
their neighbour than the rest are from each other. A comp failing
its exclusion test stays admitted — its distance places the
target. Position on growth, margin, retention; a weakness the
admitted peers lack pushes low; a set the artifacts flag as not
like-for-like earns a confidence note.

The range is entered as two recomputable products, decimals
split: `3.7x × $20.0M = 3 × $20.0M + 0.7 × $20.0M = $60.0M +
$14.0M = $74.0M`. Admitted multiples price the enterprise; the
buyer's equity is that less net debt — one line, or marked to
confirm. The ask is restated as an implied multiple of the
corrected base with the gap in money and percent; no ask entered,
and the range stands as guidance.

## The resolution — what the board resolved

- **proceed** — no blocking entry outstanding. The record says it
  in words: nothing on this file is a deal-breaker. Outstanding
  lighter entries become instructions; an ask above range becomes
  a pricing instruction — the first one — never a reason to
  decline.
- **proceed, subject to a deliverable** — one blocking entry
  outstanding, and its closure is a signature a named party can
  simply execute: consent, amendment, agreement. The resolution
  carries it as a condition precedent — unsigned, no signing;
  refused, the resolution converts to pass.
- **request more data** — blocking entries outstanding whose
  closure is an outcome, not a signature: a ruling, an
  investigation, an audit, a reversal of a position already
  stated. The request is minuted as an instruction: the record
  needed, its issuer, its deadline, and which way each answer
  moves the resolution.
- **pass** — an entry the record cannot cure: an unconditional
  refusal on the books; a term or rule barring the transfer
  itself, including a material share of the priced base — and a
  funding stream, licence or grant whose own terms prohibit
  transfer to this class of buyer bars that share the same way,
  no instruction signs over a term that defines the buyer out; a
  priced exclusive right with a fixed expiry inside the hold,
  where the evidence shows successors already claiming the
  landscape, ends on a schedule no party can move. Short of that,
  nothing declines: an unrequested signature is outstanding, a
  decision pending elsewhere is outstanding, revenue at risk is
  a pricing entry, an ask above range is a pricing instruction.

## The standing instructions

Minuted one per numbered line, each citing its entry: a signable
document → condition precedent (no paper, no signing); a bounded
liability → specific indemnity, escrowed where the record sizes
it; revenue hostage to an outside event → contingent
consideration; a quantified gap the plan omits → price reduction;
people the value walks out with → retention and non-compete;
lighter entries → covenant, holdback or 100-day instruction. Each
names its finding, the satisfying evidence, its timing, and the
consequence if it fails. The record sizes, dates and names nothing
the artifacts did not give. Data requests are minuted last.

## Entering the minute — the memo as filed

Only the finished record leaves the board. Line one:
`**Recommendation: <resolution>** — <the entry that decides it>;
<supported range vs the ask>`.

1. `## 1. Thesis` (≤150 words) — why the asset, the primary entry
   — one the table must carry.
2. `## 2. Risk table` — every entry: finding | tier filed | status
   labelled | standing on the evidence | consequence (blocks the
   resolution / moves the price / instruction / post-close);
   outstanding blocking count in words beneath.
3. `## 3. Valuation` (≤300 words + tables) — repriced base,
   admitted/excluded peer entries with reasons, positioning,
   both range products, the debt bridge, implied ask and gap.
4. `## 4. Recommendation` (≤140 words) — the resolution, the
   entry behind it, what would change it. Equals line one.
5. `## 5. Conditions` — the standing instructions, numbered,
   ≤45 words each.

The minute reads as a decision record drafted by the board's
senior member: settled, specific, every claim pinned to its entry
or figure. No sentence frame used twice. A complete record inside
the bounds stands above a longer one cut mid-entry.
