# Motions-cases — a sitting written out

Three artifacts came in for **Meridian Cold-Chain Foods**, a regional
frozen-food distributor. The committee sat; the register was settled,
the base struck, the peers marked, the vote moved, the papers
attached. Below: the sitting, then the minute as it was written.

## What the artifacts said (compressed)

Risk register:

| Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| National grocer contract (19% of revenue) renews in 7 months; renewal negotiation open | Must-Address | Resolved |
| Founder/CEO personally holds the three largest protein-supplier relationships; no post-sale employment agreement signed | Must-Address | Unresolved |
| Two fleet refrigeration units failed temperature-compliance audit; remediation vendor engaged | Manageable | In-Progress |
| Warehouse pest-control citation from Q2; re-inspection scheduled | Informational | In-Progress |

Narrative: LTM revenue $58.2M including a $4.0M one-time bulk sale
to a liquidator and $6.1M of freight surcharges billed through at
cost. EBITDA $5.9M includes a $0.8M "one-time" consulting add-back
that has appeared each of the last three years. Cash $2.1M; debt
$14.0M. The grocer's account team says renewal talks have stalled
and the grocer has invited two competing distributors to bid. Asking
price $52.0M.

Comps: GlacierPoint Foods 7.8x EV/EBITDA (9% growth) · Heartland
Provisions 6.4x (5%) · BlueRidge Cold Chain 8.9x (12%) ·
MetroGrocers Supply 11.2x (22%) — a take-private by a strategic
buyer · NationalFoodlines NM (negative growth).

## The sitting

Motion 1 — the register: blocking tier is Must-Address. Row 1
carries `Resolved`, but the narrative defeats it: talks stalled,
rivals invited to bid. That is a closing act undone — the row posts
**open**. Row 2 is `Unresolved` — **open**. Rows 3 and 4 sit below
the blocking tier: flagged, never promoted. **Two blocking rows
stand open.**

Motion 2 — the base: $58.2M reported, less $4.0M one-time bulk sale,
less $6.1M freight billed through at cost = **$48.1M** the buyer
keeps. The $0.8M consulting add-back recurs every year — it is a
cost, so EBITDA corrects to **$5.1M**. The grocer's 19% is at risk,
not gone: it stays in the base and moves the placement and the
papers.

Motion 3 — the peers: MetroGrocers is cut — a control-premium
take-private filed among trading multiples. NationalFoodlines is
kept — NM only on the earnings metric's fringe, and its decline is
real information about the bottom of the trade. Meridian grows
slower than BlueRidge, in line with Heartland; margin unremarkable.
Middle of the kept set: 6.5x–7.5x.

`6.5x × $5.1M = 6 × $5.1M + 0.5 × $5.1M = $30.6M + $2.55M = $33.2M`
`7.5x × $5.1M = 7 × $5.1M + 0.5 × $5.1M = $35.7M + $2.55M = $38.3M`

Enterprise range $33.2M–$38.3M; net debt $14.0M − $2.1M = $11.9M
comes off: **equity $21.3M–$26.4M**. The $52.0M ask implies 10.2x
the corrected base — about 36% above the top of the range.

Motion 4 — the vote: two blocking rows open, each with a named
record that settles it (the grocer's renewal position in writing;
a signed employment and non-compete agreement). **Request more
data** moves.

Motion 5 — the papers, as minuted below.

## The minute (the memo as delivered)

**Recommendation: request more data** — the grocer renewal is open
on the evidence and the CEO's supplier relationships are unsigned;
supported equity range $21.3M–$26.4M against a $52.0M ask.

## 1. Thesis
Meridian is a profitable cold-chain route network with a defensible
regional position; the acquisition case is recurring distribution
cash flow at a mid-single-digit multiple. The primary risk is the
grocer contract: 19% of revenue renews in seven months and the file
marked Resolved describes a negotiation that has stalled and gone
competitive. That risk, not the refrigeration findings, decides the
vote.

## 2. Risk table

| Finding | Tier filed | Status labelled | Status on evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grocer renewal (19% of revenue) | Must-Address | Resolved | **Open** — talks stalled, competitors invited to bid | Blocks the vote |
| CEO supplier relationships unsigned | Must-Address | Unresolved | Open — no agreement exists | Blocks the vote |
| Refrigeration audit failures | Manageable | In-Progress | As filed — remediation engaged | Structure |
| Pest-control citation | Informational | In-Progress | As filed | Post-close |

Two blocking rows stand open. Row 1's label and the evidence
disagree: the row keeps its filed status, and the memo says plainly
why the committee should not rely on it.

## 3. Valuation
The transferable base is not the reported one. Bridge: $58.2M
reported, less $4.0M one-time bulk sale, less $6.1M pass-through
freight = $48.1M of revenue the buyer keeps. The $0.8M consulting
add-back has recurred three years running; treated as cost, EBITDA
is $5.1M. The grocer's 19% stays in the base — at risk is not gone —
and its exposure is priced through the placement instead.

Of five comps, MetroGrocers Supply is excluded: a take-private that
carried a control premium, not a trading price. The kept set runs
6.4x–8.9x EV/EBITDA. Meridian's growth sits between Heartland and
BlueRidge, its margins are ordinary, so the middle of the kept set
applies: 6.5x–7.5x.

- 6.5x × $5.1M = 6 × $5.1M + 0.5 × $5.1M = $30.6M + $2.55M = **$33.2M**
- 7.5x × $5.1M = 7 × $5.1M + 0.5 × $5.1M = $35.7M + $2.55M = **$38.3M**

Enterprise value $33.2M–$38.3M; net debt of $11.9M ($14.0M debt
less $2.1M cash) comes off, giving equity of **$21.3M–$26.4M**. The
$52.0M ask implies 10.2x the corrected EBITDA — roughly 36% above
the top of the range, and richer than every kept peer.

## 4. Recommendation
**Request more data.** Two blocking rows stand open and each is
answerable by a named record: the grocer's renewal position in
writing (does the RFP exclude incumbent terms, and when does it
close), and a signed employment and non-compete agreement from the
CEO. If the grocer's process confirms renewal on comparable terms,
the first row settles; if the CEO signs, the second settles, and
the memo then supports proceed at no more than $26.4M of equity.
If either record comes back adverse, the call turns to pass.

## 5. Conditions
1. Grocer renewal position, in writing from the grocer's
   procurement office, before signing — if the renewal is lost or
   re-bid on materially worse terms, the committee re-votes.
2. Executed CEO employment and non-compete agreement covering the
   three named supplier relationships, before signing — no
   signature, no signing.
3. Contingent consideration on the grocer account: the 19% of
   revenue it carries is paid for as it survives the renewal —
   the instrument, not the price, absorbs the timing risk.
4. Refrigeration remediation completion certificate from the
   engaged vendor, post-close; an escrow sized only on a figure
   the vendor quote provides.
5. Price: equity proceeds at no more than $26.4M; the $52.0M ask
   is not supported on the corrected base.

## Variant sittings — the same pack, different record

**If the papers had arrived signed** (grocer renewal executed on
comparable terms; CEO agreement in escrow): the blocking count is
zero. §4 reads: *"**Proceed** — no finding on the register is a
deal-breaker; say so aloud. Equity proceeds at no more than $26.4M;
the price is the first paper."* §5 leads with the price ceiling,
then the grocer contingency as contingent consideration, then the
remediation certificate.

**If the grocer had terminated on record** (written notice, no
successor named): the break is incurable — 19% of the priced base
has already left and no closing event restores it. §4 reads:
*"**Pass** — the grocer contract is terminated on record; the
base the committee was asked to price no longer exists. A new
pack on a smaller Meridian is a new matter."* §5 shrinks to what
would reopen the file: a signed replacement account of comparable
size.

## The sitting that fails — three ways to lose this case

1. **Copying the status column.** A memo that calls the grocer row
   resolved because the register says so, and proceeds, is the
   capped failure: the narrative disproves the label.
2. **Inventing the missing evidence.** "Renewal probability ~70%"
   or "the CEO is expected to sign" — neither appears in the
   artifacts; a fabricated specific floors the memo regardless of
   call.
3. **Punishing the small rows.** Promoting the pest citation or the
   refrigeration findings to deal-breakers manufactures drama the
   committee did not ask for; non-blocking rows take conditions and
   a flag, not a veto.

## A second sitting — the quiet register

Different pack: **Halden Office Interiors**, a contract furniture
dealer. Its register carried five rows and not one blocking label:
two `Manageable` rows still open (a warehouse lease dispute in
mediation; a product-liability claim tendered to the insurer), one
`Manageable` marked `Resolved` with a regulator filing still
awaiting its stamp, and two `Informational` rows (headcount churn
above trade median; a top-customer share of 24%).

Motion 1 counted **zero open blocking rows — by construction**.
Nothing on the register could be promoted; the heavy reads stayed
inside their filed tier, each flagged and priced. The vote moved
**proceed**, and the minute said the thing aloud: *no finding on
this register is a deal-breaker.* What the committee still wanted
— the mediator's position statement, the regulator's stamped
approval, the insurer's coverage letter — rode inside proceed as
named documents in §5, not as a reason to wait. The deferral
motion exists to settle a blocking row; with none filed, it had
no work.

The failed version of this sitting: the member who "deferred
pending the lease mediation" promoted a `Manageable` row into a
gate the file never gave it — the flag was right, the veto was
not.
