# Rulings applied — invented matter, form only

## How the rulings ran on one file

The file: a route-optimization SaaS, $11.4M transferable base
($13.2M reported less a completed $1.8M project), one open
blocking row — an unsigned assignment consent covering 29% of
revenue — plus a Manageable violation row and an Informational
lease row. Ask $27M against a $27.4–$33.1M defended range.

- Ruling 1: row 2 open; rows 3–4 stand or note.
- Ruling 2: `Blocking rows open: one.`
- Ruling 6: the one open row answers to an executable consent →
  request more data.
- Ruling 8 then renders, §4 reading:

> **Request more data.** Row 2 is the sole open blocking row and
> answers to a document — the executed consent. Rows 3–4 are
> priced or monitored inside closings; they do not hold the call.
> The ask prices under the range. Verified: primary risk = row 2;
> figures identical across sections.

## The count changes, the ruling changes

- `Blocking rows open: none` → proceed, with closings; and where
  no blocking-tier row was filed at all: *no finding here is a
  deal-breaker* — written aloud, every time.
- Count zero, ask above range → `proceed, at a price inside
  $X–$Y`; the ceiling is the defended top, not a guess.
- A blocking row whose right ends on a statutory date the thesis
  prices → `pass — the clearing record cannot exist on this
  timeline; the file reopens only if the rule changes.`
- A counterparty 'indicating it may not continue if the buyer is
  not X' → a position, not a refusal: the exit hangs on an
  unasked fact and the seat refills; open-blocking but curable →
  request more data: the buyer's status, a successor's commitment.

## Open rows below blocking weight cannot defer the call

A second file: every risk row filed Manageable or Informational;
three rows still open — pending state approvals, a tribunal claim
mid-briefing, an NRR decline awaiting cohort evidence. Each wants
a document. The temptation is deferral; the ruling is not:

- Ruling 1: the filing column governs — no row carries blocking
  weight, whatever the wording threatens.
- Ruling 2: `Blocking rows open: none.`
- Ruling 6: second branch → proceed. The pending records do not
  wait outside; they enter as numbered closings with issuer,
  timing and consequence. §4 reads:

> **Proceed, with conditions.** No finding here is a deal-breaker:
> the file's highest tier is Manageable, so approval is the call
> and the open rows are its terms. Closing 1 — executed state
> approvals before close; consequence walk. Closing 2 — tribunal
> settlement or counsel's exposure cap; consequence price reset.
> The NRR decline is priced in the range, not gated.

## A bar is not a document gap

A third file: federal-grant revenue at 31% of the base; the grant
terms state the award is not transferable to a for-profit
acquirer; the buyer is for-profit. Ruling 6's first branch:

- A 'consent' cannot exist — the governing terms deny it to this
  buyer class. Asking for one is asking the rule to change.
- The revenue the buyer cannot hold is stripped; the remaining
  base prices below what the thesis needs. No act before signing
  alters the grant's own wording → pass:

> **Pass.** The transfer bar sits in the grant terms, not in a
> missing signature; no document on this timeline converts
> federal research funding into acquirable revenue.
