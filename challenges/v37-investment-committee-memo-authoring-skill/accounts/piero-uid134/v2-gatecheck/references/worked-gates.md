# Worked example — the gatecheck memo

A complete run on a fictional deal. The shape transfers to any
input: the checks run in order, every row keeps its filed label,
the call answers what the checks returned. Numbers are invented for
illustration; the reasoning pattern is the deliverable.

## The input (abridged for illustration)

Risk table filed:

| # | Finding | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Municipal data charter for the Chicago deployment (28% of installed devices); amendment transferring it to a new operator pending before the transit authority | Must-Address | Unresolved |
| 2 | UL safety certification on the sensor line filed renewed; narrative says the renewal inspection is scheduled, not performed | Must-Address | Resolved |
| 3 | One property-management group carries 33% of revenue on annual terms; renewal in negotiation | Manageable | Unresolved |
| 4 | Sole firmware engineer owning the OTA update pipeline has given notice effective in 9 months | Manageable | Unresolved |
| 5 | Datacenter lease runs three more years | Informational | Resolved |

Narrative: LTM revenue $11.6M ($9.9M recurring); a $1.4M one-time
installation program completed last quarter; headline growth 31%,
recurring growth 22%; EBITDA $0.9M blended, -$0.3M on the recurring
base. Ask $48M. Comps with a broker note: LiftMetrics 3.9x,
Doorwise 2.8x, ShaftSense 4.2x, BuildingPulse 6.1x "acquired by a
strategic at a platform premium last spring."

## The memo

**request more data** — two gating items open on the charter
amendment and the UL renewal; ask $48M as stated.

## 1. Deal thesis

Kestrel Lift Analytics is a monitoring platform on a sticky
installed base: 85% recurring revenue, devices that renew rather
than repurchase, 22% recurring growth in a vertical with few scaled
vendors. The economics that transfer justify a market multiple — but
the headline $11.6M includes a completed one-time installation
program, so the priced base is $10.2M recurring, not the filed
revenue. The primary risk is the municipal data charter covering
28% of installed devices: filed Unresolved, and until the transit
authority executes the amendment, the revenue the ask prices is
not the buyer's to keep. The UL renewal filed Resolved is not yet
a record.

## 2. Tiered risk table

| # | Finding | Filed | Gate verdict | Tier carried |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Municipal data charter amendment pending; 28% of devices | Unresolved | **gates** — the deciding signature sits with the transit authority; nothing on record bounds the timeline | Must-Address |
| 2 | UL certification renewal filed Resolved | Resolved | **open** — the row's own narrative dates only a scheduled inspection; scheduled is not certified. The issued certificate settles it | Must-Address |
| 3 | Property-management group, 33% of revenue, renewal in negotiation | Unresolved | **open** — live negotiation named; a signed renewal or a diversification plan carries it | Manageable |
| 4 | Sole OTA-pipeline engineer noticed, 9 months | Unresolved | **open** — dated runway, single-point dependency; retention terms and a documented handover carry it | Manageable |
| 5 | Datacenter lease, 3 years | Resolved | **noted** — informational, outside the decision | Informational |

**Gate tally:** 1 gates / 3 open / 0 stands / 1 noted

## 3. Defended valuation

Base: the seller's $11.6M includes the completed $1.4M installation
program that will not recur; the recurring base is $10.2M, and the
growth that prices is the recurring 22%, not the blended 31%.
EBITDA is -$0.3M on the economics that transfer, so EV/EBITDA is
not meaningful here — EV/Revenue carries the number.

Comps: BuildingPulse is excluded — the broker note itself flags a
strategic platform premium, and a 6.1x paid for control is a
transaction price, not a market multiple. Included: Doorwise 2.8x,
LiftMetrics 3.9x, ShaftSense 4.2x — a 2.8x–4.2x band with a 3.9x
median. Kestrel's 22% recurring growth and 85% recurring share sit
at the strong end of that set; no further adjustment is argued.

Arithmetic: 2.8x × $10.2M = $28.6M; 4.2x × $10.2M = $42.8M.
The justified range is $28.6M–$42.8M, with the defensible band
around the $36M–$40M neighborhood.

Ask-versus-range: $48M is 4.7x on the recurring base — above the
band's top. The gap answers as a price condition, not a pass.

## 4. Recommendation

Request more data. Two gating items stand open, and each clears on
a named record a deciding party can issue: the transit authority's
executed charter amendment, and the UL certificate issued after the
scheduled inspection completes. Neither is a wall — they are
documents. Proceed is unavailable while a must-address row is open;
pass is not supported because both items are askable within the
deal's timeline — nothing on record refuses, bars the transfer, or
makes the clearing record impossible. The $48M ask at 4.7x repriced
against the range is the negotiation the data request carries.

## 5. Conditions and next steps

1. Charter amendment executed by the transit authority before
   signing — closing deliverable; the transfer is not assumed.
2. UL renewal certificate issued post-inspection before signing —
   closing deliverable; a scheduled inspection is not a record.
3. Property-group renewal: a signed two-year commitment before
   close, or contingent consideration on the 33% cohort's
   post-close retention.
4. Retention agreement with the OTA engineer at close, plus a
   documented pipeline handover to a named second owner.
5. [to confirm: device-safety incident reports for the trailing
   24 months — the artifacts are silent.]

Bottom line — a sticky platform priced off the wrong base, with two
gating documents outstanding. Pay for the $10.2M recurring base,
not the $11.6M headline; the amendment and the certificate are the
seller's homework before signing.

## If the file were clean — the restraint shape

When no gate fires, the memo still says so explicitly rather than
hunting for blockers. Had every row stood as filed and the ask sat
inside the range, the same skeleton compresses to:

**proceed** — no open must-address finding; the ask sits inside the
justified range at 3.9x recurring.

## 5. Conditions and next steps

None gating. Standing items only: the property-group renewal tracked
on its negotiation calendar, the engineer handover documented as
routine hygiene.

Everything else — the table, the restated base, the comp arithmetic —
runs identically. Restraint changes the call and the conditions
list; it never changes the audit.

## Second shape — when a rule on the books bars the transfer

Worked mini-example for the pass branch. Input: Halden Locks, an
access-control hardware maker for municipal housing. The risk table
files its only real asset — a citywide security charter covering
61% of revenue — as "Must-Address / Resolved," but the charter's own
terms state it expires on any change of control; renewal requires a
fresh award the city grants only on a four-year cycle, last awarded
18 months ago. Asking price $52M on $14.0M revenue; comps 2.4x–3.0x.

## 1. Deal thesis

Halden has real hardware economics inside the charter footprint, but
the deal as offered buys revenue the buyer cannot hold: the charter
terminates by its own terms the day the deal closes, and no record
either party can produce restarts a four-year award cycle.

## 2. Tiered risk table

Abridged: the charter row carries **gates** with verdict "filed
Resolved — reads open: the charter self-terminates on change of
control by its own terms"; every other row stands or carries as
filed.

## 3. Defended valuation

Even granting the base, 2.4x–3.0x on $14.0M is $33.6M–$42.0M against
a $52M ask — and the correct base excludes the non-transferable
charter revenue (~$8.5M), on which the surviving $5.5M supports
$13.2M–$16.5M. The ask prices revenue the buyer would not own.

## 4. Recommendation

Pass — the charter that carries 61% of revenue expires on change of
control by its own written terms, and the only clearing record (a
fresh award) cannot exist on this deal's timeline. What would reopen
the file: a charter amendment executed by the city that expressly
survives the transfer.

## 5. Conditions and next steps

None offered: the deciding record sits on a four-year cycle outside
both parties' control, and a deal that cannot close its own gate
should not be conditioned into existence.

Bottom line — the pass is the deliverable: name the broken premise,
show the corrected base arithmetic, and stop.
