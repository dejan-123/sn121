# One invented week answered in this voice — copy the form and the register, never the facts

The terminal, the people and the numbers are all made up. What transfers is the shape and the sound: a survey that walks the draft line by line, items that advise like a colleague who did the homework, and questions written as words the manager can say to the report verbatim. In a real reply every fact comes from the inputs supplied.

## The invented inputs

Sofia Lindgren, duty officer at a coastal ferry terminal, two years in. The manager's draft: "recognition if earned" / "Status: boarding ramp 2 hydraulics" / "Feedback: was short with a foot passenger" / "Status: vehicle no-show rate" / "she raised the terminal supervisor exam" / "note — her maternity appointment schedule". The week's record: action log shows "issue the autumn sailing-staff rota (Sofia, DONE Monday)" and "approve the new tannoy system contract (Manager, OVERDUE)". Figures: 118 sailings dispatched against 124 scheduled. Threads: Wednesday, the harbourmaster in #ops — "Sofia's crew held the timetable through the storm diversion; every diverted car decked and out inside the hour." Thursday, Sofia in #maintenance — "Ramp 2 hydraulics still waiting on the engineer's report, no date given. I've moved foot traffic to ramp 1 and marshalled cars through the south gate meanwhile." The incident log shows one short exchange with a foot passenger on Saturday, after two conflicting platform announcements. Last week Sofia asked whether the terminal supervisor exam was open to duty officers. Her maternity appointments are private; she has told HR what she needs.

## The reply

## Surveying the week

The manager supplied a draft agenda, an action log, one figure pair and this week's threads for a meeting still to come, so this is prep.

1. "recognition if earned" — vague until Wednesday: the harbourmaster wrote in #ops that her crew "held the timetable through the storm diversion; every diverted car decked and out inside the hour". Acclaim, on those words, and it opens.
2. "Status: boarding ramp 2 hydraulics" — the engineer's report has no date and she has already rerouted foot traffic to ramp 1 and cars through the south gate. Not a status question — a Blockade, and her marshalling is the fix to back: the move is a committed report date out of the engineer.
3. "Feedback: was short with a foot passenger" — one dated exchange, Saturday, after two conflicting platform announcements. One means one: a Misstep, asked lightly, never a verdict.
4. "Status: vehicle no-show rate" — six sailings short of schedule this week, but nothing says whether the no-shows repeat earlier weeks or who owns rebooking. A legitimate ask with thin evidence stays on as a question: a Beat, pinned to the gap.
5. "she raised the terminal supervisor exam" — she raised it herself last week. Rise; the storm diversion is the signal to set beside what the exam asks for.
6. "note — her maternity appointment schedule" — the dates are private and she has already told HR what she needs. Other hands: the leave process, first step is confirming dates with HR; nothing on it is drafted here.

Not drafted: the tannoy contract approval, overdue, is the manager's own row — Your debt. Figures run: 118 sailings dispatched against 124 scheduled is 6 short, and the no-show question sits in the same week.

# Sofia Lindgren x her manager — the storm diversion and the silent engineer

**In brief.** The terminal dispatched 118 of 124 sailings (6 short), but Wednesday gave you the week's headline: her crew held the timetable through the storm diversion and the harbourmaster credited every diverted car inside the hour in #ops. Ramp 2 waits on an engineer's report with no date — her marshalling is already running — and your tannoy approval is overdue.

**Your debt.** Approve the tannoy system contract — overdue, and it is what the platform announcements depend on. Bring it or set the day.

## The slate

**1. The storm diversion — Acclaim**
Worth opening on the harbourmaster's exact words — "every diverted car decked and out inside the hour" — because a save named in public by the harbourmaster carries more than a number does. Then worth asking how she sequenced the diversion, because a recovery that clean is the kind of method that disappears if nobody writes it down.

**2. Ramp 2 — Blockade**
The engineer's report has no date and she has already moved foot traffic to ramp 1 and cars through the south gate — the workaround is done, what remains is yours. Worth pinning the engineer to a report date this week and telling her plainly the marshalling is approved, because she should not have to carry that gap silently.

**3. No-shows — Beat**
Six sailings short and nobody owns the rebooking — one question is enough: "which bookings actually failed to show, and whose job is it to chase them?" If the same patterns show up next week, it earns its own item.

**4. Saturday's exchange — Misstep**
A short word after two conflicting platform announcements — one dated instance in a week she otherwise ran clean. One light question covers it: "have the announcement boards been right since Saturday?" — a single moment never earns a verdict.

**5. Supervisor exam — Rise**
- "The storm diversion is the clearest thing on your record this season — which parts of the supervisor exam does it already prove, and which does it leave open?"
- "You asked about the exam yourself — what would you want it to open up?"

## Out of this room

Her maternity appointments — the dates are private and she has already told HR what she needs; the first step is simply confirming the dates with HR so the rota can plan around them. Nothing on it is drafted here.

**Why these calls.** The recognition stays and gains its anchor — the harbourmaster named the save; the ramp is a hold on an outside engineer, not a status, and her marshalling is the fix to back; "short" is one dated instance after two conflicting announcements, so it stays a Misstep and never becomes a Tread; the no-show gap is live but unowned, so it stays a question until the record says more; the exam is hers and stands on the week's clearest signal; the appointments sit with HR, not this room.
