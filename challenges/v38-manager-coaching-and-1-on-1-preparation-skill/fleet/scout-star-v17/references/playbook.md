# Field guide — read it before writing anything

## Gate: is this even the job?

Check the packet before the draft. Minutes, notes or a transcript of a meeting already held describe last week — they cannot prepare the next one. Same for review drafts and skip-level asks. All of them get one short reply:

- heading `# Not prep for this week`, under 180 words, no weighing section;
- name what it is and why it cannot prepare the next conversation;
- name what would — this week's open items, current numbers, anything raised since;
- where the record hides next steps, one line saying they exist — never listed, never re-sorted;
- anything personal is "the personal matter", nothing more.

A genuine week packet — draft agenda plus the records around it — moves on.

## Labels

One label per draft line; precedence **Praise → Waiting → Pattern → Path → FYI → Off-agenda**, earlier wins.

- **Praise**: quotable thanks exists — a client, a peer, work named out loud. Opens the meeting in the words and number it arrived in. Nothing quotable → the page starts on the first real item; invented warmth costs more than it buys.
- **Waiting**: someone else must act — unsigned approval, quiet dependency, queue too big for one person, colleagues stuck behind it. The manager leaves with a move of their own. Snappish messages after ignored chases are the blockage talking, not character. Record shows a bigger blast than the draft admits — several teams on one person, a senior leader already in the thread — say so and move it up, whatever rank the draft gave it.
- **Pattern**: same behaviour on two-plus occasions, or a goal the report chose. Questions, never lectures. Evidence contradicts the manager's impression — "disengaged" while flags and picked-up work say otherwise — drop it: say the draft was wrong, list the contradicting signals, the item becomes whatever is actually blocking it. Report already found the cause → coach one step earlier (spot the change sooner), pinned to real numbers, never the symptom they named.
- **Path**: the report raised their own future — training, scope, a next role or career step, review timing — only when they or their record raised it. One honest question plus a promise you can keep.
- **FYI**: nothing to fix, celebrate or decide. Closes the meeting in a line or two, never opens one.
- **Off-agenda**: wrong room — personal matters ("the personal matter", nothing more), pay, HR cases, other teams' conflicts, next quarter. Route it to where it gets decided, reason in a line; it leaves the numbered items and sits under **Set aside**, never a page line.

## Standing calls

- Manager-owned rows — open, overdue, NOT STARTED — land in **Your half**, a concrete move each.
- "Low"/"quick" on the draft describes memory, not evidence — a cross-team block outranks it; the weighing says so.
- Draft vs record disagrees → record wins; the weighing shows the working.
- Order is earned: earned praise first, housekeeping last, blockages while there is time to act.
- Resolved stays resolved unless a newer artifact directly contradicts it.

## The page

`# 1-on-1 page: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]`, 350–700 words, addressed to the manager as "you", the report's words in quotes. `Context:` one line. Items in conversation order: `**name — Label**` plus one or two sentences of things to say and ask, with the real names and numbers. No praise earned → a line above item 1 reads `No recognition opener:` and says what would have supported one. Pattern and Path items end in a question naming a thread, a figure or the report's own words — a question that fits any report's page has no signal. Then `Set aside` (what left the room, why), `Your half` (the manager's open rows, a move each), `The reasoning` (a few plain sentences on what moved and why — enough for the manager to defend the page).

## The weighing ahead of it

`## Weighing the draft` opens the reply: one numbered entry per draft item in draft order, each two or three plain sentences — the manager's wording in quotes, what the week shows (who logged or said what, where, when, which figure), then the label and reason said like a colleague would. No `Evidence:`/`Verdict:` fields — a note, not a form. Then `Off the draft:` what the manager owes or missed (their open rows first) and `The arithmetic:` each figure as "current versus plan (difference)".

Invent nothing. Thin packet → the page still ships: the weighing shrinks to three or four sentences (what arrived, what did not, what each label would need); every item becomes a question — what is unknown, what to ask, what to listen for — no opener, no coaching; `Your half` says what to pull. Shorter, never padded; a pay or personal item still leaves the room, no talking points.

## One worked week (invented — take the rigour, never the facts)

Signals, Tuesday through Friday: a print-shop floor lead, four years running the presses. Job board: the paper-stock purchase order has waited unsigned in the office tray for three days; her re-typeset menu boards shipped to the hotel group and their events manager emailed "the sharpest boards we've hung"; the run count reads 52 jobs finished against 60 booked, two days left. Press log: she chased the purchase order Tuesday, asked Wednesday about colour-management training, snapped at the apprentice Friday after the third jam on press two. Draft agenda: "paper PO — status; colour training; menu boards came out well I think; run count."

## Weighing the draft

1. "paper PO — status" — the job board shows it unsigned three days in your tray, she chased it Tuesday, and three tickets are held for stock. Not a status item — this is **Waiting**, and the block is yours.
2. "colour training" — she asked about it herself on Wednesday, straight out of the press log. That makes it **Path**: she raised it, so it earns an honest answer and whatever you can commit to.
3. "menu boards came out well I think" — they shipped, and the client's events manager wrote "the sharpest boards we've hung" about them. That is **Praise** — her words open the meeting.
4. "run count" — 52 finished against 60 booked with two days left, inside her usual band. An **FYI** — confirm it in a line and move on.

**Off the draft:** your unsigned purchase order, three days old — that lands in Your half, and it is also why item 1 is waiting. Friday's snapped tone reads as the third press jam talking; watch it, do not agenda it.
**The arithmetic:** 52/60 runs (8 behind), her shift's spoilage 2.1% against the shop's 3.4%.

# 1-on-1 page: Ada Osei x her manager — Friday

**Context:** Four years running the presses, steady numbers, one stalled order.

**1. Menu boards — Praise**
Open here: they shipped and the client's events manager wrote "the sharpest boards we've hung". Say her words, ask what she would re-typeset next.

**2. Paper PO — Waiting**
Three days unsigned in your tray and a chase logged. Sign it today, then ask which jobs sit exposed if the stock slips again.

**3. Colour training — Path**
She raised the training. Say what the budget allows and what she would need to show.

**4. Run count — FYI**
52 of 60 with two days left — inside her usual band. Confirm and move on.

**Set aside:** nothing formal this week. **Your half:** sign the purchase order today; answer the training question with real dates and a real budget. **The reasoning:** the draft filed a signature problem as "status" and a path question as housekeeping — the record re-sequenced both, and the praise it flattened is what opens the conversation.
