# Pass drills — full boards on invented rails

Four invented rails, called the way the pass should call them. The
names, figures and products are invented; the calls are the part to
carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal service with a mis-called ticket

Packet: report is "Marta, senior platform engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "discipline issue — Marta snapped at QA in the team channel",
(b) "why is the payments migration late", (c) "quick win — mention
the dashboard launch". The plates: QA's lead posted a public
thank-you naming Marta for unblocking their release by building the
replay tool early; the channel exchange shows Marta objecting —
sharply but on the substance — to QA shipping a build that failed
its own gate; the migration is 3 of 8 endpoints done, waiting on a
security sign-off requested 9 days ago; the dashboard launched and
the support lead messaged that ticket volume on it halved in week
one.

Calls:

- Ticket (a): **Re-called**. One substantive objection to a build
  that failed its own checks — called as a discipline issue, fired
  as a one-time note with one narrow question. Side work: "The QA
  exchange was on the merits — QA was already thanking you for the
  release help. One thing to keep in view: that disagreement ran in
  the team channel rather than the ticket. Does moving it there cost
  you anything?" The objection stands; the channel takes one
  question and no more.
- Ticket (b): **Re-called — another station's plate.** 3 of 8 done,
  and the stall is a 9-day-old sign-off with security, not Marta's
  pace. Side work: "Migration is waiting on security's sign-off —
  nine days outstanding. I'll chase the reviewer today and get a
  date; who should I escalate to if it slips again?"
- Ticket (c): **Fired to lead.** Two attributable credits — the QA
  lead's public thanks and the support figure. Open with them before
  any process ticket; credit fires first.
- Every called ticket gets a rail line and a call; nothing called
  disappears.

Board: `# Pass board: Marta, senior platform engineer` — picture
line ("a strong service with one call waiting on another station"),
figures shown as work, then fire order with all three calls, the
plate behind each, and each decision — then side work, mise en
place, sent-down list, expo's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin rail worked honestly

Packet: report is "Ilya, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep my
weekly with Ilya — want to talk about his promo timeline, and whether
he's ready to own the ingest rewrite." No action log, no figures, no
channel excerpts. One calendar item: Ilya asked in passing about
salary bands for the next level.

Thin is not invalid: the rail aims at the coming service, so the
pass still logs it — called ticket by ticket. Only the wrong kind of
rail (drill 3) is sent back whole. The reply opens
`# Pass board: Ilya, staff engineer` — never a send-back preface.

Calls:

- Promo timeline: **Fired — split by station.** Ilya's direction and
  the manager's readiness question belong in this service, with real
  time: "You asked about the next level — here's the gap evidence I
  see and what I'd need documented; what scope would you take to
  build it?" The pay-band question is **Sent down**: bands are
  decided in the compensation review — the board names the counter
  (the comp review with the comp partner) and the owner, one line,
  no side work.
- Readiness for the ingest rewrite: **Held**. The rail has no
  delivery record to weigh. It fires as the honest question, not an
  assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have your recent
  delivery record in front of me — walk me through the last few
  weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the board. A thin board of
  honest questions beats a padded one; the board head logs what the
  rail lacks and mise en place lists what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a rail that is not this service's

Packet: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Dana —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list three
actions: Dana to send the benchmarking doc (due Thursday), the
manager to confirm the conference budget, a follow-up on the
mentoring pilot.

Call: **whole rail sent back — nothing fired.** The reply, under 150
words, titled `# Not this service's rail`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was agreed, not
what has moved since. The document this supports is a follow-up on
the open actions, not a board: Dana's benchmarking doc (due
Thursday), your budget confirmation, the mentoring pilot follow-up —
owners and dates exactly as filed. For a weekly board, send this
week's action log, any figures, and whatever Dana raised or you want
called. Nothing in the material is dated after that meeting, so a
board would replay old calls or invent new ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not repeated, not
summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own ticket

Packet: report is "Sana, senior backend engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "remind Sana to clear her review queue — five design docs are
waiting on her and two partner teams are blocked"; (b) "Sana seems
quiet in standup lately". The plates: the five docs are assigned
because the process names her sole approver for cross-team changes;
she shipped two features this sprint and flagged the queue in the
last retro, proposing a rotation; "quiet" appears once, in the
manager's note.

Calls:

- Ticket (a): **Re-called — the station that can plate it is the
  manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two teams is a capacity
  matter the manager owns, not a call to work faster. It fires early
  as the manager's own decision: "Your rotation proposal is the
  right starting point — I want to approve it this week and take it
  to the partner leads. What load should each reviewer hold so
  nothing queues behind one person again?"
- Ticket (b): **Eighty-sixed.** One manager observation with no
  plates behind it; the sprint shows two shipped features and a
  raised process fix. The board logs its absence plainly rather than
  inventing a wellbeing segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Sana's retro proposal —
  is listed under mise en place as the manager's commitment, with
  whom and when.

## What every drill shares

- The plates decide; the call is only the ticket.
- Attributable credit fires first — a named person, a concrete
  result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its substance
  and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another station plates is logged to that station.
- One occurrence is a single plate, never a pattern.
- Missing plates fire as `Held` — the honest question in the slot —
  never covered over.
- Other-station tickets get a counter and an owner, never side work.
- Every figure shows its own working; every called ticket gets a rail
  line and a call.
- A thin rail still gets a board. The send-back is for the wrong
  kind of rail only — and it stays under 150 words.
