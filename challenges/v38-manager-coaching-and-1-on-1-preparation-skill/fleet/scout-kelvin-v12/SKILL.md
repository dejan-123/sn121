# The prep card

Keep this card open while you work. It has three questions and two rules for answering.

## Question 1 — what did the week actually do?

Read the raw material before the manager's agenda: tickets closed and opened, chat threads, review comments, shipped numbers, compliments that arrived in writing, the manager's own task list. Open means open — anything open, overdue, or untouched counts; finished stays finished unless a newer item proves otherwise.

While reading, keep two running tallies: items where the report is owed something (a named compliment, a decision, a promised answer) and items where the manager owes something. A claim floating in the agenda with nothing underneath it is just a claim — log it as unsupported.

## Question 2 — does each agenda line match the record?

For each line the manager drafted, ask which drawer it belongs in:

- **Credit** — a concrete thing worth saying thank you for. Goes first, using the actual words that were written and any figure attached. No quotable instance, no opener — then one line above the first item admits there is nothing to open with.
- **Stuck elsewhere** — progress waits on another person, team, or supplier. The report should not leave carrying it; the manager leaves with an unblock step. Frustration aimed at a held-up dependency is a logistics problem, not an attitude problem — and when the log shows the hold-up reaching beyond this desk (queues forming, a director copied), say so plainly even if the agenda filed it under minor.
- **Habit** — the same behaviour on two or more separate dated occasions, or a goal the report volunteered. One thread of messages about one incident is one incident. A mistake the report already caught and fixed earns a sentence of feedback, not a development conversation. When the report already diagnosed their own cause and is tracking it, coach the earlier step instead — catching it sooner — tied to the real numbers.
- **Looking ahead** — the report themselves raised courses, growth, scope, or when reviews happen. One sincere question, one honest commitment; nothing promised that cannot be kept.
- **Logistics** — routine facts needing no decision and no repair. Brief, and never first — leading with housekeeping spends the freshest attention on the smallest line.
- **Different table** — compensation, HR processes, a dispute belonging to another team, private life. Routed where it belongs, with one line of explanation. Private matters stay private: "the personal matter", never elaborated.

Drop any line the material cannot support — a feeling with no signal behind it gets named as dropped, not smuggled in.

## Question 3 — what goes on the page?

Two possible answers:

**When this is genuinely preparation**, produce two sections.

First, `## Against the record` — one row per agenda line, in the agenda's order: the manager's wording in quotes; **Found:** what the material actually shows — names, dates, ticket or figure; **Call:** the drawer it landed in, or "holds" when the draft was right, or parked / dropped / merged. After the rows: **Missed by the draft:** what the tally found that the agenda skipped, and **Arithmetic:** any number worth checking, computed from its components — "4 escalations of 41 tickets, roughly a tenth" — the operands visible, points never confused with percent.

Then `# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]`, 350–700 words. **Context:** one honest line. Items as `**[short name] ([Drawer], ~N min)**`, ordered credit first and housekeeping last, each written as a sentence or two of things to actually say — real names and figures inside. After the items: **Held back** (parked or dropped lines, each with its reason), **Manager's homework** (every open row the manager owns, each with one concrete action — finished before the report is asked for anything, especially a row an agenda item depends on), and **The reasoning** (two or three lines on why items landed where they did).

Invent nothing. A thin packet still produces a page: the record section names what is missing, and the brief asks — what the manager cannot yet know, the question to pose, what a real answer would look like.

**When the input is a finished meeting** — minutes, a transcript, dated notes of a conversation already held, whatever the subject line claims — produce only one short note under `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request`, under 180 words, no record section:

- Say plainly that it records a past meeting rather than preparing the next one — and name the document and the meeting it records.
- Say what would turn it into preparation: this week's open items, current figures, the manager's rows, anything raised since.
- Where open actions survive inside it, write the follow-up they earned — owner, action, date as given — in a line or two; never a brief, never the old items re-sorted.
- Personal material mentioned in passing stays personal — "the personal matter", nothing more.

Three awkward cases, settled the same way. Praise the agenda claims but nothing written supports: the record-check says the instance is absent, and the brief opens on the first real item under a one-line admission. A thin week: the record-check lists what never arrived, and the brief still exists — built on the report's own raised topic where one exists, then the three questions. A transcript or minutes arriving as input: the short note above, follow-ups extracted where open actions survive, nothing more. Same questions, same drawers — the facts in any example are illustrations, never evidence.
