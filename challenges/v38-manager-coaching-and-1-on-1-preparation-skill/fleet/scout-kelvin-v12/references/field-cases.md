# Field cases

Invented material. The point of each case is the move, not the facts.

## Case 1 — a full week

**The packet.** A bakery wholesale manager's draft for driver Tomás: "route sheet (status)", "compliment for Ana (recognition)", "Saturday overtime (coaching — his attitude)", "van inspection (status)". Plus: delivery log (3 late drops of 96, all Friday, all pinned to a road-closure diversion), a customer email thanking Tomás by name for an early delivery — "he waited twenty minutes so we could count the order properly", the manager's own open task "renew van insurance — overdue 6 days", and one line in the agenda margin: "morale seems off".

**Against the record:**

1. "route sheet (status)" — Found: 3 late drops of 96, Friday only, diversion logged. Call: Logistics — mention inside the numbers, not a line of its own.
2. "compliment for Ana" — Found: the email thanks *Tomás*, quoted words exist. Call: Credit — the person named in the draft was wrong; the opener belongs to Tomás, in the customer's words.
3. "Saturday overtime (attitude)" — Found: the frustration traces to Friday's diversion, a logistics cause; no second occasion anywhere. Call: not a habit — fold into the numbers item as a quick word.
4. "van inspection" — Found: no record at all. Call: dropped, named as such.
5. "morale seems off" — Found: nothing underneath it. Call: dropped, named as such.

Missed by the draft: the overdue insurance is the manager's row — and the van inspection cannot be scheduled until it is settled. Arithmetic: 3 of 96 drops late, about a thirtieth, one day only.

**The brief** opens with the customer's sentence verbatim, gives the late drops their honest cause (a diversion, not the driver), books the overtime remark as one quick line inside that item, puts the insurance under Manager's homework with "renew before Friday" — the inspection returns to the agenda only once it clears — and names both dropped lines under Held back.

## Case 2 — praise that cannot be quoted

The manager writes "she had a great week — lead with praise". The material shows steady work and no written compliment, no named save, no figure that stands out. The record-check says: praise claimed, instance absent. The brief places one line above the first item — "no named thing to open with this week" — and starts with the first real item instead. Invented warmth reads exactly like what it is.

## Case 3 — a thin week

Two items arrive: a note that the report asked about flexible hours, and a draft line "discuss performance". The record-check says what is missing — no tickets, no threads, no numbers. The brief still exists: the hours question is the report's own raise and earns its slot ("Looking ahead" family — asked sincerely, answered honestly or promised honestly), and the rest of the page is three questions: what the manager has not yet seen, what to ask the report directly, what a real answer would sound like.

## Case 4 — the input is a transcript

A transcript of last Tuesday's one-on-one arrives labelled "prep for Friday". It is a record of a meeting that already happened, so the answer is a short note — what it is, that it is not preparation, follow-ups for the two open actions inside it (who owes what by when), and the list of what to send for the coming meeting. No brief. The facts inside a finished conversation are already stale; building next Friday's plan on them bakes last week's week into this one.
