# Rounds drills — full charts on invented packets

Four invented packets, triaged the way the chart should triage them.
The names, figures and products are invented; the dispositions are the
part to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal week with a mis-presented complaint

Packet: report is "Marta, senior platform engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "discipline issue — Marta snapped at QA in the team channel",
(b) "why is the payments migration late", (c) "quick win — mention the
dashboard launch". The findings: QA's lead posted a public thank-you
naming Marta for unblocking their release by building the replay tool
early; the channel exchange shows Marta objecting — sharply but on the
substance — to QA shipping a build that failed its own gate; the
migration is 3 of 8 endpoints done, waiting on a security sign-off
requested 9 days ago; the dashboard launched and the support lead
messaged that ticket volume on it halved in week one.

Dispositions:

- Complaint (a): **Re-charted**. One substantive objection to a build
  that failed its own checks — presented as a discipline issue,
  admitted as a one-time note with one narrow question. Note: "The QA
  exchange was on the merits — QA was already thanking you for the
  release help. One thing to keep in view: that disagreement ran in
  the team channel rather than the ticket. Does moving it there cost
  you anything?" The objection stands; the channel takes one question
  and no more.
- Complaint (b): **Re-charted — the case belongs to another hand.**
  3 of 8 done, and the stall is a 9-day-old sign-off with security,
  not Marta's pace. Note: "Migration is waiting on security's
  sign-off — nine days outstanding. I'll chase the reviewer today and
  get a date; who should I escalate to if it slips again?"
- Complaint (c): **Admitted as the lead.** Two attributable credits —
  the QA lead's public thanks and the support figure. Open with them
  before any process case; credit lands first.
- Every filed complaint appears on the chart with its intake line;
  nothing filed disappears.

Chart: `# Rounds chart: Marta, senior platform engineer` — picture
line ("a strong week with one decision waiting on another team"),
figures shown as work, then rounds order with all three presentations,
the finding behind each, and each disposition — then notes,
pre-rounds, transfers, attending's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin packet triaged honestly

Packet: report is "Ilya, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep my
weekly with Ilya — want to talk about his promo timeline, and whether
he's ready to own the ingest rewrite." No action log, no figures, no
channel excerpts. One calendar item: Ilya asked in passing about
salary bands for the next level.

Thin is not invalid: the packet aims at the coming encounter, so
intake still sees it — disposed complaint by complaint. Only the
wrong kind of document (drill 3) is bounced whole. The reply opens
`# Rounds chart: Ilya, staff engineer` — never a bounce-back preface.

Dispositions:

- Promo timeline: **Admitted — split by service.** Ilya's direction
  and the manager's readiness question belong in this encounter, with
  real time: "You asked about the next level — here's the gap
  evidence I see and what I'd need documented; what scope would you
  take to build it?" The pay-band question is **Transferred**: bands
  live in the compensation review, not a weekly — the chart names the
  venue (the comp review with the comp partner) and the owner, one
  line, no notes.
- Readiness for the ingest rewrite: **Observed**. The packet has no
  delivery record to weigh. It is admitted as the honest question,
  not an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have your recent
  delivery record in front of me — walk me through the last few
  weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the chart. A thin chart of
  honest questions beats a padded one; the chart head notes what the
  packet lacks ("no action log, figures or channel record this
  week") and pre-rounds lists what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a packet that is not this week's chart

Packet: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Dana —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list three
actions: Dana to send the benchmarking doc (due Thursday), the
manager to confirm the conference budget, a follow-up on the
mentoring pilot.

Disposition: **whole packet bounced — nothing triaged.** The reply,
under 150 words, titled `# Not this week's chart`, four short
paragraphs and no extra headings:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was agreed, not
what has moved since. The document this supports is a follow-up on
the open actions, not a chart: Dana's benchmarking doc (due
Thursday), your budget confirmation, the mentoring pilot follow-up —
owners and dates exactly as filed. For a weekly chart, send this
week's action log, any figures, and whatever Dana raised or you want
on the table. Nothing in the material is dated after that meeting,
so a chart would replay old findings or invent new ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not repeated,
not summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own case

Packet: report is "Sana, senior backend engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "remind Sana to clear her review queue — five design docs are
waiting on her and two partner teams are blocked"; (b) "Sana seems
quiet in standup lately". The findings: the five docs are assigned
because the process names her sole approver for cross-team changes;
she shipped two features this sprint and flagged the queue in the
last retro, proposing a rotation; "quiet" appears once, in the
manager's note.

Dispositions:

- Complaint (a): **Re-charted — the service that can act is the
  manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two teams is a capacity
  case the manager owns, not a note to work faster. It is admitted
  high on the chart as the manager's own decision: "Your rotation
  proposal is the right starting point — I want to approve it this
  week and take it to the partner leads. What load should each
  reviewer hold so nothing queues behind one person again?"
- Complaint (b): **Discharged.** One manager observation with no
  findings behind it; the sprint shows two shipped features and a
  raised process fix. The chart notes its absence plainly rather
  than inventing a wellbeing segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Sana's retro proposal —
  is listed under pre-rounds as the manager's commitment, with whom
  and when.

## What every drill shares

- The findings dispose; the presentation is only the intake form.
- Attributable credit leads — a named person, a concrete result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its substance
  and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another service controls is charted to that service.
- One occurrence is a single reading, never a pattern.
- Thin records are admitted as `Observed` — the honest question in
  the slot — never covered over.
- Other-service cases get a venue and an owner, never notes.
- Every figure shows its own working; every filed complaint gets an
  intake line and a disposition.
- A thin packet still gets a chart. The bounce-back is for the wrong
  kind of document only — and it stays under 150 words.
