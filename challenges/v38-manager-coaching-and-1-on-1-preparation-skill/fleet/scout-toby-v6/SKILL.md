# Triage — present every complaint, dispose it from the findings

A manager's draft agenda is a list of presenting complaints, each stated
the manager's way. The presentation is a first hunch, not a diagnosis.
Every complaint is taken at intake and disposed from the week's findings
alone — what the record documents, nothing it does not. The output is a
rounds chart the manager reads on the way in: every complaint presented,
every disposition traceable to a finding, the count matching the
complaints filed.

## Intake — is this this week's chart?

Three inputs look like a weekly chart and are not: notes, minutes or a
transcript of a meeting already held (even when the manager asks to plan
the next one from it — it documents last week's encounter, not this
week's findings; what it supports is a follow-up on the actions);
preparation for a skip-level or a different meeting; a performance-review,
rating or disciplinary draft. For these, see no complaints. Reply in under
150 words titled `# Not this week's chart`: what the input is, which
document it actually supports and who owns it, what to bring for a weekly
chart — ending with the one-line reason (nothing in the material is dated
after that encounter, so a chart would replay old findings or invent new
ones). Where the input is last week's minutes, list each open action with
owner and date exactly as filed; open actions carry forward as entries,
never as assumed current status.

The bounce-back triggers on the document's kind and nothing else — a
record of a held meeting, another meeting's preparation, a review draft.
A packet of the manager's own topics for the coming encounter is always
this week's chart, even when it carries no findings at all: a bare
agenda, a two-line note, topics with no figures behind them. Scarcity is
disposed complaint by complaint (Observed, below); it never triggers a
bounce-back. For every packet that is this week's chart — full or thin —
the reply opens `# Rounds chart:` with no preamble; the thinness is
noted inside the chart, never as a document of its own.

## Intake — what gets charted

Only what the packet documents gets charted: an action item and its
state (the manager's own included), a sprint or ticket figure, a quoted
line, the manager's note, the report's own topic. No invented figure,
quote, date, name or mood. Where a disposition needs a finding the
packet lacks, the lack is charted — never filled with a comfortable
guess.

## Vitals — how findings weigh

Findings weigh by kind, not volume. One documented occurrence is a
single reading — it can support a note, it cannot support a course.
The same conduct documented on two or more occasions is a pattern.
Attributed commendation — a named person's thanks or a concrete result —
is credit earned; effort, hours or unverifiable praise is not. The
report's own account is self-reported: chartable, weighed below an
independent record. An impression the packet cannot source ("seems
flat lately") is a reason to ask, not a finding.

## Closed cases stay closed

A complaint the record shows resolved is charted as a one-line close —
unless another documented finding cannot stand with the resolution
(a process still running, a commitment still open). The complaint's
own presentation never reopens it. Where a later finding and the
logged resolution genuinely conflict, chart the conflict in writing;
never re-label silently.

## Which service owns it

A complaint belongs to the service that can act on it. Work waiting on
an input another person or team controls is that service's case — a
stalled dependency goes on the chart as the manager's own action to
move it, never as the report's fault to answer for. A sole reviewer or
approver whose queue stalls several teams is a capacity case the
manager owns: it is admitted high as the manager's own decision — who
should hold the load, with the fix the report already proposed as the
starting point — not as a reminder to the report to work faster. Pay,
health, family, formal ratings, complaints about colleagues and
disciplinary process belong to other services entirely: transferred
with venue and owner, no talking points, one private line at most.

## The dispositions

Every complaint takes exactly one disposition, and the presentation
never decides it — the findings do:

- **Admitted**: the findings support the complaint as presented. It
  takes its slot with an anchored question or a concrete manager
  action.
- **Re-charted**: the complaint is admitted under a corrected label.
  A conduct or attitude complaint where the findings show work
  stalled at another team's door is re-charted to the blocked case it
  is; a routine check on finished, credited work is re-charted to the
  credit. The chart names the presentation and the re-label both.
- **Transferred**: a real case for another service — named
  destination, named owner, no talking points.
- **Discharged**: no documented finding supports the complaint — the
  recognition with no attributable credit, the topic presented as
  padding. Its absence is charted plainly.
- **Observed**: the case is live but the record is thin. It is
  admitted as the honest question — what is the actual state, what
  does the missing record show — never as coverage invented to fill
  the slot.

A development complaint requires a pattern (the vitals rule). A single
incident is admitted as a one-time note — with one narrow question
about the setting, never as a course of treatment. Where a request was
substantively justified but ran in a public channel, the request is
admitted as presented and exactly one question addresses the channel;
the justified case is not folded into the channel point. Where the
report raises their own direction — a level, a path, a stretch scope —
that is their case and it is admitted with real time in this
encounter; only the formal decision (a promotion case, a rating, a
band) is transferred.

## The chart

300 to 650 words, written for the manager to read, not to audit. The
encounter runs 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise, and the
items' minutes add to it.

- **Chart head**: `# Rounds chart: <name>, <role>` — then three
  lines: the week's picture in one sentence; every derivable figure
  written as shown work (`2 of 5 closed, 3 open`, `14 → 23 screens,
  +64%`), each pair against its own basis, never a ratio without its
  operands; which record types the packet lacks, noted plainly.
- **Rounds order**: every complaint, numbered, as
  `n. Disposition · topic · ~minutes`, then under each its intake
  line: `presented: <the manager's complaint> → <disposition>: <the
  finding in a few words>` — every complaint carries this line,
  including `as presented` where complaint and disposition agree. No
  complaint filed disappears: a merged complaint keeps its own intake
  line under the case that absorbed it; transferred and discharged
  complaints name their disposition. Order: earned credit first when
  it exists — a concrete, named case the report can hear land; then
  what blocks work or needs the manager's call; then a one-time note;
  then the report's own direction with real time (if the encounter
  cannot hold it, book a dedicated session rather than squeeze it
  into the last two minutes); quick closes last.
- **Notes**: questions and concrete manager actions only, each
  anchored to a named finding — the thread, ticket, figure or
  person — at most three sentences per case. A direction the report
  never raised stays a career case only when the packet holds their
  topic or an agreed cadence; otherwise it is one open question at
  the end and the chart notes the packet holds none of theirs. A
  question that could be asked in any week is not a note.
- **Pre-rounds**: the manager's own open or overdue items — cleared
  or owned first of all when they touch the same case — and the
  records to pull for any gap intake noted.
- **Transfers**: the other-service cases with venue and owner.
- **Attending's note**: three to five plain sentences closing the
  chart — each re-chart, transfer or discharge with the finding that
  forced it; why the first case leads or why there is none; what a
  headline figure does and does not show.

When a figure explains only part of a gap, the rest stays open: the
chart names the share still unexplained and puts it to the report as
a status question rather than treating the explanation as settled.
