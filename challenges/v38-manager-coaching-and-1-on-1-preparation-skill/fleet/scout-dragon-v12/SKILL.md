# Prep like a deputy

Do not start with the agenda. Start with the week's record — tickets, messages, figures, praise — because the agenda was written from memory at the end of a long week, and memory distorts in predictable ways. The stalled job becomes "status". The client's letter becomes "went well". One bad afternoon becomes "a pattern". So the record gets read first, the draft gets held up against it — and that holding happens where the manager can see it, on the page above the brief. Shown working builds confidence; hidden working gets second-guessed.

## Reading the record

Take it at face value. Open, overdue and NOT STARTED all count as open. Resolved stays resolved unless something newer contradicts it directly — the original complaint never reopens a closed row. A number that lives only in the manager's head does not exist; anything computed is written as current versus plan with the difference. Note every open row the manager owns — those are preparation, not trivia — and every piece of praise that landed by name.

## Calling each item

Each draft item, in the draft's own order, gets a call:

- **Recognition** — written praise exists — a named note, a client's words, work thanked by name. Opens the meeting in the giver's own words, the figure attached; never "good week".
- **Blocked** — another desk holds it — an approval not given, a dependency gone quiet, more requests than one pair of hands. The manager leaves carrying a move of their own, not the report. Sharp messages after ignored chases are the hold-up talking, not a tone problem. And if the record shows it biting harder than the draft's label pretends — other teams queued behind it, an exec already involved — say so and it climbs the order.
- **Coaching** — one behaviour on two or more separate dated occasions, or a goal the report chose. It runs on questions, never lectures. An impression the record contradicts — "disengaged" while the log shows early flags — gets dropped, the signals named. When the report already found the cause and is tracking it, coach the step before it — flag changes sooner next time — anchored to the real figures, not the symptom they already found.
- **Career** — the report themselves raised growth, a course, wider scope, or when reviews happen. One sincere question; promise only what is keepable.
- **Status** — nothing to repair, praise or settle. Last, squeezed to a line or two — opening on housekeeping spends the freshest minutes on the smallest line.
- **Parked** — its room is elsewhere — pay, HR matters, a dispute a different team owns, next quarter's plan. Sent there with a line saying why. Personal matters stay private — "the personal matter", elaborated never.

Finer calls along the way: a complaint that traces back to somebody else's hold-up is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. One problem raised five times is still one problem; Coaching takes separate dated occasions. A feeling the record cannot back gets dropped. A slip the report caught and logged themselves gets a quick word, not a coaching slot. Recognition opens only with a quote behind it; when nothing quotable exists, a line above item one says so.

## What lands on the page

**## Item by item** — the working, printed first. One numbered entry per draft item, in the draft's order: the label in quotes; **On the record:** what the record shows — who said or logged what, where, when, with the row or figure; **The call:** the class and the reason — "holds" when the draft had it right — or parked, dropped, folded into another. Then **The draft missed:** — what the manager owes or skipped — and **Figures:** the arithmetic, worked from its operands — "214 against 210 is four over plan"; a point is not a percent.

**# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]** — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line — report, tenure, the week in a phrase. Items in meeting order, **name — Call, ~N min** and a sentence or two written as things to say and ask, carrying the real numbers and names. Then **Set aside** (parked or dropped, with reasons), **Before the meeting** (every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move — handled before the report is asked for anything — first the row an agenda item depends on), and **Thinking** (three or four lines on why the calls landed as they did).

Never invent a fact. A thin packet — draft only, nothing behind it — still gets a page: the working says what is missing and the brief asks instead of asserting — what the manager does not know yet, the question to put to the report, and what a real answer sounds like.

A full prep, run start to finish on a different week, — record, calls, page — sits in `references/bench-notes.md`. Follow the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When what arrives is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already had — whatever its label — the reply is one short note, under 180 words, with no working section:

- Say plainly that it records a past meeting rather than preparing the next one — and name the document and the meeting it records.
- Say what would turn it into preparation: this week's open items, current figures, the manager's rows, anything flagged since.
- Where open actions survive inside it, write the follow-up they earned — owner, action, date as given — in a line or two; never a brief, never the old items re-sorted.
- Personal material mentioned in passing stays personal — "the personal matter", nothing more.
