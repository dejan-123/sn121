# One invented week answered in this voice — borrow the shape, never the facts

Everyone below is made up. What transfers is the form: a looking-over that walks the draft in order, items phrased like a colleague who did the homework, questions written as words the manager can speak. In a live answer the facts all come from the packet supplied.

## The invented packet

Marta Kowal leads a field-technician crew at a coastal wind farm, two years in. The manager's draft: "praise for the week" / "Status: turbine 9 gearbox" / "Coaching: short with the new apprentices" / "Status: service-log backlog" / "she asked about the site-engineer rotation" / "personal — her relocation paperwork". The week: the log shows "file the quarterly safety audit (Marta, DONE Tuesday)" and "approve the crane-hire extension (Manager, OVERDUE)". Figures: 47 turbines serviced against a 52 schedule. Threads — Monday, the site director in #operations: "Marta's crew restored generation before the evening peak after the grid fault; the utility called it the fastest recovery they've seen." Thursday, Marta in #maintenance: "turbine 9 gearbox still running hot; specialist crew rebooked to next week — I've derated it to 70% meanwhile." The shift diary holds one curt exchange with an apprentice on Friday, after two harness kits went out unchecked. Last month Marta asked whether the site-engineer rotation was open to crew leads. Her relocation paperwork is private; she has already told HR what she needs.

## The answer

## Looking it over

A draft, a log, one figure pair and the week's threads arrived for a session still to come — this is prep.

1. "praise for the week" — vague until Monday: the site director posted publicly that her crew restored generation before the evening peak after the grid fault, with the utility calling it their fastest recovery on record. Toast on that signal; it leads.
2. "Status: turbine 9 gearbox" — the specialist crew rebooked to next week and she has already derated the unit to 70%. Not a status question: a Tie-up on the specialists, her derate is the remedy to back, the move a confirmed visit.
3. "Coaching: short with the new apprentices" — one dated exchange Friday, after two harness kits went out unchecked. A Scuff: recount it, ask once, no verdict.
4. "Status: service-log backlog" — 47 turbines on a 52 schedule and the backlog sits inside the gap, yet nothing says who owns the logging or whether it repeats. Thin evidence on a real question: an Eddy pinned to the five-turbine shortfall.
5. "she asked about the site-engineer rotation" — her own ask last month. Beacon; the grid-fault recovery is the signal to set beside what the rotation wants.
6. "personal — her relocation paperwork" — the paperwork is private and she has already told HR what she needs. Wrong floor: the relocation process, starting with confirming dates at HR; nothing gets written on it in this room.

Not drafted: the overdue crane-hire approval is the manager's own row. Figures run: 47 on a 52 schedule leaves 5 short, and the backlog question lives inside that gap.

# Marta Kowal with her manager — the evening-peak save and the hot gearbox

The crew serviced 47 turbines on a 52 schedule (5 short), and Monday gave the week its headline: generation restored before the evening peak after the grid fault — the utility called it their fastest recovery on record. Turbine 9's gearbox runs hot on a rebooked specialist crew — her 70% derate is already holding — and the crane-hire approval waiting on you is overdue.

## The order

**1. The evening-peak recovery — Toast**
Open on the site director's public words — a save the utility itself praised is the kind that carries beyond the site. Then ask how her crew sequenced the night; a recovery that fast is a method worth writing down before it fades.

**2. Turbine 9 gearbox — Tie-up**
The specialist crew is rebooked to next week and her 70% derate is holding meanwhile — the workaround is hers, what remains is yours. Pin the visit to a confirmed date and tell her plainly the derate is approved; she should not carry the gap quietly.

**3. Service-log backlog — Eddy**
Five short and the log entries sit unowned inside it — one question: "where do the service records actually pile up, and whose desk catches them?" If the same gap shows next week it earns its own item.

**4. Friday — Scuff**
A curt word after two unchecked harness kits went out — one dated moment in a week she otherwise ran clean. One gentle question covers it: "have the harness kits gone out checked since Friday?" A single moment never earns a verdict.

**5. Site-engineer rotation — Beacon**
- "The fault recovery is the clearest thing on your record this year — which parts of the engineer rotation does it already prove, and which does it leave open?"
- "You asked about the rotation yourself — where would you want it to take you?"

## Another room

Her relocation paperwork — private, and HR already has what they need; the step is confirming dates so the rota can plan around it. Nothing on it is drafted here.

## Ahead of the room

Approve the crane-hire extension — overdue, and the specialist visit she just rebooked leans on it. Bring it or set the day.

**Why the sheet reads this way.** The praise stays, anchored — the director named the save in public. The gearbox is a hold on an outside crew, not a status, and her derate is the remedy to back. "Short" is one dated instance after unchecked kits, so a Scuff rather than a Theme. The backlog is live but unowned, so it stays a question until the record says more. The rotation is hers and stands on the year's clearest signal. The relocation paperwork belongs to HR, not this room.
