# Worked example

## A worked week (invented — take the reasoning, never the facts)

The week, from the record: a courier-depot dispatcher, six years in. Board: the east-route van is off the road waiting on a part with two supplier chases unanswered; the client for the hospital contract emailed "your driver coverage this week was flawless"; a fuel-card audit sits NOT STARTED on the manager's list. Chat: she flagged the van Monday, escalated Wednesday, asked Friday about the depot-supervisor pathway. Draft agenda: "van situation; hospital contract; supervisor chat; fuel cards."

## Sorting the items

1. "van situation" — **Shown:** two supplier chases unanswered, her Monday flag, part still inbound. **Call:** **Blocked** — yours to chase; give her a real return date.
2. "hospital contract" — **Shown:** client email: "driver coverage this week was flawless". **Call:** **Recognition** — opens the meeting, in the client's words.
3. "supervisor chat" — **Shown:** she asked about the pathway Friday. **Call:** **Career** — she raised it; an honest picture of steps and timing.
4. "fuel cards" — **Shown:** the audit is your row, NOT STARTED. **Call:** yours — off her draft, onto your own list.

**Missed:** the fuel-card audit — your row. **Arithmetic:** on-time deliveries 97.1% against a 96% floor (+1.1).

# 1-on-1 brief: Sam Whitfield x his manager — Friday

**Context:** Six years dispatching; the hospital client wrote in unprompted.

**1. Hospital contract — Recognition, ~3 min**
The client wrote "coverage this week was flawless" — read it to him, ask what coverage trick he used Thursday.

**2. The van — Blocked, ~7 min**
Two supplier chases unanswered. Tell him when you will call and the return date you are holding them to.

**3. Supervisor pathway — Career, ~10 min**
He asked. Lay out the steps, the timing, and what he would need to show.

**4. Numbers — Status, ~3 min**
97.1% on-time against a 96% floor. One line and done.

**Left off:** the audit came off into Before the meeting; nothing else moved. **Before the meeting:** chase the supplier today; start the fuel-card audit before Friday close. **Why this order:** memory flattened the van into a "situation" and blurred a client letter into "contract" — the record restored both, and the audit was the item the draft never wrote down.
