# An invented week answered end to end — copy the form, never the facts

The workplace, people and numbers below are invented. What carries over is the shape: a record reading that walks the draft line by line in plain sentences, a title built from the week's signals, sections that print even when empty, questions pinned to named evidence. In a real reply every fact comes from the inputs supplied.

## The reply

## Reading the record

The manager supplied a draft agenda, an action log, one figure pair and this week's threads for a meeting still to come, so this is prep.

1. "something to celebrate" — real but unanchored until Monday: the centre director wrote in #ops that her timetable rebuild "absorbed the two instructor absences without a single cancelled class — that has never happened before a school break". Laurel, on those words, and it opens.
2. "Status: pool lane-line repairs" — the contractor's survey has no date and she has already roped off lane 5 and moved the squads. Not a status question — a Standstill; her workaround is the fix to back, and the move is a committed survey date out of the contractor.
3. "Coaching: tense with the duty manager" — one dated exchange, Saturday, after a double-booked hall sat unflagged for two days. One means one: a Slip, asked lightly, never a verdict.
4. "Status: lesson cancellation rate" — five cancelled lessons against 36 scheduled this week, but nothing says whether that repeats earlier weeks or who owns the follow-up. A legitimate ask with thin evidence stays on as a question: an Idle, pinned to the five misses.
5. "she asked about the aquatics coordinator role" — she raised it herself last week. Summit; the timetable save is the signal to set beside what the role asks for.
6. "note — her visa renewal" — the council's HR team owns it and the dates are private. Off-ramp: the HR renewal process, first step is confirming receipt; nothing on it is drafted here.

Missed by the draft: the chlorine supplier contract renewal, overdue, is the manager's own row — Your rows. The sums: 31 lessons delivered against 36 scheduled is 5 cancelled, and the five sit in the same week.

## On the table

**1. The timetable rebuild — Laurel**
The centre director's words to her, not a summary: "that has never happened before a school break". Then ask how she sequenced the cover — that method is the process worth keeping.

**2. Lane-line repairs — Standstill**
Waiting on a contractor survey with no date; lane 5 roped off and squads already moved by her. Your move: pin the contractor to a survey date this week, and confirm her lane plan holds if it slips again.

**3. Cancellations — Idle**
Five lessons against 36 — who makes the rebook calls, and do the same slots appear in earlier weeks? One question; a repeat pattern earns its own item next time.

**4. Saturday's exchange — Slip**
A sharp word after a double-booked hall sat unflagged two days — one dated instance in a week she otherwise carried. One light question: "has the hall flag been fixed since?" — never a verdict on a single moment.

**5. Aquatics coordinator — Summit**
- "The rebuild is the clearest thing on your record this quarter — which parts of the coordinator role does it already cover, and which does it leave open?"
- "You asked about the role yourself — what would you want it to open up?"

## Sent elsewhere

The visa renewal — the council's HR team owns it and the dates are private; first step is confirming the paperwork is received. Nothing on it is drafted here.

**Why it reads so.** The celebration stays and gains its anchor — the director named the save; the lane repair is a hold on a contractor, not a status, and her roping plan is the fix to back; "tense" is one dated instance after a two-day unflagged double-booking, so it stays a Slip and never becomes a Streak; cancellations are live but unowned, so they remain a question until the record says more; the coordinator role is hers and stands on the week's clearest signal; the visa sits with HR, not this room.
