# Three invented answers — copy the form, never the facts

Every workplace, person and number below is made up. What carries over is the shape: a sizing that walks the draft line by line in plain sentences, a title built from the week's signals, sections that print even when empty, questions pinned to named evidence. In a real reply every fact comes from the inputs supplied.

## Case one: a full week

Tomas Eriksen, ground-crew lead at a regional airport, three years in. The manager's draft: "credit for the turnaround push" / "Status: de-icing rig 2" / "Coaching: clipped with the dispatcher again" / "Status: baggage misroutes" / "Development: he keeps asking about duty officer" / "fyi — his mother's surgery". The week's record: action log shows "post the winter turnaround rota (Tomas, DONE Monday)" and "sign the ground-handling audit response (Manager, OVERDUE)". Figures: 38 turnarounds cleared against a target of 34. Threads: Tuesday, the ops director in #ramp — "Tomas's crew cleared the backlog after the fog delay in under four hours; fastest recovery we've logged this winter." Wednesday, Tomas in #equipment — "De-icing rig 2 still grounded on the hydraulic part, supplier says maybe Friday. I've split its routes across rigs 1 and 3 meanwhile." The shift log notes one sharp exchange with the dispatcher on Friday, after two unacknowledged slot changes. Last week Tomas asked the manager whether the duty officer posting was still coming. His mother's surgery date is private; occupational health handles leave.

## The reply

## Sizing the week

The manager supplied a draft agenda, an action log, one figure pair and this week's threads for a meeting still to come, so this is prep.

1. "credit for the turnaround push" — vague until Tuesday: the ops director wrote in #ramp that his crew "cleared the backlog after the fog delay in under four hours; fastest recovery we've logged this winter". Salute, on those words, and it opens.
2. "Status: de-icing rig 2" — the supplier gives maybe Friday and he has already split the routes across rigs 1 and 3. Not a status question — a Logjam, and his split is the fix to back: the move is a committed delivery date out of the supplier.
3. "Coaching: clipped with the dispatcher again" — one dated exchange, Friday, after two unacknowledged slot changes. One means one: a Slip-up, asked lightly, never a verdict.
4. "Status: baggage misroutes" — six misroutes logged this week, but nothing says whether that repeats earlier weeks or who owns tracing. A legitimate ask with thin evidence stays on as a question: a Ticker, pinned to the six.
5. "Development: he keeps asking about duty officer" — he raised it himself last week. Ladder; the fog recovery is the signal to set beside what the posting asks for.
6. "fyi — his mother's surgery" — private, and occupational health owns the leave. Another door: the OH process, first step is the welfare check-in; nothing on it is drafted here.

Left out: the ground-handling audit response, unsigned and overdue, is the manager's own row — Owes to you. Number work: 38 turnarounds against a 34 target is 4 over, up 12%; the six misroutes are the open figure.

# Tomas Eriksen x his manager — the fog recovery and the grounded rig

**The lay of it.** Turnarounds ran 38 against a 34 target (4 over, up 12%), capped by a fog-delay recovery the ops director called the fastest logged this winter. Rig 2 stays grounded on a hydraulic part with maybe-Friday delivery; he has already split its routes. Your audit response is unsigned and overdue.

**Owes to you.** Sign the ground-handling audit response — overdue; it gates the compliance filing Tomas is scheduling around. Bring it or set the day.

## Running order

**1. The fog recovery — Salute**
The ops director's words to him, not a summary: "fastest recovery we've logged this winter". Then ask what he changed in the sequencing to clear it in four — that method is the playbook worth writing down.

**2. Rig 2 — Logjam**
Grounded on a hydraulic part, maybe Friday, six routes already split across rigs 1 and 3 by him. Your move: get a committed delivery date out of the supplier this week, and confirm the split holds if Friday slips.

**3. Misroutes — Ticker**
Six this week — who owns the tracing calls, and do the same flights appear in earlier weeks? One question; a repeat pattern earns its own item next time.

**4. Friday's exchange — Slip-up**
A sharp word after two slot changes went unacknowledged — one dated instance in a week he otherwise ran clean. One light question: "has the slot protocol been sorted since?" — never a verdict on a single moment.

**5. Duty officer — Ladder**
- "The fog recovery is the strongest thing on your record this quarter — which parts of the duty officer role does it already cover, and which does it leave open?"
- "You asked about the posting yourself — what would you want it to lead to?"

## Reassigned

His mother's surgery — the dates are private and occupational health owns the leave; first step is the welfare check-in OH already offers. Nothing on it is drafted here.

**The audit trail.** The credit stays and gains its anchor — the ops director named the save; the rig is a hold on a supplier, not a status, and his route split is the fix to back; "clipped" is one dated instance after two ignored slot changes, so it stays a Slip-up and never becomes a Rerun; misroutes are live but unowned, so they remain a question until the record says more; the posting is his and stands on the week's clearest signal; the surgery sits with OH, not this room.

## Case two: a bare draft

## The reply

## Sizing the week

Left out: no log supplied, so nothing is owed by the manager. Number work: none can be worked — no numbers came with the draft.
