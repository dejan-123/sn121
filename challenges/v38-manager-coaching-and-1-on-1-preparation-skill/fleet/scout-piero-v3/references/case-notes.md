# Case notes

Invented packets worked end to end. Learn the moves; never carry
the people, firms, or phrasing into a real answer.

## Packet A — a "low-priority" note that isn't

Input: manager of Ivo Marchetti, Release Coordinator at Halden
Aerospace Supply, lists "certification pipeline status," "quick
point (low): Ivo slow on approvals," "a positive mention,"
"his growth if time." Board: Ivo's batch-traceability report
DONE Wednesday; manager's delegated-approver memo NOT SENT;
five batches held awaiting Ivo's sole sign-off while three
lines depend on them. This week 9 releases shipped versus 7
before. Line lead Osei posted that Ivo's traceability check
caught a misstamped lot before it left. Ivo's own ask: "Can
I spend time on the supplier-qualification side?"

The page:

# Ivo Marchetti — forward note
Release Coordinator, Halden Aerospace Supply

**Where it stands.** Releases are ahead — 9 shipped against 7
last week (+2, ~29%). Five batches wait on one person's
sign-off across three lines, and Ivo has already designed the
fix. The manager's delegated-approver memo is still unsent;
the "slow on approvals" note belongs on the design, not on
Ivo.

**1. Worth naming — the misstamped lot.** Osei's post credits
Ivo's traceability check with stopping a misstamped lot
pre-shipment. Say what it caught and what it saved — this is
the real positive the manager remembered.

**2. Approval load — raised from "low."** Three lines, one
signer. "Your stand-in idea is the right one. Which lines go
first, and what authority do you need?" The unsigned memo is
the manager's deliverable, not Ivo's pace problem.

**3. Certification pipeline — status.** "What moved this week,
and what's the next thing waiting?"

**4. Supplier qualification — his ask.** "What pulls you
toward that side — the audit work or the supplier contact?"
Real time now; formal moves come from a separate process.

**Manager to-dos.** Send the delegated-approver memo, approve
the pilot handoff, pull the qualification-path criteria.

## Packet B — thin week, honest page

Manager of Nia Duskova, Network Technician at Ferrow Utilities,
writes only: "status on the grid-sensor rollout," "say
something nice," "she raised money and her path." No figures,
no log, no praise source.

# Nia Duskova — forward note
Network Technician, Ferrow Utilities

**Where it stands.** The packet carries no current state —
no rollout position, no completed work to name, no threads.
Open the tracker before the meeting.

**1. Sensor rollout — status.** "Where does the rollout
stand this week, and what do you need from me next?" A fair
question; the record cannot support a verdict.

**2. Her path — kept in the room.** She raised it. "What
kind of work do you want more of as you grow?" Her direction
gets time even when the file is thin; no level promises
without criteria.

**Other rooms.** No sourced result, so nothing opens the
page — find a real one first. Pay is a separate
compensation conversation owned by the manager; nothing
here gets scripted about it.

## Packet C — minutes, not a week

A manager pastes last Tuesday's 1-on-1 minutes for tech lead
Bruno Aldis — release-slip review, manager owed a capacity
quote by Thursday, Bruno owed a rollback plan by Friday, a
family care note — and says "prep this week."

No page gets written. Reply:

These are the minutes of the meeting already held — they
record what was agreed then, not what has happened since.
Two commitments were noted: your capacity quote Thursday
and Bruno's rollback plan Friday. Whether either landed,
these notes can't say.

Send current states, this week's figures or messages, and
any new topics, and I'll write the page from live material.
The family note stays out of prepared content.
