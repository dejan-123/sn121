# Harbor drills — full sheets on invented manifests

Four invented manifests, berthed the way the log should berth
them. The names, figures and products are invented; the berths are
the part to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal manifest with a mis-berthed vessel

Manifest: report is "Priya, senior data engineer". Manager's
draft: (a) "attitude — Priya argued with the analyst team in the
shared channel", (b) "why is the customer-360 pipeline behind",
(c) "mention the cost dashboard going live". The record: the
analyst lead posted a public thank-you naming Priya for catching
a schema break before it hit their dashboards; the channel
exchange shows Priya objecting — bluntly but on the merits — to a
table change that broke her pipeline's contract; the pipeline is
4 of 9 feeds migrated, waiting on an access approval requested
11 days ago; the cost dashboard shipped and the ops lead
messaged that it cut their monthly billing review from a day to
an hour.

Berths:

- Vessel (a): **Re-berthed**. One substantive objection to a
  change that broke a documented contract — manifested as an
  attitude issue, berthed as a one-time note with one narrow
  question. Harbor note: "The analyst exchange was on the merits
  — they were already thanking you for the schema catch. One
  thing to keep in view: that disagreement ran in the shared
  channel rather than on the ticket. Does moving it there cost
  you anything?" The objection stands; the channel takes one
  question and no more.
- Vessel (b): **Re-berthed — another port's cargo.** 4 of 9
  migrated, and the stall is an 11-day-old approval with the
  access team, not Priya's pace. Harbor note: "The pipeline is
  waiting on the access approval — eleven days outstanding. I'll
  chase the approver today and get a date; who should I escalate
  to if it slips again?"
- Vessel (c): **Docked first.** Two attributable credits — the
  analyst lead's public thanks and the ops figure. Open with
  them before any process vessel; credit berths first.
- Every manifested vessel gets a log line and a berth; nothing
  manifested disappears.

Sheet: `# Harbor sheet: Priya, senior data engineer` — picture
line ("a strong week with one vessel waiting on another port"),
figures shown as work, then berth order with all three
manifested items, the entry behind each, and each berth — then
harbor notes, port master's desk, transferred list, harbor
master's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin manifest berthed honestly

Manifest: report is "Tomas, staff engineer". Manager's note:
"prep my weekly with Tomas — want to discuss his promotion
timeline, and whether he can take over the search rebuild." No
action log, no figures, no channel excerpts. One calendar item:
Tomas asked in passing about equity bands for the next level.

Thin is not invalid: the manifest aims at the coming meeting, so
the harbor still logs it — berthed vessel by vessel. Only the
wrong kind of manifest (drill 3) is turned back whole. The reply
opens `# Harbor sheet: Tomas, staff engineer` — never a
turn-back preface.

Berths:

- Promotion timeline: **Docked — split by port.** Tomas's
  direction and the manager's readiness question belong in this
  meeting, with real time: "You asked about the next level —
  here's the gap evidence I see and what I'd need documented;
  what scope would you take to build it?" The equity question is
  **Transferred**: bands are decided in the compensation review
  — the sheet names the destination (the comp review with the
  comp partner) and the owner, one line, no harbor notes.
- Readiness for the search rebuild: **Anchored**. The manifest
  has no delivery record to weigh. It berths as the honest
  question, not an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't
  have your recent delivery record in front of me — walk me
  through the last few weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what
  you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the sheet. A thin sheet of
  honest questions beats a padded one; the sheet head logs what
  the manifest lacks and the port master's desk lists what to
  pull.

## Drill 3 — a manifest that is not this week's tide

Manifest: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with
Grace — please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes
list three actions: Grace to circulate the API deprecation plan
(due Friday), the manager to confirm the conference budget, a
follow-up on the on-call rota change.

Berth: **whole manifest turned back — nothing logged.** The
reply, under 150 words, titled `# Not this week's tide`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was agreed,
not what has moved since. The document this supports is a
follow-up on the open actions, not a sheet: Grace's deprecation
plan (due Friday), your budget confirmation, the rota follow-up —
owners and dates exactly as written. For a weekly sheet, send
this week's action log, any figures, and whatever Grace raised
or you want addressed. Nothing in the material is logged after
that meeting, so a sheet would replay old entries or invent new
ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not repeated,
not summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own vessel

Manifest: report is "Aisha, senior backend engineer". Manager's
draft: (a) "push Aisha to clear her review backlog — six spec
docs are waiting on her and three services are blocked";
(b) "Aisha seems disengaged in reviews lately". The record: the
six docs are assigned because the process names her sole
approver for cross-service changes; she shipped three features
this sprint and raised the queue herself in the last retro,
proposing a rotation; "disengaged" appears once, in the
manager's note.

Berths:

- Vessel (a): **Re-berthed — the port that can berth it is the
  manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling three services is a
  capacity matter the manager owns, not a vessel told to sail
  faster. It berths early as the manager's own decision: "Your
  rotation proposal is the right starting point — I want to
  approve it this week and take it to the service leads. What
  load should each reviewer hold so nothing queues behind one
  person again?"
- Vessel (b): **Refused.** One manager observation with no
  entries behind it; the sprint shows three shipped features and
  a raised process fix. The sheet logs its absence plainly
  rather than inventing an engagement segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Aisha's retro proposal
  — is listed under the port master's desk as the manager's
  commitment, with whom and when.

## What every drill shares

- The record decides; the manifest is only the filing.
- Attributable credit berths first — a named person, a concrete
  result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its
  substance and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another port berths is logged to that port.
- One occurrence is a single entry, never a pattern.
- Missing records berth as `Anchored` — the honest question in
  the slot — never covered over.
- Other-port vessels get a destination and an owner, never
  harbor notes.
- Every figure shows its own working; every manifested vessel
  gets a log line and a berth.
- A thin manifest still gets a sheet. The turn-back is for the
  wrong kind of manifest only — it stays under 150 words and
  carries no harbor structure.
