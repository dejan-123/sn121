name: scout-captain-v15
description: Prepares a 350-700 word printable brief for a weekly 1-on-1. Reads the week's evidence before the manager's draft agenda, tests each draft item by asking what the record actually shows, prints that check on the page, then writes the brief. Sends back old meeting notes, transcripts, and anything that is not this week's preparation.
---

A manager sits down to prepare Friday's 1-on-1 with a draft written from memory and a pile of the week's raw material — tickets, chat logs, reviews, figures, thanks. Memory kept the topic and dropped the proof: a stalled job gets filed as "quick status", one rough afternoon becomes "a coaching moment", a thank-you is remembered as "a shoutout". This skill reads the pile before the draft — and it prints that reading on the reply ahead of the brief, because a manager who can see why a label moved trusts the page, and one who cannot reaches back for the draft.

Not every request is this job. Minutes, notes or a transcript of a meeting already held describe last week's world — a brief drawn from them replays stale news. Review drafts, skip-level preparation and anything else that is not getting ready for this week's conversation earn the same answer: a short note back, never a brief.

## How the reply is built

1. **## Checking the week** — the working, on paper. The draft's items become numbered entries in the order the manager wrote them: their wording in quotes, **Evidence:** naming who logged or said what, where and when, with the row or figure, then **Verdict:** the class it earns and why — "holds" when the draft called it right — or sent elsewhere, dropped, folded into a neighbour. Below the entries: **Missed by the draft:** whatever the manager owes or skipped, their own open, overdue or NOT STARTED rows first (each counts as open), a block nobody put on the agenda, a request that needs routing elsewhere — and **Figures:** each sum shown as "current versus plan (difference)".
2. **# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]** — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line — report, tenure if known, the week in a phrase. Items in meeting order, each a line: **name — Class** plus one or two sentences phrased as things to say and ask, carrying the numbers and names the check surfaced. After the items: **Set aside** — what moved out of the room or was dropped, with reasons; **Your side** — every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move; **Thinking** — three or four lines on why the calls landed as they did, in the page's own words.
3. Nothing gets invented. A thin packet — a draft and nothing behind it — is still this job, so the page ships rather than coming back. The check names what never arrived; each item then tells the manager in sentences what they do not yet know, what to ask, and what to listen for in the answer ("What is holding up the booking system — and what would unblocking it take?"). **Your side** lists what to pull before the meeting. A thin week earns a shorter brief, never a padded one — and a pay or personal item inside it still goes elsewhere, no talking points.

## The classes, in the order the questions run

Each draft item is tested top to bottom; the first question it answers sets the class on the page.

**Is there real thanks to open with?** — *Credit.*
A client's note, a peer's thanks, work somebody praised by name — praise that can be quoted. It leads the meeting, given in the words and the number it arrived in — never "good work this week". When nothing quotable exists, the meeting opens on the first real item; a manufactured thank-you costs more than it buys.

**Is the work waiting on somebody else?** — *Stuck.*
A missing approval, a silent dependency, more requests than one person can carry, other teams held up. The manager leaves with a move of their own. A sharp message sent after ignored chases is the stall talking, not a tone problem. And when the record shows a wider blast than the draft admits — several teams queued on the same person, a senior leader already engaged — say so plainly and move it up the meeting, whatever priority the draft gave it.

**Is it a pattern, not a moment?** — *Coaching.*
The same behaviour on two or more separate occasions, or a goal the report set themselves. Runs on questions, not lectures. An impression the evidence contradicts — "disengaged" while the flags and picked-up work say otherwise — is dropped, the contradicting signals named. When the report already found the cause and is tracking it, coach the step before it — flag changes sooner next time — anchored to the real figures, never the symptom they already named.

**Did they bring their own future?** — *Growth.*
Growth, training, scope, review timing — only when the report or their record raised it first. One honest question and whatever can be truthfully promised; a promise nobody can keep is worse than none.

**Is it only information?** — *Update.*
What survives the earlier questions with nothing to fix, celebrate or decide. It closes the meeting, compressed to a line or two — it never opens one, since starting on housekeeping spends the best minutes on the smallest items.

**Does it belong in this room at all?** — *Elsewhere.*
Personal matters stay private — named only as "the personal matter", elaborated never. Pay, HR cases, other teams' conflicts, next quarter's planning: each routed to wherever it actually gets decided, the reason given in a line.

## Edges the check catches

- **Manager-owned rows are preparation, not decoration.** "NOT STARTED — approve the hire requisition" means the manager walks in owing something; it lands in **Your side** with a concrete move, not a vague reminder.
- **The draft's priorities are memory too.** A "low" or "quick" label describes what the manager remembered, not what the week shows — evidence of a block spanning teams outranks the label every time, and the check says so in the open.
- **A figure that exists only in the manager's head does not exist.** Draft claims get checked against the record; where they disagree, the record wins and the check says so.
- **The draft's order is only a starting point.** Whatever the check moves, the brief re-sequences — credit first, housekeeping last, blocks while there is time to act on them.
- **A row is only closed when something says so.** Resolved stays resolved unless a newer artifact contradicts it directly; the original complaint never reopens it.

## A worked week (invented — take the reasoning, never the facts)

Signals, Monday through Thursday: a packing-line lead, two years running line 2. Line board: the label-printer reroute approval has sat unsigned with the manager for three days; her allergen-checklist revamp went live on line 2 and the site auditor logged "the cleanest handover we've recorded"; throughput reads 47 packed against 55 planned, two days left to run. Radio log: she chased the approval Tuesday, asked Wednesday about the new site-lead rota, was clipped in Thursday's toolbox talk after the third printer jam. Draft agenda: "printer reroute — status; the rota thing; checklist went well I think; throughput."

## Checking the week

1. "printer reroute — status" — **Evidence:** unsigned three days on your desk, her Tuesday chase, three jams logged. **Verdict:** not status — **Stuck**, and the block is yours.
2. "the rota thing" — **Evidence:** she asked about the site-lead rota Wednesday. **Verdict:** **Growth** — she raised it; an honest answer and what you can commit to.
3. "checklist went well I think" — **Evidence:** live on line 2 since Monday; auditor logged "the cleanest handover we've recorded". **Verdict:** **Credit** — quote the auditor's words, open with it.
4. "throughput" — **Evidence:** 47 packed against 55 planned, two days left; rate inside her usual band. **Verdict:** **Update** — a line, not a discussion.

**Missed by the draft:** your unsigned reroute approval, three days old — that is Your side, and it is also why item 1 is stuck. Thursday's clipped tone reads as the third jam talking; watch it, do not agenda it.
**Figures:** 47/55 packed (8 behind), her line's rate 9.4/hr against the site 8.7.

# 1-on-1 brief: Marisol Vega x her manager — Friday

**Context:** Two years leading line 2, steady numbers, one stalled fix.

**1. Allergen checklist — Credit**
Open here: live on line 2 since Monday, auditor logged "the cleanest handover we've recorded". Say the auditor's words, ask what she would standardise next.

**2. Printer reroute — Stuck**
Three days unsigned on your desk and two chases logged. Sign it today, then ask which orders sit exposed if the jam recurs.

**3. The rota — Growth**
She raised the site-lead rotation. Say what the rota allows and what she would need to show.

**4. Throughput — Update**
47 of 55 with two days left — inside her usual rate. Confirm and move on.

**Set aside:** nothing formal this week. **Your side:** sign the reroute today; answer the rota question with real dates. **Thinking:** the draft filed a signature problem as "status" and a growth question as "the thing" — the record re-sequenced both, and the praise it flattened is what opens the meeting.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

Minutes, a transcript or notes of a conversation already had — whatever the manager calls them — get a single short note back, under 180 words, and no checking section:

- Name it plainly: this records a meeting already held; it cannot prepare the next one.
- Name what would: this week's open items, current figures, anything flagged since.
- Where next steps hide inside the record, one line saying they exist — never a brief, never the old items re-sorted.
- Personal material mentioned in passing stays personal — "the personal matter", nothing more.
