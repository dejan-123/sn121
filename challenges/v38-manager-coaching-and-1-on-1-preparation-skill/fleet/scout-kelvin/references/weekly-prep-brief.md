ask that gets the bounce — then the entries that flip when the record
changes. Every name, team, and figure is invented.

# 1-on-1 brief: Tobias Ferro, Support Engineer, Tier 2

**This week:** Ahead of average on volume — 41 resolved against a
38-ticket trailing mean — with 11 parked tickets all charged to one
upstream counterparty, the Engineering bugfix queue. His runbook draft
landed four hours before this meeting. The coaching entry on the draft
does not balance: the record shows his escalations were real bugs,
confirmed in writing.

**Order:** The escalations item leads — it is both the week's real
question and the entry that clears his name. Backlog status follows;
his own runbook closes.

## Agenda

### 1. Escalations to Engineering · Status
Manager's label: "coaching — escalates instead of working the queue" →
Status: Ines's written record says the escalations were the correct
call — real bugs, repro cases attached, Engineering confirming.
The narrow surviving question is format, not behavior: "Your escalations
are landing as real bugs — is the current path the right one, or should
we batch them differently?" Nothing about tone belongs here; posting a
coaching item on this record would charge him for doing his job.

### 2. Ticket backlog · Status
Manager's label: "backlog status", kept.
41 resolved against a 38 average — volume is fine. The open liability is
the 11 parked tickets, all blocked on ENG-4419 upstream, not user error.
"What is a realistic clearing date on ENG-4419, and should the batch go
up as one escalation rather than ticket-by-ticket?" The chase is the
manager's side of the ledger, not his.

### 3. Runbook refresh · Status
Manager's label: not filed → Added: his Thursday 09:00 post, four hours
before this meeting.
"You filed the runbook this morning — anything you want walked through
in this session, or is it posted for review only?"

## Parking lot
- Career note "if there's time": no entry behind it — his own submitted
  topic reads "Nothing this week." An unfilled row stays unfilled; if a
  real topic surfaces later it earns its own session.
- Night-shift headcount request: the manager's overdue item to the
  director — on the manager's side of the ledger, out of this meeting's
  scope.

No recognition opener: Ines's DM corroborates competence but names no
discrete achievement — the honest positive is the queue volume itself,
carried in the header, not staged as a ceremony.

## A third shape — the input is a record of a meeting already held

Sometimes what arrives is last week's notes — "use this to prep the next
one." However the manager frames it, that input cannot post as this
week's entry: notes are a closed ledger, not an open one. The correct
render is the bounce, under ~180 words:

# Not a 1-on-1 prep input

This is a record of last Thursday's session — what was discussed and
the actions agreed — not this week's signals. A brief posted against it
would prep a meeting that already happened: settled rows charged again,
everything that moved since invisible.

What would make next week's brief post: the open action items and their
states, the week's queue or ticket figures, any threads worth knowing
about, and the manager's own topics for the session.

From the notes, the open entries to carry forward: the manager's
escalation draft — due two days before the meeting; the report's
coverage proposal — due the day before. And one entry to hold off every
ledger in this brief: a family situation that may need December
flexibility — a support conversation, not a posted item.

## Entries that flip when the record changes

Same first week, alternate postings:

- **If Ines's DM had instead read** "his escalations skip the checklist —
  three went up without a repro case," the item stands as Coaching:
  signal-borne, pattern-level, anchored to the DM. The question changes
  shape: "Walk me through the last escalation — what did the checklist
  miss?"
- **If the 11 parked tickets were user-error parked to age out**, the
  backlog item carries as delinquent, not blocked — and a coaching entry
  posts as queue hygiene, anchored to the ticket list.
- **If a second signal had shown a public sharp exchange** — escalations
  still correct, but a posted message reading "Engineering ignores
  these" in a shared channel — a narrow venue-coaching item opens:
  correct escalation, wrong channel. The behavior is right; the venue
  is what gets coached.
- **If his submission had named a certification topic**, the career item
  posts for real — his own filed entry earns the session's closing
  slot, not the manager's "if there's time" framing.
- **If an item had posted low while carrying real weight** — a "quick
  question" whose dependency three other teams wait on: filed priority
  is itself a claim. Re-weight it in the order, with the waiting
  parties named.
- **If the report had flagged the pattern himself** — a coaching item
  where his own DM names the failure and its cause: it posts as a
  process question, not a lecture — "what catches the next one
  early?" — carried against his own entry.
- **If the manager's own row caused the miss** — an overdue deliverable
  on the manager's side that would have prevented the report's flag:
  the charge posts to the manager's side of the ledger, named plainly,
  never debited to the report.

The ledger's rule across all branches: every item posts against a named
entry — a thread, a ticket, a figure, a submission. An item without an
entry stays open; it never gets filled in from the manager's hunch.
