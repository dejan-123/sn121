# Ledger — post every claim, weigh the balance, state the standing

This skill treats the input as a ledger. Every item is posted twice: once as
the claim, once as the counterweight (what the record actually supports).
The standing of each row falls out of the balance — never from how the item
sounds. The output is a ledger a reviewer can re-foot: every item posted,
every standing traceable, the tally reconciling to the row count.

## Post 0 — Is this input a ledger entry at all?

Before any claim is posted, test whether the input itself belongs on
these books. Three arrivals cannot be posted as prep:

- **Only a record of a meeting already held** — minutes, notes, a
  transcript. It posts against last week's books, not this one's: a
  brief built on it charges settled topics again and misses everything
  that moved since. (Notes arriving beside real signals are fine — the
  signals are the entries, the notes are context.)
- **Prep addressed to a different account** — a skip-level, a panel,
  a review the manager must write. Wrong books, wrong brief.
- **Review paperwork** — a rating draft, a calibration write-up. A
  different instrument entirely.

Each gets a bounce, not a brief: under ~180 words, headed
`# Not a 1-on-1 prep input` — what the input actually is and the entry that
shows it, why a brief posted against it would mislead, what to send
instead (this week's signals — open action items, the week's figures,
threads, the manager's own topics), and any open actions the input does
carry, posted with the owners and dates it gives. Nothing else — no
agenda, no talking points, no transfers.

## Posting rules

**Post 1 — The context line.** Write the anchor before any row: the
report, the meeting date, what the brief must answer. Items mean nothing
without the books they post against — a loud item the books do not depend
on is still a memo line, and a quiet item the books cannot balance without
is the whole account.

**Post 2 — Whose.** Every claim has an owner. The subject's own channel is
a claim, not a confirmation — it posts as "self-reported" and asks for
corroboration. An independent primary record posts heavier than any retold
version. Anonymous, unattributed, or aggregated posts carry no weight on
any topic. A name one letter off is a different party until proven
otherwise — the identity check is itself a posting.

**Post 3 — Standing.** Before weighing, check whether the row is already
posted closed. A row the record marks settled STAYS settled — unless
ANOTHER artifact posts a present fact it cannot balance with (a process
still running, a notice already served, a cure nobody can document). The
row's own claim text never reopens it: those words are what the closer
already weighed. Suspicion does not post; only a contradicting artifact
does. When a later artifact and the posted status genuinely disagree, book
the dispute in writing ("posted settled; the record shows X; carried as
open") — never re-classify silently.

*What this post forecloses:* if every row is settled, nothing downstream
can legitimately reopen the books — and forcing one open anyway is its own
mis-posting.

**Post 4 — Balance.** Each open row gets its counterweight from the record
alone. Two artifacts that disagree are posted as disagreement with the
tiebreaker named — never averaged into a comfortable middle. A balance the
record cannot strike is carried open with the named record that would
strike it.

## Which balances can hold the account

Only rows the input itself entered — filed by the manager's draft agenda
or the report's own submission, never invented by the ledger — can hold
the account, and only while unreconciled. An
unreconciled balance on a lower tier never holds anything: it posts with a
named disposition — the record, the owner, the timing — and the books
still close. A blocker = a filed top-tier row AND an open balance; nothing
else qualifies, however large the amount reads. Size and tier are different
ledgers.

## The figures ledger

Post every number the same way — claim versus what the record supports:

- **Derived entries, not raw claims.** The input's raw figures are the
  claim; point changes, completion rates, blocked counts and scope deltas
  are the postings owed back. Each posts as a journal entry: the reported
  figures, the operation, the derived result.
- **Blocked versus delinquent.** An overdue row held by an upstream
  dependency posts as blocked, never as neglect — the dependency charges
  to the manager's side of the ledger, not the report's.
- **Due dates posted against the meeting date.** A deadline is an interval,
  not a label — the gap to the meeting is computed, never copied.
- **Every pair posted.** Before the header is drafted, every comparable
  pair the input offers gets its own line — each raw figure against the
  figure it belongs to — so no derivable entry is left unposted.
- **Reconciliation.** Every figure is written expanded —
  `3 of 8 = 37.5%`, `61 → 74 = +13 (+21%)` — and re-footed from the input
  alone. A number that cannot be re-derived does not post.

## The standing

Every entered row carries exactly one standing — four postings, never
fewer than the entries allow:

- **Posted**: the entries carry the row as filed — it posts with its
  anchored question. When the ledger allows it, the row stands — demoting
  it is as wrong as carrying over an open balance.
- **Posted-with-adjustments**: the row stands but re-classed — a coaching
  entry the signals soften to a question, a status row that is really a
  charge to the manager's side. Keep this middle posting; do not collapse
  it into either neighbor.
- **Transferred**: a real balance booked to another account — a sensitive
  topic, a dedicated conversation, an escalation the manager owns. Named
  venue, never talking points.
- **Written-off**: no entry warrants the row — the recognition with no
  signal behind it, the padding topic. Its absence posts plainly.

A condition is a named adjusting entry: which record clears it, who posts
it, by when — and the restatement that follows if it does not arrive.
"Follow up" is not an entry.

## The reply

Two renders only — the brief or the bounce, never a hybrid.

**The brief** — 350 to 700 words, the page a manager carries in:

- `# 1-on-1 brief: <report name>, <role>`
- `**This week:**` two to four sentences stating the week's balance —
  what matters most and why. Figures post with their working (`94% SLA
  against 89% last month`, `2 of 3 actions done; the overdue one is the
  manager's`), every comparison showing both operands. When a class of
  entry is absent, say which ("no ticket figures or threads this week").
- `**Order:**` one sentence on why the items run as they do — an earned
  recognition first, then what blocks work or needs the manager's call,
  feedback and coaching with real time, quick closes last.
- `## Agenda` — numbered items `### 1. <short topic> · <Type>`, the type
  drawn from Recognition / Status / Feedback / Coaching / Career — the
  balance's call, not the label's. Under each heading, one line:
  `Manager's label: <their word>, kept` or `Manager's label: <their
  word> → <Type>: <the entry that decides it>` — or `Added: <entry>` for
  an item the record demands but the draft omits. Every entered item
  appears once: one merged into another keeps its own label line under
  the item that absorbed it. Then the talking points: questions only,
  each charged to a named entry — a thread, a ticket, a figure, a
  person — at most three sentences; statements and unanchored questions
  do not post. Status points name what the manager must decide or clear,
  with no deadline the input does not give.
- `## Parking lot` — one line per item transferred off the books: named
  neutrally, its destination account, its owner — zero talking points.
  Sensitive matters, the manager's own escalations, and any item no
  entry warrants land here; the last kind gets one clause on why it
  does not hold. `Nothing this week.` when empty.
- Recognition opens the brief only when an entry warrants it — a named
  credit, or `No recognition opener: <reason>` as the first line under
  `## Agenda`.
- Where the books are silent on a quantity, deadline, or owner, say so
  plainly — an unposted gap is a finding, never a plug figure.
- The brief is the ledger's render, not the ledger's vocabulary: prose
  and named lines a manager reads plainly — a reader sees a good brief,
  never the posting rules behind it, and nothing audits itself after
  the parking lot.
- Never reuse one sentence frame across items — each item gets the
  words its own facts need.
- A worked example lives in references/weekly-prep-brief.md — the brief
  written end-to-end on invented weeks: the item the label mis-posts,
  the sensitive item transferred, the thin week with its gaps named,
  and a notes-only ask answered with the bounce. Read it once with
  read_reference before writing; carry its shape and checks, never its
  sentences.
