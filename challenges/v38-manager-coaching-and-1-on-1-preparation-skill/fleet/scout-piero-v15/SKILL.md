# Strip board: clear the plan on conditions

A draft agenda records what the manager meant to cover. It does not prove what this week actually showed. Treat each proposed item like a filed movement: keep the manager's wording visible, test it against the action rows, figures, messages and the report's own request, then clear it under the call the record supports. The board shows that clearance on every strip, so a changed topic does not look arbitrary.

## First: is this the right document?

A packet is still this week's preparation even when it is only a draft and contains no supporting records. Thin evidence means honest questions and fetch steps, not a redirect. A request to prepare a coming weekly 1-on-1 always receives a board; never call it `not suitable`, `not a prep request`, or the wrong document merely because signals are missing.

The wrong document is different. Minutes, notes or a transcript of a 1-on-1 already held describe a conversation that is over, even when the manager asks for next week's preparation from them. A review draft, disciplinary record, skip-level request or preparation for someone else's meeting is also the wrong document. For those, return the redirect below and no board.

## What may appear on the board

Use only what the packet supplies: names, dates, owners, rows, figures, quoted messages, the manager's note and the report's submitted topic. If a needed fact is absent, name the absence and ask for the record that would settle it. Never fill the gap from a plausible guess.

Sort each material claim before writing:

- **confirmed**: the packet records it;
- **planned or attempted**: somebody says they will do it, tried to do it, or set a condition for doing it;
- **impression**: a belief with no dated event;
- **absent**: no input supplies it.

Only confirmed facts get completed verbs. A timestamped intention does not become a fact when its time passes: if the packet says the report planned to escalate at noon, write `planned to escalate` and ask whether it happened; never write `escalated`, `has escalated`, or `completed the escalation` without a later record. An impression is tested or left off; an absent fact is requested.

## Clearance rules

Every draft item receives one clearance. Its label does not choose the clearance; the record does.

- **Cleared** — the record supports the item as filed. Give it a grounded question or a manager move.
- **Re-routed** — the item is real but belongs under another conversation. A finished, praised result filed as status moves to earned credit. A stall at another team's door moves to the manager's unblock. One dated slip filed as a pattern moves to a one-time note. A repeated omission filed as a manner judgment moves to the actual repeated behavior.
- **Diverted** — the matter belongs outside the weekly meeting. Pay, health, family circumstances, accommodations, formal HR, a complaint owned elsewhere, and a level or promotion decision get a venue and owner, not talking points.
- **Grounded** — no signal supports the item: a vague shoutout, a manner judgment with no dated conduct, or a topic included only as filler. Its absence is visible.
- **Holding** — the item is live but the record cannot answer it yet. Keep it as a precise question and name what would settle it.

Count dated occasions before calling behavior a pattern. One owned incident is a one-time note. Two or more dated occurrences, or a skill the report chose, can carry development questions. A named prior sprint with concrete planned-versus-actual figures is a second dated occasion; an exact day is not required. If the report already found the cause, credit that and coach the earlier lever—what should have been flagged sooner, to whom and when—rather than repeating the symptom.

Work waiting on an approval, supplier, access, another team, an unmanageable assigned queue or a decision the report cannot make belongs to whoever can move it. An assigned queue or sole-reviewer design is the thing blocking other teams—write `the assigned queue blocks four teams`, never `the report is blocking four teams` or `the report is a blocker`. When the report proposes a rotation, escalation or repair, the board backs that proposal and gives the manager the next move: approve, broker, reassign, escalate or set a date. Several messages chasing the same hold are one dependency, not a conduct pattern.

Earned credit needs a named person, a concrete result or a specific artifact credited this period. Open with at most one such item. A manager's memory that the report "did something good" is not enough.

A report's own role, level, training, promotion packet or direction question receives protected time when the packet supports it. The report's unfinished packet step stays a question; the formal decision goes to its own venue. A career item created only by the manager remains a modest invitation.

## The carried board

Write `# Strip board: <report name>, <role>` and 350-700 words. It should read like a colleague prepared the manager, not like an audit form.

**Board head** — three short lines:

- the week in one sentence: what moved, what waits, and on whom;
- figures with their operands, such as `14 → 23 screens, +64%`, and `Action items: X of Y done (Z%)` when the log allows it;
- any missing record types the packet does not contain.

**Departure order** — number every filed plan, in meeting order:

- earned credit is strip 1 whenever the record supports it; an urgent dependency never moves it down;
- dependencies and manager-owned decisions;
- one-time feedback or development questions;
- the report's own direction or growth topic;
- live status and quick closes.

Number every draft item, including an item cleared as merged, diverted or grounded. A signal the draft missed may become an additional strip marked `not on the draft`, but it never replaces the filed item that must still be accounted for.

Each numbered strip keeps a line in this form:

`filed: <manager's draft item> → <clearance>: <the condition that earned it>`

A merged item remains visible under the strip that absorbed it. Diverted and grounded items keep their clearances. Do not silently drop a draft line.

Under each strip, use at most three sentences. Give the signal, the manager's move, or one quoted question the manager can say to the report. Attach it to a named thread, ticket, figure, date or the report's own words; a question that could be used for anyone is not finished. Where useful, say what to listen for.

Finish with:

- **Pre-flight** — every action-log row owned by the manager that is open, overdue or not started, plus the missing records to pull. Done manager rows are closed and do not appear. A dependency owned by another team is a manager-moving agenda item, not an action-log row; report-owned open rows may become questions but are not manager debts.
- **Diversions** — routed matters with destination and owner, or `Nothing diverted this week.`
- **Controller's log** — three to five natural sentences explaining which filings moved, what stayed, what had no evidence, and why the first strip earned the opening.

## Figures

Show the basis beside the result:

- change: `(current - baseline) / baseline`, signed;
- planned versus actual work or scope: the same comparison;
- rates and scores: percentage-point movement, not percent movement;
- action log: count every row, including manager and done rows;
- unrelated counts stay separate—reviews assigned and teams waiting are not a ratio.

A cause that explains part of a gap leaves the remainder open. Ask about what the cause does not explain instead of treating the gap as settled.

## Thin packet

A future-facing draft with no log, figures or messages still receives a board. This is mandatory preparation, not a redirect. The head lists what did not arrive. Each plan is `Holding`, `Diverted`, or another clearance its own wording supports. Ask what the record would show and who can provide it; no praise, pattern, completion or finding may be invented. A workload check is a neutral question, not a diagnosis. Pay moves to a pay conversation; a manager-created career check is an invitation.

## Redirect for a meeting already held

Return only a note under 180 words, no board and no working section. Use this shape:

`# <report name>, <role>: not a 1-on-1 prep request`

- `**Redirect.**` Name the held meeting and the detail that proves it—date, attendees, discussed items or agreed actions. Say a board from it would replay old positions or invent current ones.
- `**What it supports:**` list each recorded open action with owner and due date, manager-owned items first. Say their current state is not supplied.
- `**What the next board needs:**` current action status, this week's messages or figures, any new report topic, and a fresh draft agenda.
- `**Private matter:**` name a family, health, pay or personal item only in the record's minimal words and route it privately. Draft no talking points.

A live packet starts with `# Strip board:`; a wrong-document packet starts with the redirect title.

## Order of work

1. Read `references/board-drills.md` once with read_reference. Its workplaces are invented; carry the decisions and shapes, never the facts or wording.
2. In the working pass, identify the document kind, classify each filed plan, separate confirmed facts from plans or impressions, work the figures, and list manager-owned, report-owned, fetch and routed items.
3. Send only the board or the redirect. Do not mention the skill, tools or working pass.
