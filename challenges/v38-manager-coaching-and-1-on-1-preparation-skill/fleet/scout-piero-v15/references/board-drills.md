# Board drills — invented packets and replies

The workplaces below are fiction. Keep the way each plan is cleared, not the names, dates or figures.

## Drill 1 — a normal packet with wrong labels

Nadia Voss is the bookings coordinator at a city ferry terminal. Her manager supplies:

- Draft: "status on the accessibility ramp"; "coaching — abrupt with contractors"; "mention the school-ferry pilot"; "ask about the assistant-controller course"; "the invoice backlog."
- Action rows: ramp risk review (Nadia, IN PROGRESS); assistant-course nomination form (Manager, OVERDUE); school-pilot feedback sheet (Nadia, DONE Tuesday).
- Figures: 18 of 24 ramp inspections complete; invoice queue is 11 items against a usual 5.
- Monday, the harbourmaster wrote that Nadia's revised school boarding sequence cut the pier wait from 14 minutes to 9.
- Tuesday, the lift contractor said the ramp was waiting for a signed variation order the manager owns.
- Friday, Nadia objected in the project channel when the contractor proposed removing a handrail to save time; there is no second conduct event.
- Thursday, Nadia asked whether the assistant-controller course would help her qualify for weekend duty.

The reply:

# Strip board: Nadia Voss, Bookings Coordinator

A strong operational week is waiting on the manager's variation order. Ramp inspections are 18 of 24 complete; the invoice queue is 11 against a usual 5, up 120%. Action items: 1 of 3 done (33%). No separate contractor-conduct pattern is documented.

1. **School-ferry pilot — earned credit · ~4 min**
`filed: "mention the school-ferry pilot" → cleared: the harbourmaster credits Nadia's boarding sequence.`
Quote the 14-to-9-minute wait improvement and the revised sequence. Ask what part of the pilot should be written into the normal boarding checklist.

2. **Accessibility ramp — manager unblock · ~8 min**
`filed: "status on the accessibility ramp" → re-routed: the contractor is waiting on the manager's signed variation order.`
Give the signing date before asking about remaining inspections. "Once the variation is signed, which of the six remaining inspections can the contractor schedule first?"

3. **Contractor exchange — one-time note · ~4 min**
`filed: "coaching — abrupt with contractors" → re-routed: one dated objection to an unsafe proposal.`
Keep it to the channel and setting: Nadia's objection was on the substance. "Would moving the next safety disagreement into the ticket make the decision clearer?"

4. **Assistant-controller course — direction · ~7 min**
`filed: "ask about the assistant-controller course" → cleared: Nadia raised the course and its path to weekend duty.`
The nomination form is the manager's overdue row. "Which part of weekend duty do you want first — live dispatch, incident control or crew scheduling?"

5. **Invoice backlog — holding · ~5 min**
`filed: "the invoice backlog" → holding: the queue is high but the packet does not show ownership or a blocked step.`
"Which of the 11 invoices are waiting on us, and which are waiting on suppliers or the finance system?" Listen for a step the manager can clear rather than asking Nadia to work through an unowned queue.

**Pre-flight**
- Sign or date the ramp variation order; it controls the remaining inspections.
- Complete the overdue assistant-course nomination form before discussing the course.

**Diversions**
- Nothing diverted this week.

**Controller's log**
The pilot becomes the opener because the harbourmaster named the work and the result. The ramp is not a status check while the manager's signature is the hold. One safety objection does not make a contractor-conduct pattern. The invoice queue stays a question until ownership is known.

## Drill 2 — only a future draft arrived

Rafi Nordin, aquarium life-support technician, has a 1-on-1 coming up. The manager sends only:

1. "How is the pump migration going?"
2. "Check whether his workload is too heavy."
3. "He asked about standby pay."
4. "Career goals, if there is time."

No action log, figures or messages arrive.

The reply:

# Strip board: Rafi Nordin, Life-Support Technician

The packet is a future-facing draft with no action log, figures or messages. It is still mandatory preparation: the board holds questions rather than findings, and it is never redirected merely for missing evidence. Standby pay belongs in the pay process.

1. **Pump migration — holding · ~8 min**
`filed: "how is the pump migration going?" → holding: no task list or dated update.`
"What stage has the pump migration reached, and is anything waiting on a contractor, permit or my decision?" Listen for a dependency the manager can move rather than asking for a general progress speech.

2. **Workload — holding · ~7 min**
`filed: "check whether his workload is too heavy" → holding: no missed commitment or capacity signal.`
"What else is competing with the migration this week, and should anything move?" Accept a manageable answer; the purpose is to learn the position, not to prove overload.

3. **Career goals — invitation · ~6 min**
`filed: "career goals, if there is time" → holding: Rafi supplied no goal.`
"Is there a part of the life-support or migration work you want more of, or should we leave this until you have a specific direction?" Do not assign him a goal to satisfy the agenda.

**Pre-flight**
- Pull the pump-migration task list and any contractor thread.
- Check the rota before asking about workload.
- Invite Rafi to send a development topic if he wants one.

**Diversions**
- Standby pay: the pay conversation with the compensation owner; book it and draft no amount or argument here.

**Controller's log**
A thin draft still gets a board because the meeting is ahead. Each item remains a question until a record arrives. Pay leaves the weekly slot, and the career item stays Rafi's choice.

## Drill 3 — the pasted packet is a finished meeting

The manager asks to prepare next week's 1-on-1 for Mina Cho, dispatch supervisor, from notes titled "Mina Cho 1-on-1 — 2 October, attendees: manager, Mina." The notes record that the manager owes a new standup format by 9 October and a leave-cover decision by 6 October; Mina owes a revised late-shift rota by 10 October. They also mention a family matter and possible time away.

The reply:

# Mina Cho, Dispatch Supervisor: not a 1-on-1 prep request

**Redirect.** The paste is the record of the 2 October meeting already held; the attendees, discussed items and agreed actions identify it. A board from it would replay old positions or invent current ones.

**What it supports:** a follow-up on its actions:
- Manager: new standup format — due 9 October.
- Manager: leave-cover decision — due 6 October.
- Mina: revised late-shift rota — due 10 October.
Their current state is not supplied.

**What the next board needs:** the present state of those actions, this week's messages or figures, any topic Mina has raised, and a new draft agenda.

**Private matter:** the family matter stays in a private conversation with the appropriate support route; no talking points are drafted here.

## Drill 4 — the queue belongs to the manager

Elif Arslan, senior reviewer at a land-records office, is the only approver for cross-office title corrections. Seven correction files assigned to Elif are holding work for four offices. The draft says "give Elif feedback on the review bottleneck", "status on the deeds-system pilot" and "Elif seems quiet in standup lately." The record shows Elif flagged the queue in Monday's operations meeting and proposed a rotation among three senior reviewers; on Wednesday she wrote that she would raise it with the office heads by Friday if no decision came. There is no later message saying that she did. Separately, the deeds-system pilot is waiting for security to approve the data extract requested eight days ago. The manager also has an overdue row to publish the rotation rules.

The useful rulings:

- The bottleneck is **re-routed to a manager-owned unblock**. Seven assigned files and four waiting offices are a capacity design, not a reminder to Elif to review faster. The board backs her rotation proposal: approve it, take it to the office heads, or set a decision date.
- Her statement about Friday is **planned or attempted, not confirmed**. Ask, "Did the office-head conversation happen, and what decision did it leave?" Never write that she escalated.
- The deeds pilot is another manager-moving hold: the manager owns the security decision path and the escalation date.
- The overdue rotation-rules row belongs in **Pre-flight** before any ask reaches Elif.

Useful strips read:

**1. Title-correction review queue — manager unblock · ~10 min**
`filed: "give Elif feedback on the review bottleneck" → re-routed: a sole-approver queue is holding four offices.`
Elif already proposed a three-reviewer rotation. Approve it or name what must change, then ask: "Did the office-head conversation happen on Friday, and what decision does it leave for me?"

**2. Quiet in standup — grounded · ~1 min**
`filed: "Elif seems quiet in standup lately" → grounded: one manager impression and no dated event; the record shows shipped work and a raised fix.`
Name the missing evidence and move on; a grounded draft item still receives a strip so it does not disappear.

**Pre-flight**
- Publish the overdue rotation rules or bring a decision date.
- Chase the security extract approval for the deeds pilot and get a date.

## Boundaries that change the answer

- If the contractor had already signed Nadia's variation order, the ramp would not be a manager unblock; the remaining inspections would be a live status question.
- If Rafi's packet had contained a held meeting record, it would be redirected; a thin future draft is still preparation.
- If Elif had sent a message saying the office-head escalation happened, the board could state it; a promise to do it is only a plan.
- If Mina's note had been a draft for next week rather than minutes, it would receive a board even with no action rows.
- A manager note saying "last sprint's three-day task took five" supplies the second dated occurrence for a pattern even without a calendar day; the coachable lever is the earlier flag, not the miss the report already found.
