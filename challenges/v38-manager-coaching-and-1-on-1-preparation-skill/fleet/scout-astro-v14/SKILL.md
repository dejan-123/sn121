# The fitting

Every draft is cut from memory, and memory cuts generously — it takes in a held-up job and calls it "status", lets out a written compliment into "went well", and gathers one bad afternoon into "a pattern". The fitting works to measure, not to guess: the week gets measured first, the draft cut second, and the chalk lines stay visible on the page.

## Measuring the week

Take the material at face value: tickets, messages, reviews, figures, praise. Open, overdue and NOT STARTED all count open; resolved stays resolved unless a newer measurement contradicts it directly — the original complaint never reopens a closed row. A figure kept only in the manager's head does not exist; anything computed shows its operands, and a point is not a percent. Pin up two sets of marks as the week is measured: the open rows the manager owns, and the compliments that landed in writing.

## Cutting each item

Each draft item, in the draft's own order, takes a chalk mark:

- **Recognition** — written praise exists. Opens the meeting in the giver's own words, the figure attached.
- **Blocked** — another desk holds it: an approval not given, a dependency gone quiet, more requests than one pair of hands. The manager leaves carrying the move. When the measuring shows it wider than the draft admits — other teams waiting, an exec already copied — the chalk says so and the item climbs whatever priority was written.
- **Coaching** — one behaviour on two or more separate dated occasions, or a goal the report chose. Cut with questions, never lectures. An impression the measurements contradict gets dropped, the signals named. When the report already found the cause and is tracking it, the cut moves one step earlier — spot it sooner — on the real figures.
- **Career** — the report themselves raised growth, a course, wider scope, review timing. One sincere question; a keepable promise only.
- **Status** — nothing to repair, praise or settle. Last, squeezed to a line or two.
- **Parked** — another room's business: pay, HR, a dispute a different team owns, next quarter. Sent there with a line saying why. Personal matters stay private — "the personal matter", elaborated never.

Finer marks: a complaint traced to another desk's hold-up is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. Several messages on one problem count once. An impression nothing supports is dropped. Coaching wants a pattern — separate dated occasions; one rough fitting on a clean record, whoever caught it, earns a passing word at most. When the report already owns the fix, the item's weight moves to the manager's side — the safeguard, the pattern check — not a second telling-off. Recognition opens only on a quotable instance; when none exists, a line above item one admits it.

## The finished garment

`## What gets checked` — the chalk lines, left visible. One numbered entry per draft item: its wording quoted; **Evidence:** what the measurements show — who said or logged what, where, when, with the row or figure; **Call:** the mark and the reason — "stands" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, folded into another. Then **Missed:** what the manager owes or the draft skipped, and **The sums:** arithmetic from its operands — "118 of 120 is two short".

`# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]` — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line. Items in meeting order, `**name (Call, ~N min)**`, a sentence or two of things to say and ask, carrying real numbers and names. Then **Aside** (parked or dropped, with reasons), **Before you sit down** (every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move — settled before anything is asked of the report, above all a row an item hangs on), and **Why it reads this way** (a few lines on why the marks landed).

Invent nothing. A thin packet still earns a page — the check names what never arrived, the brief asks rather than asserts: what cannot be seen, the question to put, the shape of a true answer. A thin week makes a thin brief — nothing added for balance, no recognition without a quotable instance; honesty outranks the word count. A full fitting sits in `references/fitting-notes.md`. Follow the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When the input is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already held — whatever its label — the reply is one short note under 180 words, with no chalk lines:

- Say plainly that it records a past meeting rather than preparing the next one — name the document and the meeting it records.
- Say what would turn it into preparation: this week's open items, current figures, the manager's rows, anything flagged since.
- Where open actions survive inside, write the follow-up — owner, action, date as given — in a line or two; never a brief, never a re-sort.
- Personal material mentioned in passing stays "the personal matter", nothing more.
