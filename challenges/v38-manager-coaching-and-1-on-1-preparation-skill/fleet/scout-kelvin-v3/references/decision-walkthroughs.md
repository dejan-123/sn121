# Five invented packets, five different decisions

These practice people and organizations are fictional. The point
is to transfer the causal reading of a work week, not to reuse the
examples' details, sentence frames, or number of agenda items.

## A supposed motivation problem is an access problem

Manager's input for Arielle Cho, Service Analytics Specialist at
Torfield Transit:

Proposed topics: "ridership report progress"; "I'm worried she's
checked out and abrupt with the fares group"; "say thanks if there
is anything"; "she asked whether to pursue analysis leadership."
The action board has Arielle's nightly export DONE and her
ridership-model note PAST DUE. The model note needs fares-table
access; Arielle requested it twice and cannot complete the joined
figures without it. Her manager's separate promise to ask the
fares director for an access decision remains open. Four model
tasks share this hold. Arielle resolved 5 reporting tickets this
week against 8 in the prior week, but separately resolved 6
ad-hoc data questions against 4 previously.

Arielle's Monday post in the shared fares channel says, "This
data request is holding the monthly report. I need a yes or no
today or I will escalate." Her Wednesday private note to the
fares lead asks courteously for a date and offers to help test
the grant. A colleague, Dina, credits her export fix with
preventing the morning service bulletin from being delayed.
Arielle submitted "Could we talk about specialist versus team
lead work next quarter?"

A useful manager-facing answer might read:

# Arielle Cho | Service Analytics Specialist

The ridership-model note is held by fares-table access, not by
inaction. Five reporting tickets closed versus eight in the
prior week, while ad-hoc questions increased from four to six;
four model tasks wait on one external permission. Arielle has
asked twice and continued delivering work she can do. The
manager owes the fares director a decision request.

**Open with the export fix.** Dina's message credits Arielle
with preventing a delay to the service bulletin. Name that
specific contribution rather than a vague "great job."

**Move the access decision.** The manager called this
"checked out"; the record shows blocked work and parallel
delivery. "Which of the four waiting tasks can move before
the fares grant? I'll ask the director for an access decision
today." Don't treat the late model note as independent
evidence of poor motivation.

**Ask narrowly about the public post.** The need for escalation
was real. The Monday channel message put a deadline and
pressure in front of the whole group, while Wednesday's
private request was measured. "Was the shared channel the
best place to push for a decision, or should we take that
pressure directly to the decision owner next time?" This is
about venue, not a general verdict on Arielle's attitude.

**Give her growth question time.** She raised two directions
herself. "Which parts of specialist analysis feel worth
deepening, and which parts of leading a team draw you?"
Bring role criteria later; don't promise a move now.

**Manager follow-through:** contact the fares director,
confirm what access decision is required and by whom, and
bring examples of both growth paths to the next conversation.

The model note, ticket count and tone concern must not be
turned into three lectures about energy. Nor should the valid
dependency erase the one real question about a public venue.

## A queue with one reviewer is a management decision

Different week, different company. Naveen Firth is a Risk
Engineer at Glassford Grid. The manager marks "inspection
workflow - low-priority feedback: Naveen is taking too long,"
"incident exercise," "sensor protocol study," and "path to
Principal." A board shows 6 safety-change requests routed to
Naveen as the only reviewer, holding 3 plant teams. Naveen
already told the manager: "Rotate review duties among the
qualified engineers and let me train a second signatory."
The operations head agreed in a thread that a single-person
queue is unsustainable; no rotation has yet been approved.

Naveen's protocol study is in review but waiting on the
equipment group's test data. He has asked that group twice.
He handled an incident exercise Friday; facilitator Ellen
wrote that his calls were calm and timely. Naveen submitted
a development question about Principal scope, and his
manager has booked a separate calibration meeting but has
not given him the criteria. The manager also needs Naveen's
list of colleagues who can describe his cross-plant work;
the list is not started.

The manager's page:

# Naveen Firth | Risk Engineer

Three plant teams cannot clear six safety changes while one
reviewer holds the entire queue. It was filed "low," but the
downstream consequence makes reviewer allocation the first
work decision. Naveen already offered a rotation and training
plan. His protocol study has a separate upstream test-data
dependency; that silence is not a missed commitment by him.

**Credit the exercise.** Ellen's Friday note gives a specific
reason: calm, timely incident calls. Open with that evidence,
not a generic compliment.

**Decide who can review.** "You offered to train another
signatory. Which requests are safe to move first, and which
qualified engineer should I authorize?" The manager owns the
allocation and approval; do not frame a one-person queue
serving three teams as feedback about speed.

**Clear the test-data hold.** "You have asked twice for the
equipment tests. If they have not come through, I can reach
their manager; which test result do you need to finish the
protocol recommendation?" This is distinct from reviewer
capacity even though both affect completion.

**Talk about Principal scope now.** Naveen raised it. "Which
cross-plant responsibility do you most want to take on,
based on your incident work and your reviewer-training
proposal?" Share the written criteria and ask him to identify
people with firsthand experience of his work. A calibration
meeting later is not a substitute for the development
conversation now; do not promise a title here.

**Manager follow-through:** authorize the second reviewer,
request test data if still outstanding, bring the criteria,
and confirm what evidence the calibration process needs.

The priority change and the manager's authority are the
actual content of this page. Merely changing the heading
from "feedback" to "status" without moving the decision
up would not fix the meeting.

## A single event and a pattern can coexist

Manager of Delia Quill, Content Operations Designer at
Hensley Audio, lists "coach her presentation habits,"
"coach her estimates," "updates," and "thank her for
the catalogue work." A Thursday preview missed one
accessibility slide; Delia added it an hour later, and
there is no other presentation incident. In contrast,
her design-time estimates slipped in two cycles: first
three days planned against five used, then four planned
against six used. The latest scope grew from 10 layouts
to 17 after localization needs emerged. Delia wrote,
"I catch translation variants after I set the estimate;
I need to flag them earlier." Content engineer Pavel
praised her catalogue annotations for eliminating
handoff questions. Two of five usability sessions are
complete; another session was moved to next week. The
manager still owes a template that documents when
translation variants must be counted.

# Delia Quill | Content Operations Designer

Two different problems require different weights. The
missed slide was one quickly corrected preview moment;
the estimates did drift in successive cycles, and
Delia has identified a plausible cause. The latest
scope went from 10 to 17 layouts, seven more or 70%
over the initial count. The missing translation
template is a manager action.

**Recognize the annotations.** Pavel named the result:
the catalogue handoff no longer prompted clarification
questions. Give that credit specifically.

**One preview repair.** "You added the accessibility
slide within an hour of Thursday's preview. What would
help it be in the deck before the next one?" One
event is feedback, not evidence of a presentation
pattern.

**Estimate earlier, together.** "Both cycles ran two
days beyond the initial estimate; the latest added
seven layouts when translation variants surfaced.
Where could we flag that scope change before the
number is committed?" This is coaching about
Delia's own diagnosis, not "you keep missing dates."
The manager supplies the translation-count template.

**Usability sessions.** Two of five done, one moved.
"Does the changed session date affect when we can
act on the first two findings?" No invented inference
that the research is behind.

**Manager follow-through:** deliver the template,
agree on an early estimate checkpoint, and confirm
the rescheduled research slot.

If the input lacked Pavel's message, the answer would
not force a recognition item. If the two estimate
misses lacked evidence of changed scope, the
question would seek the cause rather than asserting
this one.

## A sparse packet is not permission to invent

For Customer Support Specialist Mei Rahman at Millstone
Parcel, the manager types only: "check the claims migration,"
"give her a nice opener," "she mentioned career goals," and
"salary question." No current task state, work figures,
compliments or employee submission is provided.

# Mei Rahman | Customer Support Specialist

There is no live claims-migration status, no named outcome to
credit, and no detail about Mei's growth interest. Pull the
task board and ask what she wants to discuss before the
meeting; do not say she is on track or stalled.

**Claims migration.** "Where does it stand this week,
and is there a decision you need from me?" The question
gathers the missing status rather than fabricating one.

**Career direction.** The manager says Mei mentioned
growth; confirm it in the meeting without assigning
her a goal. "You mentioned wanting to talk about your
next direction. What kind of work interests you?"

There is no sourced achievement for the proposed
praise; find a real one instead of inserting a token
opener. The manager should arrange a separate
compensation conversation using the proper process,
without salary talking points on this weekly page.

This page is short because its evidence is short.
It still preserves Mei's possible development
question while sending the pay ruling elsewhere.

## Old minutes ask for a handoff, not a meeting page

Manager pastes only notes from last week's conversation
with Route Planner Isaac Vale at Larchfield Couriers.
They reviewed dispatch delays. The manager agreed to
request an updated vehicle policy by Thursday; Isaac
agreed to draft a depot handoff by Friday. Isaac also
mentioned a private family concern. The notes contain
no progress since that conversation. The manager asks
to "prepare our next one-on-one."

A correct short response:

These are notes from the meeting already held, not
current-week updates. They list your vehicle-policy
request due Thursday and Isaac's depot handoff due
Friday; the notes do not tell us whether either
happened.

Please send the current state of both actions, this
week's dispatch figures or messages, and any topics
either of you wants to raise next. Then I can prepare
the next conversation. Keep the personal matter in a
private, employee-led support discussion rather than
in prepared talking points.

Do not append an agenda below the handoff, count old
due dates as fresh misses, or repeat the private
details. If current evidence later arrives alongside
the notes, the new signals may support a real page.
