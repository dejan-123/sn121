# Rounds journal

The weekly draft is a memory document. Written at the week's end, it flattens a held-up job into "status", softens a written compliment into "went well", and blurs one bad afternoon into "a pattern". The journal's discipline is simple: the record walks first, the draft follows, and the proof is printed where the manager can read it — a verdict shown is a verdict believed.

## Walking the record

Read the week's materials for what they literally say — tickets, messages, reviews, figures, praise. Every row still open, overdue or marked NOT STARTED is treated as open; a resolved row stays resolved unless something newer flatly contradicts it, and the original complaint never reopens one. Figures kept only in the manager's head do not count — every computed number is written out from its operands, points never dressed as percents. Two tallies run alongside the walk: the open rows that belong to the manager, and every compliment that arrived in writing.

## The six judgments

- **Recognition** — real praise exists in writing. Opens the meeting, spoken in the giver's words with the number beside it.
- **Blocked** — held up somewhere else: an approval withheld, a dependency gone silent. The manager leaves owning the move; and where the trail shows it bigger than the draft admits — other teams queuing, a senior name copied — the judgment says so and it climbs whatever priority was written.
- **Coaching** — the same behaviour on separate dated occasions, or a goal the report raised themselves. It works through questions, never lectures; and when the cause is already found and being tracked, coach the step before it — notice it sooner — anchored to the actual figures.
- **Career** — the report raised growth, training, scope or review timing themselves. One sincere question, one keepable promise.
- **Status** — nothing needing repair, praise or settlement. Goes last, kept to a line or two.
- **Parked** — business for another room: pay, HR matters, a dispute owned by another team, next quarter. Sent on with its reason. Personal matters remain "the personal matter", never elaborated.

The subtleties the walk catches: a complaint that really traces to another desk's hold-up is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. Several flags on one problem count once. An impression with nothing beneath it is dropped, the signals named. Coaching requires a pattern — so a single rough moment on a clean record, whoever spotted it, earns a passing word at most. When the report already owns the repair, the weight shifts to the manager's side — the safeguard, the process — rather than a second telling-off. Recognition opens only where something quotable exists; otherwise a line above the first item says so.

## The page itself

`## What the week proves` — the proof, printed. Each draft item, in the draft's order, gets one numbered entry: the item's own words quoted; **Proof:** what the record actually holds — who said or logged it, where, when, which row or figure; **Judgment:** the call with its reason — "stands" where the draft was right — or parked, dropped, merged into another. Then **Missed:** whatever the manager owes or the draft skipped, and **The sums:** every figure worked from its operands — "41 of 50 is nine behind".

`# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]` — 350–700 words. Begin with **Context:** — report, tenure, the week in a phrase. Agenda items in meeting order, `**name (Judgment, ~N min)**`, each carrying a sentence or two of what to say and ask with real names and numbers inside. Then **Off the list** (anything parked or dropped, reasons attached), **Settle first** (each open row the manager owns, with its concrete move — cleared before the report hears an ask, above all the row an item hangs on), and **Why it reads this way** (a few lines on how the judgments landed).

Nothing gets invented. A thin packet still earns a page — the proof names what never arrived and the brief asks instead of asserting: what cannot be seen, the question to put, the shape of a true answer. A thin week makes a thin brief — nothing padded in for balance, no praise without a quotable source; honesty outranks length. `references/dairy-notes.md` holds a full week's walk. Follow the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

Some input is a record of a meeting that already happened — minutes, a transcript, someone's notes, whatever the label says. That earns one short note under 180 words, nothing more: name the document and say plainly it looks backward at a held conversation rather than forward to the next one; list what the present week would need — this week's open items, current figures, the manager's own rows, anything flagged since; and where open actions survive inside the record, give each its follow-up line — owner, action, date as written. No proof section, no brief, no re-sorting of old items into a fresh agenda. Any personal material mentioned in passing stays "the personal matter", nothing more.
