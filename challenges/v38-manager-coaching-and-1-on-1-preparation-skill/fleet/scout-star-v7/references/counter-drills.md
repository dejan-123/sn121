# Counter drills — full sheets on invented packets

Four invented batches, decided the way the window should decide them.
The names, figures and products are invented; the decisions are the
part to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal batch with a mis-filed application

Packet: report is "Marta, senior platform engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "discipline issue — Marta snapped at QA in the team channel",
(b) "why is the payments migration late", (c) "quick win — mention
the dashboard launch". The documents: QA's lead posted a public
thank-you naming Marta for unblocking their release by building the
replay tool early; the channel exchange shows Marta objecting —
sharply but on the substance — to QA shipping a build that failed
its own gate; the migration is 3 of 8 endpoints done, waiting on a
security sign-off requested 9 days ago; the dashboard launched and
the support lead messaged that ticket volume on it halved in week
one.

Decisions:

- Application (a): **Re-drawn**. One substantive objection to a build
  that failed its own checks — filed as a discipline issue, granted
  as a one-time note with one narrow question. Condition: "The QA
  exchange was on the merits — QA was already thanking you for the
  release help. One thing to keep in view: that disagreement ran in
  the team channel rather than the ticket. Does moving it there cost
  you anything?" The objection stands; the channel takes one
  question and no more.
- Application (b): **Re-drawn — another office's stamp.** 3 of 8
  done, and the stall is a 9-day-old sign-off with security, not
  Marta's pace. Condition: "Migration is waiting on security's
  sign-off — nine days outstanding. I'll chase the reviewer today
  and get a date; who should I escalate to if it slips again?"
- Application (c): **Granted to lead.** Two attributable credits —
  the QA lead's public thanks and the support figure. Open with them
  before any process item; credit leads.
- Every filed application gets a window line and a decision; nothing
  filed disappears.

Sheet: `# Permit sheet: Marta, senior platform engineer` — picture
line ("a strong week with one decision waiting on another office"),
figures shown as work, then grant order with all three filings, the
document behind each, and each decision — then conditions,
pre-filing, routed-out list, clerk's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin batch processed honestly

Packet: report is "Ilya, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep my
weekly with Ilya — want to talk about his promo timeline, and whether
he's ready to own the ingest rewrite." No action log, no figures, no
channel excerpts. One calendar item: Ilya asked in passing about
salary bands for the next level.

Thin is not invalid: the batch aims at the coming meeting, so the
window still logs it — decided application by application. Only the
wrong kind of filing (drill 3) is sent back whole. The reply opens
`# Permit sheet: Ilya, staff engineer` — never a send-back preface.

Decisions:

- Promo timeline: **Granted — split by office.** Ilya's direction
  and the manager's readiness question belong in this meeting, with
  real time: "You asked about the next level — here's the gap
  evidence I see and what I'd need documented; what scope would you
  take to build it?" The pay-band question is **Routed out**: bands
  are decided in the compensation review — the sheet names the
  counter (the comp review with the comp partner) and the owner, one
  line, no conditions.
- Readiness for the ingest rewrite: **Awaiting-docs**. The batch has
  no delivery record to weigh. It is granted as the honest question,
  not an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have your recent
  delivery record in front of me — walk me through the last few
  weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the sheet. A thin sheet of
  honest questions beats a padded one; the sheet head logs what the
  batch lacks and pre-filing lists what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a filing that is not this week's batch

Packet: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Dana —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list three
actions: Dana to send the benchmarking doc (due Thursday), the
manager to confirm the conference budget, a follow-up on the
mentoring pilot.

Decision: **whole filing sent back — nothing logged.** The reply,
under 150 words, titled `# Not this week's batch`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was agreed, not
what has moved since. The document this supports is a follow-up on
the open actions, not a sheet: Dana's benchmarking doc (due
Thursday), your budget confirmation, the mentoring pilot follow-up —
owners and dates exactly as filed. For a weekly sheet, send this
week's action log, any figures, and whatever Dana raised or you want
filed. Nothing in the material is dated after that meeting, so a
sheet would replay old filings or invent new ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not repeated, not
summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own file

Packet: report is "Sana, senior backend engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "remind Sana to clear her review queue — five design docs are
waiting on her and two partner teams are blocked"; (b) "Sana seems
quiet in standup lately". The documents: the five docs are assigned
because the process names her sole approver for cross-team changes;
she shipped two features this sprint and flagged the queue in the
last retro, proposing a rotation; "quiet" appears once, in the
manager's note.

Decisions:

- Application (a): **Re-drawn — the office that can stamp it is the
  manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two teams is a capacity
  matter the manager owns, not a note to work faster. It is granted
  early as the manager's own decision: "Your rotation proposal is
  the right starting point — I want to approve it this week and take
  it to the partner leads. What load should each reviewer hold so
  nothing queues behind one person again?"
- Application (b): **Refused.** One manager observation with no
  documents behind it; the sprint shows two shipped features and a
  raised process fix. The sheet logs its absence plainly rather than
  inventing a wellbeing segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Sana's retro proposal —
  is listed under pre-filing as the manager's commitment, with whom
  and when.

## What every drill shares

- The documents decide; the filing is only the application.
- Attributable credit leads — a named person, a concrete result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its substance
  and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another office stamps is logged to that office.
- One occurrence is a single exhibit, never corroborated conduct.
- Missing documents are granted as `Awaiting-docs` — the honest
  question in the slot — never covered over.
- Other-office matters get a counter and an owner, never conditions.
- Every figure shows its own working; every filed application gets a
  window line and a decision.
- A thin batch still gets a sheet. The send-back is for the wrong
  kind of filing only — and it stays under 150 words.
