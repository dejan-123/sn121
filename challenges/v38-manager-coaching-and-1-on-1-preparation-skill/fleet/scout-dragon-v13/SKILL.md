# Kitchen notebook

A draft written on a Friday is a memory document. Memory keeps the headline and drops the ingredient: the delayed job becomes "status", the written compliment becomes "went well", one rough service becomes "a pattern". The notebook's rule is the taster's rule — try the record before trusting the menu, and write the tasting where the manager can read it.

## Tasting the record

Work through what the week left behind — tickets, messages, reviews, figures, praise — taking each at face value. Whatever sits open, overdue or NOT STARTED stays on the board as open; whatever resolved stays finished unless a newer entry plainly contradicts it, and an old complaint never reopens a closed row. Any figure living only in the manager's head counts as nothing — show each computation from its operands, and never let a point read as a percent. Along the way, gather two collections: every open row belonging to the manager, and every compliment that landed by name.

## The calls

Each draft item, in the draft's own order, takes a call:

- **Recognition** — praise on paper exists. Opens the meeting in the giver's own words, the figure attached. Never "good week".
- **Blocked** — another desk holds it: approval not given, a dependency gone quiet, more requests than one pair of hands. The manager leaves carrying the move. Sharp messages after ignored chases are the hold-up talking, not a tone problem — and when the trail shows it wider than the draft admits, other teams waiting or an exec already copied, say so and the item climbs whatever priority was written.
- **Coaching** — one behaviour seen on two or more separate dated occasions, or a goal the report chose themselves. Runs on questions, never lectures. An impression the record contradicts gets dropped, the signals named. When the report has the cause already and tracks it, the call steps earlier — spot it sooner — anchored to real figures, not the symptom.
- **Career** — the report raised growth, a course, wider scope, review timing themselves. One sincere question; a keepable promise only.
- **Status** — nothing to repair, praise or settle. Comes last, squeezed to a line or two.
- **Parked** — belongs in a different room: pay, HR, a dispute another team owns, next quarter. Sent there with a line saying why. Personal matters stay private — "the personal matter", elaborated never.

The finer calls: a problem raised five times is still one problem. A feeling nothing supports is dropped. Coaching asks for a pattern across separate dated occasions — so one rough service on an otherwise clean record, whoever noticed it, earns a passing word at most. When the repair already belongs to the report, the item's weight moves to the manager's side — the safeguard, the checklist — not a second telling-off. Recognition opens only where a quotable instance exists; without one, a line above the first item admits it plainly.

## The page

`## Item by item` — the calls, printed. Each draft item gets one numbered entry in the draft's order: the item's wording quoted; **On the record:** what the record shows — who said or logged what, where, when, with the row or figure; **The call:** the verdict and its reason — "stands" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, folded into another. Then **Missed:** what the manager owes or the draft forgot, and **Figures:** arithmetic from its operands — "214 against 210 is four over plan".

`# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]` — 350–700 words. Open with **Context:** one line — report, tenure, the week in a phrase. Items in meeting order, `**name — The call, ~N min**`, each a sentence or two of things to say and ask with the real numbers and names inside. Then **Set aside** (whatever was parked or dropped, reasons kept), **Before the meeting** (every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move — handled before the report is asked for anything, the row an item depends on first), and **Thinking** (a few lines on why the calls landed where they did).

Invent nothing. A thin packet still earns a page — the calls name what is missing and the brief asks rather than asserts: what cannot be seen, the question to put, what a real answer sounds like. A thin week makes a thin brief — nothing added for balance, no recognition without a quotable instance; honesty outranks the word count. A full service's worth sits in `references/kitchen-notes.md`. Take the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When what arrives is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already had — whatever its label — one short note under 180 words goes back, no item-by-item:

- Name the document and say straight out that it records a meeting behind you, not preparation for the one ahead.
- List what the coming week would need — this week's open items, current figures, the manager's own rows, whatever was flagged since.
- Where open actions survive inside it, give each its follow-up — owner, action, date as given — in a line or two; never a brief, never old items re-sorted.
- Personal material mentioned in passing stays "the personal matter", nothing more.
