# Meeting prep sheet

A draft agenda tells you what the manager intends to raise, not what the week proves. Check every draft line against the action log, figures, messages, notes and the report's own topic. Keep the manager's wording visible, make the evidence-based call beside it, then write a sheet the manager can use directly in the room.

Never fill gaps. Do not invent a name, date, owner, quote, result, diagnosis, mood, completion or pattern. A date can be used only where the packet gives it; an unanswered point stays an honest question.

## Scope the packet

A forward-looking draft is preparation even when it is bare. Sparse evidence changes the questions and the manager's fetch list; it does not turn the request away.

Return only a redirect for the wrong source: notes, minutes, a transcript, decisions or actions from a meeting already held; preparation for a skip-level, team meeting or third-person conversation; or a formal review, rating or disciplinary document. A held-meeting record gets the note below and no prep sheet, even when the manager asks to prepare the next meeting from it.

## Evidence tests

Concrete signals come from supplied action states, figures, quoted messages, manager notes and report-raised topics. An impression without a source is not evidence.

- **Credit** needs named praise or a measured result. Effort, hours and vague approval do not open the meeting; independent attribution is stronger than self-report.
- **Unblock** covers work waiting on approval, access, a supplier, a calendar, another team, a shared process or an assigned queue. The move belongs to whoever can release it. A sole reviewer or assigned queue holding several teams is the manager's capacity/routing decision, never the report personally blocking those teams. If the report proposed the fix, the manager's move is to back, broker, approve, escalate or reassign it.
- **Status** is live work the report can answer now and that is not primarily waiting elsewhere.
- **Quick feedback** is one dated event with one narrow remaining question.
- **Coaching** needs the same behaviour on two or more dated occasions, or a named prior cycle with plan-versus-actual figures. When the report already diagnosed the cause, coach the earlier detection or flag — what should have surfaced sooner, to whom and when — rather than repeating the symptom.
- **Career** protects a direction, level, programme or training topic the report raised. A manager-created career check remains a modest invitation through named work, not the report's aspiration.
- **Route elsewhere** covers pay, health, family circumstances, accommodation, formal HR, third-party complaints, ratings, promotion decisions and owner-specific processes. Give destination and owner, with no talking points.

A promise, request, warning, attempt or scheduled step stays a plan until the packet says it happened. Write `planned to escalate` and ask whether it happened; never turn intention into outcome. A justified public objection keeps its substance; at most, ask one narrow channel question.

## Reconcile the inputs

Before writing, account for the packet privately:

- `N` manager draft topics require `N` numbered agenda entries.
- Each entry's `filed:` line preserves that draft topic's exact wording.
- Do not merge, split, duplicate, substitute or drop a draft topic; shared evidence may be cited under separate entries.
- Every open action row not represented by a draft topic gets one later entry marked `filed: not on the draft`, with owner and state.
- With a log, report `DONE rows / all supplied rows`; manager-owned and completed rows count in the denominator. Only explicit `DONE` counts; `IN PROGRESS`, `NOT STARTED`, open, overdue and partial rows do not.
- A `DONE` row provides evidence or closure, never a `Manager handles first` task. Manager-owned open or overdue rows go there; report-owned open rows become report questions; external waits stay in the conversation plan.

## The sheet

Write 350–700 words under `# <full name>, <role>: <short evidence-based heading>`. Keep the wording natural and printable.

**Week at a glance.** Three or four sentences: what moved, what waits and on whom, what the report raised, the action arithmetic and worked figures. Show operands and bases. Rates and scores move in percentage points; planned-versus-actual uses matching bases; unrelated counts are not ratios; a partial cause leaves the remainder open.

**Manager handles first.** Each open or overdue manager-owned action row, what it releases and whether to settle it before the meeting or name the day. Then one bullet per missing record. If none is owed, say so while still listing needed fetches. Never put report-owned rows, external waits, DONE rows or routed topics here.

## Conversation plan

Number the entries in meeting order: earned credit first; unblocks and decisions; quick feedback or coaching; report-raised career time; simple closes last. Use `**N. <Call> — <topic> (~minutes)**`.

Under every entry include:

`filed: <exact draft wording> → <call>: <the signal or gap behind it>`

Then use at most three sentences: the evidence, the manager's concrete move, and one anchored question where needed. Anchor each question to a row, figure, thread, date or the report's own words; generic questions are unfinished.

## Route elsewhere

List each diverted matter, destination and owner. If none applies, say so. Do not draft advice or talking points for those matters.

## Why this order

Close with two to four plain sentences explaining the calls that changed, the evidence behind them and what remains unknown. Do not mention the skill, reference, tools or private counting.

## Sparse input

A bare draft still receives the full sheet. Write `Action items: no log supplied`, not `0 of 0`. Keep every draft topic as its own numbered entry and give it the provisional call its wording supports. Under it, state the unknown, ask a grounded question and name the record that would settle it. Put one fetch bullet per missing record under `Manager handles first`.

## Held-meeting record

Return only a note under 180 words — no prep sheet, agenda, evidence section or process text. Begin:

`# <report name>, <role>: not a 1-on-1 prep request`

State that the input records a meeting already held and give the proof: attendees, discussed topics, decisions or agreed actions. Explain that a new sheet would replay old positions or invent current ones. List each recorded open action with exact owner, action and due date, manager-owned rows first; say current status is not supplied. Ask for current action status, this week's signals, any new report topic and a fresh draft agenda. Route pay, health, family, personal, formal or third-party matters privately in the record's own few words, with no talking points.
