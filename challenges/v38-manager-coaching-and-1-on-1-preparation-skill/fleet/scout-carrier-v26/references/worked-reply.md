# One invented week, answered in the register the sheet should use — form only

The people, the theatre and the numbers are made up. What transfers is the voice: every item reads like a colleague who did the reading and is telling you what they would do — not a form, not clipped instructions. In a real reply every fact comes from the inputs supplied.

## The invented inputs

Aoife Byrne, stage manager at a community theatre, two seasons in. The manager's draft: "win for the week" / "Status: fly rail inspection" / "Feedback: sharp with the front-of-house lead" / "Status: ticketed audience drop" / "she's asked about the production manager track" / "heads up — her flat move". The week's record: action log shows "issue the strike schedule for the current run (Aoife, DONE Sunday)" and "counter-sign the premises licence renewal (Manager, OVERDUE)". Figures: 612 tickets sold against 700 seats across the run. Threads: Wednesday, the artistic director in #production — "Aoife reset the whole scene-change sequence in a day when the revolve jammed; opening night went up on time because of it." Thursday, Aoife in #safety — "Fly rail inspection still unbooked; the certified inspector hasn't confirmed a date. I've roped the counterweight line and scheduled manual changes for the weekend shows." The rota log shows one clipped exchange with the front-of-house lead on Saturday, after an unannounced interval extension went unflagged twice. Last week Aoife asked whether the production manager track was open to her. Her flat move dates are private; she has already told HR what she needs.

## The reply

## Laying out the week

The manager supplied a draft agenda, an action log, one figure pair and this week's threads for a meeting still to come, so this is prep.

1. "win for the week" — vague until Wednesday: the artistic director wrote in #production that she "reset the whole scene-change sequence in a day when the revolve jammed; opening night went up on time because of it". Hat-tip, on those words, and it opens.
2. "Status: fly rail inspection" — the inspector has no confirmed date and she has already roped the counterweight line and scheduled manual changes. Not a status question — a Freeze, and her workaround is the fix to back: the move is a booked date out of the inspector.
3. "Feedback: sharp with the front-of-house lead" — one dated exchange, Saturday, after the interval extension went unflagged twice. One means one: a Blip, asked lightly, never a verdict.
4. "Status: ticketed audience drop" — 612 sold against 700 seats, but nothing says whether that repeats earlier runs or who owns the promo push. A legitimate ask with thin evidence stays on as a question: a Hum, pinned to the gap.
5. "she's asked about the production manager track" — she raised it herself last week. Skyline; the opening-night save is the signal to set beside what the track asks for.
6. "heads up — her flat move" — the dates are private and she has already told HR what she needs. Down the hall: the leave process, first step is confirming the dates with HR; nothing on it is drafted here.

Left out: the premises licence renewal, unsigned and overdue, is the manager's own row — Your open items. Number work: 612 sold against 700 seats is 88 empty, roughly one seat in eight; the licence is the open row.

# Aoife Byrne x her manager — the opening-night save and the unbooked inspector

**The picture so far.** The run is selling 612 of 700 (88 empty seats, about one in eight), but Wednesday gave you the week's headline: she rebuilt the scene-change sequence in a day when the revolve jammed, and the artistic director credited opening night to it in #production. The fly-rail inspection has no inspector date — she has the workaround running already — and your licence counter-signature is overdue.

**Your open items.** Counter-sign the premises licence renewal — overdue, and it is the one document the venue cannot trade without. Bring it or set the day.

## The meeting order

**1. The opening-night save — Hat-tip**
Worth opening on the artistic director's exact words — "opening night went up on time because of it" — because a public save from the AD lands differently than a number. Then it is worth asking what she would write down from that day, because a one-day reset like that is exactly the kind of craft that disappears if nobody names it.

**2. Fly rail inspection — Freeze**
The certified inspector has not confirmed a date, and she has already roped the counterweight line and scheduled manual changes for the weekend — the workaround is done, what is left is yours. Worth pinning the inspector to a booked date this week and telling her plainly the manual plan is approved, because she should not have to guess whether carrying the risk herself was the right call.

**3. Audience drop — Hum**
612 of 700 is a gap worth one question, not a verdict: "do the same nights keep coming up empty, and is anyone owned the promo push on them?" If a pattern shows, it earns its own item next time.

**4. Saturday's exchange — Blip**
A clipped word after the interval extension went unflagged twice — one dated instance in a week she otherwise carried the show. One light question is enough — "has the flag been sorted since?" — because a single moment never deserves a verdict.

**5. Production manager track — Skyline**
- "The one-day reset is the clearest thing on your record this season — which parts of the production manager track does it already cover, and which does it leave open?"
- "You asked about the track yourself — what would you want it to lead to?"

## Off the agenda

The flat move — the dates are private and she has already told HR what she needs; the first step is simply confirming the dates with HR so the rota can plan around them. Nothing on it is drafted here.

**The thinking behind it.** The win stays and gains its anchor — the AD named the save; the inspection is a hold on an outside inspector, not a status, and her rope-off plan is the fix to back; "sharp" is one dated instance after two ignored flags, so it stays a Blip and never becomes a Reel; the audience gap is live but unowned, so it remains a question until the record says more; the track is hers and stands on the week's clearest signal; the move sits with HR, not this room.
