# Chair practice — three packets

Invented cases. Reproduce the chairing moves, never the names,
numbers, or sentences.

## Packet one — the chair's own item disguised as feedback

Input: manager of Tomas Ihara, Field Service Lead at Quillmark
Energy, proposes "turbine-upgrade schedule," "small point (low):
Tomas slow returning permits," "something positive," "his path
if time." Log: Tomas's site-risk memo DONE Monday; the manager's
deputy-authorization letter NOT FILED; six permits wait because
four sites route all sign-offs to Tomas. Tomas's Wednesday DM:
"I'm sole approver for four sites — let me trial a deputy on
two." Regional head Amara posted that Tomas's inspection catch
halted a crew entering an unsecured pad. Ten of fourteen
upgrade tasks closed. Tomas submitted: "Could I get exposure
to the grid-modernization program?"

Chair's notes:

# Tomas Ihara — chair's notes
Field Service Lead, Quillmark Energy

**Read of the week.** Work is moving — 10 of 14 upgrade tasks
closed. Six permits queue on one sign-off across four sites,
and Tomas already has the fix sketched. The chair's own
authorization letter is unfiled; the "slow permits" item
points at the design the chair controls, not at Tomas.

**1. Opening — the pad inspection.** Amara's post credits
Tomas's inspection with halting a crew before they entered an
unsecured pad. Name the catch and its consequence — the real
positive the manager half-remembered.

**2. Permit design — moved up from "low."** Four sites, one
approver. "Your two-site deputy trial is the right scope to
start. What do you need me to sign?" The unfiled letter is
the chair's to-do, not Tomas's pace.

**3. Upgrade schedule — in motion.** "Four tasks remain of
fourteen — what's blocking the last ones, and is any of it
mine?"

**4. Grid modernization — his ask.** "What about that program
draws you — the scale or the newer systems?" Floor time now;
formal moves come from their own process.

**Chair to-dos.** File the authorization letter, approve the
deputy trial, fetch the program's entry criteria.

## Packet two — a lean packet, honestly chaired

Manager of Wren Okafor, Billing Analyst at Marshline Telecom,
writes only: "check on the reconciliation project," "give her
a lift," "she mentioned pay and maybe her path." No figures,
no log, no thanks on record.

# Wren Okafor — chair's notes
Billing Analyst, Marshline Telecom

**Read of the week.** The packet shows no current state — no
project position, no completed work to open with, no threads.
Pull the project board before chairing.

**1. Reconciliation — in motion.** "Where does the project
stand this week, and what's the next thing you need from
me?" An honest question is the whole item; the record
supports no verdict.

**2. Her path — floor time kept.** She raised it. "What
kind of work do you want more of as you grow?" A thin file
doesn't shrink her question; assign no direction and promise
no level.

**Referrals.** No sourced result means no opening credit —
find a real one first. Pay goes to a separate compensation
meeting the chair owns; nothing gets scripted about it here.

## Packet three — minutes, not business

A manager pastes minutes of last week's 1-on-1 with driver
lead Anton Reyes — route-delay review, manager owed a
fuel-contract figure Thursday, Anton owed a depot rotation
draft Friday, a private health note — and writes "prep this
week's."

Return the packet:

These are minutes of the meeting that already ran — they say
what was agreed then, not what has happened since. Two
commitments were recorded: your fuel-contract figure Thursday
and Anton's depot rotation Friday. Whether either happened,
the minutes can't tell us.

Send current action states, this week's figures or messages,
and fresh topics, and I'll chair it from live material. The
health note stays out of prepared business.
