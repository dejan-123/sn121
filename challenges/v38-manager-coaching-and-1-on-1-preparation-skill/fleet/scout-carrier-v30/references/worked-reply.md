# One invented week, answered in this voice — borrow the shape, never the facts

Everyone below is made up. What carries over is the form: a weighing that walks the draft in order, items phrased like a colleague who did the homework, questions written as words the manager can speak. In a live answer the facts all come from the packet supplied.

## The invented packet

Amara Osei coordinates volunteers at a regional food-bank warehouse, two years in. The manager's draft: "praise for the week" / "Status: cold-storage inventory" / "Coaching: abrupt with the Saturday crew" / "Status: donor receipt backlog" / "she asked about the ops-lead rotation" / "personal — her father's care visits". The week: the log shows "file the summer grant report (Amara, DONE Wednesday)" and "sign off the new racking layout (Manager, OVERDUE)". Figures: 9,460 meals packed against a target of 10,000. Threads — Tuesday, the regional director in #warehouse: "Amara's crew turned the dairy surplus around same-day; first time a partner has called us to say thank you." Thursday, Amara in #logistics: "cold-store inventory is short two pallets; supplier delivery slipped to next week — I've stretched remaining stock across priority routes meanwhile." The rota notes one curt exchange with a Saturday volunteer on the 14th, after a double-booked loading bay. Last month Amara asked whether the ops-lead rotation covered warehouse coordinators. Her father's care visits are private; she has already told HR what she needs.

## The answer

## Weighing the draft

A draft, a log, one figure pair and the week's threads arrived for a session still to come — this is prep.

1. "praise for the week" — vague until Tuesday: the regional director posted publicly that her crew turned the dairy surplus around same-day, adding it was the first partner thank-you call the site had received. Kudos on that signal; it leads.
2. "Status: cold-storage inventory" — the supplier slipped to next week and she has already stretched remaining stock across priority routes. Not a status question: a Tangle on the supplier, the remedy to back being her reallocation, and the move a confirmed delivery date.
3. "Coaching: abrupt with the Saturday crew" — one dated exchange on the 14th, after a double-booked loading bay. A Flare: recount it, ask once, no verdict.
4. "Status: donor receipt backlog" — 9,460 meals against the 10,000 target and the backlog sits inside the gap, yet nothing says who chases it or whether it repeats. Thin evidence on a real question: a Murmur pinned to the 540-meal shortfall.
5. "she asked about the ops-lead rotation" — her own ask last month. Vista; the dairy turnaround is the signal to set beside what the rotation wants.
6. "personal — her father's care visits" — the visits are private and she has already told HR what she needs. Next door: the leave process, starting with confirming dates at HR; nothing gets written on it in this room.

Not drafted: the overdue racking sign-off is the manager's own row. Figures run: 9,460 on a 10,000 target leaves 540 short, and the backlog question lives inside that gap.

# Amara Osei with her manager — the same-day save and the slipping supplier

**At a glance.** The crew packed 9,460 meals on a 10,000 target (540 short), and Tuesday gave the week its headline: the dairy surplus turned around same-day, with the regional director calling it the site's first partner thank-you. Cold storage runs short two pallets while the supplier slips — her reallocation is already covering — and the racking sign-off waiting on you is overdue.

**Owed by you.** Sign off the new racking layout — overdue, and the pallet shortage is exactly what it relieves. Bring it or set the day.

## On the agenda

**1. The dairy turnaround — Kudos**
Open on the regional director's public words and what they made possible — a partner ringing to say thanks is worth more than the tonnage itself. Then ask how she sequenced the same-day turn; a save that fast is a method worth capturing before it fades.

**2. Cold storage — Tangle**
The supplier slipped to next week and her stretch across priority routes is holding meanwhile — the workaround is hers, what remains is yours. Pin the delivery to a confirmed date and tell her plainly the reallocation is approved; she should not carry the gap quietly.

**3. Receipt backlog — Murmur**
540 short and the donor receipts sit unowned inside it — one question: "where do the outstanding receipts actually pile up, and whose desk catches them?" If the same queue shows next week it earns its own item.

**4. The 14th — Flare**
A curt word after a double-booked loading bay — one dated moment in a week she otherwise ran clean. One light question covers it: "has the loading-bay rota stopped double-booking since then?" A single moment never earns a verdict.

**5. Ops-lead rotation — Vista**
- "The dairy turn is the clearest thing on your record this quarter — which parts of ops-lead does it already prove, and which does it leave open?"
- "You asked about the rotation yourself — where would you want it to take you?"

## Routed out

Her father's care visits — private, and HR already has what they need; the step is confirming dates so the rota can plan around them. Nothing on it is drafted here.

**The reasoning.** The praise stays, anchored — the director named the save in public. Cold storage is a hold on an outside supplier, not a status, and her stretch is the remedy to back. "Abrupt" is one dated instance after a double-booked bay, so a Flare rather than a Drift. The backlog is live but unowned, so it stays a question until the record says more. The rotation is hers and stands on the week's clearest signal. The care visits belong to HR, not this room.
