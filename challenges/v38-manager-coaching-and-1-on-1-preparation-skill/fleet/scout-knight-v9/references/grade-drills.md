# Grade drills — full sheets on invented batches

Four invented manifests, graded the way the manual grades
them. Names, figures and products are invented; the grades
are the part to carry.

## Case 1 — normal batch, one mislabelled lot

Manifest: report "Jo, senior product engineer". Filed:
(a) "Jo's friction with marketing — she challenged their
numbers in the project channel", (b) "why is the checkout
rebuild behind", (c) "acknowledge the analytics launch".
Record: marketing lead posted public thanks naming Jo for
finding the double-counting bug; the channel exchange shows
Jo objecting — bluntly, on substance — to publishing numbers
she had just disproved; the rebuild is 5 of 9 screens done,
waiting on a legal sign-off requested 8 days ago; analytics
launched and support tickets labelled "can't find" fell by
a third in week one.

Grades:

- (a) **Re-graded → one-time note** with one narrow channel
  question: "The marketing exchange was on the merits —
  they were already thanking you for the counting fix. One
  thing: it ran in the project channel rather than on the
  ticket. Does moving it there cost anything?" Substance
  stands; channel takes one question and no more.
- (b) **Re-graded → manager's own follow-up.** 5 of 9 done,
  stalled on an 8-day legal sign-off, not Jo's pace: "I'll
  chase the reviewer today for a date; who do I escalate to
  if it slips again?"
- (c) **Accepted first.** Two attributable credits — public
  thanks, and a third fewer tickets. Credit opens the sheet.
- Every filed lot gets a manifest line and a grade; nothing
  filed disappears.

Sheet: `# Inspection sheet: Jo, senior product engineer` —
picture line, worked figures, grade order with all three
lots, inspector's notes, lead's own lots, transferred list,
inspector's close.

## Case 2 — thin manifest, graded honestly

Manifest: report "Sam, staff engineer". Filed note: "prep my
weekly with Sam — his path to principal, and whether he can
own the notifications rebuild." No action log, no figures,
no excerpts. One note: Sam asked about the promotion-cycle
calendar.

Thin is not invalid: the manifest aims at the coming
meeting, so it is still this batch — graded lot by lot.
Only a wrong-kind manifest (case 3) goes back. First line:
`# Inspection sheet: Sam, staff engineer`.

Grades:

- Path to principal: **Accepted — split by department.**
  Sam's direction and the readiness question stay in the
  meeting with real time: "You asked about the next level —
  here's the gap evidence I see and what I'd need
  documented; what scope would you take to build it?" The
  cycle-calendar question is **Transferred** — it sits with
  the promotion review; destination and owner named, one
  line, no talking points.
- Rebuild readiness: **Held**. No delivery record in the
  packet; it stands as the honest question: "I don't have
  your recent record in front of me — walk me through the
  last few weeks: shipped, stuck, and what you'd take on."
- Nothing is invented to fill the sheet. A thin sheet of
  honest questions beats a padded one.

## Case 3 — wrong manifest, returned whole

Manifest: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1
with Lee — please prep next week's agenda from them." Three
open actions: Lee's latency report (due Tuesday), the
manager's training-budget confirmation, a docs-migration
follow-up.

**Whole manifest returned — no lots graded.** Under 150
words, headed `# Not this batch`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was
agreed, not what has moved since. This supports a follow-up
on the open actions, not a sheet: Lee's latency report (due
Tuesday), your budget confirmation, the docs follow-up —
owners and dates exactly as filed. For a sheet, send this
week's action items, any figures, and whatever Lee raised
or you want addressed. Nothing in the material is dated
after that meeting, so a sheet would replay finished lots
or invent untested ones.

Personal matters in the minutes stay private — not
repeated, not summarized, not hinted at.

## Case 4 — the queue that is the manager's own lot

Manifest: report "Alba, senior infrastructure engineer".
Filed: (a) "Alba must clear her review queue — eight RFCs
wait on her, three teams blocked"; (b) "Alba seemed flat in
the review". Record: the eight RFCs sit with her because
the process names her sole approver for cross-team work;
she shipped two features this sprint and raised the queue
in retro herself, proposing a rotation; "flat" appears
once, in the manager's note.

Grades:

- (a) **Re-graded → manager's capacity call.** A
  sole-approver rule stalling three teams is owned by the
  manager, early on the sheet: "Your rotation proposal is
  the right starting point — I'll approve it this week and
  take it to the team leads. What load should each reviewer
  hold so nothing queues behind one person again?"
- (b) **Rejected.** One unsourced observation; the sprint
  shows two shipped features and a raised process fix. Its
  absence is recorded plainly.
- The manager's own open item — acting on the retro
  proposal — sits under lead's own lots with whom and when.

## Constants across all four

- The record decides; the filed label is only the filing.
- Attributable credit is accepted first.
- Justified substance keeps its place; the channel takes
  one question.
- Another department's work transfers there.
- One occurrence is never a pattern.
- Missing records stand `Held` — the honest question —
  never covered over.
- Transferred lots get destination and owner, never talking
  points.
- Every figure shows its arithmetic; every filed lot gets a
  manifest line and a grade.
- A thin manifest still gets a sheet. The return is for the
  wrong document only — under 150 words, no inspection
  structure.
