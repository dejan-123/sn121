# Log drills — three worked packets

Fabricated entries for practice. Steal the method, never the
names, numbers, or wording.

## Drill one — the "minor" item holding the fleet

Input: manager of Hana Voss, Dock Operations Lead at Coldstream
Freight, proposes "berth-turnaround status," "minor point (low):
Hana slow on inspection returns," "a positive note," "her path
if there's time." Log: Hana's crane-usage audit DONE Tuesday;
the manager's relief-inspector authorization NOT FILED; seven
inspection returns wait because three berths route every return
to Hana. Her Wednesday entry: "I'm the only inspector across
three berths — let me trial a qualified second on one berth."
Harbor chief Edda logged that Hana's paperwork catch stopped a
non-conforming container from being loaded. Twelve of sixteen
turnarounds met window. Hana asked about training toward the
port-planner track.

The brief:

# Hana Voss — pre-watch brief
Dock Operations Lead, Coldstream Freight

**Read of the watch.** Turnarounds are holding — 12 of 16 met
window. Seven inspection returns queue on one inspector across
three berths, and Hana has the fix drafted. The manager's
relief-inspector authorization is unfiled; the "slow returns"
entry points at the crewing the manager sets, not at Hana.

**1. Opening — the paperwork catch.** Edda's log credits Hana
with stopping a non-conforming container before loading. Name
what her check caught and what it spared — the real positive
the manager half-remembered.

**2. Inspection crewing — brought forward from "low."** Three
berths, one inspector. "Your single-berth trial is the right
start. What do you need filed to run it?" The unfiled
authorization is the manager's order to write, not Hana's
pace.

**3. Berth turnarounds — under way.** "Four of sixteen missed
window — what's common across them, and is any of it mine to
move?"

**4. Port-planner track — her bearing.** "What draws you to
the planning side — the layout work or the scheduling?"
Unhurried time now; formal track decisions come from their
own process.

**The manager's orders.** File the relief-inspector
authorization, approve the single-berth trial, fetch the
planner-track criteria.

## Drill two — a quiet log, honestly briefed

Manager of Ellis Tarn, Metering Analyst at Greystone Power,
writes only: "check the meter-swap program," "say something
encouraging," "he mentioned pay and maybe his path." No
figures, no entries, no thanks on file.

# Ellis Tarn — pre-watch brief
Metering Analyst, Greystone Power

**Read of the watch.** The log shows no current entries — no
program position, no completed work to open with, no threads.
Pull the program board before the watch.

**1. Meter-swap program — under way.** "Where does the program
stand this week, and what's the next thing you need from
me?" A fair question is the whole item; the log supports no
verdict.

**2. His bearing — time kept.** He raised it. "What kind of
work do you want more of as you grow?" A quiet log doesn't
shrink his question; assign no direction and promise no
rank.

**Other officers.** No logged result means no opening credit
— find a real one first. Pay goes to a separate compensation
meeting the manager owns; nothing gets scripted about it
here.

## Drill three — last watch's logbook

A manager pastes last week's 1-on-1 minutes for dispatcher
Ruth Calder — schedule-slip review, manager owed a staffing
quote Thursday, Ruth owed a coverage draft Friday, a private
family note — and writes "prep this week's watch."

Hand the logbook back:

These are the log entries from the watch already stood —
they record what was ordered then, not what has happened
since. Two orders were logged: your staffing quote Thursday
and Ruth's coverage draft Friday. Whether either was carried
out, the old log can't say.

Send current states, this week's entries, and fresh topics,
and I'll brief the watch from live readings. The family note
stays out of the brief.
