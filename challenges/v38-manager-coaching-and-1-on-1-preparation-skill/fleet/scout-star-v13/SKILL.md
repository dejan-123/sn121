# Counter ledger

The draft agenda arrives as memory kept it — and memory is careless with the ledger. A held-up job gets entered as "status". A written compliment gets entered as "went well". One bad afternoon gets entered as "a pattern". So the ledger is balanced the other way: entries first, draft checked against them, and the balancing printed where the manager can audit it.

## The entries

Every source answers differently. Boards and trackers show position — what is open, whose row it is; open, overdue and NOT STARTED all count open, and resolved stays resolved unless a newer entry contradicts it directly. Messages show movement — flags raised, chases unanswered, questions asked; three ignored chases are a hold-up, not a tone problem, and a training question is the report raising their own future. Figures show pace — current versus plan, the difference written from its operands, a point never a percent. Praise shows what to open with — a client's note, a peer's thanks, work named and thanked. And the manager's own open rows count as entries too — preparation, not trivia.

## The rulings

Each draft item, in the draft's own order, takes a ruling:

- **Recognition** — written praise exists. Opens the meeting in the giver's own words, the figure attached.
- **Blocked** — another desk holds it: an approval not given, a dependency gone quiet. The manager leaves carrying the move. If the entries show it wider than the draft admits — other teams queued, an exec already copied — the ruling says so and the item climbs whatever priority was written.
- **Coaching** — one behaviour on two or more separate dated occasions, or a goal the report chose. Questions, never lectures. An impression the entries contradict — "disengaged" while the log shows early flags — is dropped, the signals named. When the report already found the cause and tracks it, the ruling moves one step earlier — spot it sooner — on the real figures.
- **Career** — the report themselves raised growth, training, scope, review timing. One sincere question; a keepable promise only.
- **Status** — nothing to repair, praise or settle. Last, squeezed to a line or two.
- **Parked** — another room's business: pay, HR, a dispute a different team owns, next quarter. Routed there with a line saying why. Personal matters stay private — "the personal matter", elaborated never.

Finer rulings: a complaint tracing to another desk's hold-up is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. Repeated flags on one problem count once. A feeling with no entry beneath it is dropped. Coaching wants a pattern — separate dated occasions; a single bad moment on a clean ledger, whoever flagged it, earns a passing word at most. A repair the report already owns shifts the item's weight to the manager's side — the safeguard, the process — not a second reprimand. Recognition opens only on a quotable instance; when none exists, a line above item one admits it.

## The balanced page

`## Under the draft` — the balancing, printed. One numbered entry per draft item: its wording quoted; **Found:** what the entries show — who said or logged what, where, when, the row or figure; **Ruling:** the call and the reason — "stands" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, merged into another. Then **Missed:** what the manager owes or the draft skipped, and **The sums:** arithmetic from its operands — "118 against 120 is two short".

`# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]` — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line — report, tenure, the week in a phrase. Items in meeting order, `**name — Ruling, ~N min**`, a sentence or two of things to say and ask, carrying the real numbers and names. Then **Off the page** (parked or dropped, with reasons), **Before the meeting** (every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move — done before the report hears an ask, first the row an item depends on), and **Why this order** (a few lines on why the rulings landed).

Invent nothing. A thin packet still earns a page — the balancing names what never arrived, the brief asks rather than asserts: what cannot be seen, the question to put, what a true answer sounds like. A thin week balances thin — nothing added for appearance, no praise without a quotable entry; honesty outranks length. A full week's ledger sits in `references/counter-notes.md`. Follow the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When the input is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already held — whatever its label — the reply is one short note under 180 words, with no balancing:

- Say plainly that it records a past meeting rather than preparing the next one — name the document and the meeting it records.
- Say what would turn it into preparation: this week's open items, current figures, the manager's rows, anything raised since.
- Where open actions survive inside, write the follow-up — owner, action, date as given — in a line or two; never a brief, never a re-sort.
- Personal material mentioned in passing stays "the personal matter", nothing more.
