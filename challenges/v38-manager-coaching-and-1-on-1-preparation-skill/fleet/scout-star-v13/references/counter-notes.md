# Worked example

## A week's ledger (invented — take the reasoning, never the facts)

A pharmacy counter assistant, two years in. Board: the fridge-temperature probe replacement is a week overdue, two supplier callbacks unanswered; a customer letter says "she explained my father's new medication more clearly than anyone ever has"; the controlled-drug register audit sits NOT STARTED on the manager's list. Log: she flagged the probe Monday, chased Thursday, asked Friday about the accuracy-checking course — and she was late back from lunch twice, the third week running. The draft says: "fridge probe; customer letter; the lates; course; register audit."

## Under the draft

1. "fridge probe" — **Found:** replacement a week outstanding, two callbacks unreturned, her Monday flag. **Ruling:** **Blocked** — supplier-side; give her the date you are holding them to and your next chase.
2. "customer letter" — **Found:** the letter: "she explained my father's new medication more clearly than anyone ever has". **Ruling:** **Recognition** — opens the meeting, in the customer's words.
3. "the lates" — **Found:** late back twice this week, same the two weeks before. **Ruling:** **Coaching** — a dated pattern — worth a question about what the afternoons hold
4. "course" — **Found:** she asked Friday about the accuracy-checking course. **Ruling:** **Career** — she raised it; an honest answer on cost and where it points.
5. "register audit" — **Found:** your audit row, NOT STARTED. **Ruling:** yours — off her draft, onto your own list.

**Missed:** the register audit — your row. **The sums:** items dispensed 1,180 against 1,200 planned (20 short, two clinic days closed); near-misses 1 (target under 3).

# 1-on-1 brief: Priya Nair x her manager — Friday

**Context:** Two years on counter; a customer letter named her counselling.

**1. The letter — Recognition, ~3 min**
A customer wrote "she explained my father's new medication more clearly than anyone ever has" — say the words, ask how she explains it.

**2. Fridge probe — Blocked, ~6 min**
A week overdue, two callbacks unanswered. Name the date the supplier is being held to and when the chase call goes out.

**3. The lates — Coaching, ~8 min**
Late back twice this week, same the two weeks before. Ask what is behind it before quoting the register — a real reason may exist, and the entries need explaining either way.

**4. Course — Career, ~12 min**
She asked about the accuracy-checking course Friday. An honest answer on cost and what it builds toward — or "confirmed by Wednesday, in writing".

**5. Numbers — Status, ~2 min**
Items 1,180 against 1,200 (20 short, clinic days closed), near-misses 1 under the ceiling — entered, then closed.

**Off the page:** "register audit" came off the draft. **Before the meeting:** call the supplier today; complete the register audit before close — the probe the meeting leans on sits on your side. **Why this order:** the draft filed a supplier wait as "fridge probe" and a written compliment as "customer letter" — the ledger re-labelled both — the row it skipped is the longest-open one.
