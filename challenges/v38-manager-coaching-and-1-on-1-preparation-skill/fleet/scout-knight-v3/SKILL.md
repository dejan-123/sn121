# A board of pinned notes

Picture a small board before the meeting: each proposed
conversation is a note pinned only if something real holds it up.
The manager's draft supplies candidate notes; the week's record
decides which pins stay, which get reworded, and which come down.
What remains is a practical card for the conversation — not the
board itself.

Before composing anything, use `read_reference` on
`references/pin-checks.md`. It walks three worked packets,
including one that never becomes a meeting card. Study the checks
applied; invent your own output language each time.

## Check whether the packet can hold pins

Pins need fresh footing: current threads, action states, this
week's numbers, live topics from either party. A packet containing
only minutes or a transcript of a 1-on-1 that already ran records
what the last meeting agreed — it cannot show what changed since.
For that input, skip the card entirely. In a few lines: identify
the notes as prior-meeting minutes, restate each recorded action
with its owner and date, point out that current status is unknown,
and request this week's signals and topics. Never re-date an old
deadline as today's overdue item. Keep any personal disclosure
private — awareness, yes; scripted content, no. Minutes paired
with fresh signals are fine: pin from the signals, treat the
minutes as context. Requests aimed at a skip-level or a formal
rating want their own document — say which and stop.

## Give each pin its footing test

Tally the manager's topics, the report's submissions, and open
commitments both directions. Every pin gets the same test: which
specific observation — a message, a ticket, a figure, the report's
own words — holds it up? An adjective ("sloppy," "checked out")
is not footing. Keep the note, reword it from the evidence, and
merge duplicates while leaving each original a visible trace. Add
a note only when a supplied fact demands one; a tidy-looking set
matters less than an honest one.

Charge blocked work to its real holder. An overdue deliverable
waiting on another team's input, or on the manager's own pending
commitment, is not evidence about the report — it is a handoff
the manager must break loose, and it pins early. One person
holding approvals for many teams is a design fault: when the
draft files it as trivial feedback yet half the operation waits
on that queue, escalate its placement and say why — a rotation
the report already suggested is the starting proposal. And judge
tone from the whole thread: if someone escalated a genuine block
in a public channel but also kept a polite private request alive,
clear the block *and* keep one precise question about whether
public pressure was the right room. Ignoring the public message
misses the feedback; treating the tone as the story misses the
block.

Pin only what the footing allows:

- **Worth noticing**: a concrete result or specific thanks —
  pin it high. A vague urge to praise pins nothing.
- **In flight**: progress, holds, handoffs — pin the question
  that moves it, not a recital of the log.
- **One bad moment**: a single, already-corrected event pins a
  short what-changed question, not a development plan.
- **A real pattern**: repeated events or an openly worked-on
  skill pin a coaching question anchored to the instances; if
  they named the cause first, co-design the early-warning habit.
- **Where they want to go**: an aspiration the report voiced
  pins unhurried conversation, even in a thin week — ask the
  direction, never assign it, and never convert talk into a
  promised level or raise.

Pay decisions, family and health specifics, formal grievances,
and broad policy questions belong in other rooms — the card gets
a single line naming the room and owner, with nothing scripted.
Career discussion is not a pay ruling; it stays. A thin packet
makes a short board; an honest "what's the current state?" is
fine — an invented verdict is not.

## Count what the record counts

Where the packet offers numbers, show the arithmetic behind every
comparison — raw pair, operation, result: `11 done of 14 filed`,
`from 58 to 71 = +13 points`, `three holds, same dependency`.
Date gaps compute only against a meeting date the input gives.
Never fill a missing denominator, owner, or deadline with a
plausible one.

## Lay out the card

Open with the report's name, role, and a compact paragraph:
what moved this week and the one decision the manager should
not leave without making, figures shown worked. Then the
conversations in their real order — each with a plain label
(worth noticing / in flight / one bad moment / a real pattern /
where they want to go), the evidence under it, and a line the
manager can actually deliver — three sentences or fewer per
note. Earned credit opens; manager-held blockers lead the work
items; the report's own development question keeps real time.
Close with what the manager owes and, only if needed, one line
for other rooms. Short is fine when the packet is thin.

Final pass: every drafted topic is pinned, merged with a named
sibling, or sent elsewhere with a reason; no private content
scripted; no name, figure, or owner invented; no board jargon
in the card itself.
