# Ruling drills — the docket written end to end

Four invented packets, ruled the way the sheet should rule them. The
names, figures and products are invented; the rulings are the part to
carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal week with a mis-captioned item

Packet: report is "Marta, senior platform engineer". Manager's draft
agenda: (a) "discipline issue — Marta snapped at QA in the team channel",
(b) "why is the payments migration late", (c) "quick win — mention the
dashboard launch". Packet's record: QA's lead posted a public thank-you
naming Marta for unblocking their release by building the replay tool
ahead of schedule; the channel exchange shows Marta objecting — sharply
but on the substance — to QA shipping a build that failed its own gate;
the migration is 3 of 8 endpoints done and waiting on the security
team's sign-off requested 9 days ago; the dashboard launched, and the
support lead messaged that ticket volume on it dropped by half in the
first week.

Rulings:

- Item (a): **Amended**. The record shows one substantive objection to a
  build that failed its own checks — filed as a discipline issue, ruled
  as a single instance with one narrow question. Talking point: "The QA
  exchange was on the merits, and QA was already thanking you for the
  release help. One thing to keep in view: that disagreement happened in
  the team channel rather than in the ticket — does moving it there cost
  you anything?" The objection stands; the venue takes one question and
  no more.
- Item (b): **Amended — charged to the deciding hand.** 3 of 8 done, and
  the stall is a 9-day-old sign-off with security, not Marta's pace.
  Talking point: "Migration is waiting on security's sign-off — 9 days
  outstanding. I'll chase the reviewer today and get a date; is there a
  contact on that team I should escalate to if it slips again?"
- Item (c): **Heard as the opener.** Two attributed credits this week —
  the QA lead's public thanks and the support figure. Open with them
  before any process item; credit lands first.
- The manager's own filed items all appear on the table; nothing filed
  disappears.

Sheet: `# Week sheet: Marta, senior platform engineer` — posture line
("a strong week with one decision waiting on another team"), figures
shown as work ("3 of 8 endpoints migrated; sign-off outstanding 9 days"),
then the docket table with all three filed captions, what the record
shows, and each ruling — then talking points, referrals, gaps.

## Drill 2 — a thin packet ruled honestly

Packet: report is "Ilya, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep my
weekly with Ilya — want to talk about his promo timeline, and whether
he's ready to own the ingest rewrite." No action log, no figures, no
channel excerpts. One calendar item: Ilya asked in passing about salary
bands for the next level.

This is thin, not invalid: it aims at the coming meeting, so a docket
is still entered — ruled item by item. Only the wrong kind of document
(drill 3) is referred whole.

Rulings:

- Promo timeline: **Heard — but split by forum.** Ilya's direction and
  the manager's readiness question belong in this meeting, with real
  time: "You asked about the next level — here's what I see as the gap
  evidence and what I'd need documented; what scope would you want to
  take to build it?" The pay-band question is **Referred**: comp bands
  live in the compensation review, not a weekly — the sheet names the
  venue (the comp review with the comp partner) and the owner, one line,
  no talking points.
- Readiness for the ingest rewrite: **Continued**. The packet has no
  delivery record to weigh. It is heard as the honest question, not as
  an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have your recent
  delivery record in front of me — walk me through the last few weeks:
  what shipped, what's stuck, what you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the page. A thin sheet with honest
  questions beats a padded one: the header names what the packet lacks
  ("no action log, figures or channel record this week").

## Drill 3 — a packet that is not a current packet

Packet: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Dana —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list three
actions: Dana to send the benchmarking doc (due Thursday), the manager
to confirm the conference budget, a follow-up on the mentoring pilot.

Ruling: **whole packet referred — no docket entered.** The reply, under
180 words, titled `# Not a current packet`:

- What it is: minutes of a meeting that already happened — they record
  what was agreed, not what has moved since.
- Why a docket misleads: the sheet would repeat settled items as if
  live and invent status the record does not have.
- What the record does support: a follow-up on the three open actions,
  listed with owners and dates exactly as filed — Dana's doc (due
  Thursday), the budget confirmation, the mentoring pilot.
- What to send instead: this week's action log, any figures, and
  whatever Dana raised or the manager wants on the table.
- Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — it is not repeated,
  not summarized, not hinted at in the referral.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own matter

Packet: report is "Sana, senior backend engineer". Manager's draft
agenda: (a) "remind Sana to clear her review queue — five design docs
are waiting on her and two partner teams are blocked"; (b) "Sana seems
quiet in standup lately". Record: five docs are assigned to Sana because
the process names her as sole approver for cross-team changes; she
shipped two of her own features this sprint and flagged the queue
problem in the last retro, proposing a rotation; "quiet" appears once,
in the manager's note.

Rulings:

- Item (a): **Amended — the deciding hand is the manager's.** A
  sole-approver rule stalling two teams is a capacity matter the
  manager owns, not feedback to deliver. It is heard as the manager's
  own decision, high in the order: "Your rotation proposal is the right
  starting point — I want to approve it this week and take it to the
  partner leads. What load should sit with each reviewer so nothing
  queues behind one person again?" Not a reminder to work faster.
- Item (b): **Struck.** One manager observation with no record behind
  it; the sprint shows two shipped features and a raised process fix.
  The docket rules it plainly absent rather than inventing a wellbeing
  segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Sana's retro proposal — is
  surfaced on the sheet as the manager's commitment, with whom and when.

## What every drill shares

- The record rules; the caption is only the filing.
- Credit opens when it is attributable — a named person, a concrete
  result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its substance
  and takes exactly one venue question.
- What another hand controls is charged to that hand.
- One occurrence is an instance, never a pattern.
- Missing records are ruled as gaps — `Continued` on the matter, the
  honest question in the slot — never covered over.
- Out-of-forum matters get a venue and an owner, never talking points.
- Every figure shows its own working; every item the manager filed gets
  a row and a ruling.
