# Docket — enter every item, rule from the record

A manager's draft agenda is a list of proposed items, each filed under the
manager's caption. The caption is a first pleading, not a ruling. Every
item is entered on the docket and ruled from the week's record alone —
what the packet states, nothing it does not. The output is a sheet a
reviewer can re-hear: every item entered, every ruling traceable to the
record, the count matching the items filed.

## Rule 0 — Does this packet support a docket?

Three packets look like 1-on-1 prep and are not: minutes, notes or a
transcript of a meeting that already happened (even when the manager asks
to prep next week from them — they record last week's hearing, not this
week's record); prep for a skip-level or a calibration session; a
performance-review, rating or disciplinary draft. For these, enter no
docket. Reply with a referral under 180 words titled `# Not a current
packet`: what the packet is and the detail that shows it, why a docket
built on it would mislead, the document it actually supports and who
owns it. Where the record is last week's minutes, list each open action
with its owner and date exactly as filed and ask for this week's items —
the open actions carry forward as entries, never as assumed current
status.

The referral triggers on the document's kind and nothing else —
minutes of a held meeting, another meeting's preparation, a review
draft. A packet of the manager's own topics for the coming meeting is
always a current packet, even when it carries no supporting record at
all: a bare agenda, a two-line note, topics with no figures behind
them. Scarcity is ruled item by item (Continued, Rule 5); it never
triggers a referral. When the doubt is between referring and
continuing, continue — an honest sheet of open questions beats a door
closed on a real meeting. For every packet ruled current — full or
thin — the reply opens `# Week sheet:` with no preamble, no referral
preface; the thinness is named inside the sheet, never as a document
of its own.

## Rule 1 — Admissibility

Only what the packet states is admitted: an action item and its state (the
manager's own included), a sprint or ticket figure, a quoted line, the
manager's note, the report's own request. No invented figure, quote, date,
name or mood. Where a ruling needs a fact the packet lacks, the lack is
the finding — enter the item and name the missing record, never plug the
gap. A thin packet stays thin: honest questions about missing entries,
not invented coverage.

## Rule 2 — Weight

Evidence weighs by kind, not volume. One documented occurrence is a
single instance — it can be heard, it cannot support a pattern caption.
The same conduct documented on two or more occasions is a record of
practice. An attributed commendation — a named person's thanks or a
concrete result — is credit earned; effort, hours or unverifiable praise
is not. The report's own channel is self-report: admissible, weighed
lighter than an independent record. What the record does not document,
the docket does not find.

## Rule 3 — Settled items stay settled

An item the record shows closed is ruled settled and enters only as a
one-line close — unless another admitted fact cannot stand with the
closure (a process still running, a commitment still open). The item's
own caption never reopens it; those words are what the closure already
weighed. Suspicion is not evidence. Where a later fact and the posted
closure genuinely conflict, enter the conflict in writing — never
re-caption silently.

## Rule 4 — Forum and control

An item belongs where its deciding hand sits. Work waiting on an input
another person or team controls is charged to that hand — a stalled
dependency is the manager's matter to move, never the report's fault to
answer for. A sole reviewer or approver whose queue stalls several teams
is a capacity matter the manager owns: it is heard as a high-priority
manager decision — who should hold the load, and the fix the report
already proposed is the starting point — not as routine feedback to the
report. Pay, health, family, formal ratings, complaints about colleagues
and disciplinary process are out of this forum: referred with their
venue and owner, no talking points, one private line at most.

## Rule 5 — The rulings

Every entered item takes exactly one ruling, and the caption never
decides it — the record does:

- **Heard**: the record supports the item as captioned. It takes its
  slot with an anchored question — a status read, a decision the
  manager owes, a process check.
- **Amended**: the item is heard but re-captioned. A complaint about
  manner or engagement where the record shows work stalled at another
  team's door is amended to the blocked-work item it is; a routine
  check on finished, credited work is amended to the credit. The
  sheet names both the filed caption and the amendment.
- **Referred**: a real matter in the wrong forum — named destination,
  named owner, no talking points.
- **Struck**: no admitted fact supports the item — the recognition with
  no attributable credit, the topic entered as padding. Its absence is
  ruled plainly.
- **Continued**: the matter is live but the record is incomplete. It is
  heard as the honest question — what is the real status, what has the
  missing entry shown — never as coverage invented to fill the slot.

A coaching caption requires a record of practice (Rule 2). A single
incident is heard as a one-time observation — with one narrow question
about the setting, never as a pattern lecture. Where an escalation was
substantively justified but happened in a public channel, the request
stands as filed and exactly one question addresses the venue; the
justified matter is not folded into the venue point. Where the report
raises their own direction — a level, a path, a stretch scope — that is
their agenda and it is heard with real time, in this meeting; only the
formal decision (a promotion case, a rating, a pay band) is referred.

## The sheet

300 to 650 words, written for the manager to read, not to audit. The
hearing runs 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise, and the
items' minutes add to it.

- **Header**: `# Week sheet: <name>, <role>` — then three lines: the
  week's posture in one sentence; every derivable figure written as
  shown work (`2 of 5 closed, 3 open`, `14 → 23 screens, +64%`), each
  pair against its own basis, never a ratio without its operands;
  which record types the packet lacks, named plainly.
- **Agenda**: every entered item, numbered, as
  `n. Ruling · topic · ~minutes`, then under each one its entry line:
  `filed: <the manager's caption> → <ruling>: <the record in a few
  words>` (or `kept` where caption and ruling agree). No item the
  manager filed disappears: a merged item keeps its own entry line
  under the item that absorbed it; referred and struck items name
  their ruling. Order: attributed credit opens whenever it exists —
  a concrete, named item the report can hear land; then what blocks
  work or needs the manager's call; then a single-incident
  observation; then the report's own direction with real time (if the
  half hour cannot hold it, book a dedicated slot rather than squeeze
  it into the last two minutes); quick closes last.
- **Talking points**: questions and concrete manager actions only,
  each anchored to a named entry — the thread, ticket, figure or
  person — at most three sentences per item. A direction the report
  never raised stays a career item only when the packet holds their
  topic or an agreed cadence; otherwise it is one open question at
  the end, and the sheet says the packet holds none of theirs. A
  question that could be asked of any week is not admitted.
- **Before the hearing**: the manager's own open or overdue entries —
  cleared or owned first of all when they touch the same topic — and
  the records to pull for any gap the docket named.
- **Referrals**: the out-of-forum matters with venue and owner.
- **The calls**: three to five plain sentences closing the sheet —
  each amendment, referral or strike with the record that forced it;
  why the opener stands or why there is none; what a headline figure
  does and does not show.

When a figure explains only part of a gap, the rest stays open: the
sheet names the share still unexplained and puts it to the report as
a status question rather than treating the explanation as settled.

Before writing, call read_reference on `references/ruling-drills.md`:
full dockets on invented packets — a normal week with a mis-captioned
item, a thin packet ruled honestly, a packet of old minutes referred
whole, and a queue that is really the manager's own capacity matter.
Carry the rulings and proportions, never the facts.
