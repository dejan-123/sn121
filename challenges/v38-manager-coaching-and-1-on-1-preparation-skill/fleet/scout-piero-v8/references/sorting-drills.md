# Sorting drills — full sheets on invented bags

Four invented bags, sorted the way the office should sort them. The
names, figures and products are invented; the sorts are the part to
carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal bag with a mis-addressed parcel

Packet: report is "Marta, senior platform engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "discipline issue — Marta snapped at QA in the team channel",
(b) "why is the payments migration late", (c) "quick win — mention
the dashboard launch". The tracking: QA's lead posted a public
thank-you naming Marta for unblocking their release by building the
replay tool early; the channel exchange shows Marta objecting —
sharply but on the substance — to QA shipping a build that failed
its own gate; the migration is 3 of 8 endpoints done, waiting on a
security sign-off requested 9 days ago; the dashboard launched and
the support lead messaged that ticket volume on it halved in week
one.

Sorts:

- Parcel (a): **Re-addressed**. One substantive objection to a build
  that failed its own checks — addressed as a discipline issue,
  delivered as a one-time note with one narrow question. Delivery
  note: "The QA exchange was on the merits — QA was already
  thanking you for the release help. One thing to keep in view:
  that disagreement ran in the team channel rather than the ticket.
  Does moving it there cost you anything?" The objection stands;
  the channel takes one question and no more.
- Parcel (b): **Re-addressed — another office's post.** 3 of 8
  done, and the stall is a 9-day-old sign-off with security, not
  Marta's pace. Delivery note: "Migration is waiting on security's
  sign-off — nine days outstanding. I'll chase the reviewer today
  and get a date; who should I escalate to if it slips again?"
- Parcel (c): **Delivered first.** Two attributable credits — the
  QA lead's public thanks and the support figure. Open with them
  before any process parcel; credit delivers first.
- Every addressed parcel gets an office line and a sort; nothing
  addressed disappears.

Sheet: `# Sort sheet: Marta, senior platform engineer` — picture
line ("a strong week with one parcel waiting on another office"),
figures shown as work, then route order with all three addresses,
the event behind each, and each sort — then delivery notes, first
collection, forwarded list, postmaster's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin bag sorted honestly

Packet: report is "Ilya, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep my
weekly with Ilya — want to talk about his promo timeline, and whether
he's ready to own the ingest rewrite." No action log, no figures, no
channel excerpts. One calendar item: Ilya asked in passing about
salary bands for the next level.

Thin is not invalid: the bag aims at the coming delivery, so the
office still logs it — sorted parcel by parcel. Only the wrong kind
of bag (drill 3) is sent back whole. The reply opens
`# Sort sheet: Ilya, staff engineer` — never a send-back preface.

Sorts:

- Promo timeline: **Delivered — split by office.** Ilya's direction
  and the manager's readiness question belong in this delivery,
  with real time: "You asked about the next level — here's the gap
  evidence I see and what I'd need documented; what scope would you
  take to build it?" The pay-band question is **Forwarded**: bands
  are decided in the compensation review — the sheet names the
  destination (the comp review with the comp partner) and the
  owner, one line, no delivery notes.
- Readiness for the ingest rewrite: **Held**. The bag has no
  delivery record to weigh. It delivers as the honest question, not
  an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have your recent
  delivery record in front of me — walk me through the last few
  weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the sheet. A thin sheet of
  honest questions beats a padded one; the sheet head logs what the
  bag lacks and first collection lists what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a bag that is not this week's post

Packet: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Dana —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list three
actions: Dana to send the benchmarking doc (due Thursday), the
manager to confirm the conference budget, a follow-up on the
mentoring pilot.

Sort: **whole bag sent back — nothing logged.** The reply, under
150 words, titled `# Not this week's post`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was agreed,
not what has moved since. The document this supports is a follow-up
on the open actions, not a sheet: Dana's benchmarking doc (due
Thursday), your budget confirmation, the mentoring pilot follow-up —
owners and dates exactly as filed. For a weekly sheet, send this
week's action log, any figures, and whatever Dana raised or you
want addressed. Nothing in the material is postmarked after that
meeting, so a sheet would replay old tracking or invent new events.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not repeated,
not summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own parcel

Packet: report is "Sana, senior backend engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "remind Sana to clear her review queue — five design docs are
waiting on her and two partner teams are blocked"; (b) "Sana seems
quiet in standup lately". The tracking: the five docs are assigned
because the process names her sole approver for cross-team changes;
she shipped two features this sprint and flagged the queue in the
last retro, proposing a rotation; "quiet" appears once, in the
manager's note.

Sorts:

- Parcel (a): **Re-addressed — the office that can deliver it is
  the manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two teams is a
  capacity matter the manager owns, not an address to work faster.
  It delivers early as the manager's own decision: "Your rotation
  proposal is the right starting point — I want to approve it this
  week and take it to the partner leads. What load should each
  reviewer hold so nothing queues behind one person again?"
- Parcel (b): **Returned.** One manager observation with no events
  behind it; the sprint shows two shipped features and a raised
  process fix. The sheet logs its absence plainly rather than
  inventing a wellbeing segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Sana's retro proposal —
  is listed under first collection as the manager's commitment,
  with whom and when.

## What every drill shares

- The tracking decides; the address is only the filing.
- Attributable credit delivers first — a named person, a concrete
  result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its
  substance and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another office delivers is logged to that office.
- One occurrence is a single scan, never a pattern.
- Missing events deliver as `Held` — the honest question in the
  slot — never covered over.
- Other-office parcels get a destination and an owner, never
  delivery notes.
- Every figure shows its own working; every addressed parcel gets
  an office line and a sort.
- A thin bag still gets a sheet. The send-back is for the wrong
  kind of bag only — and it stays under 150 words.
