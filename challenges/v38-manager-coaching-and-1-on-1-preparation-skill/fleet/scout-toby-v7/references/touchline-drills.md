# Touchline drills — full sheets on invented squads

Four invented squad lists, picked the way the sheet should pick them.
The names, figures and products are invented; the calls are the part
to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal match with a mis-named pick

Packet: report is "Marta, senior platform engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "discipline issue — Marta snapped at QA in the team channel",
(b) "why is the payments migration late", (c) "quick win — mention
the dashboard launch". The form: QA's lead posted a public
thank-you naming Marta for unblocking their release by building the
replay tool early; the channel exchange shows Marta objecting —
sharply but on the substance — to QA shipping a build that failed
its own gate; the migration is 3 of 8 endpoints done, waiting on a
security sign-off requested 9 days ago; the dashboard launched and
the support lead messaged that ticket volume on it halved in week
one.

Calls:

- Pick (a): **Re-positioned**. One substantive objection to a build
  that failed its own checks — named as a discipline issue, started
  as a one-time note with one narrow question. Sideline note: "The
  QA exchange was on the merits — QA was already thanking you for
  the release help. One thing to keep in view: that disagreement ran
  in the team channel rather than the ticket. Does moving it there
  cost you anything?" The objection stands; the channel takes one
  question and no more.
- Pick (b): **Re-positioned — another side's ball.** 3 of 8 done,
  and the stall is a 9-day-old sign-off with security, not Marta's
  pace. Sideline note: "Migration is waiting on security's sign-off —
  nine days outstanding. I'll chase the reviewer today and get a
  date; who should I escalate to if it slips again?"
- Pick (c): **Started to open.** Two attributable credits — the QA
  lead's public thanks and the support figure. Open with them before
  any process pick; credit starts first.
- Every named pick gets a sheet line and a call; nothing named
  disappears.

Sheet: `# Team sheet: Marta, senior platform engineer` — picture
line ("a strong week with one call waiting on another side"),
figures shown as work, then starting order with all three names,
the performance behind each, and each call — then sideline notes,
warm-up, sent-across list, coach's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin list worked honestly

Packet: report is "Ilya, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep my
weekly with Ilya — want to talk about his promo timeline, and whether
he's ready to own the ingest rewrite." No action log, no figures, no
channel excerpts. One calendar item: Ilya asked in passing about
salary bands for the next level.

Thin is not invalid: the list aims at the coming match, so the
sheet still logs it — decided pick by pick. Only the wrong kind of
list (drill 3) is sent back whole. The reply opens
`# Team sheet: Ilya, staff engineer` — never a send-back preface.

Calls:

- Promo timeline: **Started — split by side.** Ilya's direction and
  the manager's readiness question belong in this match, with real
  time: "You asked about the next level — here's the gap evidence I
  see and what I'd need documented; what scope would you take to
  build it?" The pay-band question is **Sent across**: bands are
  decided in the compensation review — the sheet names the venue
  (the comp review with the comp partner) and the owner, one line,
  no sideline notes.
- Readiness for the ingest rewrite: **Fitness watch**. The list has
  no delivery record to weigh. It starts as the honest question, not
  an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have your recent
  delivery record in front of me — walk me through the last few
  weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the sheet. A thin sheet of
  honest questions beats a padded one; the sheet head logs what the
  list lacks and warm-up lists what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a list that is not this week's squad

Packet: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Dana —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list three
actions: Dana to send the benchmarking doc (due Thursday), the
manager to confirm the conference budget, a follow-up on the
mentoring pilot.

Call: **whole list sent back — nothing started.** The reply, under
150 words, titled `# Not this week's squad`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was agreed, not
what has moved since. The document this supports is a follow-up on
the open actions, not a sheet: Dana's benchmarking doc (due
Thursday), your budget confirmation, the mentoring pilot follow-up —
owners and dates exactly as filed. For a weekly sheet, send this
week's action log, any figures, and whatever Dana raised or you want
named. Nothing in the material is dated after that meeting, so a
sheet would replay old form or invent new.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not repeated, not
summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own call

Packet: report is "Sana, senior backend engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "remind Sana to clear her review queue — five design docs are
waiting on her and two partner teams are blocked"; (b) "Sana seems
quiet in standup lately". The form: the five docs are assigned
because the process names her sole approver for cross-team changes;
she shipped two features this sprint and flagged the queue in the
last retro, proposing a rotation; "quiet" appears once, in the
manager's note.

Calls:

- Pick (a): **Re-positioned — the side that can make the call is the
  manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two teams is a capacity
  matter the manager owns, not a name to play faster. It starts
  early as the manager's own decision: "Your rotation proposal is
  the right starting point — I want to approve it this week and take
  it to the partner leads. What load should each reviewer hold so
  nothing queues behind one person again?"
- Pick (b): **Dropped.** One manager observation with no form behind
  it; the sprint shows two shipped features and a raised process
  fix. The sheet logs its absence plainly rather than inventing a
  wellbeing segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Sana's retro proposal —
  is listed under warm-up as the manager's commitment, with whom and
  when.

## What every drill shares

- The form decides; the name is only the pick.
- Attributable credit starts first — a named person, a concrete
  result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its substance
  and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another side owns is logged to that side.
- One occurrence is a single outing, never a run of form.
- Missing performances start as `Fitness watch` — the honest
  question in the slot — never covered over.
- Other-side calls get a venue and an owner, never sideline notes.
- Every figure shows its own working; every named pick gets a sheet
  line and a call.
- A thin list still gets a sheet. The send-back is for the wrong
  kind of list only — and it stays under 150 words.
