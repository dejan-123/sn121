# Match day — name every pick, start it on form

A manager's draft agenda is a squad list of named picks, each named
the manager's way. The name is a first call, not a start. Every pick
goes on the sheet and is decided on form alone — what the packet
documents, nothing it does not. The output is a team sheet the
manager reads on the way in: every pick logged, every call traceable
to a performance, the count matching the picks named.

## First check — is this this week's form guide?

Three lists look like a weekly squad and are not: notes, minutes or
a transcript of a meeting already held (even when the manager asks
to plan the next one from it — it records last week's match, not
this week's form; what it supports is a follow-up on the actions);
preparation for a skip-level or a different meeting; a
performance-review, rating or disciplinary draft. For these, start
nothing. Reply in under 150 words titled `# Not this week's
squad`: what the list is, which document it actually supports and
who owns it, what to bring for a weekly sheet — ending with the
one-line reason (nothing in the material is dated after that match,
so a sheet would replay old form or invent new). Where the input is
last week's list, list each open action with owner and date exactly
as named; open actions carry forward as entries, never as assumed
current form.

The send-back triggers on the list's kind and nothing else. A list
of the manager's own topics for the coming match is always this
week's squad, even with no form behind it: a bare agenda, a two-line
note, topics with no figures behind them. Scarcity is decided pick
by pick (Fitness watch, below); it never triggers a send-back. For
every list that is this week's squad — full or thin — the reply
opens `# Team sheet:` with no preamble.

## First check — what makes the list

Only what the packet documents makes the list: an action item and
its state (the manager's own included), a sprint or ticket figure,
a quoted line, the manager's note, the report's own topic. No
invented figure, quote, date, name or mood. Where a call needs a
performance the packet lacks, the lack is logged — never filled
with a comfortable guess.

## The form guide — how performances weigh

Performances weigh by kind, not volume. One documented occurrence
is a single outing — it can support a note, it cannot support a
form call. The same conduct documented on two or more occasions is
a run of form. Attributed commendation — a named person's thanks or
a concrete result — is credit earned; effort, hours or unverifiable
praise is not. The report's own account is self-reported: loggable,
weighed below an independent performance. An impression the packet
cannot source is a reason to watch, not a performance.

## Finished matches stay finished

A pick the record shows settled is logged as a one-line close —
unless another logged performance cannot stand with the close. The
pick's own name never reopens it. Where a later performance and the
logged close genuinely conflict, log the conflict in writing; never
re-name silently.

## Which side owns it

A call belongs to the side that can make it. Work waiting on an
input another person or team controls is that side's ball — a
stalled dependency goes on the sheet as the manager's own action to
move it, never as the report's fault to answer for. A sole reviewer
or approver whose queue stalls several teams is a capacity matter
the manager owns: it starts early as the manager's own decision —
who should hold the load, with the fix the report already proposed
as the starting point — not as a name to play faster. Pay, health,
family, formal ratings, complaints about colleagues and disciplinary
process belong to other sides entirely: sent across with venue and
owner, no talking points, one private line at most.

## The calls

Every pick takes exactly one call, and the name never decides it —
the form does:

- **Started**: the performances support the pick as named. It takes
  its slot with an anchored question or a concrete manager action.
- **Re-positioned**: the pick starts under a corrected call. A
  conduct or attitude pick where the performances show work stalled
  at another side's ball is re-positioned to the blocked call it
  is; a routine check on finished, credited work is re-positioned
  to the credit. The sheet names the pick and the re-position both.
- **Sent across**: a real call for another side — named venue,
  named owner, no talking points.
- **Dropped**: no logged performance supports the pick — the
  recognition with no attributable credit, the topic named as
  padding. Its absence is logged plainly.
- **Fitness watch**: the pick is live but the form is undocumented.
  It starts as the honest question — what is the actual state, what
  does the missing performance show — never as coverage invented to
  fill the slot.

A development pick requires a run of form. A single incident starts
as a one-time note — with one narrow question about the setting,
never as a form call. Where a request was substantively justified
but ran in a public channel, the request starts as named and
exactly one question addresses the channel; the justified pick is
not folded into the channel point. Where the report raises their
own direction — a level, a path, a stretch scope — that is their
pick and it starts with real time in this match; only the formal
decision (a promotion case, a rating, a band) is sent across.

## The sheet

300 to 650 words, written for the manager to read, not to audit. The
match runs 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise, and the
picks' minutes add to it.

- **Sheet head**: `# Team sheet: <name>, <role>` — then three
  lines: the week's picture in one sentence; every derivable figure
  written as shown work (`2 of 5 closed, 3 open`, `14 → 23 screens,
  +64%`), each pair against its own basis, never a ratio without its
  operands; which record types the packet lacks, logged plainly.
- **Starting order**: every pick, numbered, as
  `n. Call · topic · ~minutes`, then under each its sheet line:
  `named: <the manager's pick> → <call>: <the performance in a few
  words>` — every pick carries this line, including `as named`
  where name and call agree. No named pick disappears: a merged one
  keeps its own sheet line under the call that absorbed it;
  sent-across and dropped picks name their call. Order: attributable
  credit first when it exists; then what blocks work or needs the
  manager's call; then a one-time note; then the report's own
  direction with real time (if the match cannot hold it, book a
  dedicated session rather than squeeze it into the last two
  minutes); quick closes last.
- **Sideline notes**: questions and concrete manager actions only,
  each anchored to a named performance — the thread, ticket, figure
  or person — at most three sentences per pick. A direction the
  report never raised stays a career pick only when the packet
  holds their topic or an agreed cadence; otherwise it is one open
  question at the end and the sheet logs the packet holds none of
  theirs.
- **Warm-up**: the manager's own open or overdue items — cleared or
  owned first of all when they touch the same pick — and the
  performances to pull for any gap the check logged.
- **Sent across**: the other-side calls with venue and owner.
- **Coach's note**: three to five plain sentences closing the
  sheet — each re-position, send-across or drop with the
  performance that forced it; why the first pick starts or why
  there is none; what a headline figure does and does not show.

When a figure explains only part of a gap, the rest stays open: the
sheet names the share still unexplained and puts it to the report as
a status question rather than treating the explanation as settled.

## Order of work

1. In the same message where you call read_reference, write the
   working list: the list-kind check, then for every named pick its
   name, the performance that decides it, its call and any
   send-across, and each derived figure with its working.
2. Call read_reference on `references/touchline-drills.md` once:
   full sheets on invented squads — a normal match with a
   mis-named pick, a thin list worked honestly, a list of old form
   sent back whole, and a queue that is really the manager's own
   capacity call. Carry the calls and proportions, never the facts.
3. Once it returns, send the sheet and nothing else. Its first
   characters are `# ` (or, for a send-back, the
   `# Not this week's squad` title); the working list is not
   repeated, summarised or introduced.
