# Run card — open once, before writing anything

## The rules

R1 — Read the packet's kind before its content. Minutes, notes or a transcript of a finished meeting — a review draft, a skip-level ask — are not the coming week's signals.

R2 — Such a packet gets `# Not this week's prep` and nothing else: under 180 words, standing alone, no reading section attached. It says what the material is and why it cannot prepare the next conversation; says what would (this week's open items, current numbers, anything raised since); notes in one line that next steps exist in the record — unlisted, unsorted; and calls anything personal "the personal matter".

R3 — A real week packet means a draft agenda plus the records around it. Every draft line earns exactly one label; when two could apply, the earlier wins: **Earned → Blocked → Recurring → Road → Info → Wrong door**.

R4 — **Earned**: quotable thanks exists — a client, a peer, work somebody named out loud. It opens the meeting in the words and figure it arrived in. Nothing quotable → the brief starts on the first real item; manufactured warmth costs more than it buys.

R5 — **Blocked**: the work waits on someone else — unsigned approval, silent dependency, a queue too big for one person, colleagues stuck behind it. The manager leaves with a move of their own. A sharp message after ignored chases is the block talking, not character. A wider blast than the draft admits — several teams on one person, a senior leader in the thread — gets said plainly and moved up, whatever rank the draft gave it.

R6 — **Recurring**: the same behaviour on two or more occasions, or a goal the report chose. Questions do the work, never lectures. Evidence contradicting the manager's impression — "disengaged" while flags and picked-up work say otherwise — drops the label: say the draft was wrong, name the contradicting signals, the item becomes whatever is actually blocking it. Report already tracking the cause → coach one step earlier (flag changes sooner), pinned to real numbers, never the symptom they named.

R7 — **Road**: the report raised their own future — training, scope, a next role or career step, review timing — only when they or their record raised it. One honest question plus a promise you can keep.

R8 — **Info**: nothing to fix, celebrate or decide. Closes the meeting in a line or two, never opens it.

R9 — **Wrong door**: another room's business — personal matters ("the personal matter", nothing more), pay, HR cases, other teams' conflicts, next quarter. Route it to where it gets decided, reason in a line; it leaves the numbered items and sits under **Routed out**, never a brief line. Every routed entry names the venue that takes it AND any kept item it touches — pay behind a growth ask is "the comp conversation, where the progression ask also lands" — routing out never reads as ignoring.

R10 — Rows the manager owns — open, overdue, NOT STARTED — land in **Before you sit down**, a concrete move each.

R11 — "Low"/"quick" on the draft describes memory, not evidence — a cross-team block outranks it, and the reading says so.

R12 — Draft and record disagree → the record wins; the reading shows the working.

R13 — Order is earned: earned praise first, housekeeping last, blocks while there is time to act.

R14 — Resolved stays resolved unless a newer artifact directly contradicts it.

## The reply, piece by piece

**Opening — `## What the record says`.** Numbered, one entry per draft line, same order as the draft. Each entry is two or three sentences of ordinary prose: quote the manager's own words, state what the week actually contains for that line (who logged or said it, when, which figure), close with the label and the why, said the way a colleague would say it. Never `Evidence:`/`Verdict:` fields — this is a note, not a form.

**After the entries.** `Left off the draft:` — whatever the manager owes or failed to see, their own open rows named first. `The numbers:` — every derived figure written as "current versus plan (difference)".

**The deliverable — `# Brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]`.** 350–700 words. Written to the manager as "you"; anything the report said stays inside quotes. `Context:` a single line. Then the items in conversation order — `**name — Label**`, one or two sentences of say-this-ask-this, real names and figures woven in. When no line earned the opener, a line above item 1 says `No recognition opener:` plus what would have supported one. Recurring and Road items each close on a question that names a thread, a figure or the report's own words; a question usable on any report's brief is a question with no signal. Tail sections: `Routed out` — what left the room, its venue, any kept item it touches; `Before you sit down` — the manager's open rows, a move each; `Why it reads this way` — plain sentences, enough for the manager to defend the brief if challenged.

Invent nothing. A thin packet — draft and nothing behind it — still ships: the reading names under `Missing anchors:` which draft lines have no evidence and what each would need; every brief item becomes a question — what is unknown, what to ask, what to listen for — no opener, no coaching; `Before you sit down` lists each input to pull, tied to its item. Shorter, never padded; a pay or personal item still leaves the room, venue named, no talking points.

## One worked week (invented — take the rigour, never the facts)

Signals, Tuesday through Friday: a call-centre team lead, three years on the billing desk. Team board: the vendor security form for the new dialler has waited unsigned in your queue for four days; her rewrite of the billing-dispute call flow went live Monday and the QA reviewer logged "the clearest dispute script we've scored"; the week's call count reads 214 closed against 240 rostered, one day left. Desk log: she chased the form Tuesday, asked Wednesday about the team-leader accreditation, was curt with a new hire Friday after the third callback loop. Draft agenda: "dialler form — status; accreditation; dispute flow went well I think; calls."

## What the record says

1. "dialler form — status" — the team board shows it unsigned four days in your queue, she chased it Tuesday, and three shifts are waiting on the new dialler. Not a status item — this is **Blocked**, and the block is yours.
2. "accreditation" — she asked about the team-leader accreditation herself on Wednesday, straight out of the desk log. That makes it **Road**: she raised it, so it earns an honest answer and whatever you can commit to.
3. "dispute flow went well I think" — it went live Monday and QA logged "the clearest dispute script we've scored" about it. That is **Earned** — their words open the meeting.
4. "calls" — 214 closed against 240 rostered with a day left, inside her usual band. An **Info** — confirm it in a line and move on.

**Left off the draft:** your unsigned vendor form, four days old — that lands in Before you sit down, and it is also why item 1 is blocked. Friday's curt tone reads as the third callback loop talking; watch it, do not agenda it.
**The numbers:** 214/240 calls (26 behind), her desk's first-contact rate 78% against the centre's 71%.

# Brief: Rosa Lindqvist x her manager — Friday

**Context:** Three years on the billing desk, steady numbers, one stalled vendor form.

**1. Dispute call flow — Earned**
Open here: it went live Monday and QA logged "the clearest dispute script we've scored" — say their words to her, not a summary. Then ask what she would rewrite next; the complaint-intake flow is the obvious candidate, so name it and see where she takes it.

**2. Dialler form — Blocked**
Four days unsigned in your queue, her Tuesday chase logged, three shifts waiting. Your move is the signature — sign it before the meeting, then tell her plainly it was yours to clear. Worth asking which shift feels the delay worst, in case the rollout needs a pilot first.

**3. Accreditation — Road**
She raised the team-leader accreditation herself. Give her the honest picture — what the programme costs, what it asks of her rostered hours — and ask what she's aiming at with it; if the answer is the senior-desk role, tell her what that path actually requires. Promise only what you can date.

**4. Calls — Info**
214 of 240 with a day left — inside her usual band, first-contact rate running ahead of the centre's. Confirm, thank, move on.

**Routed out:** nothing formal this week. **Before you sit down:** sign the vendor form today — it is both your row and item 2's block; come back to her on the accreditation with real dates and a real number. **Why it reads this way:** the draft filed a signature problem as "status" and a road question as housekeeping — the record re-sequenced both, and the praise it flattened is what opens the conversation.
