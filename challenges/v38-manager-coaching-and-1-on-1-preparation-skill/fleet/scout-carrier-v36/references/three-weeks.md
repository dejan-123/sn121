# Three invented packets and the replies they earn

These workplaces are fiction. The examples demonstrate decisions and voice, not facts to reuse. The first is a busy week; the second has a draft and almost no evidence; the third is a record of a conversation that has already occurred. In a live reply, every name, number, date and claim must come from the actual packet.

## Case A: a live week with competing labels

Inez Sato runs visitor programmes at a small maritime museum. Her manager is preparing their weekly one-on-one and sends the following:

- Draft: "Shoutout for the school visit"; "Status on the audio guide"; "Coaching: too blunt with volunteers"; "Feedback on the delayed group handoff"; "The interpretation lead role"; "Her question about compressed hours."
- Action log: update the school safety route (Inez, DONE on Tuesday); approve the replacement headset order (Manager, OVERDUE); send the new volunteer rota to the front desk (Inez, IN PROGRESS).
- Figures: 12 school groups hosted this week, against a usual 16.
- Monday message from the head teacher: "Inez noticed the tide display was crowded and led our class through the chart room instead. Every child still got to try the navigation table."
- Tuesday thread: Inez said the audio-guide headsets were failing and proposed lending the old wired sets; the supplier's response promised an update on Friday, not a delivery date.
- The volunteer coordinator records a Wednesday handoff that omitted the arrival-time briefing. A second, separate handoff on Friday omitted it again. The draft's "delayed group handoff" refers to that same Friday event, not a third incident.
- On Thursday Inez asked what experience would make her a candidate for the interpretation lead role. Her compressed-hours question is about her working arrangement, not a request for a development slot.

The reply:

## Read of the week

This is a draft for a meeting ahead, with a log, figures and messages from this week; it is preparation.

1. "Shoutout for the school visit" has a source: on Monday the head teacher credited Inez with moving the class from the crowded tide display without losing the navigation activity. Keep Recognition and open with the actual work rather than an unspecific compliment.
2. "Status on the audio guide" is waiting on functioning headsets and a supplier who promised only an update. It becomes an Unblock: Inez offered a wired-set workaround, while you still owe the replacement order.
3. "Coaching: too blunt with volunteers" has no quoted remark behind "blunt", but the Wednesday and Friday handoffs both missed the arrival-time briefing. Two occasions support Coaching on that repeated omission, not on Inez's manner.
4. "Feedback on the delayed group handoff" points back to Friday. Fold it into that same conversation; counting the event twice would manufacture an extra issue.
5. "The interpretation lead role" comes from Inez's own Thursday question. Keep Career and connect it to what she demonstrated with the school group.
6. "Her question about compressed hours" belongs in a dedicated working-arrangements conversation, not as an agenda item about performance or advancement.

Not in the draft: you have not approved the replacement order; Inez's rota is still in progress and may need a short status question if it affects the next visits. Numbers: 12 groups against a usual 16 is (12 - 16) / 16, down 25%. The safety route is done, the headset approval overdue, and the rota still in progress.

# Inez Sato: the school visit succeeded while the headsets failed

The school group still reached the navigation table after Inez moved them around a crowded display; the head teacher noticed the judgment behind that change. Twelve groups visited against a usual 16, down 25%, but the packet does not tell us how much of that difference the failed headsets caused. The audio guide remains unreliable, the supplier has promised an update rather than a delivery date, and the replacement order is overdue on your side. Of the three logged actions, the safety route is complete, the rota remains in progress and your approval is outstanding.

## In the room

**1. The chart-room detour - Recognition**
Start with the head teacher's Monday message, especially the part about every child still getting to try the navigation table. It credits a concrete decision Inez made under pressure, not just the outcome of a pleasant visit. Ask what she noticed soon enough to change the route; there may be a way to share that judgment with other guides before their next crowded session.

**2. The audio guide - Unblock**
Inez has already suggested the wired sets. Tell her whether you can authorize that stopgap, approve the replacement order before asking her to chase progress, and get the supplier to give a date rather than another open-ended update. "You proposed using the wired sets on Tuesday; which visits need them first while I settle the order?" Listen for a group already booked that cannot use the workaround.

**3. Volunteer handoffs - Coaching**
There were two separate handoffs, on Wednesday and Friday; both missed the arrival-time briefing, while the packet records no blunt words. Keep the questions on that repeat, not on Inez's manner. "What kept the arrival-time briefing out of Wednesday's handoff and Friday's?" Then: "Where could you put that information so the next volunteer receives it before a group arrives?" Listen for a missing rota or a timing constraint you can remove.

**4. The front-desk rota - Status**
The log says in progress, not delivered. "What is left before the new volunteer rota can reach the front desk, and is any part waiting on me?" If the answer is a dependency, take ownership of clearing it rather than leaving her with a second vague status check.

**5. Interpretation lead - Career**
Inez raised the role on Thursday, so give her room to ask about it. The change of route during Monday's school visit is a real example of interpreting the collection for visitors: "Which parts of the lead role would you want to try more of after that visit, and what do you think you still need to show?" Do not promise a decision on appointment in this conversation.

## Elsewhere

Her compressed-hours request needs a separate conversation about working arrangements; you book it with Inez and the appropriate people this week. No detail about her reasons belongs on this printed page.

## Before you sit down

Approve the replacement headset order, overdue in your action log, or bring Inez a date you can keep. Your decision determines whether the wired sets are a short bridge or the whole plan.

**Why this order.** The school visit is worth opening with because the teacher named Inez's contribution. What looked like audio-guide status is a dependency with two moves you can make. Friday is part of the two handoffs, not a second feedback item, and her question about the lead role deserves its own time. The working-arrangements request is important but has a different venue.

## Case B: the draft is all that arrived

For a forthcoming one-on-one with Dev Malik, allotment seed bank coordinator, the manager sends only this draft: "Where are we with the catalog migration?"; "Quick check on Dev's workload"; "He asked about a pay adjustment"; "Career goals check-in." There are no messages, action items, figures or performance observations. In particular, the draft does not say Dev raised a development goal or that the migration has started.

Here is a complete prep reply despite the missing record:

## Read of the week

A draft for the next meeting arrived alone. This is thin preparation, not notes of a conversation already held; the log, figures and messages have not been supplied.

1. "Where are we with the catalog migration?" is a Status question, not a claim that the work is on schedule. A task list and a dated project update would tell you what has been done and whether anything waits on another person.
2. "Quick check on Dev's workload" can stay as a modest Status check-in about the migration and his other assignments. No workload figure or message supports a verdict about capacity, and the answer may reveal something for you to redistribute.
3. "He asked about a pay adjustment" is the one report-owned request the draft actually records. Pay has a separate venue; book that conversation rather than drafting a response here.
4. "Career goals check-in" comes from the manager, not Dev. Keep a short Career question without claiming he wants a particular position; ask him what, if anything, he wants to explore.

Not in the draft: there is no action log, so no known manager row can be called open or closed. Numbers: no counts or baselines were provided; neither completion nor progress can be calculated.

# Dev Malik: two questions before the catalog plan can be settled

The only source for this week is your proposed agenda. It gives no task status, date, count of entries or account from Dev, so a prepared conversation must ask rather than report how the migration is going. There is no evidence for a recognition opener, a behaviour pattern or a claim that workload is too heavy. The pay question is real as a request on the draft, but its amount and merits cannot be assessed here. The aim in this meeting is to learn what the catalog work needs and let Dev choose whether he wants a development conversation.

## In the room

**1. Catalog migration - Status**
Open with the named work, not an assertion of progress: "I don't have the catalog task list today. Which part of the migration is finished, what is next, and is anything waiting on access or a decision from me?" Listen for a dependency you can clear, rather than assigning Dev the job of chasing another team's answer. If he describes a completed portion, ask what record captures it before describing it as finished in a follow-up. If the next step lacks an owner, agree one in the room and write it into the action log after the conversation.

**2. Workload around the migration - Status**
The draft proposes a check-in, not a finding about Dev's health, motivation or performance. "Alongside the catalog migration, what other work is competing for your time this week, and is there anything I should help reprioritize?" Listen for work only you can move, or a deadline that needs to change. If he says the load is manageable, accept that answer; do not turn a check-in into a prepared conclusion about strain. If he identifies a specific bottleneck, come back with the action you will own rather than asking him to solve an organizational constraint alone.

**3. Development check-in - Career**
You pencilled this in, and Dev has not sent a goal. Leave him room to decline or name one: "Is there any part of the seed bank or catalog work you'd like to do more of, or should we leave this open until you've had time to think?" Avoid telling him what his next role ought to be. If he does name a path, ask what he would like to try first, then bring the relevant criteria to the next meeting instead of inventing them today.

There is no coaching slot because the draft supplies no dated behaviour to discuss. There is no praise slot either: the catalog title alone cannot establish a result. This is a useful meeting without either label, as long as the questions elicit facts rather than fill in missing ones.

## Elsewhere

Dev's pay-adjustment request needs a dedicated pay conversation. You arrange a time with him and the relevant pay contact, without an amount, promise or pay script on this page.

## Before you sit down

No action log was provided; no manager-owned task can honestly be called overdue or complete. Ask Dev or whoever tracks the catalog work for its task list and latest update, and ask for any figures that show how many entries remain. Invite Dev to send a development topic if he has one, without making his answer a condition of this meeting. If the records do not arrive in time, keep the three questions above on the page instead of substituting assumed answers.

**Why this order.** The migration and workload stay questions because the draft has no evidence to settle either. The career check is yours, not a goal Dev submitted, so its wording leaves him in control. His pay request matters, but its proper venue is separate.

## Case C: the supplied meeting has already happened

The manager says "prepare next week's one-on-one from these notes" and pastes a record titled "Last meeting with Leila Osman, box-office coordinator, 3 March." It lists the manager and Leila as attendees, a discussion of evening queues and a family matter, and these actions: manager to ask facilities for a second ticket scanner by 6 March; Leila to draft the school matinee seating plan by 8 March; manager to confirm the foyer staffing budget by 9 March. It says nothing about whether any of these actions have since been done.

The entire reply is:

# Leila Osman: not preparation for a future one-on-one

These are notes of the meeting already held on 3 March, with attendees, discussion and agreed actions. A new brief built from them would treat the old evening-queue discussion as news, so I would follow up on the actions instead:

- Manager: ask facilities for a second ticket scanner, due 6 March.
- Manager: confirm the foyer staffing budget, due 9 March.
- Leila: draft the school matinee seating plan, due 8 March.

The notes do not say whether any of those steps happened. For the next meeting, bring their current state, this week's messages or figures, and a fresh draft agenda. The family matter mentioned in the record belongs in a private conversation with Leila; no discussion of it is drafted here.

## Boundaries the examples do not change

If the headset approval had been Inez's overdue row, it would not appear as a manager debt; the manager could ask her about its status without pretending to own her approval. If Dev had supplied a dated record saying the catalog move was complete, the status call would change; his draft alone cannot establish that. If the museum's supplier had given a firm delivery date, that date would replace the request for one, but it would not turn the current headset failures into a personal coaching problem.

A separate example of a tempting misread: on Thursday an archivist writes, "If the invoice has not arrived by noon, I will call finance." No later record follows. The brief may say that a call was planned if the invoice stayed missing, and ask whether either event occurred. It cannot report that the archivist called on Thursday. A dated intention is not a dated completion.
