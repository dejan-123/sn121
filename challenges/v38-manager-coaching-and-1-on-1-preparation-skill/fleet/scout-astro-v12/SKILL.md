There is a quarter hour before the meeting, and this is how it gets spent. The first ten minutes belong to the record, not the draft — the draft was written from memory at week's end, and memory recalls the subject but not the substance. The last five belong to the page. And the checking is written down where the manager can see it, before the brief: visible reasoning earns trust; hidden reasoning invites the manager to fall back on the draft.

## Minutes ten to five — read the record

Tickets, messages, figures, praise. Take what they show at face value: anything open, overdue or NOT STARTED counts open; closed stays closed unless a newer artifact directly contradicts it; a number only in the manager's head does not exist. Note what the manager owes — their open rows are preparation, not trivia. Note what praise landed by name. Mark anything the record contradicts — an impression like "disengaged" while the log shows early flags and picked-up work is a wrong note, dropped with the signals named.

## Minutes five to zero — check the draft

Each draft item, in the draft's order, gets one question: what does the record make it? The answers land in six classes:

- **Recognition** — written praise exists. Opens the meeting, in the giver's own words with the figure attached.
- **Blocked** — another desk holds it: an approval not given, a dependency gone quiet, more than one pair of hands can carry. The manager leaves carrying the move; sharp messages after ignored chases are the hold-up talking, not a tone problem. When the trail shows it spreading wider than the draft admits — other teams held up, leadership already aware — the call says so and the item climbs, whatever priority was written.
- **Coaching** — one behaviour on two or more separate dated occasions, or a goal the report chose. Questions, never lectures. When the report already found the cause and tracks it, coach the step before it — spot it sooner next time — anchored to real figures, not the symptom they found.
- **Career** — the report themselves raised growth, a course, wider scope or review timing. One sincere question; a keepable promise only.
- **Status** — nothing to repair, praise or settle. Last, squeezed — opening on housekeeping spends the best minutes on the smallest line.
- **Parked** — another room's business: pay, HR, a dispute a different team owns, next quarter. Sent there with a line saying why. Personal matters stay private — "the personal matter", elaborated never.

Calls the pass sharpens: sharpness that started with another desk's hold-up is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. A stack of messages on a single problem counts once; Coaching wants separate dated occasions. An impression nothing underneath supports is dropped. One self-caught slip earns a quick word, never a coaching slot. Recognition opens only on a quote; when none exists, a line above item one admits it.

## The page itself

**## Checking each label** — the checking, printed. One numbered entry per draft item, in the draft's own order: its wording quoted; **Grounds:** what the record shows — who logged or said what, where and when, the row or figure beside it; **So:** the class and the reason — "stands" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, merged into a sibling. Then **Not on the draft:** — what the manager owes or skipped — and **The sums:** anything computed, worked from its operands — "118 of 120 is two short" — operands shown, points not percent.

**# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]** — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line. Items in meeting order, **name (Call, ~N min)** and a sentence or two written as things to say and ask, carrying the real numbers and names. Then **Off the page** (parked or dropped, with reasons), **Before you sit down** (every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move — settled before anything is asked of the report, above all a row an agenda item hangs on), and **Why it reads this way** (a few lines on why the calls landed where they did).

Invent nothing. A thin packet still earns a page — the check names what is missing and the brief asks rather than asserts: what the manager cannot yet see, the question to put, and the shape of a true answer.

A worked quarter-hour — record read, labels checked, page written — lives in `references/practice-notes.md`. Borrow the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When what arrives is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already had — whatever its label — the reply is one short note, under 180 words, with no checking section:

- Say plainly: this is a record of a conversation already held, not preparation for the next.
- Where open actions survive inside it, write the follow-up — each action with its owner and date — in a line or two; never a brief, never a re-sort.
- Preparation needs this week's open items, current figures, and anything flagged since.
- Anything personal stays "the personal matter", nothing more.
