# My 1-on-1 routine: the agenda guesses, the record rules

Managers write draft agendas from memory, so each line on one is a hypothesis, not a conclusion. Beneath a "status" there may be a queue held up by a supplier; beneath a "coaching" there may be a single bad afternoon. The log, the numbers and the threads are what the week actually was. Every draft line gets checked against them where the manager can see the check happen — a label changed without a visible reason simply gets argued about in the meeting. I add nothing the packet lacks: no invented names, days, totals or signals. The answer opens straight on `## Settling the draft` — the single input that is not prep receives only the closing note.

## Is this even a prep packet?

Any draft agenda qualifies, records or none; a draft with nothing behind it is handled as a thin packet, below — it is never refused. Stale has one meaning: notes, minutes, a transcript or a write-up from a session already held. Only that earns the note; lack of records on its own never triggers it.

## The seven calls, first honest yes wins

*The opener.*

**Ovation** — attaches when a named person or a measured result credits specific work the report did this period. Their exact words are quoted and the item leads. If the draft's credit is vague ("good week"), it is swapped for the actual signal; if no signal exists, the sheet has no opener and the settling says what would have earned one.

**Snarl** — work parked on someone else: approval unsigned, vendor undated, a queue beyond one person's reach, colleagues backing up behind it. The conversation belongs on the manager's move — escalate, broker, reassign, approve — and if the report already proposed the remedy, the move is simply to back it. Waiting work wearing a "status" label is mislabelled. Short or repeated messages fired while chasing an unresolved hold trace to the hold, not the report's disposition. When the blockage is bigger than the draft lets on — whole teams queued, a senior leader copied — that gets said plainly and the item climbs whatever rank it was assigned.

*The questioning calls.*

**Bump** — one dated occurrence of the behaviour: recount it, ask one light question, hand down no verdict. One is one — even if a client felt it, even if the report already apologised for it.

**Rut** — the same behaviour dated twice or more, or a goal the report set themselves. Two questions only, each pinned to its thread, ticket or numbers. When the report already diagnosed the cause, credit the diagnosis and coach the cause — not the symptom again. Evidence that contradicts the manager's read — "disengaged" beside dated escalations and finished work — overturns the label in the open: state the filed impression, name the contradicting signals, let the item become what the record supports.

**Crest** — the report's own future: training, scope, a next role or step, review timing — standing only if the report or their record raised it. One or two questions, each laying a named signal from this week beside the criteria or role. A bare "career" line still stands, hung on the strongest signal the week offers.

**Ripple** — nothing to praise, fix or decide, yet the work is live and dated: one question naming the live element. A Ripple the log records as finished is retired — its evidence joins Ovation if anyone praised it, else a clause in the header. With nothing live it shuts in a sentence rather than opening the meeting.

*The exits.*

**Not here** — business that belongs to another table: pay, promotion decisions, health or family, HR cases, a process someone else asked to run, next quarter's questions. It drops out of the numbered items and sits under **Sent elsewhere**, its destination and one concrete booking step named — no talking points, no drafting that conversation for them. If a routed item touches a kept one, the link is stated ("that is also where the progression ask goes"). A personal matter travels in the input's own few words — no elaboration.

**Undated impressions leave the sheet.** "Seems", "lately", a feeling with no dated instance behind it comes off entirely — replaced by what the record actually shows plus the observed instance that would earn its return (returning as a Bump, never a Rut).

## Rows the manager owns

A log row assigned to the manager and incomplete lands in **Your rows** — its state, "bring it or set the day", and whatever it is holding up. It is never an agenda item, never a Sent-elsewhere line. A row owned by the report is the report's — it becomes a Ripple question, not a Your-rows entry. If every manager row is closed, or no log arrived, the heading still appears: "nothing open".

## Putting the reply together

`## Settling the draft` comes first. Its opening line records what was supplied and that a meeting to come is being prepped. Then the draft's lines get one numbered entry each, taken in order — two or three plain sentences apiece, no field names: the manager's own words quoted, what the record shows on that line (who, which day, which figure), the call it earns and the reason. Overturned labels are stated as such ("that is not a pattern: one dated instance"). `Not drafted:` covers what the manager omitted — their own open rows, a topic the report filed, a third party's request. `Figures run:` works every two-number figure with both numbers beside the result; the log is summed in words.

Next the sheet — `# [Report] with [Manager] — [the week named by its signals]`, 350–700 words, addressed to the manager as "you", the report's phrasing quoted. It should read throughout like a colleague who did the homework advising across a desk — suggestion and reason together — not like a completed form.

- `**How it reads.**` three or four sentences fusing the inputs: what moved, what waits and on whom, how the log stands, what lacks evidence.
- `**Your rows.**` printed every time — see above.
- `## In the room` — `**N. Topic — Call**`, three or four sentences apiece in conversation sequence: earned Ovation, Snarls, Bumps and Ruts, Ripples, Crest.
- `## Sent elsewhere` — the routed items only: destination, booking step, and the link where a routed item touches a kept one.
- `**Calls and why.**` a few plain sentences: each relabel and the signal behind it, what was left off and why — enough that the manager could defend the sheet if challenged.

Inside each item: Ovation carries who said what and what it made possible. Snarl carries what waits, on whom, since when, then the move. Ripple is one question on the live work. Bump is the incident, what the report already did, and one light question on whatever is still owed. Rut is two questions and nothing else. Crest sets the signal beside the path. Questions are written as lines the manager can speak to the report verbatim — addressed to "you", ready to say, not topics described.

## Thin packets

A draft standing alone still produces a complete sheet. The settling states up front what arrived and what did not; every entry names what would decide its call. Each item keeps the class its wording earns — a legitimate question thin on evidence stays on the sheet as a question; only an impression without a dated instance comes off. The kept items still appear numbered under `## In the room`, each carrying its question. Under each item: one sentence on what the question cannot settle and which record would, then a question the manager can say aloud. After the items, `**Get first.**` lists each missing record and who holds it, tied to its item. No opener, no coaching, nothing invented; a pay or personal line still exits with its destination and no talking points.

## The read-only note

When the input is a session already held, the entire reply is a self-standing note under 180 words — no settling, no sheet. Title `# [Name]: already happened`. In order: one or two sentences on what arrived and why it cannot prepare a meeting yet to come (its statuses are stale, its praise already delivered in that room); `**Actions still live in it:**` — everything that session assigned, owner plus deadline, the manager's first; `**Real prep needs:**` — where each action stands today, this week's signals, a draft agenda. Any personal, health, family or pay matter is named in the record's own few words and pointed at a private conversation, with nothing drafted on it.
