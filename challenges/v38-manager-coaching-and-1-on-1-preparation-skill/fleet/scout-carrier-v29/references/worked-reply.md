# One made-up week, answered in this voice — take the shape and the sound, never the facts

Everyone and everything below is invented. What carries over is the form: a settling that walks the draft in order, items written like a colleague who did the homework, and questions phrased as words the manager can say aloud. In a live reply the facts all come from the packet.

## The invented packet

Jakub Nowak runs a shift at a regional parcel-sorting hub, eighteen months in. Draft from the manager: "credit for the week" / "Status: scanner belt 4" / "Coaching: short with the temp workers" / "Status: missort rate" / "he asked about the ops-analyst secondment" / "personal — his custody schedule". The week: action log — "post the Christmas-period rota (Jakub, DONE Tuesday)" and "approve the handheld scanner refresh (Manager, OVERDUE)". Figures — 41,200 parcels sorted on a 44,000 shift target. Threads — Monday, the hub controller in #ops: "Jakub's team cleared the weekend backlog by Tuesday noon; earliest we've ever opened a week at full rate." Wednesday, Jakub in #maintenance: "Belt 4 scanner is intermittent again; engineer not confirmed this week. I've rerouted its lanes through belts 2 and 5 for now." The shift log has one sharp exchange with a temp worker on Friday, after two unlabelled cages came down the line. Last week Jakub asked whether the ops-analyst secondment was open to shift leads. The custody schedule is private; he has told HR what he needs.

## The answer

## Settling the draft

A draft agenda, an action log, one figure pair and the week's threads arrived for a meeting still to come — this is prep.

1. "credit for the week" — vague until Monday: the hub controller posted in #ops that his team "cleared the weekend backlog by Tuesday noon; earliest we've ever opened a week at full rate". Ovation on those words, and it leads.
2. "Status: scanner belt 4" — the engineer is unconfirmed this week and he has already rerouted its lanes through belts 2 and 5. That is not a status question; it is a Snarl — his reroute is the remedy to back, and the move is a confirmed engineer date.
3. "Coaching: short with the temp workers" — one dated exchange, Friday, after two unlabelled cages came down the line. One is one: a Bump, asked lightly, no verdict.
4. "Status: missort rate" — 41,200 against 44,000 and the missorts hide inside the gap, but nothing says who owns the tracing or whether it repeats. A real question with thin evidence stays on the sheet: a Ripple pinned to the 2,800 shortfall.
5. "he asked about the ops-analyst secondment" — he raised it himself last week. Crest; the Monday clear-down is the signal to lay beside what the secondment asks.
6. "personal — his custody schedule" — the dates are private and he has already told HR what he needs. Not here: the leave process, starting with confirming dates at HR; nothing on it gets drafted.

Not drafted: the overdue scanner-refresh approval is the manager's own row — Your rows. Figures run: 41,200 parcels on a 44,000 target leaves 2,800 short, and the missort question sits inside that gap.

# Jakub Nowak with his manager — the record Monday and the intermittent belt

**How it reads.** The hub sorted 41,200 on a 44,000 target (2,800 short), but Monday gave the week its headline: his team cleared the weekend backlog by noon and the hub controller called it the earliest full-rate opening ever in #ops. Belt 4's scanner is intermittent on an unconfirmed engineer — his reroute is already running — and the scanner-refresh approval waiting on you is overdue.

**Your rows.** Approve the handheld scanner refresh — overdue, and the intermittent units are precisely what it replaces. Bring it or set the day.

## In the room

**1. The record Monday — Ovation**
Open on the hub controller's exact words — "earliest we've ever opened a week at full rate" — a save named in public by the controller lands harder than a number does. Then ask how he sequenced the clear-down; a recovery that fast is a method that vanishes if nobody writes it down.

**2. Belt 4 — Snarl**
The engineer is unconfirmed this week and his reroute through belts 2 and 5 is already holding — the workaround is his, what remains is yours. Pin the engineer to a confirmed date and tell him plainly the reroute is approved; he should not be carrying that gap silently.

**3. Missort rate — Ripple**
2,800 short and the missorts sit inside it with nobody owning the trace — one question is enough: "where are the missorts actually coming from, and whose desk do they land on?" If the same lanes surface next week it earns its own item.

**4. Friday's exchange — Bump**
A sharp word after two unlabelled cages came down the line — one dated moment in a week he otherwise ran clean. One light question covers it: "have the cages come through labelled since Friday?" A single moment never earns a verdict.

**5. Ops-analyst secondment — Crest**
- "The Monday clear-down is the clearest thing on your record this quarter — which parts of the analyst role does it already prove, and which does it leave open?"
- "You asked about the secondment yourself — what would you want it to lead to?"

## Sent elsewhere

His custody schedule — the dates are private and he has already told HR what he needs; the step is confirming the dates with HR so the rota can plan around them. Nothing on it is drafted here.

**Calls and why.** The credit stays, anchored now — the controller named the save. The belt is a hold on an outside engineer, not a status, and his reroute is the remedy to back. "Short" is one dated instance after two unlabelled cages, so it stays a Bump rather than a Rut. The missort gap is live but unowned, so it stays a question until the record says more. The secondment is his and stands on the week's clearest signal. The custody arrangement belongs to HR, not this room.
