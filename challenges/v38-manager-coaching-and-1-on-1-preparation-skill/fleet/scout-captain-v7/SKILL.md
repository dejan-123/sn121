# The edit bay — slate every shot, cut it on the dailies

A manager's draft agenda is a reel of slated shots, each slated the
manager's way. The slate is a first intention, not a keep. Every shot
goes into the bay and is decided on the dailies alone — what the
packet documents, nothing it does not. The output is a board the
manager reads on the way in: every shot logged, every decision
traceable to the dailies, the count matching the shots slated.

## First look — are these this week's dailies?

Three reels look like a weekly shoot and are not: notes, minutes or
a transcript of a meeting already held (even when the manager asks
to plan the next one from it — it records last week's takes, not
this week's footage; what it supports is a follow-up on the
actions); preparation for a skip-level or a different meeting; a
performance-review, rating or disciplinary draft. For these, keep
nothing. Reply in under 150 words titled `# Not this week's
dailies`: what the reel is, which document it actually supports and
who owns it, what to bring for a weekly board — ending with the
one-line reason (nothing in the material is dated after that shoot,
so a board would replay old takes or invent new ones). Where the
input is last week's reel, list each open action with owner and
date exactly as slated; open actions carry forward as entries,
never as assumed current take.

The send-back triggers on the reel's kind and nothing else. A reel
of the manager's own topics for the coming screening is always this
week's dailies, even with no footage behind them: a bare agenda, a
two-line note, topics with no figures behind them. Scarcity is
decided shot by shot (Held-in-lab, below); it never triggers a
send-back. For every reel that is this week's dailies — full or
thin — the reply opens `# Cut board:` with no preamble.

## First look — what goes on the reel

Only what the packet documents goes on the reel: an action item and
its state (the manager's own included), a sprint or ticket figure,
a quoted line, the manager's note, the report's own topic. No
invented figure, quote, date, name or mood. Where a decision needs
footage the packet lacks, the lack is logged — never filled with a
comfortable guess.

## The screening — how footage weighs

Footage weighs by kind, not volume. One documented occurrence is a
single take — it can support a note, it cannot support a pattern
keep. The same conduct documented on two or more occasions is a
pattern. Attributed commendation — a named person's thanks or a
concrete result — is credit earned; effort, hours or unverifiable
praise is not. The report's own account is self-reported: loggable,
weighed below an independent take. An impression the packet cannot
source is a reason to ask, not a take.

## Wrapped shots stay wrapped

A shot the dailies show finished is logged as a one-line close —
unless another logged take cannot stand with the wrap. The shot's
own slate never reopens it. Where a later take and the logged wrap
genuinely conflict, log the conflict in writing; never re-slate
silently.

## Which department owns it

A shot belongs to the department that can finish it. Work waiting on
an input another person or team controls is that department's
footage — a stalled dependency goes on the board as the manager's
own action to move it, never as the report's fault to answer for.
A sole reviewer or approver whose queue stalls several teams is a
capacity matter the manager owns: it keeps early as the manager's
own decision — who should hold the load, with the fix the report
already proposed as the starting point — not as a slate to work
faster. Pay, health, family, formal ratings, complaints about
colleagues and disciplinary process belong to other departments
entirely: handed off with desk and owner, no talking points, one
private line at most.

## The decisions

Every shot takes exactly one decision, and the slate never decides
it — the dailies do:

- **Kept**: the dailies support the shot as slated. It takes its
  slot with an anchored question or a concrete manager action.
- **Re-slated**: the shot keeps under a corrected slate. A conduct
  or attitude slate where the dailies show work stalled at another
  department's desk is re-slated to the blocked shot it is; a
  routine check on finished, credited work is re-slated to the
  credit. The board names the slate and the re-slate both.
- **Handed off**: real footage for another department — named desk,
  named owner, no talking points.
- **Cut**: no logged take supports the shot — the recognition with
  no attributable credit, the topic slated as padding. Its absence
  is logged plainly.
- **Held-in-lab**: the shot is live but the footage is undeveloped.
  It keeps as the honest question — what is the actual take, what
  does the missing footage show — never as coverage invented to
  fill the slot.

A development slate requires a pattern. A single incident keeps as
a one-time note — with one narrow question about the setting, never
as a pattern keep. Where a request was substantively justified but
ran in a public channel, the request keeps as slated and exactly one
question addresses the channel; the justified shot is not folded
into the channel point. Where the report raises their own direction —
a level, a path, a stretch scope — that is their shot and it keeps
with real time in this screening; only the formal decision (a
promotion case, a rating, a band) is handed off.

## The board

300 to 650 words, written for the manager to read, not to audit. The
screening runs 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise, and the
shots' minutes add to it.

- **Board head**: `# Cut board: <name>, <role>` — then three lines:
  the week's picture in one sentence; every derivable figure written
  as shown work (`2 of 5 closed, 3 open`, `14 → 23 screens, +64%`),
  each pair against its own basis, never a ratio without its
  operands; which record types the packet lacks, logged plainly.
- **Reel order**: every shot, numbered, as
  `n. Decision · topic · ~minutes`, then under each its slate line:
  `slated: <the manager's shot> → <decision>: <the daily in a few
  words>` — every shot carries this line, including `as slated`
  where slate and decision agree. No slated shot disappears: a merged
  one keeps its own slate line under the shot that absorbed it;
  handed-off and cut shots name their decision. Order: attributable
  credit first when it exists; then what blocks work or needs the
  manager's call; then a one-time note; then the report's own
  direction with real time (if the screening cannot hold it, book a
  dedicated sitting rather than squeeze it into the last two
  minutes); quick closes last.
- **Continuity notes**: questions and concrete manager actions only,
  each anchored to a named take — the thread, ticket, figure or
  person — at most three sentences per shot. A direction the report
  never raised stays a career shot only when the packet holds their
  topic or an agreed cadence; otherwise it is one open question at
  the end and the board logs the packet holds none of theirs.
- **Pre-production**: the manager's own open or overdue items —
  cleared or owned first of all when they touch the same shot — and
  the takes to pull for any gap the look logged.
- **Handed off**: the other-department shots with desk and owner.
- **Editor's note**: three to five plain sentences closing the
  board — each re-slate, hand-off or cut with the daily that forced
  it; why the first shot keeps or why there is none; what a headline
  figure does and does not show.

When a figure explains only part of a gap, the rest stays open: the
board names the share still unexplained and puts it to the report as
a status question rather than treating the explanation as settled.

## Order of work

1. In the same message where you call read_reference, write the
   working reel: the reel-kind check, then for every slated shot its
   slate, the daily that decides it, its decision and any hand-off,
   and each derived figure with its working.
2. Call read_reference on `references/bay-drills.md` once: full
   boards on invented reels — a normal screening with a mis-slated
   shot, a thin reel worked honestly, a reel of old dailies sent
   back whole, and a queue that is really the manager's own capacity
   shot. Carry the decisions and proportions, never the facts.
3. Once it returns, send the board and nothing else. Its first
   characters are `# ` (or, for a send-back, the
   `# Not this week's dailies` title); the working reel is not
   repeated, summarised or introduced.
