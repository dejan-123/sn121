# Booking drills — full sheets on invented request books

Four invented request books, booked the way the desk should book
them. The names, figures and products are invented; the bookings
are the part to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal book with a mis-booked request

Book: report is "Elena, senior mobile engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "Elena's friction with design — she pushed back hard on their
proposal in the team channel", (b) "why is the offline-sync epic
slipping", (c) "mention the beta cohort launch". The record: the
design lead posted a public thank-you naming Elena for flagging an
accessibility regression before the proposal shipped; the channel
exchange shows Elena objecting — bluntly but on the substance —
to a design change that dropped keyboard navigation; the epic is
5 of 12 tickets done, waiting on a vendor API contract the vendor
was asked for 12 days ago; the beta cohort launched and the PM
messaged that crash-free sessions rose from 96.1% to 98.4%.

Bookings:

- Request (a): **Re-booked**. One substantive objection to a
  change that dropped a documented requirement — written as
  friction, booked as a one-time note with one narrow question.
  Desk note: "The design exchange was on the merits — they were
  already thanking you for the accessibility catch. One thing to
  keep in view: that disagreement ran in the team channel rather
  than on the ticket. Does moving it there cost you anything?"
  The objection stands; the channel takes one question and no
  more.
- Request (b): **Re-booked — another desk's business.** 5 of 12
  done, and the stall is a 12-day-old vendor contract, not
  Elena's pace. Desk note: "The epic is waiting on the vendor's
  API contract — twelve days outstanding. I'll chase the vendor
  today and get a date; who should I escalate to if it slips
  again?"
- Request (c): **Booked first.** Two attributable credits — the
  design lead's public thanks and the crash-free figure
  (96.1 → 98.4%, +2.3 points). Open with them before any process
  request; credit books first.
- Every written request gets a desk line and a booking; nothing
  written disappears.

Sheet: `# Concierge sheet: Elena, senior mobile engineer` —
picture line ("a strong week with one request waiting on another
desk"), figures shown as work, then booking order with all three
written requests, the entry behind each, and each booking — then
desk notes, manager's own list, referred list, concierge's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin book booked honestly

Book: report is "Kofi, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep my
weekly with Kofi — want to talk about his staff-to-principal
timeline, and whether he can lead the payments migration." No
action log, no figures, no channel excerpts. One calendar item:
Kofi asked in passing about the bonus cycle timing.

Thin is not invalid: the book aims at the coming meeting, so the
desk still takes it — booked request by request. Only the wrong
kind of book (drill 3) is handed back whole. The reply opens
`# Concierge sheet: Kofi, staff engineer` — never a hand-back
preface.

Bookings:

- Promotion timeline: **Booked — split by desk.** Kofi's
  direction and the manager's readiness question belong in this
  meeting, with real time: "You asked about the path to
  principal — here's the gap evidence I see and what I'd need
  documented; what scope would you take to build it?" The bonus
  question is **Referred**: bonus timing sits with the
  compensation review — the sheet names the destination (the
  comp review with the comp partner) and the owner, one line, no
  desk notes.
- Readiness for the payments migration: **Waitlisted**. The book
  has no delivery record to weigh. It books as the honest
  question, not an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't
  have your recent delivery record in front of me — walk me
  through the last few weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what
  you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the sheet. A thin sheet of
  honest questions beats a padded one; the sheet head records
  what the book lacks and the manager's own list names what to
  pull.

## Drill 3 — a book that is not this week's

Book: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Ren —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list three
actions: Ren to send the load-test results (due Wednesday), the
manager to confirm the training budget, a follow-up on the
intern mentor pairing.

Booking: **whole book handed back — nothing logged.** The reply,
under 150 words, titled `# Not this week's book`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was agreed,
not what has moved since. The document this supports is a
follow-up on the open actions, not a sheet: Ren's load-test
results (due Wednesday), your budget confirmation, the mentor
pairing follow-up — owners and dates exactly as written. For a
weekly sheet, send this week's action log, any figures, and
whatever Ren raised or you want addressed. Nothing in the
material is recorded after that meeting, so a sheet would replay
old entries or invent new ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not repeated,
not summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own request

Book: report is "Vera, senior platform engineer". Manager's
draft: (a) "Vera needs to get through her spec reviews — seven
are waiting on her and two teams are blocked"; (b) "Vera seems
flat in sprint demos lately". The record: the seven specs are
assigned because the process names her sole approver for
cross-team changes; she shipped two features this sprint and
raised the queue herself in the last retro, proposing a
rotation; "flat" appears once, in the manager's note.

Bookings:

- Request (a): **Re-booked — the desk that can fulfil it is the
  manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two teams is a
  capacity matter the manager owns, not a request to work
  faster. It books early as the manager's own decision: "Your
  rotation proposal is the right starting point — I want to
  approve it this week and take it to the team leads. What load
  should each reviewer hold so nothing queues behind one person
  again?"
- Request (b): **Declined.** One manager observation with no
  entries behind it; the sprint shows two shipped features and
  a raised process fix. The sheet records its absence plainly
  rather than inventing an engagement segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Vera's retro proposal —
  is listed under the manager's own list as the manager's
  commitment, with whom and when.

## What every drill shares

- The record decides; the written booking is only the filing.
- Attributable credit books first — a named person, a concrete
  result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its
  substance and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another desk fulfils is referred to that desk.
- One occurrence is a single entry, never a pattern.
- Missing records book as `Waitlisted` — the honest question in
  the slot — never covered over.
- Other-desk requests get a destination and an owner, never desk
  notes.
- Every figure shows its own working; every written request gets
  a desk line and a booking.
- A thin book still gets a sheet. The hand-back is for the wrong
  kind of book only — it stays under 150 words and carries no
  desk structure.
