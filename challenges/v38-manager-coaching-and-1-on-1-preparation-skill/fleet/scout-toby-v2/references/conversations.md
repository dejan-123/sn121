# Three ways a manager's input can arrive

All people and organizations here are invented. Borrow the
judgment, not the language or facts. The output does not need
the same number of conversations every week.

## A week in which the draft spends time on the wrong problem

Manager's input for a weekly meeting with Leena March, Scheduling
Lead at Sunward Ferry:

Draft topics: "operations dashboard status," "coaching: she keeps
slowing service changes," "recognize her for something," and
"career if time." The action list says Leena's updated sailing
plan is OVERDUE; the manager's cross-port permission request
is also OVERDUE; an incident handoff Leena owned was completed
on Monday. This week's board shows 26 sailings confirmed
against 22 last week and 5 changes stalled on the same
cross-port permission. Leena's Tuesday message asks for the
permission for the third time and offers a temporary
same-port routing plan. Thursday's note from harbor chief
Omar credits her completed handoff with preventing a crew
mix-up. Leena submitted a question about training to
coordinate the wider regional schedule. No supplied message
shows a repeated behavior of delaying changes.

The finished page:

# Leena March - weekly meeting
Scheduling Lead, Sunward Ferry

**The week in one read:** 26 sailings confirmed against 22 last
week: four more, about 18% higher than that baseline. Five
changes cannot clear until the cross-port permission arrives.
That same missing permission holds up Leena's sailing plan
and is the manager's overdue request, not Leena's lack of
drive. Her proposed temporary routing could keep some work
moving while the manager secures approval.

**Recognition - name the handoff.** Omar's Thursday note credits
Leena's completed incident handoff with preventing a crew
mix-up. Open by naming that work and why it mattered; the
draft's generic "recognize her" now has an actual source.

**Status - get the permission moving.** The dashboard and the
alleged "slowing changes" item are mostly one conversation.
Five changes and her sailing plan wait on the same approval,
which she requested three times. "Which changes can use
your Tuesday same-port plan while I chase the permission?"
The manager takes the overdue request; there is no record
supporting a coaching session about effort.

**Career - regional scheduling.** Leena asked about coordinating
the wider schedule herself. "Your handoff helped prevent
Thursday's mix-up; which parts of a regional coordination
role would you want to practice next?" Give the question
room rather than leaving it as a rushed close.

**For the manager afterward:** secure the cross-port
permission, decide whether to approve the temporary
route, and bring the regional role requirements before
discussing progression.

There is no need to repeat the four draft labels in a formal
table. Every filed topic has been answered: the generic
recognition becomes a real opener, the two work topics
merge at the dependency, and Leena's own career topic
receives time. The manager can take a real action.

If Leena had also posted a needlessly sharp message in a
public operations channel, the reason she escalated could
still be sound while her choice of venue deserved one
specific feedback question. That narrow question would
not justify calling the whole delay a character flaw.

## A real issue with a small feedback question

In another invented week, Julian Mensah, Museum Events
Coordinator at Fenslow Gallery, has an outreach calendar
that moved from 72% to 79% confirmed (+7 percentage
points). The manager lists "status," "coaching on
presenting," "talk about his role," and "discuss his
mother's medical needs." The only presentation record
is one exhibition preview in which he skipped the
sponsor slide, then sent the missing slide the same
day. No pattern of presentation trouble is supplied.
He submitted "could I help curate next year's program?"
The gallery registrar, Neve, thanked him publicly for
catching a scheduling conflict before invitations went
out. The manager's updated sponsor brief is overdue.

# Julian Mensah - weekly meeting
Museum Events Coordinator, Fenslow Gallery

**The week in one read:** Outreach confirmation went from
72% to 79%, up 7 percentage points. The preview had
one recoverable slip; Julian sent the skipped slide
afterward. The missing sponsor brief is on the
manager's side, and Julian has raised a real
development question.

**Recognition - the invitation catch.** Neve's public
thanks identifies the scheduling conflict Julian
caught before invitations went out. Open with that
specific contribution.

**Status - outreach calendar.** "The calendar rose
from 72% to 79% confirmed; what is behind the
remaining unconfirmed invitations, and where can I
help?" The percentages are an update, not an
excuse to invent a cause.

**Feedback - one preview moment.** "At the exhibition
preview the sponsor slide was skipped, and you
sent it afterward. What check would make sure
it is in the deck before the next preview?"
This is one observed instance, not evidence that
Julian needs a general presentation coaching
program. The manager must provide the overdue
sponsor brief.

**Career - curatorial interest.** Julian himself
asked about next year's program. "Which curatorial
responsibility interests you most, and what would
be a useful first opportunity?" Do not imply that
a new role is promised.

**Outside this meeting:** The family health matter
needs a private, employee-led support conversation
arranged by the manager. Do not draft questions
about Julian's mother's condition into this brief.

The private matter is acknowledged without becoming
an agenda script. If no positive signal from Neve
had existed, there would be no recognition item.

## A thin week does not need to be filled up

Another manager supplies only "status on the new
system," "she must have done something great," and
"salary discussion" for an employee named Imani
Voss. No metrics, completed work, action states,
threads or topic submitted by Imani are available.
Do not assume the system is late or manufacture
praise.

# Imani Voss - weekly meeting
Systems Support Specialist

**The week in one read:** There is no current
work record, named achievement, or action-item
state in the material provided. Pull the system
board and ask Imani what she wants from this
meeting.

**Status - ask, do not assert.** "Where does the
new system stand, and which decision would help
you most?" Nothing in the input supports saying
she is ahead or behind.

**Outside this meeting:** No sourced result
supports a recognition opener. Find a genuine
instance before giving praise. The salary
decision needs a separate compensation discussion
owned by the manager, not pay talking points
for this weekly meeting.

The missing data is the most useful preparation
fact this week. The page is short because the
evidence is.

## When the input is old minutes, not a current week

Suppose the manager asks "use these to prep next
week" but sends only notes from a one-on-one
already held with Suri Vale, a community services
planner. The notes list discussion of outreach
numbers and a private family matter. They assign
two actions: the manager will request a revised
partner timetable by Thursday; Suri will draft
a weekend staffing outline by Friday. There is
no later status report or new agenda.

Do not produce another weekly meeting page.
Answer in the form of a short handoff instead:

These are notes from the conversation already held,
not an update on this week's work. They identify
two follow-ups: your revised partner-timetable
request, due Thursday, and Suri's weekend staffing
outline, due Friday. The notes do not say whether
either was completed.

Please send their current states, new outreach
figures or team messages, and any topics either of
you wants to discuss next week. With those I can
prepare the meeting. The family matter belongs
in a private support conversation, without
prepared questions about personal details.

The same old notes can be useful history if the
manager later supplies current signals. They are
not themselves proof that last week's topics
are still open.
