# Stock-room drills — full sheets on invented lists

Four invented pick lists, picked the way the stock room should
pick them. The names, figures and products are invented; the
picks are the part to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal list with a mis-binned line

List: report is "Pavel, senior backend engineer". Manager's
draft: (a) "Pavel's tension with the SRE team — he disputed
their deploy freeze in the shared channel", (b) "why is the
billing-service migration behind", (c) "mention the
disaster-recovery test going well". The record: the SRE lead
posted a public thank-you naming Pavel for catching a bad
config before it hit production; the channel exchange shows
Pavel objecting — bluntly but on the substance — to an
emergency change made during the freeze without a rollback
plan; the migration is 4 of 10 endpoints done, waiting on a
finance sign-off requested 10 days ago; the DR test ran and
the platform lead messaged that recovery took 11 minutes
against a 60-minute target.

Picks:

- Line (a): **Re-binned**. One substantive objection to an
  undocumented freeze-break — binned as tension, picked as a
  one-time note with one narrow question. Stock note: "The SRE
  exchange was on the merits — they were already thanking you
  for the config catch. One thing to keep in view: that
  disagreement ran in the shared channel rather than on the
  incident ticket. Does moving it there cost you anything?"
  The objection stands; the channel takes one question and no
  more.
- Line (b): **Re-binned — another department's stock.** 4 of
  10 done, and the stall is a 10-day-old finance sign-off, not
  Pavel's pace. Stock note: "The migration is waiting on
  finance's sign-off — ten days outstanding. I'll chase the
  approver today and get a date; who should I escalate to if
  it slips again?"
- Line (c): **Picked first.** Two attributable credits — the
  SRE lead's public thanks and the DR figure (11 minutes vs a
  60-minute target). Open with them before any process line;
  credit picks first.
- Every written line gets a stock line and a pick; nothing
  written disappears.

Sheet: `# Stock sheet: Pavel, senior backend engineer` —
picture line ("a strong week with one line waiting on another
department"), figures shown as work, then pick order with all
three written lines, the entry behind each, and each pick —
then stock notes, manager's own list, forwarded list,
storekeeper's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin list picked honestly

List: report is "Mei, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep
my weekly with Mei — want to discuss her path to principal,
and whether she can take on the data-platform rebuild." No
action log, no figures, no channel excerpts. One calendar
item: Mei asked in passing about the sabbatical policy.

Thin is not invalid: the list aims at the coming meeting, so
the stock room still lists it — picked line by line. Only the
wrong kind of list (drill 3) is handed back whole. The reply
opens `# Stock sheet: Mei, staff engineer` — never a
hand-back preface.

Picks:

- Path to principal: **Picked — split by department.** Mei's
  direction and the manager's readiness question belong in
  this meeting, with real time: "You asked about the next
  level — here's the gap evidence I see and what I'd need
  documented; what scope would you take to build it?" The
  sabbatical question is **Forwarded**: policy sits with the
  people team — the sheet names the destination (the people
  partner) and the owner, one line, no stock notes.
- Readiness for the rebuild: **Held**. The list has no
  delivery record to weigh. It picks as the honest question,
  not an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have your
  recent delivery record in front of me — walk me through the
  last few weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what you'd take
  on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the sheet. A thin sheet
  of honest questions beats a padded one; the sheet head
  counts what the list lacks and the manager's own list names
  what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a list that is not this week's stock

List: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Ana —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list
three actions: Ana to send the capacity plan (due Monday), the
manager to confirm the headcount request, a follow-up on the
new-hire onboarding doc.

Pick: **whole list handed back — nothing counted.** The
reply, under 150 words, titled `# Not this week's stock`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was
agreed, not what has moved since. The document this supports
is a follow-up on the open actions, not a sheet: Ana's
capacity plan (due Monday), your headcount confirmation, the
onboarding doc follow-up — owners and dates exactly as
written. For a weekly sheet, send this week's action log,
any figures, and whatever Ana raised or you want addressed.
Nothing in the material is counted after that meeting, so a
sheet would replay old entries or invent new ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not
repeated, not summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own line

List: report is "David, senior systems engineer". Manager's
draft: (a) "David needs to get through his review queue —
nine architecture docs are waiting on him and two squads are
blocked"; (b) "David seemed checked out in the planning
session". The record: the nine docs are assigned because the
process names him sole approver for cross-squad architecture
changes; he shipped two features this sprint and raised the
queue himself in the last retro, proposing a rotation;
"checked out" appears once, in the manager's note.

Picks:

- Line (a): **Re-binned — the department that can fill it is
  the manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two squads
  is a capacity matter the manager owns, not a line to fill
  faster. It picks early as the manager's own decision:
  "Your rotation proposal is the right starting point — I
  want to approve it this week and take it to the squad
  leads. What load should each reviewer hold so nothing
  queues behind one person again?"
- Line (b): **Returned.** One manager observation with no
  entries behind it; the sprint shows two shipped features
  and a raised process fix. The sheet counts its absence
  plainly rather than inventing an engagement segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on David's retro
  proposal — is listed under the manager's own list as the
  manager's commitment, with whom and when.

## What every drill shares

- The record decides; the written bin is only the filing.
- Attributable credit picks first — a named person, a
  concrete result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its
  substance and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another department fills is forwarded there.
- One occurrence is a single count, never a pattern.
- Missing records pick as `Held` — the honest question in
  the slot — never covered over.
- Other-department lines get a destination and an owner,
  never stock notes.
- Every figure shows its own working; every written line
  gets a stock line and a pick.
- A thin list still gets a sheet. The hand-back is for the
  wrong kind of list only — it stays under 150 words and
  carries no stock-room structure.
