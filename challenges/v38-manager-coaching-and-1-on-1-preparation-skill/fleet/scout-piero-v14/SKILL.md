# Duty memo — the weekly check

The draft arrives at the end of the week, written from memory. Memory keeps the topic and drops the proof: the stalled job becomes "status", the client letter becomes "went well", one bad day becomes "a pattern". So the duty order stands — record first, draft held against it, and the checking printed on the reply above the brief. A check the manager can audit is a check they will trust.

## What the record gets asked

Open, overdue and NOT STARTED all count open. Resolved stays resolved unless something newer directly contradicts it — the original complaint never reopens a closed row. A figure only in the manager's head does not exist; what gets computed shows its operands, points never percent. Note every open row the manager owns — those are preparation — and every compliment that arrived in writing.

## How each item gets called

- **Recognition** — written praise exists. Opens the meeting, quoted in the giver's words, the figure attached.
- **Blocked** — another desk holds it: an approval not given, a dependency gone quiet, more than one pair of hands can lift. The manager leaves with the move. If the trail shows it wider than the draft admits — other teams behind it, a senior name copied — the call says so and the item climbs whatever priority was written.
- **Coaching** — one behaviour on two or more separate dated occasions, or a goal the report chose. Runs on questions, never lectures; when the report already found the cause and tracks it, coach the step before it — spot it sooner — anchored to the real figures.
- **Career** — the report themselves raised growth, training, scope, review timing. One honest question; promise only what is keepable.
- **Status** — nothing to repair, praise or settle. Last, compressed to a line or two.
- **Parked** — its room is elsewhere: pay, HR, a dispute a different team owns, next quarter. Sent there with a line saying why. Personal matters stay "the personal matter", elaborated never.

Finer calls the check makes: a tone complaint tracing to another desk's hold-up is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. Repeated flags on one problem count once. A feeling with nothing under it is dropped, the signals named. Coaching wants a pattern — separate dated occasions; a single rough moment on a clean record, whoever caught it, gets a passing word at most. A repair the report already owns moves the item's weight to the manager's side — the safeguard, the template — not a second telling-off. Recognition opens only on a quotable instance; when none exists, a line above item one admits it.

## What the page looks like

`## Checking the list` — the check, printed. One numbered entry per draft item, in the draft's order: its wording quoted; **Shows:** what the record holds — who said or logged it, where, when, the row or figure; **Call:** the verdict and the reason — "stands" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, merged into another. Then **Missed:** what the manager owes or the draft skipped, and **Arithmetic:** anything computed, operands shown — "18 against 15 is three over plan".

`# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]` — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line — report, tenure, the week in a phrase. Items in meeting order, `**name — Call, ~N min**`, a sentence or two of things to say and ask, the real numbers and names inside. Then **Left off** (parked or dropped, with reasons), **Manager's own list** (every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move — done before the report is asked for anything, first the row an item depends on), and **The reasoning** (a few lines on why the calls landed).

Thin packet? Still a page — the check names what is missing, the brief asks rather than asserts: what cannot be seen, the question to put, what a real answer sounds like. A thin week makes a thin brief — nothing added for balance, no praise without a quotable source; length yields to honesty. A full duty week sits in `references/desk-notes.md`. Take the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When what arrives is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already had — whatever its label — the reply is one short note under 180 words, no checking:

- Say plainly: it records a past meeting, not preparation for the next — name the document and the meeting it records.
- Where open actions survive inside it, write the follow-up — owner, action, date as given — in a line or two; never a brief, never old items re-sorted.
- Name what would make it preparation: this week's open items, current figures, anything raised since.
- Personal material mentioned in passing stays "the personal matter", nothing more.
