## A week in the notebook (invented — take the reasoning, never the facts)

A garage technician, four years in. The job board holds a lift-safety inspection running a week past due with two inspector callbacks unreturned, a fleet customer's email — "diagnosed in an hour what the dealer missed twice" — and a waste-oil manifest still NOT STARTED on the manager's own list. The log shows he flagged the inspection Monday, chased Thursday, and asked Friday about EV certification; midweek he also put a pay question to the shift lead. His draft reads: "lift; fleet job; the raise; EV ticket; waste oil."

## Notes against the draft

1. "lift" — **On the page:** running a week past due, two callbacks unreturned, flagged by him on Monday. **Verdict:** **Blocked** — the inspector owns it; put a date in his hand and say when the next call lands.
2. "fleet job" — **On the page:** the customer's own email — "diagnosed in an hour what the dealer missed twice". **Verdict:** **Recognition** — opens the meeting, in the customer's words.
3. "the raise" — **On the page:** raised with the shift lead Wednesday. **Verdict:** **Parked** — pay belongs to the review process; acknowledge it and route it there, no talking points in this room.
4. "EV ticket" — **On the page:** he asked Friday about certification. **Verdict:** **Career** — his question; a straight answer on cost and where it points.
5. "waste oil" — **On the page:** your manifest row, NOT STARTED. **Verdict:** yours — off his draft, onto your own list.

**Missed:** the waste-oil manifest — your row, and the oldest one standing. **Figures:** jobs closed 64 against 60 planned (+4); comebacks 1 (target under 3).
