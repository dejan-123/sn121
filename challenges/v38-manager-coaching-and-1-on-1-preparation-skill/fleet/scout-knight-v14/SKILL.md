# The notebook

Draft agendas come from Friday-afternoon memory, and memory is a careless clerk — it keeps the headline and drops the evidence underneath. A held-up job gets filed as "status". A written compliment becomes "went well". One bad afternoon becomes "a pattern". The notebook reverses that order: record laid out first, draft weighed against it, and the weighing written where the manager can read it.

## Laying out the record

Take tickets, messages, reviews, figures and praise at face value. Rows open, overdue or NOT STARTED count open; resolved stays resolved unless a newer entry directly contradicts it — an old complaint never reopens a closed row. A number living only in the manager's head does not exist; each computed figure is shown from its operands, a point never a percent. Two collections grow during the read: the open rows that are the manager's, and the praise that landed by name.

## Weighing each item

Six verdicts cover every draft item. **Recognition** needs a quotable instance in the record and opens the meeting on the giver's words, number attached. **Blocked** means the hold sits elsewhere — an approval withheld, a dependency silent, more requests than one pair of hands; the manager leaves owning the move, and where the trail shows the hold wider than the draft admits — other teams queuing, a senior name copied — it climbs whatever priority was written. **Coaching** is earned by a pattern — the same behaviour on separate dated occasions, or a goal the report chose; it runs on questions never lectures, and where the report already found the cause and tracks it, the coaching moves a step earlier — spot it sooner — on the real figures. **Career** is growth, training, scope or review timing the report raised themselves: one sincere question, one keepable promise. **Status** covers what needs no repair, praise or settlement — last, a line or two. **Parked** is another room's business — pay, HR, a dispute owned elsewhere, next quarter — sent on with a reason, and personal matters stay "the personal matter", elaborated never.

Finer verdicts the page catches: frustration pointed at another desk's hold-up is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. A cluster of messages on one problem counts as one issue. An impression with nothing under it is dropped, signals named. One bad moment on an otherwise clean record, whoever flagged it, earns a passing word at most — Coaching wants a pattern. When the report already owns the fix, the remaining weight belongs on the manager's side — the safeguard, the gate — not a second lecture. Recognition opens only on a quotable instance; absent one, a line above item one admits it.

## What lands on the page

`## Notes against the draft` — the weighing, printed. Per draft item, one numbered entry in draft order: quote its wording, then **On the page:** the record's account — who said or logged it, where, when, row or figure — then **Verdict:** the call plus its reason, "stands" where the draft got it right, otherwise parked, dropped or folded into another item. Close the section with **Missed:** (what the manager owes or the draft skipped) and **Figures:** (arithmetic written out from operands — "18 against 15 is three over plan").

`# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]` — 350–700 words. Open with **Context:** — report, tenure, the week in a phrase — then items in meeting order, `**name — Verdict, ~N min**`, each a sentence or two of what to say and ask with real names and numbers inside. Close with three short sections: **Set aside** for anything parked or dropped and why; **Before the meeting** listing every open row the manager owns with its concrete move — cleared before the report hears an ask, the row an item leans on first; and **Thinking**, a few lines on why the verdicts landed.

Invent nothing. When the packet is thin the page still gets written — the notes name what never arrived and the brief asks instead of asserting: what cannot be seen, the question to put, what a real answer sounds like. Slim is correct; nothing is padded for balance, no recognition without a quotable instance — honesty outranks length. `references/garage-notes.md` holds a full week. Borrow the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

Sometimes the input is the record of a meeting already held — minutes, a transcript, someone's notes under any label. Send back one short note under 180 words and nothing else.

Name the document and say it looks backward at a held conversation, not forward to the next one. Say what preparation would need — this week's open items, current figures, the manager's rows, whatever was flagged since. Where open actions survive inside, each gets a follow-up line — owner, action, date as written. No brief, no re-sort of old items. Personal material mentioned in passing stays "the personal matter", nothing more.
