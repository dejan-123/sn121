# Three invented packets and their replies

These workplaces are fiction. Copy the calls and the page mechanics, never the names, counts or dates. The first packet contains competing labels, the second is almost empty, and the third records a conversation that is already over.

## Case one: a live week with competing labels

Inez Sato runs visitor programmes at a small maritime museum. Her manager supplies:

- Draft: "Shoutout for the school visit"; "Status on the audio guide"; "Coaching: too blunt with volunteers"; "Feedback on the delayed group handoff"; "The interpretation lead role"; "Her question about compressed hours."
- Action log: school safety route update (Inez, DONE Tuesday); replacement headset order (Manager, OVERDUE); new volunteer rota to front desk (Inez, IN PROGRESS).
- Figure: 12 school groups hosted against a usual 16.
- Monday, the head teacher wrote: "Inez noticed the tide display was crowded and led our class through the chart room instead. Every child still got to try the navigation table."
- Tuesday, Inez reported failing audio-guide headsets and proposed lending the wired sets. The supplier promised a Friday update, not a delivery date.
- Wednesday and Friday each had a volunteer handoff that omitted the arrival-time briefing; the draft's "delayed group handoff" is Friday's second instance, not a separate problem.
- Thursday, Inez asked what would make her a candidate for interpretation lead. Separately she asked about compressed hours.

The reply:

## Reading the week

A draft, log, figure and messages arrived for a meeting still ahead, so this is preparation.

1. "Shoutout for the school visit"
**Signals:** Monday's head-teacher message credits Inez's chart-room detour and preserved the navigation activity.
**Call:** Recognition, using the concrete judgment rather than vague thanks.

2. "Status on the audio guide"
**Signals:** headsets are failing; the supplier promised only an update; the manager's replacement order is overdue.
**Call:** Unblock. Inez proposed the wired sets; the manager owes approval and a dated supplier answer.

3. "Coaching: too blunt with volunteers"
**Signals:** no quoted blunt remark; two separate handoffs missed the same arrival-time briefing.
**Call:** Coaching on the repeated omission, not on an unsupported manner judgment.

4. "Feedback on the delayed group handoff"
**Signals:** it refers to Friday's second handoff.
**Call:** merge into the coaching conversation; it is not an extra event.

5. "The interpretation lead role"
**Signals:** Inez asked about it Thursday.
**Call:** Career, connected to the judgment she used on the school visit.

6. "Her question about compressed hours"
**Signals:** working-arrangement request.
**Call:** route to a dedicated conversation; no talking points here.

**Not on the draft:** the overdue headset order is the manager's open row; Inez's rota remains in progress.
**Figures:** action items: 1 of 3 done (33%). Twelve groups against a usual 16 is (12 - 16) / 16, down 25%.

# 1-on-1 brief: Inez Sato, Visitor Programmes Lead

The school group still reached the navigation table because Inez changed route when the tide display became crowded; the head teacher saw that judgment. Twelve groups visited against a usual 16, down 25%, while the audio guide remains unreliable and the manager's replacement order is overdue. Action items: 1 of 3 done (33%). The handoff issue is one repeated briefing omission, and compressed hours belong elsewhere.

**1. Chart-room detour - Recognition**
Use the teacher's account of the crowded display and the navigation table. It credits a specific decision under pressure, not merely a pleasant visit.

**2. Audio-guide headsets - Unblock**
Inez has already proposed the wired sets. Approve the stopgap and the replacement order, then ask the supplier for a date rather than another update. "Which visits need the wired sets first while the order is settled?"

**3. Arrival-time briefing in the volunteer handoffs - Coaching**
Wednesday and Friday each omitted the same briefing. "What kept the arrival-time note out of both handoffs, and where should it live so the next volunteer receives it before a group arrives?"

**4. Front-desk rota - Status**
The rota is still in progress. "What remains before it reaches the desk, and is any part waiting on me?" If the answer is a dependency, the manager clears it; if only proofreading remains, agree the delivery time so the next volunteer handoff can use it.

**5. Interpretation lead - Career**
The chart-room detour is evidence of interpretation judgment. "Which parts of the lead role would you want more of after that visit, and what would you still need to show?" If she names an evidence gap, agree one place in the next programme where she can demonstrate it rather than promising the appointment.

**Parking lot**
- Compressed-hours request: dedicated working-arrangements conversation with the appropriate owner; book it this week. No reason or detail is drafted here.

**Before the meeting**
- Clear the overdue replacement-headset order or bring a decision date; it determines whether the wired sets are a bridge or the plan.
- Ask the supplier for a firm delivery window.
- Check whether the volunteer rota can reach the front desk before the next booked visit.

**Why these calls**
The teacher's message turns the shoutout into earned Recognition. Headset work becomes an Unblock because the next moves sit with the manager and supplier. The manner claim drops, while the repeated missing briefing remains Coaching. Compressed hours leave the agenda because they need a different process.

## Case two: only the draft arrived

For Dev Malik, allotment seed-bank coordinator, the manager sends only:

"1. Where are we with the catalog migration? 2. Quick check on Dev's workload. 3. He asked about a pay adjustment. 4. Career goals check-in."

No log, figures, messages or performance observations came with it.

## Reading the week

A draft for a future meeting arrived alone, so this is thin preparation; it is not a record of a meeting already held.

1. "Where are we with the catalog migration?"
**Signals:** no task list or dated update.
**Call:** Status; ask what is done and what waits.

2. "Quick check on Dev's workload"
**Signals:** no observed overload or missed commitment.
**Call:** neutral Status, not a capacity finding.

3. "He asked about a pay adjustment"
**Signals:** a real request, but pay belongs elsewhere.
**Call:** route to a dedicated pay conversation.

4. "Career goals check-in"
**Signals:** manager created the item; Dev supplied no goal.
**Call:** Career as an invitation he can decline.

**Not on the draft:** no action log was supplied, so no manager row can be claimed open or closed.
**Figures:** none supplied; no completion or trend can be calculated.

# 1-on-1 brief: Dev Malik, Seed-Bank Coordinator

The draft names the catalog migration, workload, pay and development, but supplies no record behind them. The useful meeting asks what the migration needs and lets Dev choose whether he wants a development conversation. Pay moves to the proper venue.

No recognition opener: no input credits a result.

**1. Catalog migration - Status**
"I do not have the catalog task list today. Which part is finished, what comes next, and is anything waiting on access or a decision from me?" Listen for a dependency to clear, not a report Dev must chase alone. If he names a missing export or another team's approval, turn the answer into a concrete manager move rather than adding another status check next week.

**2. Workload around the migration - Status**
"Alongside the catalog work, what else is competing for your time this week, and is there anything I should reprioritize?" Listen for a deadline or assignment only the manager can move. If Dev says the load is manageable, accept that answer; the absence of a finding is itself a finding, not a reason to press for strain.

**3. Development check-in - Career**
"Is there any part of the seed-bank or catalog work you would like more of, or should we leave this open until you have had time to think?" Do not assign Dev a goal he did not name. If he chooses one, ask which recent task would give him a useful first piece of evidence.

**Parking lot**
- Pay adjustment: dedicated pay conversation with the pay contact; set a date and draft no amount or argument here.

**Before the meeting**
- Ask whoever tracks the catalog for the task list and current entry count.
- Pull any recent channel thread on the migration.
- Check whether the seed-bank roster shows competing duties that would explain the workload item.
- Invite Dev to send a development topic if he wants one.

**Why these calls**
The packet is thin, so each surviving item remains a question. The workload check stays neutral, the career item leaves Dev in control, and pay goes to the right process. A brief can still be useful without inventing evidence.

## Case three: the pasted meeting already happened

The manager asks to prepare next week's one-on-one from "Last meeting with Leila Osman, box-office coordinator, 3 March." The note lists attendees, topics and agreed actions: the manager will ask facilities for a second ticket scanner by 6 March; Leila will draft the school matinee seating plan by 8 March; the manager will confirm the foyer staffing budget by 9 March. No later status is given.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

This is the record of the 3 March meeting already held, not a fresh packet for next week. It supports a follow-up on its actions: manager, second ticket scanner, due 6 March; manager, foyer staffing budget, due 9 March; Leila, school matinee seating plan, due 8 March. For the next brief, send the current state of those actions, this week's messages or figures, and a new draft agenda. The family matter named in the record belongs in a private conversation and is not drafted here.

## Boundaries

If the headset approval had been Inez's row, it would not be a manager debt. If the supplier had given a firm delivery date, that fact would replace the request for one. A message saying "I will call finance if the invoice has not arrived by noon" remains a plan; the reply says it was planned and asks whether it happened. A repeated miss already diagnosed by the report leads to an earlier-flag question, not another lecture on the miss.
