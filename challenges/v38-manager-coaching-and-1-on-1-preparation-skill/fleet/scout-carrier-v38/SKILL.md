# Weekly 1-on-1 brief

A draft agenda is memory, not evidence. Read the action log, figures and messages first; then show the manager the call each draft item earned before giving them a page they can use. A changed label needs its signal visible. The page should remain fair if the report read it: no invented praise, no pattern from one incident, no generic question, and no sensitive matter argued in the weekly slot.

Read `references/cases-first.md` once with read_reference. Its workplaces are invented; copy the decisions and shape, never its facts or wording.

## Reading the week

For preparation, the reply begins `## Reading the week`. Its first line identifies what arrived and confirms that the meeting is still ahead.

Then take every draft item in draft order. Use one numbered entry each:

`1. "<the draft item or label>"`  
`**Signals:**` the dated row, message, figure or report request that tests it.  
`**Call:**` the earned conversation and the reason: holds, becomes another tag, merges, drops or routes.

After the items, `**Not on the draft:**` names anything the agenda missed: open manager rows first, then report-owned open rows tied to a topic, a submitted topic, a third-party request or a sensitive matter. `**Figures:**` works the useful numbers and summarises the log.

When a record of a meeting already held is supplied, even alongside a request to prepare the next one, do not use it as current evidence. A skip-level, rating draft or meeting about another person is likewise not this weekly prep. Those inputs take the redirect below. A draft for a future meeting with little or no evidence is never a redirect: do not use `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request`, do not explain that a brief is the correct response, and begin directly with `## Reading the week`.

## Work the figures first

Derive before writing, with operands beside results:

- change from a baseline: `(current - baseline) / baseline`, signed;
- estimate or scope change: the same comparison between planned and actual;
- rates and scores: percentage-point change, not percent change;
- action log: state each row's status, then `Action items: X of Y done (Z%)` when the denominator is clear;
- separate counts stay separate; nine reviews and five waiting teams are not a ratio.

A cause that explains part of a gap leaves the remainder open. No number, name, owner, date or outcome may be supplied from memory.

## Conversation calls

- **Recognition**: a named person or measured result credits specific work this period. Quote the credit and its effect. One earned opener at most; a vague "strong week" is not enough.
- **Unblock**: work held by another team, supplier, approval, access, calendar or an assigned load one person cannot clear. The manager leaves with their own move: approve, escalate, broker, reassign or set a date. A report's proposed remedy is backed, not repackaged as the manager's idea. Repeated messages chasing one hold are one dependency, not a character pattern.
- **Status**: live work whose current position, next decision or date is genuinely unknown. Finished or already praised work is not status.
- **Quick feedback**: one dated event owned by the report, with a clear remaining adjustment. Credit a repair already made, then ask one light question. A senior observer or severe result does not create a second instance.
- **Coaching**: the same report-owned behaviour on two or more dated occasions, or a skill the report chose. Ask two evidence-linked questions. If the report already found the cause, credit that and ask about the earlier lever—what should have been flagged sooner, to whom and when—not the symptom again.
- **Career**: a role, level, course, programme, promotion case or direction the report raised. Give it real time beside this week's strongest relevant signal. A manager-pencilled check-in with no report topic stays a modest invitation. An open report-owned packet step is asked as a next step, never implied complete.

An imposed queue is an Unblock even when the draft calls it feedback: if the report flags an unmanageable assigned load and proposes a rotation or triage, the manager's job is to decide or broker it, not tell the report to carry it differently. A tense message sent while chasing an unresolved dependency is context for that dependency unless separate dated conduct supports a behaviour call.

## Outcome and confidence checks

Before fixing a tag, name the outcome the item must produce: acknowledge the work, clear a path, learn the position, repair one moment, build a skill, develop a direction or route it elsewhere. If two tags could serve it, choose the one that produces that outcome in the room.

Grade each material fact in the audit language: **confirmed** when the packet records it, **stated plan** when someone says they will do it, **impression** when the manager suspects it without an event, and **absent** when no input supplies it. Only confirmed facts get past-tense verbs. A stated plan is asked about; an impression is tested or dropped; an absence is named, not filled.

Keep a small ledger while reading: manager owes, report owes, evidence to fetch, venue to book. `Before the meeting` resolves the first and third; the agenda can question the second; `Parking lot` holds the fourth.

## Precision and venue

A promise is a plan, not a fact: "I'll chase at noon" or "I'm going to escalate if they stay silent" does not prove it happened after noon. Write that the report planned it and ask whether it happened; never write `you planned`, `you proposed` or `you escalated` about the report. Outside a quoted question, use `the manager` and the report's name. Inside a quoted question, `you` may address the report.

Every question must be speakable and attached to this week's thread, ticket, figure or the report's own words. If it could be printed unchanged for another employee, make it specific. Where useful, say what the manager should listen for.

Pay, health, family circumstances, accommodation, formal HR, a promotion or level decision, a third-party complaint, and a process owned elsewhere go to `Parking lot` with topic, venue and booking step. Draft no talking points. The report's unfinished action toward a promotion packet can remain a Career question; the decision itself goes to its booked forum.

Count all rows in the log summary, including DONE rows. `Before the meeting` always lists every manager-owned row that is NOT STARTED, OVERDUE or otherwise open; DONE manager rows are closed, and an open manager task is never dismissed as "outside this brief" even when it is a team-wide process. Report-owned open rows are not manager debts, but they may become questions when they support a listed topic.

## The carried page

After the reading, write `# 1-on-1 brief: <name>, <role>` and 350-700 words. Count from the brief title to the end before sending; if it is under 350, add grounded questions, manager preparation or fetch steps—never repeat the audit or pad with generalities.

The opening context is two or three plain sentences: what moved, what is stuck and on whom, the report's own topic, the figures that matter and `Action items: X of Y done (Z%)` when available.

Then agenda items headed `**N. <topic in this week's words> - <Class>**`:

1. Recognition first when earned. If none is supported, begin above item 1 with `No recognition opener:` and name the missing signal.
2. Unblocks and other manager-moving holds.
3. Quick feedback or Coaching.
4. Live Status.
5. Career with protected time, especially a report-submitted topic.

Under each heading use at most three sentences: the signal, the manager's move or a quoted question the manager can say, and what to listen for where useful. Agenda body sentences name the report in third person; `you` may appear only inside a quoted question addressed to the report. One signal gets one item; a parked or dropped draft line gets no number.

End with:

- `**Parking lot**`: routed topics only, venue and owner, or `Nothing to park this week.`
- `**Before the meeting**`: open manager rows to settle and missing records to fetch. Settle what the manager owes before asking the report for more.
- `**Why these calls**`: a few natural sentences explaining moved labels, dropped impressions, routed subjects and the signal behind each. Do not restate the audit.

The page should sound like a colleague prepared the manager, not a checklist.

## Thin packet

A draft with no supporting records is still live prep. The reading says what did not arrive, then each draft item stays in the class its own words support. On the page, each item says what is unknown, asks a grounded question and names what to listen for. A workload check without evidence is neutral Status, not a finding; a manager-only career check is an invitation. `Before the meeting` names the log, figures, thread or task sheet to fetch and who can provide it. Reach 350 words through useful preparation, never invented evidence.

## Not a prep request

For a held-meeting record, return only `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request`, under 180 words. Say what identifies it as a meeting already held and why a forward brief would replay old facts. List its recorded open actions exactly with owner and due date, manager first. Ask for the current state of those actions, this week's messages or figures and a new draft agenda. Mention a personal, health, family or pay matter only in the record's minimal words and route it privately. No working note, agenda or fabricated current status.

A live reply starts with `## Reading the week`; a redirect starts with `# Not a 1-on-1 prep request`.
