# Claim drills — full sheets on invented files

Four invented claim files, settled the way the manual settles
them. Names, figures and products are invented; the
settlements are the part to carry.

## Case 1 — normal file, one mislabelled claim

File: report "Marcus, senior platform engineer". Filed:
(a) "Marcus's tension with the security team — he disputed
their findings in the shared channel", (b) "why is the
identity migration behind", (c) "recognise the backup-restore
test". Record: the security lead posted public thanks naming
Marcus for flagging an auth bypass before release; the
channel exchange shows Marcus objecting — bluntly, on
substance — to a finding he had already shown was a false
positive; the migration is 4 of 11 services done, waiting on
a vendor contract amendment requested 13 days ago; the
restore test completed in 22 minutes against a 90-minute
target.

Settlements:

- (a) **Re-assessed → one-time note** with one narrow
  channel question: "The security exchange was on the
  merits — they were already thanking you for the bypass
  catch. One thing: it ran in the shared channel rather
  than on the finding ticket. Does moving it there cost
  anything?" Substance stands; channel takes one question
  and no more.
- (b) **Re-assessed → manager's own follow-up.** 4 of 11
  done, stalled on a 13-day vendor amendment, not Marcus's
  pace: "I'll chase the vendor today for a date; who do I
  escalate to if it slips again?"
- (c) **Paid first.** Two attributable credits — public
  thanks, and 22 minutes vs a 90-minute target. Credit
  opens the sheet.
- Every filed claim gets a file line and a settlement;
  nothing filed disappears.

Sheet: `# Desk sheet: Marcus, senior platform engineer` —
picture line, worked figures, settlement order with all
three claims, desk notes, adjuster's own claims, referred
list, adjuster's close.

## Case 2 — thin file, settled honestly

File: report "Yuki, staff engineer". Filed note: "prep my
weekly with Yuki — her route to principal, and whether she
can lead the caching rework." No action log, no figures, no
excerpts. One note: Yuki asked in passing about conference
leave.

Thin is not invalid: the file aims at the coming meeting,
so it is still this week's file — settled claim by claim.
Only an archive (case 3) goes back. First line:
`# Desk sheet: Yuki, staff engineer`.

Settlements:

- Route to principal: **Paid — split by desk.** Yuki's
  direction and the readiness question stay in the meeting
  with real time: "You asked about the next level — here's
  the gap evidence I see and what I'd need documented; what
  scope would you take to build it?" The leave question is
  **Referred** — it sits with the people team; destination
  and owner named, one line, no talking points.
- Caching readiness: **Pending**. No delivery record in the
  packet; it stands as the honest question: "I don't have
  your recent record in front of me — walk me through the
  last few weeks: shipped, stuck, and what you'd take on."
- Nothing is invented to fill the sheet. A thin sheet of
  honest questions beats a padded one.

## Case 3 — archive file, returned whole

File: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with
Priya — please prep next week's agenda from them." Three
open actions: Priya's capacity report (due Wednesday), the
manager's headcount sign-off, a new-hire pairing follow-up.

**Whole file returned — no claims settled.** Under 150
words, headed `# Not this week's file`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was
agreed, not what has moved since. This supports a follow-up
on the open actions, not a sheet: Priya's capacity report
(due Wednesday), your headcount sign-off, the pairing
follow-up — owners and dates exactly as filed. For a
sheet, send this week's action items, any figures, and
whatever Priya raised or you want addressed. Nothing in
the material is filed after that meeting, so a sheet would
re-settle closed claims or invent unfiled ones.

Personal matters in the minutes stay private — not
repeated, not summarized, not hinted at.

## Case 4 — the queue that is the manager's own claim

File: report "Owen, senior systems engineer". Filed:
(a) "Owen must clear his review backlog — nine design docs
wait on him, two squads blocked"; (b) "Owen seemed
disengaged in the demo". Record: the nine docs sit with
him because the process names him sole approver for
cross-squad work; he shipped three features this sprint
and raised the backlog in retro himself, proposing a
rotation; "disengaged" appears once, in the manager's
note.

Settlements:

- (a) **Re-assessed → manager's capacity settlement.** A
  sole-approver rule stalling two squads is owned by the
  manager, early on the sheet: "Your rotation proposal is
  the right starting point — I'll approve it this week
  and take it to the squad leads. What load should each
  reviewer hold so nothing queues behind one person
  again?"
- (b) **Denied.** One unsourced observation; the sprint
  shows three shipped features and a raised process fix.
  Its absence is recorded plainly.
- The manager's own open item — acting on the retro
  proposal — sits under adjuster's own claims with whom
  and when.

## Constants across all four

- The record decides; the filed label is only the filing.
- Attributable credit pays first.
- Justified substance keeps its place; the channel takes
  one question.
- Another desk's claim refers there.
- One occurrence is never a pattern.
- Missing records stand `Pending` — the honest question —
  never covered over.
- Referred claims get destination and owner, never talking
  points.
- Every figure shows its arithmetic; every filed claim gets
  a file line and a settlement.
- A thin file still gets a sheet. The return is for
  archives only — under 150 words, no desk structure.
