# Handover drills — full boards on invented packets

Four invented boards, marked the way the board should be marked.
The names, figures and products are invented; the marks are the
part to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal board with a mis-headed line

Board: report is "Naomi, senior frontend engineer". Manager's
draft: (a) "Naomi's run-in with marketing — she disputed their
numbers in the project channel", (b) "why is the checkout
redesign behind", (c) "shout-out for the accessibility audit".
The record: the marketing lead posted a public thank-you naming
Naomi for catching a tracking bug that had inflated their funnel
figures; the channel exchange shows Naomi objecting — bluntly
but on the substance — to a funnel claim based on the buggy
data; the redesign is 6 of 11 screens done, waiting on legal
copy approval requested 8 days ago; the audit shipped and the
QA lead messaged that it cleared 31 outstanding accessibility
tickets.

Marks:

- Line (a): **Re-entered**. One substantive objection to a claim
  built on buggy data — headed as a run-in, marked as a
  one-time note with one narrow question. Shift note: "The
  marketing exchange was on the merits — they were already
  thanking you for the tracking catch. One thing to keep in
  view: that disagreement ran in the project channel rather
  than on the ticket. Does moving it there cost you anything?"
  The objection stands; the channel takes one question and no
  more.
- Line (b): **Re-entered — another lead's board.** 6 of 11
  done, and the stall is an 8-day-old approval with legal, not
  Naomi's pace. Shift note: "The redesign is waiting on legal's
  copy approval — eight days outstanding. I'll chase the
  reviewer today and get a date; who should I escalate to if
  it slips again?"
- Line (c): **Passed on first.** Two attributable credits — the
  marketing lead's public thanks and the QA figure (31 tickets
  cleared). Open with them before any process line; credit
  marks first.
- Every written line gets a board line and a mark; nothing
  written disappears.

Board: `# Shift board: Naomi, senior frontend engineer` —
picture line ("a strong week with one line waiting on another
lead"), figures shown as work, then running order with all
three written lines, the entry behind each, and each mark —
then shift notes, manager's own lines, handed-off list, shift
supervisor's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin board marked honestly

Board: report is "Omar, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep
my weekly with Omar — want to discuss his path to senior staff,
and whether he can own the auth service rebuild." No action
log, no figures, no channel excerpts. One calendar item: Omar
asked in passing about the title-change process.

Thin is not invalid: the board aims at the coming meeting, so
it still gets marked — line by line. Only the wrong kind of
board (drill 3) is handed back whole. The reply opens
`# Shift board: Omar, staff engineer` — never a hand-back
preface.

Marks:

- Path to senior staff: **Passed on — split by lead.** Omar's
  direction and the manager's readiness question belong in this
  meeting, with real time: "You asked about the next step —
  here's the gap evidence I see and what I'd need documented;
  what scope would you take to build it?" The title-process
  question is **Handed off**: the process sits with the
  promotion review — the board names the destination (the
  review cycle with the manager) and the owner, one line, no
  shift notes.
- Readiness for the auth rebuild: **Left open**. The board has
  no delivery record to weigh. It marks as the honest question,
  not an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have your
  recent delivery record in front of me — walk me through the
  last few weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what you'd take
  on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the board. A thin board
  of honest questions beats a padded one; the board head
  enters what the packet lacks and the manager's own lines
  name what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a board that is not this week's

Board: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Jun —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list
three actions: Jun to publish the latency report (due Tuesday),
the manager to confirm the offsite venue, a follow-up on the
docs migration.

Mark: **whole board handed back — nothing entered.** The reply,
under 150 words, titled `# Not this week's board`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was
agreed, not what has moved since. The document this supports
is a follow-up on the open actions, not a board: Jun's latency
report (due Tuesday), your venue confirmation, the docs
migration follow-up — owners and dates exactly as written. For
a weekly board, send this week's action log, any figures, and
whatever Jun raised or you want addressed. Nothing in the
material is entered after that meeting, so a board would
replay old lines or invent new ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not
repeated, not summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own line

Board: report is "Lena, senior infrastructure engineer".
Manager's draft: (a) "Lena has to clear her review pile —
eight infra RFCs are waiting on her and three teams are
blocked"; (b) "Lena seemed flat in the last sprint review".
The record: the eight RFCs are assigned because the process
names her sole approver for cross-team infrastructure changes;
she shipped two features this sprint and raised the pile
herself in the last retro, proposing a rotation; "flat"
appears once, in the manager's note.

Marks:

- Line (a): **Re-entered — the lead who can close it is the
  manager.** A sole-approver rule stalling three teams is a
  capacity matter the manager owns, not a line to work faster.
  It marks early as the manager's own decision: "Your rotation
  proposal is the right starting point — I want to approve it
  this week and take it to the team leads. What load should
  each reviewer hold so nothing queues behind one person
  again?"
- Line (b): **Crossed off.** One manager observation with no
  entries behind it; the sprint shows two shipped features
  and a raised process fix. The board enters its absence
  plainly rather than inventing an engagement segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Lena's retro
  proposal — is listed under the manager's own lines as the
  manager's commitment, with whom and when.

## What every drill shares

- The record decides; the written heading is only the filing.
- Attributable credit marks first — a named person, a concrete
  result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its
  substance and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another lead closes is handed off to that lead.
- One occurrence is a single entry, never a pattern.
- Missing records mark as `Left open` — the honest question
  in the slot — never covered over.
- Other-lead lines get a destination and an owner, never shift
  notes.
- Every figure shows its own working; every written line gets
  a board line and a mark.
- A thin board still gets marked. The hand-back is for the
  wrong kind of board only — it stays under 150 words and
  carries no board structure.
