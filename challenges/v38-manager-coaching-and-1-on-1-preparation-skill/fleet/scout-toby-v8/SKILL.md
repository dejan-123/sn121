# The board — log every line, mark it on the record

A manager's draft agenda is a board of handover lines, each headed
the manager's way. The heading is a first intention, not a
handover. Every line goes on the board and is marked on the shift
record alone — what the packet documents, nothing it does not. The
output is a board the manager reads on the way in: every line
logged, every mark traceable to a record entry, the count matching
the lines written.

## Board check — is this this week's board?

Three boards look like a weekly board and are not: notes, minutes
or a transcript of a meeting already held (even when the manager
asks to plan the next one from it — it records last week's
handover, not this week's record; what it supports is a follow-up
on the actions); preparation for a skip-level or a different
meeting; a performance-review, rating or disciplinary draft. For
these, mark nothing. Reply in under 150 words titled
`# Not this week's board`: what the board is, which document it
actually supports and who owns it, what to bring for a weekly
board — ending with the one-line reason (nothing in the material
is entered after that meeting, so a board would replay old lines
or invent new ones). The hand-back carries no board structure: no
lines logged, no marks, no order — it is a plain reply, not a
board. Where the input is last week's board, list each open
action with owner and date exactly as written; open actions carry
forward as entries, never as assumed closed.

The hand-back triggers on the board's kind and nothing else. A
board of the manager's own topics for the coming meeting is
always this week's board, even with no record behind it: a bare
agenda, a two-line note, topics with no figures behind them.
Scarcity is marked line by line (Left open, below); it never
triggers a hand-back. For every board that is this week's —
full or thin — the reply opens `# Shift board:` with no
preamble.

## Board check — what gets entered

Only what the packet documents gets entered: an action item and
its state (the manager's own included), a sprint or ticket
figure, a quoted line, the manager's note, the report's own
topic. No invented figure, quote, date, name or mood. Where a
mark needs a record entry the packet lacks, the lack is entered
— never filled with a comfortable guess.

## The tally — how entries weigh

Entries weigh by kind, not volume. One documented occurrence is
a single entry — it can support a note, it cannot support a
pattern mark. The same conduct documented on two or more
occasions is a pattern. Attributed commendation — a named
person's thanks or a concrete result — is credit earned; effort,
hours or unverifiable praise is not. The report's own account is
self-reported: enterable, weighed below an independent entry. An
impression the packet cannot source is a reason to ask, not an
entry.

## Closed stays closed

A line the record shows finished is entered as a one-line close —
unless another logged entry cannot stand with the finish. The
line's written heading never reopens it. Where a later entry and
the entered finish genuinely conflict, enter the conflict in
writing; never re-mark silently.

## Which lead owns it

A line belongs to the lead who can close it. Work waiting on an
input another person or team controls is that lead's board — a
stalled dependency goes on the board as the manager's own action
to move it, never as the report's fault to answer for. A sole
reviewer or approver whose queue stalls several teams is a
capacity matter the manager owns: it marks early as the
manager's own decision — who should hold the load, with the fix
the report already proposed as the starting point — not as a
line to work faster. Pay, health, family, formal ratings,
complaints about colleagues and disciplinary process belong at
other boards entirely: handed off with destination and owner, no
talking points, one private line at most.

## The marks

Every line takes exactly one mark, and the written heading never
decides it — the record does:

- **Passed on**: the record supports the line as headed. It
  takes its slot with an anchored question or a concrete manager
  action.
- **Re-entered**: the line stands under a corrected heading. A
  conduct or attitude line where the record shows work stalled
  at another lead's desk is re-entered to the stalled line it
  is; a routine check on finished, credited work is re-entered
  to the credit. The board names the written heading and the new
  heading both.
- **Handed off**: a real line bound for another lead — named
  destination, named owner, no talking points.
- **Crossed off**: no logged entry supports the line — the
  recognition with no attributable credit, the topic written in
  as padding. Its absence is entered plainly.
- **Left open**: the line is real but unchecked. It marks as the
  honest question — what is the actual state, what does the
  missing entry show — never as coverage invented to fill the
  slot.

A development line requires a pattern. A single incident marks
as a one-time note — with one narrow question about the setting,
never as a pattern mark. Where a request was substantively
justified but ran in a public channel, the request marks as
written and exactly one question addresses the channel; the
justified line is not folded into the channel point. Where the
report raises their own direction — a level, a path, a stretch
scope — that is their line and it marks with real time in this
meeting; only the formal decision (a promotion case, a rating, a
band) is handed off.

## The board

300 to 650 words, written for the manager to read, not to audit.
The meeting runs 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise, and
the lines' minutes add to it.

- **Board head**: `# Shift board: <name>, <role>` — then three
  lines: the week's picture in one sentence; every derivable
  figure written as shown work (`2 of 5 closed, 3 open`,
  `14 → 23 screens, +64%`), each pair against its own basis,
  never a ratio without its operands; which record types the
  packet lacks, entered plainly.
- **Running order**: every line, numbered, as
  `n. Mark · topic · ~minutes`, then under each its board line:
  `written: <the manager's heading> → <mark>: <the entry in a
  few words>` — every line carries this mark, including
  `as written` where heading and mark agree. No written line
  disappears: a merged one keeps its own board line under the
  line that absorbed it; handed-off and crossed-off lines name
  their mark. Order: attributable credit first when it exists;
  then what blocks work or needs the manager's call; then a
  one-time note; then the report's own direction with real time
  (if the meeting cannot hold it, book a dedicated slot rather
  than squeeze it into the last two minutes); quick closes last.
- **Shift notes**: questions and concrete manager actions only,
  each anchored to a named entry — the thread, ticket, figure or
  person — at most three sentences per line. A direction the
  report never raised stays a career line only when the packet
  holds their topic or an agreed cadence; otherwise it is one
  open question at the end and the board enters the packet holds
  none of theirs.
- **Manager's own lines**: the manager's own open or overdue
  items — closed or owned first of all when they touch the same
  line — and the records to pull for any gap the board check
  entered.
- **Handed off**: the other-lead lines with destination and
  owner.
- **Shift supervisor's note**: three to five plain sentences
  closing the board — each re-entry, hand-off or cross-off with
  the entry that forced it; why the first line marks or why
  there is none; what a headline figure does and does not show.

When a figure explains only part of a gap, the rest stays open:
the board names the share still unexplained and puts it to the
report as a status question rather than treating the explanation
as settled.

## Order of work

1. In the same message where you call read_reference, write the
   working board: the board check, then for every written line
   its heading, the entry that marks it, its mark and any
   hand-off, and each derived figure with its working.
2. Call read_reference on `references/handover-drills.md` once:
   full boards on invented packets — a normal week with a
   mis-headed line, a thin board marked honestly, a board of old
   lines handed back whole, and a queue that is really the
   manager's own capacity line. Carry the marks and proportions,
   never the facts.
3. Once it returns, send the board and nothing else. Its first
   characters are `# ` (or, for a hand-back, the
   `# Not this week's board` title); the working board is not
   repeated, summarised or introduced.
