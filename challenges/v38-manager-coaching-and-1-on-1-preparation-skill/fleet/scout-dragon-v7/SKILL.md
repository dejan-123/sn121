# The yard — tag every wagon, marshal it on the sheet

A manager's draft agenda is a consist of tagged wagons, each tagged the
manager's way. The tag is a first intention, not a departure order.
Every wagon goes on the yard sheet and is marshalled on what the sheet
shows — what the packet documents, nothing it does not. The output is
a board the manager reads on the way in: every wagon logged, every
order traceable to the sheet, the count matching the wagons tagged.

## Gate check — is this this shift's manifest?

Three manifests look like a weekly shift and are not: notes, minutes
or a transcript of a meeting already held (even when the manager asks
to plan the next one from it — it records last shift's yard, not
this shift's movements; what it supports is a follow-up on the
actions); preparation for a skip-level or a different meeting; a
performance-review, rating or disciplinary draft. For these, marshal
nothing. Reply in under 150 words titled `# Not this shift's
manifest`: what the manifest is, which document it actually supports
and who owns it, what to bring for a weekly board — ending with the
one-line reason (nothing in the material is dated after that shift,
so a board would replay old positions or invent new ones). Where the
input is last week's manifest, list each open action with owner and
date exactly as tagged; open actions carry forward as entries, never
as assumed current position.

The send-back triggers on the manifest's kind and nothing else. A
manifest of the manager's own topics for the coming shift is always
this shift's traffic, even with no movements logged: a bare agenda,
a two-line note, topics with no figures behind them. Scarcity is
marshalled wagon by wagon (Siding, below); it never triggers a
send-back. For every manifest that is this shift's — full or thin —
the reply opens `# Yard board:` with no preamble.

## Gate check — what goes on the sheet

Only what the packet documents goes on the sheet: an action item and
its state (the manager's own included), a sprint or ticket figure, a
quoted line, the manager's note, the report's own topic. No invented
figure, quote, date, name or mood. Where an order needs a movement
the packet lacks, the lack is logged — never filled with a
comfortable guess.

## The weighbridge — how movements weigh

Movements weigh by kind, not volume. One documented occurrence is a
single movement — it can support a note, it cannot support a pattern
order. The same conduct documented on two or more occasions is a
pattern. Attributed commendation — a named person's thanks or a
concrete result — is credit earned; effort, hours or unverifiable
praise is not. The report's own account is self-reported: loggable,
weighed below an independent movement. An impression the packet
cannot source is a reason to ask, not a movement.

## Arrived wagons stay arrived

A wagon the sheet shows delivered is logged as a one-line close —
unless another logged movement cannot stand with the delivery. The
wagon's own tag never reopens it. Where a later movement and the
logged delivery genuinely conflict, log the conflict in writing;
never re-tag silently.

## Which yard owns it

A wagon belongs to the yard that can move it. Work waiting on an
input another person or team controls is that yard's wagon — a
stalled dependency goes on the board as the manager's own action to
move it, never as the report's fault to answer for. A sole reviewer
or approver whose queue stalls several teams is a capacity matter
the manager owns: it departs early as the manager's own decision —
who should hold the load, with the fix the report already proposed
as the starting point — not as a tag to work faster. Pay, health,
family, formal ratings, complaints about colleagues and disciplinary
process belong at other yards entirely: forwarded with destination
and owner, no talking points, one private line at most.

## The orders

Every wagon takes exactly one order, and the tag never decides it —
the movements do:

- **Departed**: the movements support the wagon as tagged. It takes
  its slot with an anchored question or a concrete manager action.
- **Re-tagged**: the wagon departs under a corrected tag. A conduct
  or attitude tag where the movements show work stalled at another
  yard's gate is re-tagged to the blocked wagon it is; a routine
  check on finished, credited work is re-tagged to the credit. The
  board names the tag and the re-tag both.
- **Forwarded**: a real wagon bound for another yard — named
  destination, named owner, no talking points.
- **Spurred**: no logged movement supports the wagon — the
  recognition with no attributable credit, the topic tagged as
  padding. Its absence is logged plainly.
- **Siding**: the wagon is live but its movements are undocumented.
  It departs as the honest question — what is the actual position,
  what does the missing movement show — never as coverage invented
  to fill the slot.

A development tag requires a pattern. A single incident departs as a
one-time note — with one narrow question about the setting, never as
a pattern order. Where a request was substantively justified but ran
in a public channel, the request departs as tagged and exactly one
question addresses the channel; the justified wagon is not folded
into the channel point. Where the report raises their own direction —
a level, a path, a stretch scope — that is their wagon and it
departs with real time in this shift; only the formal decision (a
promotion case, a rating, a band) is forwarded.

## The board

300 to 650 words, written for the manager to read, not to audit. The
shift runs 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise, and the
wagons' minutes add to it.

- **Board head**: `# Yard board: <name>, <role>` — then three lines:
  the shift's picture in one sentence; every derivable figure written
  as shown work (`2 of 5 closed, 3 open`, `14 → 23 screens, +64%`),
  each pair against its own basis, never a ratio without its
  operands; which record types the packet lacks, logged plainly.
- **Departure order**: every wagon, numbered, as
  `n. Order · topic · ~minutes`, then under each its sheet line:
  `tagged: <the manager's wagon> → <order>: <the movement in a few
  words>` — every wagon carries this line, including `as tagged`
  where tag and order agree. No tagged wagon disappears: a merged
  one keeps its own sheet line under the wagon that absorbed it;
  forwarded and spurred wagons name their order. Order: attributable
  credit first when it exists; then what blocks work or needs the
  manager's call; then a one-time note; then the report's own
  direction with real time (if the shift cannot hold it, book a
  dedicated slot rather than squeeze it into the last two minutes);
  quick closes last.
- **Switching notes**: questions and concrete manager actions only,
  each anchored to a named movement — the thread, ticket, figure or
  person — at most three sentences per wagon. A direction the report
  never raised stays a career wagon only when the packet holds their
  topic or an agreed cadence; otherwise it is one open question at
  the end and the board logs the packet holds none of theirs.
- **Pre-shift**: the manager's own open or overdue items — cleared
  or owned first of all when they touch the same wagon — and the
  movements to pull for any gap the gate logged.
- **Forwarded**: the other-yard wagons with destination and owner.
- **Yardmaster's note**: three to five plain sentences closing the
  board — each re-tag, forward or spur with the movement that forced
  it; why the first wagon departs or why there is none; what a
  headline figure does and does not show.

When a figure explains only part of a gap, the rest stays open: the
board names the share still unexplained and puts it to the report as
a status question rather than treating the explanation as settled.

## Order of work

1. In the same message where you call read_reference, write the
   working sheet: the manifest-kind check, then for every tagged
   wagon its tag, the movement that decides it, its order and any
   forwarding, and each derived figure with its working.
2. Call read_reference on `references/yard-drills.md` once: full
   boards on invented shifts — a normal shift with a mis-tagged
   wagon, a thin manifest worked honestly, a manifest of old
   movements sent back whole, and a queue that is really the
   manager's own capacity wagon. Carry the orders and proportions,
   never the facts.
3. Once it returns, send the board and nothing else. Its first
   characters are `# ` (or, for a send-back, the
   `# Not this shift's manifest` title); the working sheet is not
   repeated, summarised or introduced.
