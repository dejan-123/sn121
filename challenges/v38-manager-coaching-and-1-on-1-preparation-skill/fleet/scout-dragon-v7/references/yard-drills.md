# Yard drills — full boards on invented shifts

Four invented manifests, marshalled the way the yard should marshal
them. The names, figures and products are invented; the orders are
the part to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal shift with a mis-tagged wagon

Packet: report is "Marta, senior platform engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "discipline issue — Marta snapped at QA in the team channel",
(b) "why is the payments migration late", (c) "quick win — mention
the dashboard launch". The movements: QA's lead posted a public
thank-you naming Marta for unblocking their release by building the
replay tool early; the channel exchange shows Marta objecting —
sharply but on the substance — to QA shipping a build that failed
its own gate; the migration is 3 of 8 endpoints done, waiting on a
security sign-off requested 9 days ago; the dashboard launched and
the support lead messaged that ticket volume on it halved in week
one.

Orders:

- Wagon (a): **Re-tagged**. One substantive objection to a build that
  failed its own checks — tagged as a discipline issue, departed as
  a one-time note with one narrow question. Switching note: "The QA
  exchange was on the merits — QA was already thanking you for the
  release help. One thing to keep in view: that disagreement ran in
  the team channel rather than the ticket. Does moving it there cost
  you anything?" The objection stands; the channel takes one
  question and no more.
- Wagon (b): **Re-tagged — another yard's wagon.** 3 of 8 done, and
  the stall is a 9-day-old sign-off with security, not Marta's pace.
  Switching note: "Migration is waiting on security's sign-off —
  nine days outstanding. I'll chase the reviewer today and get a
  date; who should I escalate to if it slips again?"
- Wagon (c): **Departed to lead.** Two attributable credits — the
  QA lead's public thanks and the support figure. Open with them
  before any process wagon; credit departs first.
- Every tagged wagon gets a sheet line and an order; nothing tagged
  disappears.

Board: `# Yard board: Marta, senior platform engineer` — picture
line ("a strong shift with one movement waiting on another yard"),
figures shown as work, then departure order with all three tags, the
movement behind each, and each order — then switching notes,
pre-shift, forwarded list, yardmaster's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin manifest worked honestly

Packet: report is "Ilya, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep my
weekly with Ilya — want to talk about his promo timeline, and whether
he's ready to own the ingest rewrite." No action log, no figures, no
channel excerpts. One calendar item: Ilya asked in passing about
salary bands for the next level.

Thin is not invalid: the manifest aims at the coming shift, so the
gate still logs it — marshalled wagon by wagon. Only the wrong kind
of manifest (drill 3) is sent back whole. The reply opens
`# Yard board: Ilya, staff engineer` — never a send-back preface.

Orders:

- Promo timeline: **Departed — split by yard.** Ilya's direction and
  the manager's readiness question belong in this shift, with real
  time: "You asked about the next level — here's the gap evidence I
  see and what I'd need documented; what scope would you take to
  build it?" The pay-band question is **Forwarded**: bands are
  decided in the compensation review — the board names the
  destination (the comp review with the comp partner) and the owner,
  one line, no switching notes.
- Readiness for the ingest rewrite: **Siding**. The manifest has no
  delivery record to weigh. It departs as the honest question, not
  an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have your recent
  delivery record in front of me — walk me through the last few
  weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the board. A thin board of
  honest questions beats a padded one; the board head logs what the
  manifest lacks and pre-shift lists what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a manifest that is not this shift's

Packet: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Dana —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list three
actions: Dana to send the benchmarking doc (due Thursday), the
manager to confirm the conference budget, a follow-up on the
mentoring pilot.

Order: **whole manifest sent back — nothing marshalled.** The reply,
under 150 words, titled `# Not this shift's manifest`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was agreed, not
what has moved since. The document this supports is a follow-up on
the open actions, not a board: Dana's benchmarking doc (due
Thursday), your budget confirmation, the mentoring pilot follow-up —
owners and dates exactly as filed. For a weekly board, send this
week's action log, any figures, and whatever Dana raised or you want
tagged. Nothing in the material is dated after that meeting, so a
board would replay old positions or invent new ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not repeated, not
summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own wagon

Packet: report is "Sana, senior backend engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "remind Sana to clear her review queue — five design docs are
waiting on her and two partner teams are blocked"; (b) "Sana seems
quiet in standup lately". The movements: the five docs are assigned
because the process names her sole approver for cross-team changes;
she shipped two features this sprint and flagged the queue in the
last retro, proposing a rotation; "quiet" appears once, in the
manager's note.

Orders:

- Wagon (a): **Re-tagged — the yard that can move it is the
  manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two teams is a capacity
  matter the manager owns, not a tag to work faster. It departs
  early as the manager's own decision: "Your rotation proposal is
  the right starting point — I want to approve it this week and take
  it to the partner leads. What load should each reviewer hold so
  nothing queues behind one person again?"
- Wagon (b): **Spurred.** One manager observation with no movements
  behind it; the sprint shows two shipped features and a raised
  process fix. The board logs its absence plainly rather than
  inventing a wellbeing segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Sana's retro proposal —
  is listed under pre-shift as the manager's commitment, with whom
  and when.

## What every drill shares

- The movements decide; the tag is only the filing.
- Attributable credit departs first — a named person, a concrete
  result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its substance
  and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another yard moves is logged to that yard.
- One occurrence is a single movement, never a pattern.
- Missing movements depart as `Siding` — the honest question in the
  slot — never covered over.
- Other-yard wagons get a destination and an owner, never switching
  notes.
- Every figure shows its own working; every tagged wagon gets a sheet
  line and an order.
- A thin manifest still gets a board. The send-back is for the wrong
  kind of manifest only — and it stays under 150 words.
