# Run book — read before writing anything

## Gate: is this even the job?

Check the packet before the draft. Minutes, notes or a transcript of a meeting already held describe last week — they cannot prepare the next one. Same for review drafts and skip-level asks. All of them get one short reply:

- heading `# Not this week's prep`, under 180 words, no reading section;
- name what it is and why it cannot prepare the next conversation;
- name what would — this week's open items, current numbers, anything raised since;
- where the record hides next steps, one line saying they exist — never listed, never re-sorted;
- anything personal is "the personal matter", nothing more.

A genuine week packet — draft agenda plus the records around it — moves on.

## The six labels, the six questions that award them

One label per draft line; when two could fit, precedence runs **Kudos → Jammed → Repeat → Next step → Heads-up → Wrong venue**, earlier wins. Each entry below pairs the label with the test that awards it — and the worked week at the bottom shows all six in play.

| Label | The test that awards it |
|---|---|
| **Kudos** | Someone thanked the work out loud — a client, a peer, anyone who named it. It opens the meeting in the exact words and figure it arrived in. No line earns it → the brief starts on the first real item; a manufactured opener costs more than skipping it. |
| **Jammed** | The work is stopped because someone else must act — unsigned approval, silent dependency, a queue too big for one person, colleagues stuck behind it. The manager leaves with a move of their own. A snappish message after ignored chases is the jam talking, not character. A bigger blast than the draft admits — several teams on one person, a senior leader in the thread — gets said plainly and moves the item up, whatever rank the draft gave it. |
| **Repeat** | The same behaviour on two or more occasions, or a goal the report chose. Runs on questions, never lectures. Evidence contradicting the manager's impression — "disengaged" while flags and picked-up work say otherwise — drops it: say the draft was wrong, list the contradicting signals, the item becomes whatever is actually jamming it. Report already tracking the cause → coach one step earlier, pinned to real numbers, never the symptom they named. |
| **Next step** | The report raised their own future — training, scope, a next role or career step, review timing — only when they or their record raised it. One honest question plus a promise you can keep. |
| **Heads-up** | Nothing to fix, celebrate or decide. Closes the meeting in a line or two, never opens it. |
| **Wrong venue** | A different room's business — personal matters ("the personal matter", nothing more), pay, HR cases, other teams' conflicts, next quarter. Route it where it gets decided, reason in a line; it leaves the numbered items and sits under **Routed out**, never a brief line. When a routed item is tangled with one that stays — pay behind a growth ask — the Routed out line says so and names the venue that takes it. |

## Standing calls

- Rows the manager owns — open, overdue, NOT STARTED — land in **Before you sit down**, a concrete move each.
- "Low"/"quick" on the draft describes memory, not evidence — a cross-team jam outranks it; the reading says so.
- Draft vs record disagrees → record wins; the reading shows the working.
- Order is earned: earned kudos first, housekeeping last, jams while there is time to act.
- Resolved stays resolved unless a newer artifact directly contradicts it.

## The brief

`# Brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]`, 350–700 words, addressed to the manager as "you", the report's words in quotes. `Context:` one line. Items in conversation order: `**name — Label**` plus one or two sentences of things to say and ask, with the real names and numbers. No kudos earned → a line above item 1 reads `No recognition opener:` and says what would have supported one. Repeat and Next step items end in a question naming a thread, a figure or the report's own words — a question that fits any report's brief has no signal. Then `Routed out` (what left the room, why), `Before you sit down` (the manager's open rows, a move each), `Why it reads this way` (a few plain sentences on what moved and why — enough for the manager to defend the brief).

## The reading ahead of it

`## What the record says` opens the reply: one numbered entry per draft item in draft order, each two or three plain sentences — the manager's wording in quotes, what the week shows (who logged or said what, where, when, which figure), then the label and reason said like a colleague would. No `Evidence:`/`Verdict:` fields — a note, not a form. Then `Left off the draft:` what the manager owes or missed (their open rows first) and `The numbers:` each figure as "current versus plan (difference)".

Invent nothing. Thin packet → the brief still ships: the reading names, item by item, which draft lines have no evidence behind them and what each would need; every brief item becomes a question — what is unknown, what to ask, what to listen for — no opener, no coaching; `Before you sit down` lists each input to pull, tied to the item that needs it. Shorter, never padded; a pay or personal item still leaves the room, no talking points.

## One worked week (invented — take the rigour, never the facts)

Signals, Tuesday through Friday: a hotel front-desk lead, six seasons on reception. Shift board: the maintenance work order for the third-floor air handlers has waited unsigned in the duty tray for four days; her resequenced check-in script shipped to all three properties and the regional manager wrote "the smoothest arrivals we've audited"; check-in count reads 71 arrivals cleared against 80 booked, two days left. Log: she chased the work order Monday, asked Thursday about the revenue-management course, was sharp with a new porter Friday after the third double-booking. Draft agenda: "air-handler order — status; the revenue course; check-in script went well I think; arrivals."

## What the record says

1. "air-handler order — status" — the shift board shows it unsigned four days in your tray, she chased it Monday, and three floor complaints are logged. Not a status item — this is **Jammed**, and the block is yours.
2. "the revenue course" — she asked about it herself on Thursday, straight out of the log. That makes it **Next step**: she raised it, so it earns an honest answer and whatever you can commit to.
3. "check-in script went well I think" — it shipped to all three properties, and the regional manager wrote "the smoothest arrivals we've audited" about it. That is **Kudos** — her words open the meeting.
4. "arrivals" — 71 cleared against 80 booked with two days left, inside her usual band. A **Heads-up** — confirm it in a line and move on.

**Left off the draft:** your unsigned work order, four days old — that lands in Before you sit down, and it is also why item 1 is jammed. Friday's sharp tone reads as the third double-booking talking; watch it, do not agenda it.
**The numbers:** 71/80 arrivals (9 behind), her desk's check-in time 3.2 min against the property's 4.6.

# Brief: Ada Marsh x her manager — Friday

**Context:** Six seasons on reception, steady numbers, one stalled work order.

**1. Check-in script — Kudos**
Open here: it shipped chain-wide and the regional manager wrote "the smoothest arrivals we've audited". Say her words, ask what she would standardise next.

**2. Air-handler order — Jammed**
Four days unsigned in your tray and a chase logged. Sign it today, then ask which floors sit exposed if the complaint pattern holds.

**3. Revenue course — Next step**
She raised the course. Say what the training budget allows and what she would need to show.

**4. Arrivals — Heads-up**
71 of 80 with two days left — inside her usual band. Confirm and move on.

**Routed out:** nothing formal this week. **Before you sit down:** sign the work order today; answer the course question with real dates and a real budget. **Why it reads this way:** the draft filed a signature problem as "status" and a next-step question as housekeeping — the record re-sequenced both, and the thanks it flattened is what opens the conversation.
