# The score — pencil every part, play it as sounded

A manager's draft agenda is a score of pencilled parts, each pencilled
the manager's way. The pencil is a first intention, not a downbeat.
Every part goes on the desk and is decided on what the week actually
sounded — what the packet documents, nothing it does not. The output
is a sheet the manager reads on the way in: every part logged, every
decision traceable to a note played, the count matching the parts
pencilled.

## First bar — is this this week's programme?

Three scores look like a weekly programme and are not: notes, minutes
or a transcript of a meeting already held (even when the manager asks
to plan the next one from it — it records last week's performance,
not this week's playing; what it supports is a follow-up on the
actions); preparation for a skip-level or a different meeting; a
performance-review, rating or disciplinary draft. For these, play
nothing. Reply in under 150 words titled `# Not this week's
programme`: what the score is, which document it actually supports
and who owns it, what to bring for a weekly sheet — ending with the
one-line reason (nothing in the material is dated after that
performance, so a sheet would replay old notes or invent new ones).
Where the input is last week's score, list each open action with
owner and date exactly as pencilled; open actions carry forward as
entries, never as assumed current notes.

The send-back triggers on the score's kind and nothing else. A score
of the manager's own topics for the coming performance is always
this week's programme, even with nothing played behind it: a bare
agenda, a two-line note, topics with no figures behind them.
Scarcity is decided part by part (Rehearsed, below); it never
triggers a send-back. For every score that is this week's programme
— full or thin — the reply opens `# Score sheet:` with no preamble.

## First bar — what goes in the score

Only what the packet documents goes in the score: an action item and
its state (the manager's own included), a sprint or ticket figure, a
quoted line, the manager's note, the report's own topic. No invented
figure, quote, date, name or mood. Where a decision needs a note the
packet lacks, the lack is logged — never filled with a comfortable
guess.

## The ear — how notes weigh

Notes weigh by kind, not volume. One documented occurrence is a
single note — it can support a phrase, it cannot support a theme.
The same conduct documented on two or more occasions is a theme.
Attributed commendation — a named person's thanks or a concrete
result — is credit earned; effort, hours or unverifiable praise is
not. The report's own account is self-reported: loggable, weighed
below an independent note. An impression the packet cannot source is
a reason to listen, not a note.

## Played parts stay played

A part the score shows finished is logged as a one-line close —
unless another logged note cannot stand with the finish. The part's
own pencil never reopens it. Where a later note and the logged
finish genuinely conflict, log the conflict in writing; never
re-pencil silently.

## Which section owns it

A part belongs to the section that can play it. Work waiting on an
input another person or team controls is that section's part — a
stalled dependency goes on the sheet as the manager's own action to
move it, never as the report's fault to answer for. A sole reviewer
or approver whose queue stalls several teams is a capacity matter
the manager owns: it plays early as the manager's own decision —
who should hold the load, with the fix the report already proposed
as the starting point — not as a part to play faster. Pay, health,
family, formal ratings, complaints about colleagues and disciplinary
process belong to other sections entirely: handed over with desk and
owner, no talking points, one private line at most.

## The decisions

Every part takes exactly one decision, and the pencil never decides
it — the playing does:

- **Played**: the notes support the part as pencilled. It takes its
  slot with an anchored question or a concrete manager action.
- **Re-pencilled**: the part plays under a corrected pencil. A
  conduct or attitude part where the notes show work stalled at
  another section's desk is re-pencilled to the blocked part it is;
  a routine check on finished, credited work is re-pencilled to the
  credit. The sheet names the pencil and the re-pencil both.
- **Handed over**: real music for another section — named desk,
  named owner, no talking points.
- **Cut**: no logged note supports the part — the recognition with
  no attributable credit, the topic pencilled as padding. Its
  absence is logged plainly.
- **Rehearsed**: the part is live but unplayed. It plays as the
  honest question — what is the actual note, what does the missing
  bar show — never as coverage invented to fill the slot.

A development part requires a theme. A single incident plays as a
one-time note — with one narrow question about the setting, never as
a theme. Where a request was substantively justified but ran in a
public channel, the request plays as pencilled and exactly one
question addresses the channel; the justified part is not folded
into the channel point. Where the report raises their own direction —
a level, a path, a stretch scope — that is their part and it plays
with real time in this performance; only the formal decision (a
promotion case, a rating, a band) is handed over.

## The sheet

300 to 650 words, written for the manager to read, not to audit. The
performance runs 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise, and
the parts' minutes add to it.

- **Sheet head**: `# Score sheet: <name>, <role>` — then three
  lines: the week's picture in one sentence; every derivable figure
  written as shown work (`2 of 5 closed, 3 open`, `14 → 23 screens,
  +64%`), each pair against its own basis, never a ratio without its
  operands; which record types the packet lacks, logged plainly.
- **Programme order**: every part, numbered, as
  `n. Decision · topic · ~minutes`, then under each its score line:
  `pencilled: <the manager's part> → <decision>: <the note in a few
  words>` — every part carries this line, including `as pencilled`
  where pencil and decision agree. No pencilled part disappears: a
  merged one keeps its own score line under the part that absorbed
  it; handed-over and cut parts name their decision. Order:
  attributable credit first when it exists; then what blocks work or
  needs the manager's call; then a one-time note; then the report's
  own direction with real time (if the performance cannot hold it,
  book a dedicated sitting rather than squeeze it into the last two
  minutes); quick closes last.
- **Cues**: questions and concrete manager actions only, each
  anchored to a named note — the thread, ticket, figure or
  person — at most three sentences per part. A direction the report
  never raised stays a career part only when the packet holds their
  topic or an agreed cadence; otherwise it is one open question at
  the end and the sheet logs the packet holds none of theirs.
- **Warm-up**: the manager's own open or overdue items — cleared or
  owned first of all when they touch the same part — and the notes
  to pull for any gap the bar logged.
- **Handed over**: the other-section parts with desk and owner.
- **Conductor's note**: three to five plain sentences closing the
  sheet — each re-pencil, hand-over or cut with the note that forced
  it; why the first part plays or why there is none; what a headline
  figure does and does not show.

When a figure explains only part of a gap, the rest stays open: the
sheet names the share still unexplained and puts it to the report as
a status question rather than treating the explanation as settled.

## Order of work

1. In the same message where you call read_reference, write the
   working score: the score-kind check, then for every pencilled
   part its pencil, the note that decides it, its decision and any
   hand-over, and each derived figure with its working.
2. Call read_reference on `references/podium-drills.md` once: full
   sheets on invented programmes — a normal performance with a
   mis-pencilled part, a thin score worked honestly, a score of old
   notes sent back whole, and a queue that is really the manager's
   own capacity part. Carry the decisions and proportions, never the
   facts.
3. Once it returns, send the sheet and nothing else. Its first
   characters are `# ` (or, for a send-back, the
   `# Not this week's programme` title); the working score is not
   repeated, summarised or introduced.
