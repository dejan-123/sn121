# Podium drills — full sheets on invented programmes

Four invented scores, played the way the desk should play them. The
names, figures and products are invented; the decisions are the part
to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal performance with a mis-pencilled part

Packet: report is "Marta, senior platform engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "discipline issue — Marta snapped at QA in the team channel",
(b) "why is the payments migration late", (c) "quick win — mention
the dashboard launch". The notes: QA's lead posted a public
thank-you naming Marta for unblocking their release by building the
replay tool early; the channel exchange shows Marta objecting —
sharply but on the substance — to QA shipping a build that failed
its own gate; the migration is 3 of 8 endpoints done, waiting on a
security sign-off requested 9 days ago; the dashboard launched and
the support lead messaged that ticket volume on it halved in week
one.

Decisions:

- Part (a): **Re-pencilled**. One substantive objection to a build
  that failed its own checks — pencilled as a discipline issue,
  played as a one-time note with one narrow question. Cue: "The QA
  exchange was on the merits — QA was already thanking you for the
  release help. One thing to keep in view: that disagreement ran in
  the team channel rather than the ticket. Does moving it there cost
  you anything?" The objection stands; the channel takes one
  question and no more.
- Part (b): **Re-pencilled — another section's music.** 3 of 8
  done, and the stall is a 9-day-old sign-off with security, not
  Marta's pace. Cue: "Migration is waiting on security's sign-off —
  nine days outstanding. I'll chase the reviewer today and get a
  date; who should I escalate to if it slips again?"
- Part (c): **Played to open.** Two attributable credits — the QA
  lead's public thanks and the support figure. Open with them before
  any process part; credit plays first.
- Every pencilled part gets a score line and a decision; nothing
  pencilled disappears.

Sheet: `# Score sheet: Marta, senior platform engineer` — picture
line ("a strong week with one part waiting on another section"),
figures shown as work, then programme order with all three pencils,
the note behind each, and each decision — then cues, warm-up,
handed-over list, conductor's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin score worked honestly

Packet: report is "Ilya, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep my
weekly with Ilya — want to talk about his promo timeline, and whether
he's ready to own the ingest rewrite." No action log, no figures, no
channel excerpts. One calendar item: Ilya asked in passing about
salary bands for the next level.

Thin is not invalid: the score aims at the coming performance, so
the desk still logs it — decided part by part. Only the wrong kind
of score (drill 3) is sent back whole. The reply opens
`# Score sheet: Ilya, staff engineer` — never a send-back preface.

Decisions:

- Promo timeline: **Played — split by section.** Ilya's direction
  and the manager's readiness question belong in this performance,
  with real time: "You asked about the next level — here's the gap
  evidence I see and what I'd need documented; what scope would you
  take to build it?" The pay-band question is **Handed over**: bands
  are decided in the compensation review — the sheet names the desk
  (the comp review with the comp partner) and the owner, one line,
  no cues.
- Readiness for the ingest rewrite: **Rehearsed**. The score has no
  delivery record to weigh. It plays as the honest question, not an
  assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have your recent
  delivery record in front of me — walk me through the last few
  weeks: what shipped, what's stuck, what you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the sheet. A thin sheet of
  honest questions beats a padded one; the sheet head logs what the
  score lacks and warm-up lists what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a score that is not this week's programme

Packet: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Dana —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list three
actions: Dana to send the benchmarking doc (due Thursday), the
manager to confirm the conference budget, a follow-up on the
mentoring pilot.

Decision: **whole score sent back — nothing played.** The reply,
under 150 words, titled `# Not this week's programme`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was agreed, not
what has moved since. The document this supports is a follow-up on
the open actions, not a sheet: Dana's benchmarking doc (due
Thursday), your budget confirmation, the mentoring pilot follow-up —
owners and dates exactly as filed. For a weekly sheet, send this
week's action log, any figures, and whatever Dana raised or you want
pencilled. Nothing in the material is dated after that meeting, so a
sheet would replay old notes or invent new ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not repeated, not
summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own part

Packet: report is "Sana, senior backend engineer". Manager's draft:
(a) "remind Sana to clear her review queue — five design docs are
waiting on her and two partner teams are blocked"; (b) "Sana seems
quiet in standup lately". The notes: the five docs are assigned
because the process names her sole approver for cross-team changes;
she shipped two features this sprint and flagged the queue in the
last retro, proposing a rotation; "quiet" appears once, in the
manager's note.

Decisions:

- Part (a): **Re-pencilled — the section that can play it is the
  manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two teams is a capacity
  matter the manager owns, not a part to play faster. It plays early
  as the manager's own decision: "Your rotation proposal is the
  right starting point — I want to approve it this week and take it
  to the partner leads. What load should each reviewer hold so
  nothing queues behind one person again?"
- Part (b): **Cut.** One manager observation with no notes behind
  it; the sprint shows two shipped features and a raised process
  fix. The sheet logs its absence plainly rather than inventing a
  wellbeing segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Sana's retro proposal —
  is listed under warm-up as the manager's commitment, with whom and
  when.

## What every drill shares

- The notes decide; the pencil is only the filing.
- Attributable credit plays first — a named person, a concrete
  result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its substance
  and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another section plays is logged to that section.
- One occurrence is a single note, never a theme.
- Missing bars play as `Rehearsed` — the honest question in the
  slot — never covered over.
- Other-section parts get a desk and an owner, never cues.
- Every figure shows its own working; every pencilled part gets a
  score line and a decision.
- A thin score still gets a sheet. The send-back is for the wrong
  kind of score only — and it stays under 150 words.
