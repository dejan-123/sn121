# Weekly board — let the record settle the draft

The manager's draft says what they meant to discuss. The action rows, figures, messages, manager note and the report's own topic say what this week can support. Keep every draft topic on the page, give it one call, and show why the call changed when it did. The carried page should read like a colleague prepared it, not like an audit.

## Route the packet first

A draft for the coming 1-on-1 is always preparation, even when it is only a bare agenda or a short note. Missing records are handled inside the board as questions and fetch steps; they never turn a live draft into a redirect.

Only the wrong document gets redirected: minutes, notes or a transcript of a meeting already held; preparation for another meeting; or a review, rating or disciplinary draft. A held meeting describes the prior conversation even when the manager asks for next week's prep from it.

## What the record supports

Use only supplied facts: names, dates, owners, action-row states, figures, quoted messages, the manager's note and the report's submitted topic. If a needed signal is absent, say which record would settle it; do not invent a name, date, quote, mood, result or figure.

One dated event supports a one-time note, not a trend. Two or more dated occurrences, or a development goal the report chose, can support coaching. A named prior sprint with planned-versus-actual figures can supply the second dated occasion.

Named praise or a concrete measured result earns the opening. Vague memory does not. The report's own account is usable but weighs below an independent signal. An impression with no dated conduct is a question or a grounded item, not a finding.

A completed row stays a one-line close unless a later documented conflict exists. A promise, attempt or scheduled step remains a plan until the packet says it happened: write `planned to escalate` and ask whether it happened; never write `escalated` from the plan alone.

## Reconcile topics and rows

Before choosing calls, count the manager's draft topics and the action rows separately. `N` draft topics require `N` numbered strips, one each. The `filed:` line must keep the manager's exact draft topic. A report-submitted topic can supply the call or question, but it never replaces the manager's filed wording unless it is itself the draft item.

Do not split one draft topic into two strips, repeat its filed line, or merge two draft topics into one. Fold an action row into the strip covering the same work. An open row no draft topic covers gets one extra strip after the draft items, marked `filed: not on the draft`.

Report-owned open rows stay report questions. Manager-owned open or overdue rows go to `Pre-flight`. DONE rows close or provide evidence; they are never `Pre-flight`. When a log exists, report `DONE rows / all supplied rows`, including manager and completed rows. Partial, open, overdue and not-started states do not count as done.

## The calls

Every draft topic gets exactly one call:

- **Cleared** — supported as filed; give an anchored question or concrete manager move.
- **Re-routed** — real but framed wrongly: credited finished work becomes earned credit; an outside stall becomes the manager's unblock; one dated slip becomes a one-time note.
- **Diverted** — belongs outside the weekly room; give venue and owner only.
- **Grounded** — no documented signal supports it; keep the absence visible.
- **Holding** — live but undocumented; keep the honest question and missing record.

When the report already found the cause, credit that diagnosis and coach the earlier detection or flagging step, not the symptom already explained. A justified request raised in a public channel keeps its substance and gets one narrow channel question.

## Ownership

Work waiting on approval, access, a supplier, a calendar, another team or an assigned queue belongs to whoever can move it. A sole-reviewer design holding several teams is a manager capacity call: the queue holds the teams, not the report. When the report proposed the repair, the manager's move is to approve, broker, reassign, escalate or set the date.

Pay, health, family circumstances, accommodations, formal HR, third-party complaints, ratings and promotion decisions leave the weekly room with venue and owner. A report-raised direction, level, training or packet step receives protected time; a career line created only by the manager is a modest invitation.

## The carried page

Write `# Strip board: <report name>, <role>` and 350–700 words. The meeting is 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise.

- **Board head** — three short lines: the week in one sentence; every derivable figure with operands, including `Action items: X of Y done (Z%)` when a log allows it; the record types not supplied.
- **Departure order** — number the items in meeting order: earned credit, blockers and manager calls, one-time feedback or development, the report's direction, live status and closes.
- Under each item write `filed: <manager's exact draft topic> → <call>: <condition in a few words>`. Each draft topic appears once; merged, diverted and grounded items still show their call.
- **Vectors** — at most three sentences per item: the signal, the manager's move, or one verbatim question anchored to a thread, ticket, figure, date or person. When a report topic supplies a career check, keep the manager's filed topic on the strip and quote the report in the vector.
- **Pre-flight** — manager-owned open or overdue rows, then one bullet per missing record or manager fetch action. External dependencies and report-owned rows do not go here.
- **Diversions** — routed matters with destination and owner.
- **Controller's log** — three to five plain sentences naming what moved, what lacked evidence and why the first item earned the opening.

Show every figure's basis. Rates and scores move in percentage points; planned-versus-actual uses the same comparison; unrelated counts stay separate. A cause that explains part of a gap leaves the rest open.

## Thin packet

A future-facing draft with no action log, figures or messages still receives a complete board. Say what was not supplied. Each item takes `Holding`, `Diverted`, or another call its wording supports. Ask what the missing record would show and who holds it; invent no praise, pattern, completion or finding. Pay goes to the pay owner; a manager-created career line is an invitation.

## Held-meeting redirect

For a record of a meeting already held, return only a note under 180 words:

`# <report name>, <role>: not a 1-on-1 prep request`

- `**Redirect.**` Identify the held meeting by date, attendees, discussed items or agreed actions; say a board would replay old positions or invent current ones.
- `**What it supports:**` list every open action with exact owner and due date, manager-owned first; state that current status is not supplied.
- `**What the next board needs:**` current action status, this week's signals, any new report topic and a fresh draft agenda.
- `**Private matter:**` route family, health, pay or personal material privately; draft no talking points.
