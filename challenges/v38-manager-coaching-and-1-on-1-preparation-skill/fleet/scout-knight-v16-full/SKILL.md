# Weighing a week before drafting

The record comes first, the draft agenda second. A draft is memory — a stalled job filed as "quick status", one rough afternoon remembered as "a coaching moment", a named thank-you shrunk to "a shoutout". The tickets, logs, reviews, figures and thanks beside it are what actually happened. Every draft line gets weighed against that record, and the weighing goes on the reply itself, ahead of the sheet — a manager who can see why a line moved trusts the page.

## First: is this even the job?

What actually arrived gets checked before anything else. Minutes, notes or a transcript of a meeting already held describe last week's world — they cannot prepare the next one, and a sheet drawn from them replays stale news. Review drafts, skip-level preparation and anything else outside the coming conversation get the same answer: one short note back, never a sheet. It does four things:

- says plainly this records a meeting already held and cannot prepare the next one;
- says what would turn it into preparation — the coming week's open items, current figures, anything flagged since;
- where next steps hide inside the record, one line naming that they exist — never a sheet, never the old items re-sorted;
- personal material mentioned in passing stays "the personal matter", nothing more.

Under 180 words, no weighing section, headed `# A note, not a sheet`. Only when the input really is the coming week's preparation do the items get worked.

For a real week, `read_reference` gets called once on `references/playbook.md` — it carries the edge cases and one invented week worked end to end; its order and rigour get taken, never its facts.

## Six questions asked of every draft line, in order

**Is there real thanks I can quote?** A client's note, a peer's praise, work somebody named out loud — that item is a **Win**, it opens the conversation, and it is quoted in the words and number it arrived in. Never "good work this week". When nothing can be quoted the sheet opens on the first real item — a manufactured thank-you costs more than it buys.

**Is the work waiting on somebody else?** A missing approval, a silent dependency, more requests than one person can carry, other teams held up — **Held**, and the manager leaves with a move of their own. A sharp message sent after ignored chases is the stall talking, not a tone problem. When the record shows a wider blast than the draft admits — several teams queued on the same person, a senior leader already engaged — that gets said plainly and the item moves up the conversation, whatever priority the draft gave it.

**Does the same thing keep happening?** Two or more separate occasions, or a goal the report set — a **Habit**, worked through questions, not lectures. An impression the evidence contradicts — "disengaged" while the flags and picked-up work say otherwise — gets dropped, the contradicting signals named; the item becomes the block behind it, not the person. When the report already found the cause and is tracking it, the coaching aims at the step before it — flag changes sooner next time — anchored to the real figures, never the symptom they already named.

**Did they raise their own future?** Training, scope, review timing — **Ambition**, only when the report or their record raised it first. One honest question plus whatever can be truthfully promised; a promise nobody can keep is worse than none.

**Is it only information?** Whatever survives the earlier questions with nothing to fix, celebrate or decide — a **Note**. It closes the conversation in a line or two and never opens it; housekeeping first spends the best minutes on the smallest items.

**Does it belong in this room at all?** Personal matters, pay, HR cases, other teams' conflicts, next quarter — **Wrong room**. Personal stays "the personal matter", elaborated never; each item gets routed to wherever it actually gets decided, the reason in a line.

## Calls never skipped

- The manager's own open, overdue or NOT STARTED rows are preparation, not trivia — they land **On your desk** with a concrete move, not a vague reminder.
- A "low" or "quick" label on the draft describes memory, not evidence — a block spanning teams outranks the label, and the weighing says so.
- Draft claims get checked against the record; where they disagree, the record wins and the weighing says so.
- Conversation order is earned, not copied: a win first, housekeeping last, blocks while there is time to act.
- Resolved stays resolved unless a newer artifact contradicts it directly; the original complaint never reopens it.

## What goes on the reply

**## Reading the record** — one numbered entry per draft item, in the draft's order: the manager's wording in quotes, **Evidence:** (who logged or said what, where, when, which row or figure), **Verdict:** (the class and why — "holds" when the draft called it right — or sent elsewhere, dropped, folded into a neighbour). Below the entries: **What the draft skipped:** (what the manager owes or missed — their own open, overdue or NOT STARTED rows first, a block nobody agenda'd, a request needing routing) and **The sums:** (each figure shown as "current versus plan (difference)").

Then **# 1-on-1 sheet: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]**, 350–700 words. **Context:** one line — report, tenure if known, the week in a phrase. Items in conversation order, each a line: **name — Class** plus one or two sentences phrased as things to say and ask, carrying the numbers and names the weighing surfaced. Then **Parked** (what moved out of the room or was dropped, with reasons), **On your desk** (every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move), **Why this order** (three or four lines on why the calls landed as they did).

When the packet is thin — a draft and nothing behind it — the page still ships. The weighing names what never arrived; each item tells the manager in sentences what is not yet known, what to ask, and what to listen for in the answer ("What is holding up the booking system — and what would unblocking it take?"). **On your desk** lists what to pull before the conversation. A thin week earns a shorter sheet, never a padded one — and a pay or personal item inside it still goes to the wrong-room list, no talking points. No fact gets invented.
