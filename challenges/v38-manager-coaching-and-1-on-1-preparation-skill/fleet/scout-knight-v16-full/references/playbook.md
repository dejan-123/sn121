# Playbook — depth notes and one worked week

The card carries the method. These notes cover the cases that most often go wrong, then one invented week worked end to end — its rigour gets taken, never its facts.

## When the input is not the coming week's preparation

Minutes, notes or transcripts of a meeting already held — a sheet built on them replays stale news, so decline the shape, not the request. The reply is headed `# A note, not a sheet`, under 180 words, with no weighing section, and it does exactly four things: says plainly this records a meeting already held and cannot prepare the next one; says what would turn it into preparation (the coming week's open items, current figures, anything flagged since); where the record hides next steps, one line naming that they exist — never a sheet, never the old items re-sorted; and treats anything personal mentioned in passing as "the personal matter", elaborated never. Review drafts, skip-level prep and other off-contract asks get the same short note.

## When the week is thin

A draft with no records behind it still earns a page. The weighing names what never arrived; each sheet item tells the manager what is not yet known, what to ask, and what to listen for in the answer. On your desk lists what to pull before the conversation. Shorter, not padded. A pay or personal item inside it still routes out, no talking points.

## One worked week (invented)

Signals, Tuesday through Friday: a marina-dock supervisor, second season running the boatyard. Yard board: the crane inspection certificate has waited unsigned in the office for four days; her berth-allocation map redrew the pontoon layout and the harbormaster wrote "the clearest dock plan we've seen"; launch count reads 31 boats hauled against 38 booked, three days left. Radio log: she chased the certificate Monday, asked Thursday about winterising courses, got short with a rigger Friday after the third scheduling clash. Draft agenda: "crane cert — status; winter courses; the new berth map went well I think; launches."

## Reading the record

1. "crane cert — status" — **Evidence:** unsigned four days in your office, her Monday chase, three scheduling clashes logged. **Verdict:** not status — **Held**, and the block is yours.
2. "winter courses" — **Evidence:** she asked about winterising courses Thursday. **Verdict:** **Ambition** — she raised it; an honest answer and what you can commit to.
3. "the new berth map went well I think" — **Evidence:** layout shipped; harbormaster wrote "the clearest dock plan we've seen". **Verdict:** a **Win** — quote the harbormaster's words, open with it.
4. "launches" — **Evidence:** 31 hauled against 38 booked, three days left; pace inside her usual band. **Verdict:** a **Note** — a line, not a discussion.

**What the draft skipped:** your unsigned crane certificate, four days old — that sits on your desk, and it is also why item 1 is held. Friday's short temper reads as the third clash talking; watch it, do not agenda it.
**The sums:** 31/38 launches (7 behind), her crew's turnarounds 4.1/day against the yard's 3.4.

# 1-on-1 sheet: Bren Vasquez x her manager — Friday

**Context:** Second season running the boatyard, steady crew numbers, one stalled certificate.

**1. Berth map — Win**
Open here: the layout shipped and the harbormaster wrote "the clearest dock plan we've seen". Say his words, ask what she would redraw next.

**2. Crane certificate — Held**
Four days unsigned in your office and a chase logged. Sign it today, then ask which lift slots sit exposed if it slips again.

**3. Winterising courses — Ambition**
She raised the courses. Say what the off-season budget allows and what she would need to show.

**4. Launches — Note**
31 of 38 with three days left — inside her usual pace. Confirm and move on.

**Parked:** nothing formal this week. **On your desk:** sign the certificate today; answer the course question with real dates and a real budget. **Why this order:** the draft filed a signature problem as "status" and an ambition question as housekeeping — the record re-sequenced both, and the praise it flattened is what opens the conversation.
