# Worked weeks

Invented material. The people and firms below exist only to show
reasoning — reproduce the moves, never the names or phrasing.

## Week one — a draft that mislabels the bottleneck

Input, invented: manager of Rafi Okafor, Dispatch Coordinator at
Dusk Harbor Logistics, lists four topics — "crane schedule status,"
"quick feedback (low): Rafi's paperwork responses slow," "say
something positive," "his growth if time." Action board: Rafi's
route manifest DONE Monday; the manager's revised sign-off rota
OVERDUE; vendor manifests for six loads pending supplier
confirmation. This week 14 dispatches cleared against 11 last
week. Channel notes show three terminals asking Rafi to approve
their release paperwork; his Wednesday post says he is sole
approver for three terminals and proposes a two-person rotation.
Harbor ops chief Nia thanked him publicly for spotting a
mislabeled container before loading. Rafi submitted: "Can we
discuss whether dispatch coordination is where I want to grow?"

Composed slate:

# Rafi Okafor — weekly slot
Dispatch Coordinator, Dusk Harbor Logistics

**The week in a paragraph.** Throughput is up — 14 dispatches
cleared versus 11 last week (+3, roughly +27%). Six loads wait on
supplier manifest confirmation. The bigger constraint: three
terminals route release paperwork through Rafi alone, and he has
already drafted a fix. The manager's own rota proposal is
unfinished — the "slow paperwork" complaint points at the
system, not at Rafi.

**1. Credit — the container catch.** Nia's channel post credits
Rafi with catching a mislabeled container before loading. Name
the catch and its cost-avoidance. This is the real positive
the manager remembered vaguely.

**2. Approval load — raised from "low."** Filed as quick feedback
on Rafi's responsiveness; actually three terminals depend on
one approver — a staffing design problem. "Your rotation
proposal is sensible. Which terminals move first, and what
authority do you need from me?" The manager's overdue rota is
their deliverable, not Rafi's fault.

**3. Vendor manifests — status.** Six loads hold on supplier
confirmation. "Which can you clear in parallel, and should I
press the supplier from my side?"

**4. His direction — growth.** Rafi raised it himself. "You asked
whether coordination is your path. What pulls you toward it —
or away? Let's map what you'd want to try before we look at
level requirements." Time here; a separate leveling packet
later.

**Manager obligations.** Circulate the rota, approve or adjust
the rotation, chase the supplier manifests.

Note what did not happen: the "feedback" label was replaced, not
debated; the six delayed loads were not laid on Rafi; the praise
request became real only because a source existed; growth got
room instead of an end-of-meeting shrug.

## Week two — sparse input, honest slate

Invented: manager of Lena Varga, Data Technician at Pellcrest
Survey, writes only "where is the dataset rebuild?", "give her a
boost," "she asked about moving up, and also pay." No figures, no
action log, no praise source, no thread.

# Lena Varga — weekly slot
Data Technician, Pellcrest Survey

**The week in a paragraph.** Nothing in this packet shows current
state — no rebuild status, no completed work to credit, no
threads. Pull the job tracker before sitting down.

**1. Dataset rebuild — status.** "Where does the rebuild stand
this week, and what's the next thing you need from me?" An honest
question; the packet cannot support a claim either way.

**2. Her direction — growth, kept in the room.** She asked about
moving up. "You mentioned wanting to discuss your path — what
kind of work do you want more of?" Even with thin evidence, her
question earns real minutes; don't promise a level without the
criteria.

**Elsewhere.** No sourced result means no credit segment — find a
real one before offering praise. Pay belongs in a separate
compensation conversation the manager owns through the proper
process; nothing about it is scripted here.

The slate is two conversations long because the record is two
conversations deep.

## When the packet is last week's minutes

Invented: a manager says "use these to prep this week's one-on-one
with Hector Ames" and pastes minutes of the meeting already held:
they reviewed release counts; the manager owed a tooling quote by
Wednesday; Hector owed a coverage draft by Friday; he mentioned a
private health appointment. No update since.

Correct reply — short, and no slate:

These notes record the meeting you already had, not this week's
work. They list two follow-ups — your tooling quote was due
Wednesday and Hector's coverage draft Friday — but nothing here
says whether either happened.

Send me current action states, this week's figures or team
messages, and what you or Hector want to raise, and I can fill
the slot from real material. The personal matter he mentioned
belongs in a private conversation, not in prepared content.

The two actions are preserved as open questions about their
*current* state — not restated as today's agenda.
