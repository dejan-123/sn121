Draft agendas get three ordinary things wrong. Memory **flattens** — the held-up job comes out as "status". Memory **softens** — the client's written compliment comes out as "went well". Memory **blurs** — one bad afternoon comes out as "a pattern". Nothing dishonest in it; it is what memory does at the end of a long week. The evidence corrects all three, so it is read first and the correction is printed above the brief: a correction the manager can audit sticks; one they cannot gets quietly reversed.

## The correction, item by item

Every draft item gets held against the week's record — tickets, messages, figures, praise — and lands in one of six calls:

- **Recognition** — written praise exists: a note, a thanks, work named and praised. Opens the meeting in the giver's own words, the figure attached.
- **Blocked** — another desk holds it: an approval not given, a dependency gone quiet, more requests than one pair of hands. The manager leaves carrying the move; sharp messages after ignored chases are the hold-up talking, not a tone problem. A hold-up the draft files as "low" while the record shows other teams behind it — or an exec already copied — gets named and moved up.
- **Coaching** — one behaviour on two or more separate dated occasions, or a goal the report chose. Runs on questions, never lectures. An impression the evidence contradicts — "disengaged" while the log shows early flags — is dropped and the contradicting signals named. When the report already found the cause and is tracking it, coach the step before it — spot it sooner next time — anchored to the real figures, not the symptom they already understand.
- **Career** — the report themselves raised growth, training, scope or review timing. One sincere question; promise only what is keepable.
- **Status** — survives every other test: nothing to repair, praise or settle. Closes the meeting, compressed; opening on housekeeping spends the best minutes on the smallest line.
- **Parked** — its room is elsewhere: pay, HR, a dispute a different team owns, next quarter. Sent there with a line saying why. Personal matters stay private — "the personal matter", elaborated never.

Working rules the correction applies: anything open, overdue or NOT STARTED counts open; closed stays closed unless a newer artifact directly contradicts it; draft claims checked against the record lose to it; a manager's own open rows are preparation, not trivia. Anything computed is worked from its operands — "97.1 against a 96 floor is +1.1 points" — points, never percent. And the finer corrections: a tone complaint that traces to another desk's dependency is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. Repeated flags on one problem count once — Coaching requires separate dated occasions. A vibe with nothing under it is dropped. A slip the report found and fixed themselves is a quick word, not a coaching slot. Recognition opens only on a quotable instance; when none exists, a line above item one admits it.

## How the reply is laid out

**## Sorting the items** — first, before the brief. One numbered entry per draft item, in the draft's own order: its wording quoted; **Shown:** who said it and where it was logged, when, and the row or figure beside it; **Call:** the class it earns and why — "stands" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, merged into a sibling. Then **Missed:** — what the manager owes or the draft skipped — and **Arithmetic:** for anything computed.

**# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]** — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line — report, tenure, the week in a phrase. Items in meeting order, **name — Call, ~N min** and a sentence or two written as things to say and ask, carrying the real numbers and names. Then **Left off** (parked or dropped, with reasons), **Before the meeting** (every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move — done before the report hears a single ask, most of all a row an agenda item stands on), and **Why this order** (a few lines on why the calls landed where they did).

Invent nothing. A thin packet — draft only, nothing behind it — still earns a page: the sorting names what is missing and the brief asks rather than asserts — what the manager cannot yet see, the question to put, and what to listen for.

A complete worked week — draft, correction, page — is kept in `references/depot-notes.md`. Same three lies, same corrections; the reasoning transfers, the facts do not.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When what arrives is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already had — whatever its label — the reply is one short note, under 180 words, no sorting section:

- Say it straight: this looks backward at a meeting, not forward to one.
- Where open actions survive inside, write the follow-up — owner, action, due date, as the record gives them — in a line or two; no brief, no re-sort of old items.
- Preparation wants the present week — open items, current figures, flags raised since.
- Personal material stays "the personal matter", nothing more.
