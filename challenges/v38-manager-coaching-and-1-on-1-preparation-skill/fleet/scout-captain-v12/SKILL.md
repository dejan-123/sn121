# Sorting a week into a 1-on-1

A manager writes the agenda at the end of a long week, from memory — and memory keeps the topic while losing the proof. A block only the manager can move gets written down as "quick status", one bad afternoon becomes "coaching", a thank-you left in a channel becomes "a shoutout". So the signals get weighed first and the draft second, and that weighing stays on the page above the brief: a manager who can see which signal moved a label trusts the page. Notes or minutes of a meeting already held are a different job — everything inside them is a week old; a brief built on them would replay stale news. They get a short note back instead, as do review drafts and skip-level requests. A week with nothing but the draft still gets a page — one that asks rather than asserts.

## Weigh everything once, first

Before the draft: each action row with owner and status word (the manager's own rows count), each figure beside whatever it compares against, each message with author and rough time, and anything the report raised themselves. Work the sums before writing, both inputs beside every result:

- a move against a baseline is (now − before) / before, a signed whole percent: 3 closed on a trailing 8 is −63%;
- an estimate against what it took, or a scope that grew, is the same sum: 3 days planned and 5 spent is +67%; 20 screens grown to 26 is +30%;
- rates and scores move in points: CSAT 72% to 80% is +8 points;
- completion means naming each row's state then counting — only "done" is done; in progress, overdue and NOT STARTED are open, the manager's rows included;
- a cause covering part of a gap leaves the rest open: 30% traced to one reason leaves 70% to ask about.

What the input lacks is named as missing and asked for, never invented — no figures, dates, moods or quotes from thin air. Two inputs that disagree are named as disagreeing.

## What each item earns

The evidence picks the class, never the draft's label or its priority — the class decides how the minutes go.

- **Recognition**: a signal names the work — a quotable line with where and when, something shipped, a figure that moved. Earned recognition opens the meeting, quoted: specific thanks lands, vague thanks is filler. Finished work somebody praised belongs here whatever the draft called it. With no such signal there is no opener, and the weighing says so.
- **Blocked**: work held up by somebody else — a missing approval, a silent dependency, more requests than one person can carry, other teams waiting. The manager leaves with a move of their own. Sharp messages after ignored chases are the block, not a tone problem.
- **Quick word**: a single slip, usually owned already. Name it, agree the fix, check what it left open. Inflating an owned slip into a development theme punishes the owning.
- **Coaching**: the same behaviour in two or more separate situations, or a goal the report set; it runs on questions, not lectures. An impression the evidence contradicts — "disengaged" while early flags and picked-up work say otherwise — is dropped, the signals named. When the report has already found the cause and is tracking it, coach the step before it — flag changes sooner next time — anchored to the real figures, not the symptom they found.
- **Career**: the report's own question about level, scope or direction, or a programme they joined; it gets real time, tied to this week's best work. When a packet or calibration decides it, their next step stays on the page and the decision itself is parked. A check-in only the manager pencilled is one open question, marked as theirs.
- **Status**: open work whose state the input does not show. Done or approved work is never status.

Off-page topics — pay, health or family, a colleague complaint, a promotion decision, a process conversation someone outside asked for — are parked with venue and arranger, no talking points; a sensitive one is named by its request alone.

## The reply

Open with `## Weighing the week`, then `# 1-on-1 brief:` — nothing comes before them.

In the weighing:

- one line on whether this is prep, and why;
- a numbered entry per draft item, in the draft's order: the label in quotes; **Evidence:** who said or logged what, where and when, with the row or figure; **Verdict:** the class it earns and the reason — "label holds" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, folded into another;
- **Missed by the draft:** what the manager owes or skipped — a row of theirs (open, overdue, NOT STARTED all count), a block nobody agenda'd, a request needing routing;
- **The sums:** the action rows named and counted, then every figure pair worked from both inputs;
- in a thin week, three or four sentences instead of entries: what arrived, what did not, what each label would need to be testable.

Then `# 1-on-1 brief:` with the report's name and role. A header of two or three sentences — the hardest finding first with its cause and who holds the next move, the figures carrying the week, "Action items: X of Y done (Z%)". Items in meeting order — earned recognition; blocks and whatever holds others up; the report's own topic; quick word or coaching; career; status — each headed `**N. <item in this week's words> - <Class>**`, three sentences at most: the signal, your move, and for coaching or career a question naming a thread, a ticket or the report's own words. "What made the Tuesday date feel fixed?" fits one person; "How can I support you?" fits anyone. The brief talks to the manager as "you"; words meant for the report sit in quotes. Parked or dropped items get no number.

Close with **Parking lot** (topic, venue, arranger — or "Nothing to park this week."), **Before the meeting** (the rows the manager owes, never done ones, plus inputs to pull; omit when empty) and **Why these calls** — three to five plain sentences: each label that moved and the signal that moved it, labels that held, why there is or is not an opener, why anything parked. The brief runs 350 to 700 words; a thin week is all questions, no opener, no coaching.

## When the paste is not prep

Notes, minutes or a transcript of a meeting already held — a date, attendees, actions agreed — record a finished conversation, even when the manager asks to prep the next one from them. The whole reply is then a single note under 180 words titled `# This isn't prep material`, with no weighing section: what the paste is and the detail that gives it away, why a brief built from it would replay last week as news, the follow-up it does support (its actions with owners and dates, as written), and what to send instead. Anything personal is "the personal matter" and nothing more.

## Weighing the week
Prep: this week's rows, counts and messages, for a meeting still to come.
1. "Status: the booking system". **Evidence:** down since the 5th; the vendor has not sent the patch date, chased 5th, 8th and 12th; the escalation approval is your row, overdue. **Verdict:** Blocked — none of it is Petra's to move.

4. "Her question about the duty-manager rota". **Evidence:** her Wednesday message asks it. **Verdict:** Career, label holds.

**The sums:** booking open, rota report done, approval overdue — 1 of 3 done (33%); 2,140 visitors on a trailing 1,760 = +22%.

# 1-on-1 brief: Petra Lindholm, front-of-house lead
The booking system has been down since the 5th waiting on a patch date the vendor has not sent; the escalation approval that forces one is yours. The school-group mornings drew praise and complaints halved; visitors up 22%. Action items: 1 of 3 done (33%).

**1. The school-group mornings - Recognition**
Read Petra the education officer's line about her rota, with the complaint figure beside it.

**2. The booking system - Blocked**
Three chases went unanswered, so this is yours. Say when the escalation will be approved, then ask which slots are exposed.

**3. The duty-manager rota - Career**
Running the school mornings end-to-end was duty-manager work. "Which part of that week would you want as your job?"

**4. The late till report - Quick word**
Corrected and flagged the same day. Agree her end-of-shift check.

**Parking lot**
- **Weekend premiums**: pay conversation with payroll; you set it up this week.

**Before the meeting**
Approve the vendor escalation you owe; item 2 hangs on it.

**Why these calls**
The booking system moved from status to blocked: three unanswered chases and your overdue approval hold it. The praise became recognition once the officer's note named the work; one late report put right the same day is a quick word. Career held because she asked; the premium question is pay, so it left the page.

# This isn't prep material
What you sent is the record of last Tuesday's 1-on-1 with Petra: attendees, discussion, actions agreed. A brief built from it would replay that meeting as news.
