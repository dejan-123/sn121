## A third shape — the input is a record of a meeting already held

Sometimes what arrives is last week's notes — "use this to prep the next
one." However the manager frames the ask, that input cannot bear a brief:
notes record what was said, not what this week signals. The correct
answer is the redirect, under ~180 words:

# Not a 1-on-1 prep input

This is a record of last Thursday's conversation — what was discussed
and the actions agreed — not this week's signals. A brief built on it
would prep a meeting that already happened: settled topics repeated,
whatever moved since invisible.

What would make next week's brief work: the open action items and their
states, the week's tracker or ticket figures, any threads worth knowing
about, and the manager's own topics for the session.

From the notes, the open actions to carry: the manager's Legal
escalation draft — due Nov 12, two days before the meeting; Renata's
coverage proposal — due Nov 13. One item to hold privately and keep out
of any brief: a family-care situation that may need December
flexibility — a support conversation, not an agenda item.

## The branches — same week, different facts

What changes each call:

- **If the short-replies signal had been a pattern** (clipped replies
  across three threads and several weeks, no coverage note): the item
  stands as Coaching — but still as a question: "I've noticed the
  dispatch replies getting clipped across a few threads — what's driving
  it?" A pattern earns the item; it never earns a verdict stated as fact.
- **If a sensitive item were filed** (she asked about a transfer to
  another division): it appears in the parking lot only — named
  neutrally, given its correct venue (a career-path conversation,
  possibly skip-level), zero talking points drafted in this brief.
- **If the blocker were hers** (blocked tickets owned by Renata, none
  upstream): item 2 becomes a delivery question anchored to the ticket
  count — same slot, opposite owner. The evidence decides who carries
  the question.
- **If an item had been filed low while carrying real weight** — a
  "quick question" on a dependency four other teams are waiting on: the
  filed weight is a claim like any other label. Re-weight it in the
  order, name the waiting parties, and say why it changed.
- **If she had already diagnosed the pattern herself** — a coaching item
  where her own DM names the failure and its cause: the session stops
  being a lecture. The question becomes the protocol — "what flags the
  next one early?" — anchored to her self-report, not the manager's
  summary of it.
- **If the manager's own overdue item caused the miss** — a deliverable
  on the manager's side that would have prevented the report's flag: it
  closes the brief as the manager's action, never as her coaching item.
  Same fact, opposite owner — the item is charged where the cause lives.

## What this shape enforces

- The input itself is questioned first — a record of a meeting already
  held gets the redirect, never a brief.
- Every filed item appears once — the manager's label shown, the
  signals' call beside it, the anchor named. Nothing is answered before
  it is classified.
- The manager's label is a claim, not a column to copy — when signal
  and label disagree, the signal wins and the label line shows the move.
- Coaching and career items survive only as questions tethered to a
  named signal. No anchor, no talking point.
- Sensitive items get a venue and an owner, not a paragraph.
- The header computes: point changes, completion rates, blocked counts —
  never restates raw numbers, and every comparison shows both operands.
- The manager's own open items surface; the reader is the manager, and
  their deliverables are part of the prep.
- A thin week produces a thin brief. Padding is a worse failure than a
  short agenda.
