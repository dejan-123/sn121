# One invented week answered in this voice — copy the form and the register, never the facts

The depot, the people and the numbers are all made up. What transfers is the shape and the sound: a reading that walks the draft line by line, items that advise like a colleague who did the homework, and questions written as words the manager can say to the report verbatim. In a real reply every fact comes from the inputs supplied.

## The invented inputs

Marek Novak, senior supervisor at a regional train maintenance depot, five years in. The manager's draft: "thanks where earned" / "Status: wheel lathe downtime" / "Coaching: blunt with the apprentice" / "Status: inspection sign-offs lagging" / "he asked about the shift-manager posting" / "private — his father's care". The week's record: action log shows "issue the revised night-shift rota (Marek, DONE Monday)" and "approve the calibrated torque-wrench order (Manager, OVERDUE)". Figures: 14 units released against a plan of 16. The log notes one blunt exchange with the apprentice on Friday, after two unrecorded torque checks. Last week Marek asked whether the shift-manager posting was open to internal candidates. His father's care arrangements are private; he has told HR what he needs.

## The reply

## Reading the room

The manager supplied a draft agenda, an action log, one figure pair and this week's threads for a meeting still to come, so this is prep.

1. "thanks where earned" — vague until Tuesday: the depot manager wrote in #ops that his crew "turned the EMU bogie swap in nine hours flat; the timetable kept every peak service because of it". Bouquet, on those words, and it opens.
2. "Status: wheel lathe downtime" — the OEM tech is unconfirmed until Friday at best, and he has already sequenced the non-critical work into the second bay. Not a status question — a Stall, and his resequencing is the fix to back: the move is a confirmed technician date out of the OEM.
3. "Coaching: blunt with the apprentice" — one dated exchange, Friday, after two torque checks went unrecorded. One means one: a Wobble, asked lightly, never a verdict.
4. "Status: inspection sign-offs lagging" — sign-offs lag on the board this week, but nothing says whether that repeats earlier weeks or who owns the chase. A legitimate ask with thin evidence stays on as a question: a Steady, pinned to the lag.
5. "he asked about the shift-manager posting" — he raised it himself last week. Peak; the nine-hour bogie swap is the signal to set beside what the posting asks for.
6. "private — his father's care" — the arrangements are private and he has already told HR what he needs. Side door: the leave process, first step is confirming dates with HR; nothing on it is drafted here.

Off the draft: the torque-wrench order approval, overdue, is the manager's own row — On your tab. Sums done: 14 units released against a 16 plan is 2 short, and the lagging sign-offs sit in the same week.

# Marek Novak x his manager — the nine-hour swap and the lathe still down

**The shape of it.** The depot released 14 units against a 16 plan (2 short), but Tuesday gave you the week's headline: his crew turned the bogie swap in nine hours and the depot manager credited every peak service to it in #ops. The wheel lathe is down on an OEM tech with no firm date — his resequencing is already running — and your wrench-order approval is overdue.

**On your tab.** Approve the calibrated torque-wrench order — overdue, and it is what the QA team needs to close the inspection backlog. Bring it or set the day.

## The run of it

**1. The bogie swap — Bouquet**
Worth opening on the depot manager's exact words — "the timetable kept every peak service because of it" — because a save named in public by the depot manager carries more than a number does. Then worth asking how he sequenced the nine hours, because a turnaround that clean is the kind of method that disappears if nobody writes it down.

**2. Wheel lathe — Stall**
The OEM tech is unconfirmed until Friday at best and he has already moved the non-critical wheel work into the second bay — the workaround is done, what remains is yours. Worth pinning the OEM to a confirmed technician date this week and telling him plainly the second-bay plan is approved, because he should not have to carry that risk silently.

**3. Sign-off lag — Steady**
The lag is on the board and nobody owns the chase — one question is enough: "which sign-offs are actually waiting, and whose desk are they sitting on?" If the same names show up next week, it earns its own item.

**4. Friday's exchange — Wobble**
A blunt word after two torque checks went unrecorded — one dated instance in a week he otherwise ran tight. One light question covers it: "has the apprentice logged the torque checks properly since Friday?" — a single moment never earns a verdict.

**5. Shift-manager posting — Peak**
- "The nine-hour swap is the clearest thing on your record this year — which parts of the shift-manager job does it already prove, and which does it leave open?"
- "You asked about the posting yourself — what would you want it to lead to?"

## Booked elsewhere

His father's care — the dates are private and he has already told HR what he needs; the first step is simply confirming the dates with HR so the rota can plan around them. Nothing on it is drafted here.

**The judgment calls.** The thanks stays and gains its anchor — the depot manager named the save; the lathe is a hold on an outside technician, not a status, and his bay resequencing is the fix to back; "blunt" is one dated instance after two unrecorded checks, so it stays a Wobble and never becomes a Groove; the sign-off lag is live but unowned, so it stays a question until the record says more; the posting is his and stands on the week's clearest signal; the care arrangement sits with HR, not this room.
