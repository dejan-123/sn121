# A margin note, then a room plan

The agenda is a memory aid, not evidence. Give the manager a short margin note for decisions, then a 350-700 word page for the room; do not narrate the sorting process twice. The packet supplies all facts. The worked file is invented; borrow its decisions, not its names, words or numbers.

## Decide whether this is tomorrow's work

A draft for a coming weekly meeting is preparation even if the manager could not pull a single action, message or figure. It receives a full brief with questions; missing signals do not turn it into an old-meeting record or a refusal. A record headed "meeting notes" with a past date, attendees, discussion and agreed follow-ups is the reverse: last week's statuses cannot honestly be presented as this week's. It receives only the short redirect at the end. Skip-level requests, review ratings and conversations about a third person also need their own artefact.

For prep begin `## Record reading`: what arrived and whether the meeting is ahead. Take each draft line in order, quote it and give the deciding signal and call in a sentence or two: retained, changed, merged, struck or routed. Name the class in this audit. `Outside the draft:` notes missed obligations; `Calculation:` gives figures and action states. The page below need not retell these decisions.

## Decide whose move this is

Where work is held by a supplier, another team, an approval or a queue too large for one report, put it in the manager's hands. Call it **Your move** in the margin, never a one-off correction or routine progress question. What will the manager approve, reassign, escalate or broker, and with whom? The report might have suggested a workaround: back their idea, do not take credit for it. An IN PROGRESS row may still be waiting on another team; a draft "status check" does not make the report accountable for their silence. Several impatient messages chasing the same hold describe one dependency, not a character pattern.

A **Where it stands** question needs live work whose next state, date or decision is unknown. Ask one question naming that work. If the input says DONE, close it in context or use it as an earned opener; there is no status check left. A report-owned open row is not the manager's debt. If a progress question reveals an outside gate, turn it into Your move instead of another report update.

When a colleague names a specific contribution or an outcome substantiates it, open with **Give credit**. Use the source and what the work enabled. The manager's generic "great job" or a plan not yet carried out is insufficient. Without that positive signal, state in the margin why there is no opener instead of manufacturing one.

## Decide what a person can fairly be asked

For a report-owned misstep, count dated occasions of the same action, not posts about one episode. **One moment** gets the event, any repair the report made, and a light question on what remains. A customer noticing it does not create a second occasion. **A repeat** needs two or more separate occasions and two questions, never a competing one-off item. A skill the report asked to build can also get a practice conversation. If they traced the cause, credit that discovery and ask about the earlier lever, not the symptom again.

A feeling that someone "seems disengaged" supplies no occasion. Say what, if anything, the dated work shows instead; leave the impression off the numbered page and identify what observed event would make it discussable. Workload assigned by other people, or sharp messages sent while waiting on an outside team, are not a report-owned fault. Test every question aloud: could it be asked of another employee with no edits? Put the particular thread, date or result into it. Would it still sound fair if the report saw this sheet? Replace any verdict with what was actually observed.

The report's own question about role, skills, training or scope deserves protected room: **The path**. Put one real piece of this week's work beside their direction and ask what they want to explore. A manager's development check without a report goal is an invitation, not an invented aspiration. A promotion decision belongs in its booked forum; the report's own unfinished packet step can still become a question here.

If two draft entries rest on the same dated event, give that event only one item. Ask what the time in the room is for: credit work, remove a hold, clarify live progress, put right one moment, explore a recurring skill, or discuss a possible path. The answer determines the call, not the draft's label.

## Account for numbers, ownership and tense

A quoted promise is a promise. "I will call on Thursday if they are still silent at noon" does not prove a call happened; a later completion record would. In the audit, header, agenda and closing alike, say "planned to call" and ask "Did the call happen?", never "called Thursday" or "after your call" without confirmation. Copy dates and names from their individual sources, never from the example or an inferred calendar. A reported cause of part of a gap does not explain the rest. When two inputs disagree, put the disagreement in the reading.

Read the entire action list. DONE manager rows count as done, just like DONE report rows; IN PROGRESS, NOT STARTED and OVERDUE do not. If the log is complete, show done/total and, where useful, the completion share. Print *only* unfinished rows actually assigned to the manager in the pre-meeting line. A manager move suggested by your analysis is worth doing but is not magically an open row in the supplied log. When no log was sent, do not claim the manager has finished everything.

Show operands: change in comparable amounts is (new - old) / old, signed. Differences between rates are points. Seven items blocking four teams are two counts, not a ratio. State what remains unaccounted for when a cause explains part of a gap. Put useful figures in the reading and header, not every item.

## Leave some subjects outside the weekly room

Pay, a promotion decision, health or family matters, accommodations, a formal HR track, a complaint about another person and a process a third party asked to own all need another venue. Name the topic with no more sensitive detail than the input uses; say who books which meeting and the concrete booking step. Write no talking points for that venue. An external process that blocks this week's work can be booked separately while its work dependency remains on the page. An unfinished manager action is a debt to bring, never a parked subject.

## Write the manager's page

After the reading, use a level-one heading `# <report>: <a true short phrase about the week>`. The page from that title onward is 350-700 words; aim for roughly 450 and trim before sending. In a short opening paragraph connect the result or evidence gap, the most important block, the action-list position and any relevant arithmetic. Outside quoted questions, "you" is the manager only: write "the report planned", not "you planned", for a plan the report described; never assign the report's work or load to the manager.

`## Agenda` gives numbered topics in the order a human would discuss them: earned thanks first if any; pressing moves the manager owes; a single repair or repeated skill; live status; the report's future question. Head each `**N. This week's topic**` without printing classification machinery on the conversation page. Each item takes at most three short sentences: what is known, what the manager will do, and a speakable question when needed. A coaching item may be two anchored questions and nothing else. A report-owned future topic does not get displaced by the manager's list. Do not give a closed task or a routed issue a numbered slot.

After the items, use only three light endings: `**Elsewhere.**` for subjects and their booking steps (or "Nothing to route"); `**Bring before meeting.**` for open manager-owned log rows with recorded status, plus missing records and likely holders on a thin week (or "No manager row open"); and `**Why this order.**` for one or two sentences on the surprising calls. Do not reproduce the audit. If the manager has no evidence beyond a draft, keep its live status and career check-ins as numbered, speakable questions with what to listen for. A workload check-in is not a diagnosis, and pay still gets a booked separate venue. No invented results, praise, pattern, manager debt or report ambition may fill the word count.

## When the meeting was already held

Write only a note under 180 words. Its first line is `# <name>: not a weekly prep request`. Explain plainly that the paste is a past one-on-one record with a date, attendees and agreed actions, so it cannot establish this week's status. Follow up its actions as recorded, manager first, preserving each owner and due date without asserting completion. Specify what to send to prepare a coming meeting: each action's state now, this week's messages or numbers, and a new agenda. A sensitive matter is named in the source's few words and sent to a private conversation, never drafted here. There is no `## Record reading` or future agenda in this case.

Read `references/three-notes.md` once with read_reference before writing. It demonstrates a full packet, a bare draft and a past-meeting redirect. No fact in it carries into the manager's actual week. Start the reply at its first heading.
