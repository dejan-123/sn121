# Three invented replies, three different jobs

The cases below are made up. Their replies illustrate when a brief is warranted, how to write it without repeating the reading, and when an old meeting needs a follow-up instead. Never carry the names, dates, arithmetic or quotations into an actual manager's reply.

## One: a week with decisions in it

The manager is preparing a weekly meeting with Asha Moreno, coordinator of a neighborhood repair hub. Their draft says: "Praise her Saturday shift"; "Status: soldering irons"; "Quick feedback: volunteer opening briefs"; "Her question about the regional trainer role"; "Discuss her pay request." The action list records Asha filing the inspection log (DONE Tuesday), the manager approving replacement soldering irons (OVERDUE), Asha distributing the volunteer rota (IN PROGRESS), and the manager booking a fire inspection (DONE Monday). The hub completed 18 repairs against a typical 24.

On Monday the city librarian wrote, "Asha's intake labels kept the Saturday line moving; nobody had to rejoin the queue." On Friday the volunteer lead noted that the safety checkpoint was missing from Asha's opening briefing; no other such incident is recorded. On Thursday the supplier wrote, "If stock clears, I'll send the quote on Monday"; no later quote is in the packet. That afternoon Asha asked about a regional trainer role. Her note also requests a pay conversation.

The reply:

## Record reading

A future agenda arrived with this week's log, figures and messages, so there is enough to decide which conversations belong in the room.

1. "Praise her Saturday shift" stays Give credit: Monday's librarian message identifies the intake labels and the queue they spared.
2. "Status: soldering irons" becomes Your move. The approval is the manager's overdue row; the supplier's Thursday message promises a conditional quote, not delivery or even a quote already sent.
3. "Quick feedback: volunteer opening briefs" becomes One moment. Friday's missed safety checkpoint is the only dated occurrence; no pattern is shown.
4. "Her question about the regional trainer role" stays The path because Asha raised it herself on Thursday. The Saturday intake change is relevant to explore, not proof of a promotion.
5. "Discuss her pay request" moves to a dedicated pay conversation, with no rate or talking points drafted here.

Outside the draft: the replacement approval is overdue, but the manager's fire-inspection booking is DONE and owes nothing. Asha's rota is still in progress and her inspection log is done. Calculation: 18 versus 24 typical repairs is (18 - 24) / 24, down 25%; two of four log rows are done.

# Asha Moreno: a good Saturday and a missing safety step

The librarian's Monday note gives you something specific to say about Saturday: Asha's intake labels stopped visitors having to rejoin the queue. The hub finished 18 repairs against a typical 24, down 25%; the packet does not establish what portion of that gap comes from the soldering irons. Your replacement approval remains overdue, and the supplier has only promised a quote if stock clears. Two of four logged actions are done, including your fire-inspection booking; the rota is in progress. Pay needs another room.

## Agenda

**1. Saturday's intake labels**
Tell Asha what the librarian noticed: visitors stayed in line rather than having to start again. That is a concrete improvement she made, not a general compliment about a busy shift. "What did you change in the labels that made the line easier to manage?" The answer may reveal something worth sharing with the next shift.

**2. Replacement soldering irons**
The approval is overdue on your side. Ask the supplier whether stock cleared and the promised quote was actually sent; then settle the approval or give Asha a date for your decision. "While I sort the replacement, which repairs are waiting on a working iron?" Do not tell her the quote arrived merely because the supplier intended to send one on Monday.

**3. Friday's opening briefing**
The volunteer lead found the safety checkpoint missing on Friday, once, not on a run of shifts. Asha may already have repaired it; ask gently: "After Friday's opening, was any safety instruction still owed to the volunteers?" A missing checkpoint matters, but one dated moment is not a judgment about how she usually briefs.

**4. Volunteer rota**
It is in progress, and no delivery date is in the log. "When will the new rota reach the volunteers, and is anything waiting on me?" If it needs the same manager decision as the replacement order, make that one move rather than giving Asha two independent chases.

**5. Regional trainer role**
Asha asked about it on Thursday; give her time rather than an assumed verdict. The intake-label change is a piece of work she can reflect on: "What part of explaining that change to other shifts would you enjoy, and what would you want to learn before training beyond this hub?" Bring the actual role criteria later instead of inventing them now.

**Elsewhere.** Her pay request goes to a separate pay discussion. You book it with Asha and the appropriate pay contact this week; the printed page carries no negotiation script.

**Bring before meeting.** The replacement-iron approval is OVERDUE. Check whether the supplier sent the conditional quote and bring a decision or a date for one; do not present Asha's unfinished rota as your action item.

**Why this order.** Specific praise opens, and your overdue decision comes before any ask for more repairs. One missed safety step gets a light question, not a coaching theme; pay leaves this room.

## Two: an agenda and nothing behind it

For the next weekly meeting with Jamal Sethi, bookmobile coordinator, the manager sends only: "Mobile reading app pilot - where are we?"; "Check on his workload"; "He wants to talk about salary"; "Career development check-in." There is no action list, figure, message, dated example or request from Jamal about a particular role.

The reply:

## Record reading

This is a draft for a meeting still to come, with no supporting record. Thin preparation is still preparation; there are no completed results or patterns to assert.

1. "Mobile reading app pilot - where are we?" stays Where it stands. A pilot task list and Jamal's latest update would distinguish a live dependency from a simple progress check.
2. "Check on his workload" is a neutral Where it stands check-in, not a finding about stress or performance. No schedule or message describes the load.
3. "He wants to talk about salary" is a report request in the draft; book a separate pay conversation rather than improvise a response here.
4. "Career development check-in" came from the manager, with no direction specified by Jamal. Keep The path as a small invitation, and let him choose whether there is a topic.

Outside the draft: no log was given, so no manager row can be marked complete or overdue. Calculation: there are no operands for a pilot completion rate or a workload trend.

# Jamal Sethi: find the pilot's next step together

You have a draft agenda, not a report on the app pilot. There is no evidence that the pilot has launched, slipped or reached a particular number of readers, so the useful opening is a question rather than invented recognition. Nor is a workload check a diagnosis: you can ask what is competing for Jamal's time and take responsibility for any trade-off only after hearing it. Salary is a separate conversation. The career item is yours, not a goal Jamal sent you, and should remain easy for him to decline.

## Agenda

**1. Mobile reading app pilot**
"What stage is the reading app pilot at, and what is the next decision you need?" Ask Jamal to separate completed work from a plan he has not carried out yet; the draft alone cannot do that. If he names a partner approval or an access problem, ask who owns it and agree what you will do to move it. If the work is on course, agree a next checkpoint instead of treating your lack of figures as evidence of delay.

**2. Workload alongside the pilot**
"With the pilot and your bookmobile rounds, what is taking the time this week, and is anything you want me to reprioritize?" Those two named areas are in this job and this draft; the question makes no claim that he is overwhelmed. Listen for a collision of commitments you can resolve, not for a reason to attach a performance label. If there is no collision, accept that and move on.

**3. Development**
You suggested this check-in; Jamal has not named a role. "Is there part of the bookmobile work or this pilot you would like more of, or is there something else you want us to leave time for?" Give him the option of returning to it next week. If he raises a role, bring its actual expectations before telling him that the pilot qualifies him for it. Until then, the page records no career aspiration as fact.

There is no recognition slot because no praise or result was supplied, and no coaching slot because no instance of a recurring behaviour was supplied. Those omissions make room for Jamal's actual account, rather than a manager's guess. Record any new commitments after the meeting with an owner and date he has agreed to.

**Elsewhere.** Jamal's salary question belongs in a dedicated pay discussion. You arrange a time with him and the pay contact; nothing on pay is decided or rehearsed in this brief.

**Bring before meeting.** There is no action log to check for manager debts. Ask Jamal or the pilot owner for the task list and latest update, and bring the bookmobile schedule if it could illuminate his load. Invite a development topic without insisting on one. If none of those records arrives, keep the three questions above rather than filling the gaps with assumptions.

**Why this order.** Pilot and workload are questions because the record is absent; the development invitation remains open because it is yours. Pay is important, but it has a different venue.

## Three: notes from a meeting that is over

The manager asks for next week's prep and pastes "One-on-one with Reva Brooks, venue coordinator, 12 May," with both names under Attendees and a discussion of weekend bookings. The agreed actions are: manager to ask facilities about an extra key by 16 May; Reva to send a seating map by 18 May; manager to confirm steward cover by 19 May. A family care matter was mentioned. Nothing says whether the actions have since been finished.

The whole answer:

# Reva Brooks: not a weekly prep request

This redirects the request: what you sent is the record of the one-on-one already held on 12 May, with attendees, discussion and agreed actions. Treating its weekend-booking discussion as fresh news would mislead the next meeting. It supports a follow-up on the recorded actions:

- Manager: ask facilities about an extra key, due 16 May.
- Manager: confirm steward cover, due 19 May.
- Reva: send a seating map, due 18 May.

Their current status is not in these notes. To prepare the next one-on-one, bring where each action stands now, this week's messages or numbers, and a new agenda. The family care matter stays in a private conversation with Reva; no talking points on it belong here.

## What the examples leave open

If the replacement approval belonged to Asha rather than her manager, the manager-debt line would disappear. A supplier's "I'll send a quote" is still a plan if Monday has passed without a confirming message. Jamal's draft alone cannot be upgraded to praise by writing a more confident sentence.

Nor is a pile of reviews assigned to one person Quick feedback because it surfaced only once: they did not choose the volume. If they propose a fairer rotation, the manager's move is to decide or broker it, not ask the report to repair their attitude.
