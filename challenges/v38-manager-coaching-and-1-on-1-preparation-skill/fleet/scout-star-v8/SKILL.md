# The desk — take every ticket, work it on the record

A manager's draft agenda is a book of work tickets, each written
the manager's way. The ticket is a first intention, not a job.
Every ticket goes through the desk and is worked on the job
record alone — what the packet documents, nothing it does not.
The output is a sheet the manager reads on the way in: every
ticket logged, every job traceable to a record entry, the count
matching the tickets written.

## Book check — is this this week's book?

Three books look like a weekly ticket book and are not: notes,
minutes or a transcript of a meeting already held (even when the
manager asks to plan the next one from it — it records last
week's jobs, not this week's record; what it supports is a
follow-up on the actions); preparation for a skip-level or a
different meeting; a performance-review, rating or disciplinary
draft. For these, ticket nothing. Reply in under 150 words
titled `# Not this week's tickets`: what the book is, which
document it actually supports and who owns it, what to bring
for a weekly sheet — ending with the one-line reason (nothing
in the material is logged after that meeting, so a sheet would
replay old jobs or invent new ones). The hand-back carries no
desk structure: no tickets logged, no jobs, no order — it is a
plain reply, not a sheet. Where the input is last week's book,
list each open action with owner and date exactly as written;
open actions carry forward as entries, never as assumed closed.

The hand-back triggers on the book's kind and nothing else. A
book of the manager's own topics for the coming meeting is
always this week's book, even with no record behind it: a bare
agenda, a two-line note, topics with no figures behind them.
Scarcity is worked ticket by ticket (Pending parts, below); it
never triggers a hand-back. For every book that is this week's —
full or thin — the reply opens `# Work order:` with no
preamble.

## Book check — what gets logged

Only what the packet documents gets logged: an action item and
its state (the manager's own included), a sprint or ticket
figure, a quoted line, the manager's note, the report's own
topic. No invented figure, quote, date, name or mood. Where a
job needs a record entry the packet lacks, the lack is logged —
never filled with a comfortable guess.

## The log — how entries weigh

Entries weigh by kind, not volume. One documented occurrence is
a single log — it can support a note, it cannot support a
pattern job. The same conduct documented on two or more
occasions is a pattern. Attributed commendation — a named
person's thanks or a concrete result — is credit earned;
effort, hours or unverifiable praise is not. The report's own
account is self-reported: loggable, weighed below an
independent entry. An impression the packet cannot source is a
reason to ask, not an entry.

## Done stays done

A ticket the record shows finished is logged as a one-line
close — unless another logged entry cannot stand with the
finish. The ticket's written job never reopens it. Where a
later entry and the logged finish genuinely conflict, log the
conflict in writing; never re-ticket silently.

## Which trade owns it

A ticket belongs to the trade that can do it. Work waiting on
an input another person or team controls is that trade's job —
a stalled dependency goes on the sheet as the manager's own
action to move it, never as the report's fault to answer for.
A sole reviewer or approver whose queue stalls several teams is
a capacity matter the manager owns: it works early as the
manager's own decision — who should hold the load, with the
fix the report already proposed as the starting point — not as
a ticket to work faster. Pay, health, family, formal ratings,
complaints about colleagues and disciplinary process belong to
other trades entirely: referred with destination and owner, no
talking points, one private line at most.

## The jobs

Every ticket takes exactly one job status, and the written
ticket never decides it — the record does:

- **Closed**: the record supports the ticket as written. It
  takes its slot with an anchored question or a concrete
  manager action.
- **Re-ticketed**: the job stands under a corrected ticket. A
  conduct or attitude ticket where the record shows work
  stalled at another trade's bench is re-ticketed to the
  stalled job it is; a routine check on finished, credited
  work is re-ticketed to the credit. The sheet names the
  written ticket and the new ticket both.
- **Referred**: a real job bound for another trade — named
  destination, named owner, no talking points.
- **Voided**: no logged entry supports the ticket — the
  recognition with no attributable credit, the topic written
  in as padding. Its absence is logged plainly.
- **Pending parts**: the job is real but unworkable as filed.
  It ticks as the honest question — what is the actual state,
  what does the missing entry show — never as coverage
  invented to fill the slot.

A development ticket requires a pattern. A single incident
works as a one-time note — with one narrow question about the
setting, never as a pattern job. Where a request was
substantively justified but ran in a public channel, the
request works as written and exactly one question addresses
the channel; the justified ticket is not folded into the
channel point. Where the report raises their own direction —
a level, a path, a stretch scope — that is their ticket and
it works with real time in this meeting; only the formal
decision (a promotion case, a rating, a band) is referred.

## The sheet

300 to 650 words, written for the manager to read, not to
audit. The meeting runs 30 minutes unless the packet says
otherwise, and the tickets' minutes add to it.

- **Sheet head**: `# Work order: <name>, <role>` — then three
  lines: the week's picture in one sentence; every derivable
  figure written as shown work (`2 of 5 closed, 3 open`,
  `14 → 23 screens, +64%`), each pair against its own basis,
  never a ratio without its operands; which record types the
  packet lacks, logged plainly.
- **Job order**: every ticket, numbered, as
  `n. Job · topic · ~minutes`, then under each its desk line:
  `ticketed: <the manager's ticket> → <job>: <the entry in a
  few words>` — every ticket carries this line, including
  `as ticketed` where ticket and job agree. No written ticket
  disappears: a merged one keeps its own desk line under the
  ticket that absorbed it; referred and voided tickets name
  their job. Order: attributable credit first when it exists;
  then what blocks work or needs the manager's call; then a
  one-time note; then the report's own direction with real
  time (if the meeting cannot hold it, book a dedicated slot
  rather than squeeze it into the last two minutes); quick
  closes last.
- **Desk notes**: questions and concrete manager actions only,
  each anchored to a named entry — the thread, ticket, figure
  or person — at most three sentences per ticket. A direction
  the report never raised stays a career ticket only when the
  packet holds their topic or an agreed cadence; otherwise it
  is one open question at the end and the sheet logs the
  packet holds none of theirs.
- **Supervisor's own jobs**: the manager's own open or overdue
  items — done or owned first of all when they touch the same
  ticket — and the records to pull for any gap the book check
  logged.
- **Referred**: the other-trade jobs with destination and
  owner.
- **Chief engineer's note**: three to five plain sentences
  closing the sheet — each re-ticket, referral or void with
  the entry that forced it; why the first job works or why
  there is none; what a headline figure does and does not
  show.

When a figure explains only part of a gap, the rest stays
open: the sheet names the share still unexplained and puts it
to the report as a status question rather than treating the
explanation as settled.

## Order of work

1. In the same message where you call read_reference, write
   the working book: the book check, then for every written
   ticket its job, the entry that closes it, its status and
   any referral, and each derived figure with its working.
2. Call read_reference on `references/ticket-drills.md` once:
   full sheets on invented books — a normal week with a
   mis-ticketed job, a thin book worked honestly, a book of
   old tickets handed back whole, and a queue that is really
   the manager's own capacity job. Carry the jobs and
   proportions, never the facts.
3. Once it returns, send the sheet and nothing else. Its
   first characters are `# ` (or, for a hand-back, the
   `# Not this week's tickets` title); the working book is
   not repeated, summarised or introduced.
