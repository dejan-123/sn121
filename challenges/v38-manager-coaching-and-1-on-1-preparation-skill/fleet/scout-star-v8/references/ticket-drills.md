# Ticket drills — full sheets on invented books

Four invented ticket books, worked the way the desk should work
them. The names, figures and products are invented; the jobs are
the part to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal book with a mis-ticketed job

Book: report is "Hana, senior full-stack engineer". Manager's
draft: (a) "Hana's clash with the data team — she challenged
their pipeline numbers in the project channel", (b) "why is
the onboarding rebuild behind", (c) "call out the search
launch". The record: the data lead posted a public thank-you
naming Hana for finding the double-counting bug behind their
inflated figures; the channel exchange shows Hana objecting —
bluntly but on the substance — to publishing numbers she had
just disproved; the rebuild is 5 of 9 screens done, waiting on
a brand-asset delivery requested 7 days ago; search launched
and the support lead messaged that "can't find" tickets fell
by a third in week one.

Jobs:

- Ticket (a): **Re-ticketed**. One substantive objection to
  figures she had just shown were wrong — ticketed as a clash,
  worked as a one-time note with one narrow question. Desk
  note: "The data exchange was on the merits — they were
  already thanking you for the counting fix. One thing to
  keep in view: that disagreement ran in the project channel
  rather than on the ticket. Does moving it there cost you
  anything?" The objection stands; the channel takes one
  question and no more.
- Ticket (b): **Re-ticketed — another trade's job.** 5 of 9
  done, and the stall is a 7-day-old brand-asset delivery,
  not Hana's pace. Desk note: "The rebuild is waiting on
  brand's asset delivery — seven days outstanding. I'll chase
  the designer today and get a date; who should I escalate
  to if it slips again?"
- Ticket (c): **Closed first.** Two attributable credits —
  the data lead's public thanks and the support figure (a
  third fewer can't-find tickets). Open with them before any
  process ticket; credit closes first.
- Every written ticket gets a desk line and a job; nothing
  written disappears.

Sheet: `# Work order: Hana, senior full-stack engineer` —
picture line ("a strong week with one job waiting on another
trade"), figures shown as work, then job order with all three
written tickets, the entry behind each, and each job — then
desk notes, supervisor's own jobs, referred list, chief
engineer's note.

## Drill 2 — a thin book worked honestly

Book: report is "Ravi, staff engineer". Manager's note: "prep
my weekly with Ravi — want to talk about his path to
principal, and whether he can lead the notifications
replatform." No action log, no figures, no channel excerpts.
One calendar item: Ravi asked in passing about visa
sponsorship timing.

Thin is not invalid: the book aims at the coming meeting, so
the desk still takes it — worked ticket by ticket. Only the
wrong kind of book (drill 3) is handed back whole. The reply
opens `# Work order: Ravi, staff engineer` — never a
hand-back preface.

Jobs:

- Path to principal: **Closed — split by trade.** Ravi's
  direction and the manager's readiness question belong in
  this meeting, with real time: "You asked about the next
  level — here's the gap evidence I see and what I'd need
  documented; what scope would you take to build it?" The
  visa question is **Referred**: sponsorship sits with the
  people team — the sheet names the destination (the people
  partner) and the owner, one line, no desk notes.
- Readiness for the replatform: **Pending parts**. The book
  has no delivery record to weigh. It works as the honest
  question, not an assessment invented for the slot: "I
  don't have your recent delivery record in front of me —
  walk me through the last few weeks: what shipped, what's
  stuck, what you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the sheet. A thin sheet
  of honest questions beats a padded one; the sheet head
  logs what the book lacks and the supervisor's own jobs
  list names what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a book that is not this week's

Book: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with Zoe —
please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes list
three actions: Zoe to circulate the incident postmortem (due
Thursday), the manager to confirm the conference ticket, a
follow-up on the buddy pairing.

Job: **whole book handed back — nothing ticketed.** The
reply, under 150 words, titled `# Not this week's tickets`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was
agreed, not what has moved since. The document this supports
is a follow-up on the open actions, not a sheet: Zoe's
postmortem (due Thursday), your ticket confirmation, the
buddy follow-up — owners and dates exactly as written. For a
weekly sheet, send this week's action log, any figures, and
whatever Zoe raised or you want addressed. Nothing in the
material is logged after that meeting, so a sheet would
replay old jobs or invent new ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not
repeated, not summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own job

Book: report is "Tessa, senior platform engineer". Manager's
draft: (a) "Tessa needs to clear her design-review backlog —
six docs are waiting on her and two partner teams are
blocked"; (b) "Tessa seemed distracted in the demo". The
record: the six docs are assigned because the process names
her sole approver for cross-team changes; she shipped two
features this sprint and raised the backlog herself in the
last retro, proposing a rotation; "distracted" appears once,
in the manager's note.

Jobs:

- Ticket (a): **Re-ticketed — the trade that can do it is
  the manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two teams
  is a capacity matter the manager owns, not a ticket to
  work faster. It works early as the manager's own decision:
  "Your rotation proposal is the right starting point — I
  want to approve it this week and take it to the team
  leads. What load should each reviewer hold so nothing
  queues behind one person again?"
- Ticket (b): **Voided.** One manager observation with no
  entries behind it; the sprint shows two shipped features
  and a raised process fix. The sheet logs its absence
  plainly rather than inventing an engagement segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Tessa's retro
  proposal — is listed under supervisor's own jobs as the
  manager's commitment, with whom and when.

## What every drill shares

- The record decides; the written ticket is only the filing.
- Attributable credit closes first — a named person, a
  concrete result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its
  substance and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another trade does is referred to that trade.
- One occurrence is a single log, never a pattern.
- Missing records work as `Pending parts` — the honest
  question in the slot — never covered over.
- Other-trade jobs get a destination and an owner, never
  desk notes.
- Every figure shows its own working; every written ticket
  gets a desk line and a job.
- A thin book still gets a sheet. The hand-back is for the
  wrong kind of book only — it stays under 150 words and
  carries no desk structure.
