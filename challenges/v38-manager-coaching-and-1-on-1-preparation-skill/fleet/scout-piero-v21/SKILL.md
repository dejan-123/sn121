# Record-check brief

A draft agenda is a memory, not a finding. The action log, figures, messages, notes and report-submitted topic decide what each item deserves. Show that decision before the brief so a changed label reads as grounded, not argumentative.

Use only supplied material: never add people, dates, results, motives, completion, praise, moods or facts. A day may be copied only from the source that carries it.

## First classify the request

A forward-looking draft is prep even when it is the only input or the week looks thin; missing evidence changes the questions, not the purpose.

A pasted agenda, minutes, transcript, decisions-and-actions record or debrief from a meeting already held is not prep input, even when the user asks for next week's meeting. Redirect it as below. Skip-levels, team meetings, conversations about a third person and formal review or rating documents also redirect with the fitting artefact or owner.

## Show the record check first

For a valid request, the reply begins with `## Record check` and no preamble. The first sentence names what was supplied and says it supports preparation for a meeting still ahead.

Under that heading, number every draft topic in the manager's order. In two or three natural sentences, quote its exact text, state the supplied evidence (source, owner and date where given), and give the earned call: `Credit`, `Unblock`, `Status`, `Quick feedback`, `Coaching`, `Career`, or `Route elsewhere`. Explain any overturned label plainly.

Then write `Outside the draft:` for omitted open work, a report-submitted topic, praise, a personal matter or another fact. Finish the check with `Figures:` and `Action log:` so derived values and open rows are settled before the page.

The brief below the check must agree with it. Nothing in the visible page may contradict a call already made in the record check.

## Earned calls

**Credit** opens the brief only when the packet names the praise or measured result: who credited which work, or what number proves it. Vague confidence, remembered effort and an unattributed "great job" are not enough. With no creditable signal, do not manufacture an opener.

**Unblock** covers work waiting on approval, access, a supplier, a calendar, another team, a shared process or an assigned queue. The move belongs to the person who can release it. When the report's row sits in a queue assigned to one reviewer, or a sole-reviewer design holds back several teams, name that as a manager/capacity decision; do not describe the report as personally blocking the teams. A plan, promise, request or scheduled step is still a plan. Write `planned to escalate`, `requested review` or `was scheduled`, then ask whether it happened or what it produced.

**Status** is live work that can be answered in the room and is not primarily blocked by someone else. Use it sparingly. Finished work provides evidence or closes; it does not need a status slot.

**Quick feedback** is one dated event with one narrow adjustment. If the report already identified the cause or corrected the event, acknowledge that and ask about the earlier detection, flagging or prevention step rather than repeating the symptom.

**Coaching** needs either the same behaviour on at least two dated occasions or a named prior cycle/sprint with plan-versus-actual figures. One event, a vague impression, a justified objection, or several messages about one shared blocker is not a pattern. When a public objection is substantively right, keep its substance and, if warranted, ask only one narrow channel question.

**Career** protects time for a direction, level, programme, training or development topic the report raised. A manager-created career check stays on the agenda as a modest invitation through the week's named work; it is not parked and not treated as the report's aspiration.

**Route elsewhere** covers pay, health, family circumstances, accommodations, formal HR or discipline, third-party complaints, ratings, promotion decisions and processes owned elsewhere. Give the destination and owner, with no drafted talking points or advice. A report's open action toward a development or promotion path may stay on the agenda, while the decision itself is routed to its proper venue.

## Reconcile before writing

Account for the draft exactly: `N` manager topics require `N` numbered entries, each carrying that topic's exact wording. Do not silently merge, split, duplicate or drop one; two entries may share a signal, but each keeps its own line and call. Cover every unrepresented open action row under `Outside the draft:`; if using a filed line, write `filed: not on the draft`.

Report `DONE rows / all supplied rows`, including manager-owned and completed rows in the denominator. Only explicit `DONE` counts; `IN PROGRESS`, `NOT STARTED`, open, overdue and partial work do not. A done row supplies evidence or closure, never a manager to-do. Manager-owned open or overdue rows go to `Manager to clear`; report-owned open rows become report questions; external dependencies stay unblocks or manager routes.

Do not turn an intention into a result. A missed estimate, a planned escalation, a promised follow-up, an attempted fix or a scheduled review stays conditional until the packet says it occurred or worked. A cause that explains only part of a variance leaves the rest open.

## Build the carried page

After the record check, write the printable brief, 350 to 700 words, in manager-facing prose. It must still read like something a manager can carry into the room, not like an audit or a process transcript.

Use this shape:

1. `# <Full name>, <role>: <short evidence-based week heading>`
2. `**Week in view.**` Three or four sentences combining what moved, what waits and on whom, what the report raised, the action-log total and the worked figures. Percentages and changes show their basis and operands. Rates and scores move in percentage points. Planned versus actual comparisons use the same basis, and unrelated counts are not made into a ratio.
3. `**Manager to clear.**` List every open or overdue manager-owned action row, what it blocks and whether the manager should settle it before the meeting or name the day. Also list each record to fetch, one bullet per missing record. If there is none, say so. Do not put report-owned rows, external dependencies, DONE rows or diverted topics here.
4. `## Conversation order` or `## Meeting order`: number every planned agenda entry with a class and a realistic minute estimate. Earned credit opens. Unblocks and needed status follow, then quick feedback or coaching, then report-raised career time. A manager-created career check may come last. Keep each entry to at most three short paragraphs or bullets:
   - Credit: name who said or measured what, the work it credits and why it matters.
   - Unblock: state what waits, on whom and since when, then the manager's concrete move and only the report question that remains.
   - Status: ask the live question about the named work.
   - Quick feedback: name the event, credit any self-correction, then ask the narrow remaining question.
   - Coaching: ask one or two anchored questions, each tied to a dated occurrence, thread, ticket or figure.
   - Career: ask through the work or evidence already supplied and connect it to the direction the report named.
5. `**Route elsewhere.**` Give each diverted matter, its proper venue and owner. Do not draft talking points. If none applies, say none.
6. `**Why this order.**` Close with two to four plain sentences explaining the calls that changed, the evidence behind them and what remains unknown. Do not mention this skill, the reference, tools or internal reasoning.

Questions must be anchored in supplied names, work, dates, threads or figures. A generic question that could be pasted into any report's brief is not acceptable.

## A thin packet

When the packet lacks a record type, say so in `Action log:` or `Figures:` and list it in `Manager to clear`, one bullet per missing record. A bare draft still receives every record-check entry and a full brief. Keep the draft's provisional class when its own words give one. Under each planned entry state what the manager does not yet know, ask a question grounded in the draft's own wording and name what answer would settle the call. Do not invent evidence to make the page look complete.

## A held-meeting record

When the pasted material records a meeting already held, send only a redirect under 180 words and no record-check entries, agenda or brief.

Use `# <report name>, <role>: not a 1-on-1 prep request`. In natural prose:

- state that the input is the held meeting's record and give the detail that shows it, such as attendees, decisions, discussion or agreed actions;
- explain that a new page would replay that meeting or invent this week's position;
- list each recorded open action with its exact owner, action and due date, putting manager-owned rows first;
- state that current status is not supplied;
- ask for current action status, this week's signals, any new report topic and a fresh draft agenda;
- route any pay, health, family, personal, formal or third-party matter privately, in the record's own few words, with no talking points.

## Working order

1. Call `read_reference` on `references/field-guide.md` once. It shows the page's shape and the close calls; never reuse its names, facts or wording.
2. For a held-meeting record, return the redirect alone. Otherwise begin the reply with `## Record check`.
3. Reconcile the draft topics and action rows, classify every signal, work the figures, then write the 350-to-700-word brief in the shape above.
