# Field guide

Everything below is invented. Use the ordering, tests and page shape; never reuse the people, facts, wording or figures.

## Close calls

- **Credit needs a source.** “She carried the launch” is not an opener until a named message or measured result says what she did. If no source exists, say so above the first agenda entry.
- **A queue is not a personality.** A ticket waiting in one assigned reviewer’s queue is the manager’s capacity or routing decision. If the report planned to escalate, say `planned to escalate` and ask what happened; do not write that they escalated.
- **Count the log.** Three rows with `DONE`, `IN PROGRESS` and `OVERDUE` are `1 / 3 supplied rows done`, not two done. Manager rows count in the denominator, and a done manager row is not something the manager still owes.
- **A diagnosis is not the coaching target.** When the report already found why the miss happened, coach the earlier detection or flag: “What would have surfaced that before the estimate became late?”
- **One fair public objection is not a pattern.** Keep the objection’s substance; only a narrow channel question is possible, and only when the packet supports it.
- **The draft keeps its count.** Four draft topics mean four record-check entries, each with the manager’s wording. An open row outside those topics appears under `Outside the draft:`, not by splitting one topic.
- **A bare draft still gets a page.** Say what never arrived, keep each draft topic visible, ask grounded questions, and fetch the missing log, figures or threads.
- **A held meeting gets no brief.** Name the report and role in the redirect title, list open actions with owner and due date, state that current status is unknown, and ask for status, new signals, a report topic and a fresh draft.

## The page it earns

## Record check
The packet contains a coming-meeting draft, action log, figures, messages and the report’s topic, so this is preparation.

1. “Credit for the renewal incident.” Priya named the renewal handoff on Monday and said it kept the customer call on schedule. Credit holds because the packet gives a person, the work and the effect.
2. “Quick feedback: blunt with Design.” Nia’s Tuesday message objects to Design assigning customers without reproduction notes. It is one documented message, not a pattern; the work question is the intake rule, so this becomes Unblock rather than feedback about her manner.
3. “Status on access cleanup.” The cleanup list is in progress and the SSO exception approval sits overdue with the manager. The blocker is a manager-owned approval, so Unblock fits and the manager’s move comes first.
4. “Career check.” Nia submitted the incident-lead question herself, so Career holds with protected time; it is her direction, not a manager-created check.

Outside the draft: the design-sync invite is already DONE, so it is evidence of closure and not a manager to-do.
Figures: 9 of 12 tickets is 75%; escalations rose from 3 to 5, a change of 2 against a base of 3.
Action log: 2 of 4 supplied rows are DONE; one report-owned row remains in progress and one manager-owned approval is overdue.

# Nia Okafor, Platform Support Engineer: renewal credit and one manager-owned block

**Week in view.** Nia completed 9 of 12 planned tickets (75%) while escalations rose from 3 to 5 (+2 on a base of 3). Priya publicly credited the renewal handoff for keeping the customer call on schedule. Access cleanup remains in progress but is waiting on the manager’s overdue SSO exception approval, and Nia’s Design message points to an intake-rule problem rather than a pattern in her conduct.

**Manager to clear.**
- SSO exception approval: OVERDUE; decide it or name the decision date before asking for the cleanup list’s next step.
- Fetch the design-intake rule or queue owner so the Design conversation starts from the process, not a guess.

## Conversation order

**1. Credit — renewal handoff (4 min)**
Quote Priya’s message about the handoff keeping the customer call on schedule. Tie the credit to the handoff itself, not to general effort.

**2. Unblock — access cleanup approval (7 min)**
The SSO exception remains overdue with you while Nia’s cleanup list stays in progress. Settle the approval or give the decision date, then ask only what remains after that dependency clears.

**3. Unblock — Design intake without reproduction notes (5 min)**
Nia’s message challenged assignments entering her queue without the required notes. Confirm who owns the intake rule and agree what you will raise with Design; if you discuss wording at all, keep it to one narrow channel question.

**4. Career — incident-lead path (14 min)**
Ask which parts of the renewal handoff show the incident-lead work Nia wants to build, and which incident or ticket would give her the next useful rep.

**Route elsewhere.** Nothing in the supplied packet needs a separate venue.

**Why this order.** Priya’s named praise earns the opener. The blunt-message item changes because the supplied evidence concerns the Design intake rule, not repeated behaviour. Access cleanup is not a neutral status check while a manager-owned approval is overdue. The design-sync row is DONE, so it closes rather than entering `Manager to clear`.
