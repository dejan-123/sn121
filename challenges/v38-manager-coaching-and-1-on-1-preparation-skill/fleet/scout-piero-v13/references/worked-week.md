# A worked week, plus three edge cases

All invented. Steal the order of the calls and the shape of the page — none of the people, figures or sentences.

## Edge case: nothing quotable

The draft says "she has been carrying the region" but no message, figure or log names a result. The page does not manufacture praise — above item one stands a line like: `No quotable win in this week's record; the impression alone does not open the meeting.` The item then folds into wherever the signals actually put it.

## Edge case: a thin week

Only a draft agenda arrives, nothing behind it. The reading section says in a few sentences what never came, then takes each item in a line as unverified. The page still ships — but it asks instead of asserting. A draft line "how is the stock count going?" becomes:

**1. The stock count — Update, ~8 min**
No update on where it stands or what holds it. Ask what is done, what comes next and what is in her way — and listen for a date that has quietly moved.

A career item only the manager pencilled gets one question, asked through the week's named work: which part of it she would want more of, and which less.

## Edge case: a record of a past meeting

The packet is the notes of Monday's 1-on-1, presented as prep for next week's. The whole reply is the short note — no reading section:

This is the record of Monday's 1-on-1 — decisions already taken, actions already assigned, nothing dated after it. A brief built on it would replay that meeting as this week's news. What it supports is a follow-up on the open actions: Marek, the spare-parts forecast, Thursday; you, the van lease renewal, Wednesday. For next week's brief, send the current status of those, this week's figures or threads, and your draft agenda. The personal matter mentioned in the notes belongs in a private conversation and goes nowhere near the page.

## The invented week

Signals, Monday to Friday: a warehouse shift lead, five years in. Board: the cold-store refit has two vendor replies unanswered; her pick-path rewrite shipped and the floor supervisor logged "errors down a third"; a forklift inspection renewal sits NOT STARTED on the manager's list. Chat: she chased the vendor Tuesday and Thursday, asked Friday about the inventory-systems course. Draft agenda: "refit status; pick path went ok; the course; inspection."

## Reading the board

1. "refit status" — **Evidence:** two unanswered vendor chases, her Tuesday flag. **Verdict:** not an update — **Stuck**, and it needs your call to the vendor.
2. "pick path went ok" — **Evidence:** shipped Monday; supervisor logged errors down a third. **Verdict:** **Recognition** — open with the number, not a compliment.
3. "the course" — **Evidence:** she asked about inventory-systems training Friday. **Verdict:** **Career** — she raised it; give an honest answer and a path.
4. "inspection" — **Evidence:** renewal NOT STARTED on your list; her side is done. **Verdict:** yours — off her draft, onto your own list.

**Missed by the draft:** the inspection renewal is your row, four days sitting. **Figures:** pick errors down 33% (14 to 9 weekly).

# 1-on-1 brief: Maya Okafor x her manager — Friday

**Context:** Five years running the floor; refit stalled on a vendor.

**1. Pick path — Recognition, ~3 min**
Errors down a third since Monday — say the number, ask what she would standardize next.

**2. Cold-store refit — Stuck, ~7 min**
Two chases unanswered. Tell her when you will call the vendor and what date you are holding them to.

**3. The course — Career, ~10 min**
She asked about inventory training. Say what the budget allows and what she needs to show for it.

**4. Figures — Update, ~3 min**
Throughput on pace; errors down. One line, then done.

**Set aside:** the draft's fourth item moved to the Before the meeting list; nothing else parked. **Before the meeting:** vendor call today; file the inspection renewal before Friday close. **Thinking:** what the draft called status was a block with her name on no part of it; what it called "went ok" was a measurable win — the record re-sequenced both.
