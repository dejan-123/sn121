# Worked example

## A worked week (invented — take the reasoning, never the facts)

Say the week looks like this: a print-shop operator, four years in. Job board: the Heidelberg service is a week overdue, two engineer callbacks unanswered; the wedding-stationery run he re-plated got "gallery-quality work" in the client's email; a paper-stock purchase order sits NOT STARTED on the manager's list. Log: he flagged the service Monday, chased Thursday, asked Friday about finishing-dept cross-training — and he was late back from break twice, the third week running. The draft says: "press update; stationery job; the lateness thing; cross-training; paper order."

## Item by item

1. "press update" — **I see:** service a week overdue, two callbacks unanswered, his Monday flag. **My call:** **Blocked** — engineer-side; give him a date and say when you will chase.
2. "stationery job" — **I see:** client email: "gallery-quality work". **My call:** **Recognition** — opens the meeting, in the client's words.
3. "the lateness thing" — **I see:** late back twice this week, and the two weeks before show the same. **My call:** **Coaching** — real, recurring, worth a question: what is pulling on the afternoons?
4. "cross-training" — **I see:** he asked Friday about finishing-dept time. **My call:** **Career** — he raised it; a real answer on when and what it builds toward.
5. "paper order" — **I see:** your PO row, NOT STARTED. **My call:** yours — off his draft, onto your own list.

**The draft missed:** the paper-stock PO — your row. **Figures:** jobs run 214 against 210 planned (+4); reprints 3 (target under 5).

# 1-on-1 brief: Marcus Lee x his manager — Friday

**Context:** Four years on press; a client emailed about his stationery run.

**1. Stationery run — Recognition, ~3 min**
The client wrote "gallery-quality work" — say the words, ask what he changed in the plating.

**2. Heidelberg service — Blocked, ~7 min**
A week overdue, two callbacks unanswered. Give him the date you are holding the engineer to and when you will call.

**3. Afternoons — Coaching, ~9 min**
Late back twice this week, same the two weeks before. Ask what is going on rather than reading him the log — there may be a real reason, and the pattern matters either way.

**4. Cross-training — Career, ~10 min**
He raised finishing-dept time. Say when it can start and what it leads to.

**5. Numbers — Status, ~3 min**
214 jobs against 210 planned; reprints at 3. One line and done.

**Set aside:** "paper order" moved to the Before the meeting list. **Before the meeting:** call the engineer today; approve the paper-stock PO before close. **Thinking:** memory gave the week five gentle labels; the record gave them sharper ones — praise worth quoting, a stall worth chasing, a pattern worth asking about, and the PO the draft forgot.
