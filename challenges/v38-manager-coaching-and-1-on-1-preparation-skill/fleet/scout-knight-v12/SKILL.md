name: scout-knight-v12
description: Turns a manager's draft agenda and the week's raw evidence into a printable 350-700 word 1-on-1 brief. Makes three passes — lay out the record, weigh each draft item against it, write the page — and prints the weighing before the brief. Returns a short note, not a brief, for old minutes and other non-prep material.
---

End-of-week agendas are written from memory, and memory keeps headlines while dropping grounds — the held-up job lands as "status", the rough patch lands as "coaching", the written compliment lands as "a shoutout". Three passes fix that, and the middle pass is printed on the reply itself: a sort the manager can audit is a sort the manager will follow.

## Pass one — lay out the record

Read the raw material before the draft: tickets, messages, reviews, figures, praise. Build the week's picture — what is open, who is waiting on whom, what the numbers say, what praise landed by name. Mark every row the manager owns — open, overdue, NOT STARTED, all count. Mark what is closed — it stays closed unless something newer directly contradicts it; the original complaint never reopens it.

## Pass two — weigh each item

Every draft item gets tested against the record and lands in one of six calls:

| Call | Earned when | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Written praise exists — a client's note, a peer's thanks, work named and thanked | First; quoted, the giver's words and any figure |
| Blocked | Another desk holds it — approval not given, dependency gone quiet, more than one pair of hands can lift | Early, while there is time to act; the manager leaves with a move. When the trail shows the hold-up reaching wider than the draft's label says — teams queuing behind it, a senior name already copied — call it what it is and climb it, whatever priority was written |
| Coaching | One behaviour on two or more separate dated occasions, or a goal the report chose | Middle; questions, never lectures |
| Career | The report themselves raised growth, a course, wider scope, review timing | Middle; a sincere question, a keepable promise |
| Status | Nothing to repair, praise or settle | Last; squeezed to a line or two |
| Parked | Another room's business — pay, HR, a dispute a different team owns, next quarter | Off the page; routed, a line saying why |

Rules the pass enforces:

- **An impression the record contradicts gets dropped.** "Disengaged" while the log shows early flags and picked-up work is not an agenda item — it is a wrong note, and the page says which signals contradict it. And when the report already found the cause and is tracking it, the coaching target is the step before it — flag changes sooner next time — anchored to the real figures, never a lecture on the symptom they already understand.
- **Complaints are read against causes.** Frustration aimed at a dependency another desk holds is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. Several messages about one problem stay one issue; Coaching wants separate dated occasions. An impression nothing underneath supports gets dropped, and a slip the report caught and owned themselves earns a quick word, not a coaching slot.
- **Recognition needs a quote.** A general sense of good work with no instance behind it earns no opener — when none exists, a line above item one says so instead of manufacturing warmth.
- **Private stays private.** Named only as "the personal matter", elaborated never, routed in a line.

## Pass three — write the page

1. **## What the record says** — the weighing, shown. One numbered entry per draft item, in the draft's own order: its wording quoted; **Record:** the name, the place, the date, and the row or figure behind it; **Call:** the verdict and the reason — "stands" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, merged into a sibling. Then **Missed:** — what the manager owes or the draft skipped — and **Arithmetic:** anything computed, written from its operands — "18 against 15 is three over plan" — a point is not a percent.
2. **# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]** — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line. Items in meeting order — **name — Call, ~N min** plus a sentence or two phrased as things to actually say and ask, carrying the names and figures the record surfaced. Then **Off the list** (parked and dropped, with reasons), **Before the meeting** (every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move — cleared before the report is asked for anything, above all a row an agenda item leans on), and **The reasoning** (a few lines on why the calls landed where they did).
3. **Invent nothing.** A thin packet — draft only, nothing behind it — still earns a page: the weighing names what is missing and the brief asks rather than asserts — what the manager cannot yet see, the question to put, and what a true answer would sound like.

A full worked week — record, weighing, finished page — lives in `references/pit-notes.md`. Same three passes, same calls; borrow the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When what arrives is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already had — whatever its label — the reply is one short note, under 180 words, with no weighing section:

- Name what it is: a record of a meeting already held — preparation looks forward, this looks back.
- Any open actions still alive inside it earn their follow-up — owner, action, date as recorded — in a line or two; no brief, no re-sort.
- Name what would make it preparation: this week's open items, fresh figures, anything raised since.
- Anything personal stays "the personal matter", nothing more.
