# The roast log

A draft agenda is memory's summary of the week, and memory flattens, softens and blurs in three ordinary ways — a held-up job comes out as "status", a written compliment as "went well", one bad afternoon as "a pattern". The log's discipline: cup the week before trusting the label, and leave the tasting notes on the page where the manager can read them.

## Cupping the week

Take the material at face value: tickets, messages, reviews, figures, praise. Anything open, overdue or NOT STARTED counts open; anything resolved stays closed unless a newer entry directly contradicts it — the original complaint never reopens it. A figure that lives only in the manager's head does not exist; anything computed is written from its operands, and a point is not a percent. Two notes accumulate: every open row the manager owns, and every compliment that landed by name.

## Grading each item

Each draft item, in the draft's own order, takes a grade:

- **Recognition** — written praise exists: a note, a thanks, work named and praised. Opens the meeting in the giver's own words, the figure attached.
- **Blocked** — another desk holds it: an approval not given, a dependency gone quiet, more requests than one pair of hands. The manager leaves carrying the move. If the log shows it wider than the draft admits — other teams queued, an exec already copied — the grade says so and the item climbs whatever priority was written.
- **Coaching** — one behaviour on two or more separate dated occasions, or a goal the report chose. Graded with questions, never lectures. An impression the entries contradict is dropped, the signals named. When the report already found the cause and is tracking it, the grade moves one step earlier — spot it sooner — anchored to real figures.
- **Career** — the report themselves raised growth, training, scope, review timing. One sincere question; promise only what is keepable.
- **Status** — nothing to repair, praise or settle. Last, squeezed to a line or two.
- **Parked** — its room is elsewhere: pay, HR, a dispute a different team owns, next quarter. Sent there with a line saying why. Personal matters stay private — "the personal matter", elaborated never.

Finer grading: a tone complaint tracing to another desk's hold-up is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. Repeated flags on one problem count once. A note with nothing under it is dropped. Coaching wants a pattern — separate dated occasions; one bad batch on a clean log, whoever cupped it, earns a passing word at most. A repair already owned moves the item's weight to the manager's side — the safeguard, the gate — not a second scolding. Recognition opens only on a quotable instance; when none exists, a line above item one admits it.

## The labelled bag

`## Reading each label` — the tasting notes, printed. One numbered entry per draft item: its wording quoted; **Found:** what the log holds — who said it and where it was logged, when, the row or figure beside it; **Call:** the grade and the reason — "stands" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, merged into a sibling. Then **Missed:** what the manager owes or the draft forgot, and **Worked out:** arithmetic from its operands — "97.1 against a 96 floor is +1.1 points".

`# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]` — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line — report, tenure, the week in a phrase. Items in meeting order, `**name — Call, ~N min**`, a sentence or two of things to say and ask, real numbers and names inside. Then **Off the list** (parked or dropped, with reasons), **Before the meeting** (every open row the manager owns, each a concrete move — done before the report hears a single ask, most of all a row an item stands on), and **The reasoning** (a few lines on why the grades landed).

Invent nothing. A thin packet still earns a page — the notes name what never arrived, the brief asks rather than asserts: what cannot be seen, the question to put, what to listen for. A thin week makes a thin brief — nothing added for balance, no praise without a quotable entry; honesty outranks length. A full batch through the log sits in `references/roast-notes.md`. The reasoning transfers; the facts do not.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When what arrives is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already had — whatever its label — the reply is one short note under 180 words, with no tasting notes:

- Say it straight: this looks backward at a meeting, not forward to one — name the document and the meeting it records.
- Where open actions survive inside, write the follow-up — owner, action, due date as given — in a line or two; no brief, no re-sort of old items.
- Preparation wants the present week — open items, current figures, flags raised since.
- Personal material stays "the personal matter", nothing more.
