# Site drills — full boards on invented sites

Four invented boards, marked the way the board should be
marked. The names, figures and products are invented; the
marks are the part to carry.

## Drill 1 — a normal board with a mis-labelled task

Board: report is "Ines, senior backend engineer". Manager's
draft: (a) "Ines's friction with QA — she pushed back on
their release block in the team channel", (b) "why is the
reporting-service split behind", (c) "acknowledge the uptime
run". The log: the QA lead posted a public thank-you naming
Ines for catching a data-loss edge case the week before; the
channel exchange shows Ines objecting — bluntly but on the
merits — to shipping a build that failed its own smoke
tests; the split is 5 of 12 services done, waiting on a DNS
change requested 12 days ago; uptime hit 99.98% for the
month, up from 99.91%.

Marks:

- Task (a): **Re-drawn**. One substantive objection to a build
  that failed its own checks — labelled as friction, marked
  as a one-time note with one narrow question. Site note:
  "The QA exchange was on the merits — they were already
  thanking you for the data-loss catch. One thing to keep in
  view: that disagreement ran in the team channel rather
  than on the ticket. Does moving it there cost you
  anything?" The objection stands; the channel takes one
  question and no more.
- Task (b): **Re-drawn — another crew's job.** 5 of 12 done,
  and the stall is a 12-day-old DNS change with the
  infrastructure team, not Ines's pace. Site note: "The
  split is waiting on infrastructure's DNS change — twelve
  days outstanding. I'll chase them today and get a date;
  who should I escalate to if it slips again?"
- Task (c): **Signed off first.** Two attributable credits —
  the QA lead's public thanks and the uptime figure (99.91
  → 99.98%). Open with them before any process task; credit
  marks first.
- Every written task gets a site line and a mark; nothing
  written disappears.

Board: `# Site board: Ines, senior backend engineer` —
picture line ("a strong week with one task waiting on another
crew"), figures shown as work, then work order with all three
written tasks, the entry behind each, and each mark — then
site notes, manager's own tasks, subbed-out list, foreman's
note.

## Drill 2 — a thin board marked honestly

Board: report is "Felix, staff engineer". Manager's note:
"prep my weekly with Felix — want to discuss his route to
senior staff, and whether he can take over the payments
gateway." No action log, no figures, no channel excerpts.
One calendar item: Felix asked in passing about remote-work
allowance.

Thin is not invalid: the board aims at the coming meeting, so
the site still lists it — marked task by task. Only the
wrong kind of board (drill 3) is handed back whole. The
reply opens `# Site board: Felix, staff engineer` — never a
hand-back preface.

Marks:

- Route to senior staff: **Signed off — split by crew.**
  Felix's direction and the manager's readiness question
  belong in this meeting, with real time: "You asked about
  the next step — here's the gap evidence I see and what I'd
  need documented; what scope would you take to build it?"
  The allowance question is **Subbed out**: allowances sit
  with the people team — the board names the destination
  (the people partner) and the owner, one line, no site
  notes.
- Readiness for the gateway: **Held**. The board has no
  delivery record to weigh. It marks as the honest question,
  not an assessment invented for the slot: "I don't have
  your recent delivery record in front of me — walk me
  through the last few weeks: what shipped, what's stuck,
  what you'd take on."
- Nothing else is fabricated to fill the board. A thin
  board of honest questions beats a padded one; the board
  head logs what the packet lacks and the manager's own
  tasks list names what to pull.

## Drill 3 — a board that is not this week's site

Board: "Here are the minutes from this week's 1-on-1 with
Sam — please prep next week's agenda from them." The minutes
list three actions: Sam to send the vendor comparison (due
Friday), the manager to confirm the travel approval, a
follow-up on the documentation push.

Mark: **whole board handed back — nothing logged.** The
reply, under 150 words, titled `# Not this week's site`:

Minutes of a meeting already held — they record what was
agreed, not what has moved since. The document this supports
is a follow-up on the open actions, not a board: Sam's
vendor comparison (due Friday), your travel approval, the
documentation follow-up — owners and dates exactly as
written. For a weekly board, send this week's action log,
any figures, and whatever Sam raised or you want addressed.
Nothing in the material is logged after that meeting, so a
board would replay old tasks or invent new ones.

Any personal matter in the minutes stays private — not
repeated, not summarized, not hinted at.

## Drill 4 — the queue that is the manager's own task

Board: report is "Ruth, senior platform engineer". Manager's
draft: (a) "Ruth needs to clear her design-doc queue — seven
are waiting on her and two teams are blocked"; (b) "Ruth
seemed quiet in the kickoff". The log: the seven docs are
assigned because the process names her sole approver for
cross-team changes; she shipped two features this sprint and
raised the queue herself in the last retro, proposing a
rotation; "quiet" appears once, in the manager's note.

Marks:

- Task (a): **Re-drawn — the crew that can do it is the
  manager's.** A sole-approver rule stalling two teams is a
  capacity matter the manager owns, not a task to work
  faster. It marks early as the manager's own decision:
  "Your rotation proposal is the right starting point — I
  want to approve it this week and take it to the team
  leads. What load should each reviewer hold so nothing
  queues behind one person again?"
- Task (b): **Struck.** One manager observation with no
  entries behind it; the sprint shows two shipped features
  and a raised process fix. The board logs its absence
  plainly rather than inventing an engagement segment.
- The manager's own open item — acting on Ruth's retro
  proposal — is listed under manager's own tasks as the
  manager's commitment, with whom and when.

## What every drill shares

- The log decides; the written label is only the filing.
- Attributable credit marks first — a named person, a
  concrete result.
- A justified matter raised in the wrong channel keeps its
  substance and takes exactly one channel question.
- What another crew does is subbed out to that crew.
- One occurrence is a single entry, never a pattern.
- Missing records mark as `Held` — the honest question in
  the slot — never covered over.
- Other-crew tasks get a destination and an owner, never
  site notes.
- Every figure shows its own working; every written task
  gets a site line and a mark.
- A thin board still gets marked. The hand-back is for the
  wrong kind of board only — it stays under 150 words and
  carries no site structure.
