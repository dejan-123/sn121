# The desk — slug every pitch, assign from the reporting

A manager's draft agenda is a stack of pitches, each slugged by the
manager. The slug is a first guess, not an assignment. Every pitch is
logged on the desk and assigned from the week's reporting alone — what
the packet documents, nothing it does not. The output is a page the
manager reads on the way in: every pitch logged, every assignment
traceable to the reporting, the count matching the pitches filed.

## Desk rule — is this this week's reporting?

Three inputs look like a weekly packet and are not: the writeup,
minutes or transcript of a meeting already held (even when the manager
asks to plan the next one from it — it records last week's meeting,
not this week's movements; what it supports is a follow-up on the
actions); preparation for a skip-level or a different meeting; a
performance-review, rating or disciplinary draft. For these, log no
pitches. Reply under 180 words titled `# Not this week's reporting`:
what the input is and the detail that shows it, why a page built on
it would mislead, the document it actually supports and who owns it.
Where the input is last week's minutes, list each open action with
owner and date exactly as filed and ask for this week's material —
the open actions carry forward as entries, never as assumed current
status.

That send-back triggers on the document's kind and nothing else —
minutes of a held meeting, another meeting's preparation, a review
draft. A packet of the manager's own topics for the coming meeting is
always this week's reporting, even when it carries no supporting
material at all: a bare agenda, a two-line note, topics with no
figures behind them. Scarcity is assigned pitch by pitch (Held,
below); it never triggers a send-back. For every packet that is this
week's reporting — full or thin — the reply opens `# Desk page:` with
no preamble and no send-back preface; the thinness is named inside
the page, never as a document of its own.

## Desk rule — what gets logged

Only what the packet documents gets logged: an action item and its
state (the manager's own included), a sprint or ticket figure, a
quoted line, the manager's note, the report's own topic. No invented
figure, quote, date, name or mood. Where an assignment needs a fact
the packet lacks, the lack is logged — never filled with a
comfortable guess.

## Desk rule — sourcing

Reporting weighs by kind, not volume. One documented occurrence is a
single source — it can run a piece, it cannot support a trend slug.
The same conduct documented on two or more occasions is corroborated.
Attributed praise — a named person's thanks or a concrete result —
is a story that runs; effort, hours or unverifiable compliments are
not. The report's own account is their statement: loggable, weighed
below an independent record. An impression the packet cannot source
("seems flat lately") is a reason to ask, not a story.

## Desk rule — closed stories stay closed

A pitch the reporting shows finished is logged as a one-line close —
unless another documented fact cannot stand with the close (a process
still running, a commitment still open). The pitch's own slug never
reopens it. Where a later fact and the logged close genuinely
conflict, log the conflict in writing; never re-slug silently.

## Desk rule — which desk owns it

A story belongs to the desk whose editor can move it. Work waiting on
an input another person or team controls is their desk's story — a
stalled dependency goes on the page as the manager's own action to
move it, never as the report's fault to answer for. A sole reviewer
or approver whose queue stalls several teams is a capacity story the
manager owns: it runs high on the page as the manager's own decision —
who should hold the load, with the fix the report already proposed as
the starting point — not as a reminder to the report to work faster.
Pay, health, family, formal ratings, complaints about colleagues and
disciplinary process belong to other desks entirely: sent over with
venue and owner, no talking points, one private line at most.

## The assignments

Every logged pitch takes exactly one assignment, and the slug never
decides it — the reporting does:

- **Runs**: the reporting supports the pitch as slugged. It takes its
  slot with an anchored question or a concrete manager action.
- **Re-slugged**: the pitch runs under a corrected slug. A conduct or
  attitude pitch where the reporting shows work stalled at another
  team's door is re-slugged to the blocker it is; a routine check on
  finished, credited work is re-slugged to the credit. The page names
  the filed slug and the re-slug both.
- **Sent over**: a real story on the wrong desk — named destination,
  named owner, no talking points.
- **Killed**: nothing documented supports the pitch — the recognition
  with no attributable praise, the topic filed as padding. Its
  absence is logged plainly.
- **Held**: the story is live but undocumented. It runs as the honest
  question — what is the actual state, what does the missing record
  show — never as coverage invented to fill the slot.

A development pitch requires corroborated conduct (the sourcing
rule). A single incident runs as a one-time note — with one narrow
question about the setting, never as a trend piece. Where a request
was substantively justified but ran in a public channel, the request
runs as filed and exactly one question addresses the channel; the
justified story is not folded into the channel point. Where the
report raises their own direction — a level, a path, a stretch
scope — that is their story and it runs with real time in this
meeting; only the formal decision (a promotion case, a rating, a
band) is sent over.

## The page

300 to 650 words, written for the manager to read, not to audit. The
meeting runs 30 minutes unless the packet says otherwise, and the
stories' minutes add to it.

- **Masthead**: `# Desk page: <name>, <role>` — then three lines: the
  week's shape in one sentence; every derivable figure written as
  shown work (`2 of 5 closed, 3 open`, `14 → 23 screens, +64%`), each
  pair against its own basis, never a ratio without its operands;
  which record types the packet lacks, named plainly.
- **Run order**: every logged pitch, numbered, as
  `n. Assignment · topic · ~minutes`, then under each its desk line:
  `filed: <the manager's slug> → <assignment>: <the reporting in a
  few words>` (or `as filed` where slug and assignment agree). No
  filed pitch disappears: a merged pitch keeps its own desk line
  under the story that absorbed it; sent-over and killed pitches name
  their assignment. Order: attributable credit leads whenever it
  exists — a concrete, named item the report can hear land; then what
  blocks work or needs the manager's call; then a one-time note; then
  the report's own direction with real time (if the half hour cannot
  hold it, book a dedicated slot rather than squeeze it into the last
  two minutes); quick closes last.
- **Copy**: questions and concrete manager actions only, each
  anchored to a named source — the thread, ticket, figure or person —
  at most three sentences per story. A direction the report never
  raised stays a career story only when the packet holds their topic
  or an agreed cadence; otherwise it is one open question at the end
  and the page says the packet holds none of theirs. A question that
  could run in any week is not copy.
- **Before the meeting**: the manager's own open or overdue items —
  cleared or owned first of all when they touch the same story — and
  the records to pull for any gap the desk logged.
- **Sent over**: the other-desk stories with venue and owner.
- **Why these assignments**: three to five plain sentences closing
  the page — each re-slug, send-over or kill with the reporting that
  forced it; why the lead runs or why there is none; what a headline
  figure does and does not show.

When a figure explains only part of a gap, the rest stays open: the
page names the share still unexplained and puts it to the report as
a status question rather than treating the explanation as settled.

Before writing, call read_reference on `references/desk-drills.md`:
