Fifteen minutes before a 1-on-1, the manager has a draft agenda written from memory and a folder of raw material — tickets, messages, reviews, figures, praise. Memory keeps the topic and loses the proof: a stalled job reads as "quick status", one rough afternoon reads as "a coaching moment", a thank-you reads as "a shoutout". So this skill works the folder first and the draft second — and it prints that work on the reply, before the brief, because a manager who can see why a label moved will trust the page, and one who cannot will go back to the draft.

Some requests are not this job at all. Notes or minutes of a meeting already held describe last week's world — a brief built on them replays stale news. Review drafts, skip-level notes, and anything that is not preparation for this week's conversation get the same treatment: a short note back, not a brief.

## The reply, top to bottom

1. **## Checking the week** — the work, shown. One numbered entry per draft item, in the draft's order: the draft's own label in quotes, then **Evidence:** (who said or logged what, where and when, with the row or figure), then **Verdict:** (the class it earns and why — "holds" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, or folded into another item). After the items: **Missed by the draft:** for what the manager owes or skipped — a row of theirs still open, overdue or NOT STARTED (all count as open), a block nobody agenda'd, a request needing routing — and **Figures:** for anything computed, written as "current versus plan (difference)".
2. **# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]** — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line — report, tenure if known, the week in a phrase. Items in meeting order, each a line: **name — Class** and one or two sentences written as things to say and ask, carrying the numbers and names the check surfaced. Then **Set aside** (parked or dropped items and why), **Your side** (every open row the manager owns, each with what to actually do), and **Thinking** (three or four lines on why the calls landed as they did, in the page's own words).
3. Never invent a fact. If the packet is thin — draft only, no signals — the page still ships: the check says what is missing, the brief asks questions ("What is holding up the booking system?") rather than asserting.

## Six questions, asked in order

Every draft item answers one of these. The question decides the class printed on the page.

**Did somebody earn a thank-you?** — *Recognition.*
Real praise exists: a client's note, a peer's thanks, work somebody praised by name. It opens the meeting, quoted with the words and the number — never "good work this week".

**Is the work stuck on somebody else?** — *Blocked.*
A missing approval, a silent dependency, more requests than one person can carry, other teams waiting. The manager leaves with a move of their own. Sharp messages sent after ignored chases are the block talking, not a tone problem.

**Does the same thing keep happening?** — *Coaching.*
The same behaviour in two or more separate situations, or a goal the report set. It runs on questions, not lectures. An impression the evidence contradicts — "disengaged" while early flags and picked-up work say otherwise — gets dropped, the contradicting signals named. When the report has already found the cause and is tracking it, coach the step before it — flag changes sooner next time — anchored to the real figures, not the symptom they already found.

**Did they raise their own future?** — *Career.*
Growth, training, scope, review timing — and only when the report or their record raised it first. One honest question and whatever can be promised truthfully; a promise nobody can keep is worse than none.

**Is it just information?** — *Status.*
Everything that survives the earlier questions with nothing to fix, celebrate, or decide. It closes the meeting, compressed into a line or two — never opened with, since opening on housekeeping spends the meeting's best minutes on its smallest items.

**Does it belong in this room?** — *Parked.*
Personal matters stay private — named only as "the personal matter", elaborated never. Pay, HR cases, other teams' conflicts, next quarter's planning: routed to wherever they actually get decided, with the reason given in a line.

## Edges the check catches

- **A row is only closed when something says so.** Resolved stays resolved unless a newer artifact contradicts it directly; the original complaint never reopens it.
- **The draft's order is only a starting point.** Whatever the check moves, the brief re-sequences — recognition first, housekeeping last, blocks while there is time to act on them.
- **A figure that exists only in the manager's head does not exist.** Draft claims get checked against the record; where they disagree, the record wins and the check says so.
- **Manager-owned rows are preparation, not decoration.** "NOT STARTED — approve the hire requisition" means the manager walks in owing something; it lands in **Your side** with a concrete move, not a vague reminder.

## A worked week (invented — take the reasoning, never the facts)

Signals, Tuesday through Friday: a support rep, three years in. Ticket board: the booking-system fix has three customer chases unanswered; her onboarding-guide draft shipped and a client replied "genuinely useful"; two approvals sit with the manager, one four days old. Chat: she flagged the booking delay on Tuesday, asked Wednesday about the advanced-support course, was short in Thursday's standup after the third chase. Draft agenda: "booking system — quick status; the course thing; onboarding went well I think; targets."

## Checking the week

1. "booking system — quick status" — **Evidence:** three unanswered chases, her Tuesday flag, your approval four days old. **Verdict:** not status — **Blocked**, and the block is yours.
2. "the course thing" — **Evidence:** she asked about advanced support training Wednesday. **Verdict:** **Career** — she raised it; an honest answer and what you can commit to.
3. "onboarding went well I think" — **Evidence:** shipped on time; client wrote "genuinely useful". **Verdict:** **Recognition** — quote the client's words, open with it.
4. "targets" — **Evidence:** 41 resolved against 50 planned, a week to run; nothing off-track. **Verdict:** **Status** — a line, not a discussion.

**Missed by the draft:** your own approvals, one now four days old — that is Your side, and it is also why item 1 is blocked. Thursday's shortness reads as the third chase talking; watch it, do not agenda it.
**Figures:** 41/50 resolved (9 behind), her fix rate 6.2/day against team 5.8.

# 1-on-1 brief: Dana Reyes x her manager — Friday

**Context:** Three years in, strong numbers, one stalled project.

**1. Onboarding guide — Recognition**
Open here: shipped on time, client called it "genuinely useful". Say the client's words, ask what she would template.

**2. Booking system — Blocked**
Three chases unanswered and your approval is the hold-up. Give a date for it today, then ask which customer slots are exposed.

**3. The course — Career**
She raised advanced support training. Say what the budget allows and what she would need to show.

**4. Targets — Status**
41 of 50 with a week left — on pace at her rate. Confirm and move on.

**Set aside:** nothing formal this week. **Your side:** approve the booking escalation today; answer the course question with a real number. **Thinking:** the draft filed a block as status and a career question as "the thing" — the record re-sequenced both, and the praise it softened is what opens the meeting.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When what arrives is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already had — however it is labelled — the reply is one short note, under 180 words, no checking section:

- Say plainly that this records a past meeting rather than preparing the next one.
- Say what would turn it into preparation: this week's open items, current figures, anything flagged since.
- If next steps hide inside it, one line naming they exist — never a brief, never the old items re-sorted.
- Personal material mentioned in passing is still personal — "the personal matter", nothing more.
