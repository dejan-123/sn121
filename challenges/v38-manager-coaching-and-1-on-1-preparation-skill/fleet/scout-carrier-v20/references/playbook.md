# Field notes — read once, before composing anything

Every draft line on the manager's agenda earns exactly one label. Ask these questions in order; the first honest "yes" decides.

## First question — is this even next week's material?

A draft agenda counts as prep material whether or not records came with it — a draft standing alone is still the coming week, and it takes the thin-packet handling below rather than a refusal. What does not count: minutes, notes or a transcript of a meeting already held; a review drafted for someone else; a request aimed at a different listener. Any of those gets `# Stale material` — under 180 words, nothing above or below it, no look-over attached. The note states what the material actually is and why it cannot prepare the next conversation, names what could (the current week's open items, fresh figures, anything raised since), allows one line that next steps exist in the record — unlisted, unsorted — and refers to anything personal only as "the personal matter". The rest of this card never runs on stale input; it exists to keep a sheet from being written off last week's records.

## Six questions, in this order

**Q1 — Toast.** Does anything quotable thank the report — a client, a peer, work somebody named out loud? If yes, that opens the meeting, in the words and figure it arrived in. If nothing quotable exists, the sheet starts on the first real item and the `No praise opener:` line says what would have supported one. Invented warmth costs more than it buys.

**Q2 — On hold.** Is the work waiting on someone else — an unsigned approval, a silent dependency, a queue too big for one person, colleagues stuck behind it? The manager leaves carrying a move of their own. A sharp message after ignored chases is the hold talking, not the person's character. When the blast radius is wider than the draft admits — several teams queueing on one person, a senior leader already in the thread — the sheet says so plainly and moves the item up regardless of the rank the draft gave it.

**Q3 — Repeat.** Same behaviour on two or more occasions, or a goal the report chose themselves? Questions do the work, never lectures. When the evidence contradicts the manager's impression — "disengaged" while flags and picked-up work say otherwise — the label drops: name what was filed, name the contradicting signals, let the item become whatever is actually holding it. When the report already tracks the cause, coach one step earlier — flag it sooner — pinned to real numbers, never to the symptom they named.

**Q4 — Trajectory.** Did the report raise their own future — training, scope, a next role or career step, review timing? Only when they or their record raised it. One honest question plus a promise the manager can keep.

**Q5 — Memo.** Nothing to fix, celebrate or decide? It closes the meeting in a line or two, never opens it.

**Q6 — Out of scope.** Is it another room's business — personal matters (named "the personal matter" and nothing more), pay, HR cases, other teams' conflicts, next quarter's business? It leaves the numbered items and sits under `Moved out`, with the venue that decides it named and the reason in a line. When a moved item touches a kept one, the link gets said — pay sitting behind a growth ask reads "the comp conversation, where the progression ask also lands". Moving it out never reads as ignoring it.

Two standing rules sit outside the order: rows the manager owns — open, overdue, NOT STARTED — land in `Yours to clear` with a concrete move each; and every number on the page is taken or derived, never estimated — derived figures are written "current versus plan (difference)".

## How the reply is built

**Opening — `## Going over the draft`.** Numbered, one entry per draft line, same order. Each entry is two or three sentences of ordinary prose: the manager's own words in quotes, what the week actually contains for that line (who logged or said it, when, which figure), then the label and the why said the way a colleague would. Never `Evidence:`/`Verdict:` fields — a colleague's reading, not a form.

**After the entries.** `Skipped by the draft:` — whatever the manager owes or failed to see, their own open rows first. `Arithmetic:` — every derived figure as "current versus plan (difference)".

**The deliverable — `# 1-on-1 — [Report] x [Manager] — [date]`.** 350–700 words, addressed to the manager as "you"; anything the report said stays in quotes. `Context:` one line. Then items in conversation order — `**name — Label**`, one or two sentences of say-this-ask-this with real names and figures woven in. Repeat and Trajectory items each close on a question naming a thread, a figure or the report's own words — a question that fits any report's sheet carries no signal. Tail sections: `Moved out` — what left the room, its venue, any kept item it touches; `Yours to clear` — the manager's open rows, a move each; `The calls behind it` — plain sentences, enough for the manager to defend the sheet if challenged.

**Thin packet.** A draft with nothing behind it still ships: the look-over lists `Missing anchors:` — which draft lines carry no evidence and what each would need; every sheet item becomes a question — what is unknown, what to ask, what to listen for; `Yours to clear` names each input to pull tied to its item. No opener, no coaching, nothing invented, shorter never padded — and a pay or personal item still leaves the room, venue named, no talking points.

## An invented week, end to end

Tomas Bekele, inbound dock lead, fourteen months in. The draft reads: "returns backlog — update", "picker accuracy", "forklift cert", "shift differential ask". Records: returns queue 48 to 12 in a week after the vendor agreed pre-sorted pallets — his Tuesday call got it moving; picker mis-scans logged on two nights, both traced to the same swapped label roll he flagged and corrected; forklift certification mentioned by him in a thread last month; the differential ask attached to "want to stay on this crew".

**Going over the draft:**

1. "returns backlog — update" — the queue went 48 to 12 after his call pushed the vendor onto pre-sorted pallets, not a routine line. That is **Toast** in substance — the win is his — and it opens.
2. "picker accuracy" — two mis-scan nights, both traced to the swapped label roll he flagged and fixed himself. **Repeat** in shape but the record says the cause is tracked — so it coaches one step earlier, on the flag coming sooner.
3. "forklift cert" — he raised it in the thread last month. **Trajectory**: his ask, an honest answer, a promise that can be dated.
4. "shift differential ask" — pay, attached to staying on this crew. **Out of scope**: the comp review is the venue, and it is the same conversation as the certification ask.

**Skipped by the draft:** your safety walkthrough sign-off, eleven days overdue — lands in Yours to clear. **Arithmetic:** picks 312/hr against a 340 target (28 under); error rate 0.8% against the 1.5% ceiling (0.7 inside).

# 1-on-1 — Tomas Bekele x his manager — Thursday

Context: Fourteen months on the inbound dock, a vendor problem he cleared himself, and a pay ask that belongs in another room.

**1. Returns backlog — Toast**
Open on his number: 48 down to 12 since his Tuesday call moved the vendor. Say it as his, because it was — then the honest follow: does he want to own the vendor relationship now that he has proved it moves?

**2. Picker accuracy — Repeat**
Two nights of mis-scans, one swapped label roll, and he caught it before anyone logged it twice. The pattern question sits earlier than the error: what would make a wrong roll surface on day one instead of night two? His answer probably names the intake check — let him propose it.

**3. Forklift cert — Trajectory**
He asked. Give the real picture — two paid days, a practical, a supervisor sign-off — and ask where he wants the ticket to take him, because the differential ask says the crew matters to him. Promise the sign-off you can date.

The draft holds no fourth work line, so the week's figures ride inside Context and item 2 — 312 against 340 with errors 0.7 inside ceiling; worth a quiet thank-you, never an opening.

Moved out: the shift differential — comp review, where the certification ask lands alongside it, not ignored, just the right room.
Yours to clear: sign off the safety walkthrough today; it has been eleven days and it is the gate on his crew expansion.
The calls behind it: the draft filed his vendor win as "update" and a pay question as agenda business — the record re-sequenced both, and the coaching on accuracy lands one step earlier than the errors he already fixed.
