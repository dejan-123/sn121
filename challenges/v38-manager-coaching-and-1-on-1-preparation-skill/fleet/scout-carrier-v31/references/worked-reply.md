# One invented week answered in this voice — borrow the shape, never the facts

Everyone below is made up. What transfers is the form: a testing that walks the draft in order, items phrased like a colleague who did the homework, questions written as words the manager can say aloud. In a live answer the facts all come from the packet supplied.

## The invented packet

Tomas Reyes leads the night shift at a wholesale bakery supplying forty cafés, fourteen months in. The manager's draft: "recognition for the week" / "Status: oven 3 thermostat" / "Coaching: blunt with the temp packers" / "Status: delivery-route timing" / "he asked about the production-planner opening" / "personal — his evening class". The week: the log shows "publish the holiday bake schedule (Tomas, DONE Monday)" and "approve the flour contract renewal (Manager, OVERDUE)". Figures: 12,300 units baked on a 13,000 quota. Threads — Wednesday, the sales director in #orders: "Tomas's crew caught the mislabelled batch before a single crate shipped; that's the save of the quarter." Friday, Tomas in #maintenance: "oven 3 thermostat drifts again; engineer visit rebooked to next week — I've staggered its load across ovens 1 and 4 meanwhile." The shift notes record one curt exchange with a temp packer on Thursday, after two trays went out unsealed. Last month Tomas asked whether the production-planner role was open to shift leads. His evening class timetable is private; he has already told HR what he needs.

## The answer

## Testing the draft

A draft, a log, one figure pair and the week's threads arrived for a session still to come — this is prep.

1. "recognition for the week" — vague until Wednesday: the sales director posted publicly that his crew caught the mislabelled batch before any crate shipped, calling it the save of the quarter. Commend on those words; it leads.
2. "Status: oven 3 thermostat" — the engineer rebooked to next week and he has already staggered the load across ovens 1 and 4. Not a status question: a Bind on the engineer, his stagger is the remedy to back, the move a confirmed visit.
3. "Coaching: blunt with the temp packers" — one dated exchange Thursday, after two trays went out unsealed. A Spark: recount it, ask once, no verdict.
4. "Status: delivery-route timing" — 12,300 units on a 13,000 quota and the route question sits inside the gap, yet nothing says who owns the retiming or whether it repeats. Thin evidence on a real question: a Buzz pinned to the 700-unit shortfall.
5. "he asked about the production-planner opening" — his own ask last month. Orbit; the mislabel catch is the signal to set beside what the planner role wants.
6. "personal — his evening class" — the timetable is private and he has already told HR what he needs. Other table: the scheduling process, starting with confirming dates at HR; nothing gets written on it in this room.

Not drafted: the overdue flour-contract approval is the manager's own row. Figures run: 12,300 on a 13,000 quota leaves 700 short, and the route question lives inside that gap.

# Tomas Reyes with his manager — the save of the quarter and the drifting oven

**Where it lands.** The crew baked 12,300 units on a 13,000 quota (700 short), and Wednesday gave the week its headline: the mislabelled batch caught before a single crate shipped — the sales director called it the save of the quarter in public. Oven 3's thermostat drifts on a rebooked engineer — his stagger is already holding — and the flour-contract approval waiting on you is overdue.

**Your calls.** Approve the flour contract renewal — overdue, and the holiday bake schedule he just published prices off it. Bring it or set the day.

## Order of play

**1. The mislabel catch — Commend**
Open on the sales director's public words — a save that never shipped is invisible by design, so having it named in the open is the recognition itself. Then ask how his crew spotted it; a catch that clean is a checking method worth writing down before it fades.

**2. Oven 3 — Bind**
The engineer is rebooked to next week and his stagger across ovens 1 and 4 is holding meanwhile — the workaround is his, what remains is yours. Pin the visit to a confirmed date and tell him plainly the stagger is approved; he should not carry the gap quietly.

**3. Route timing — Buzz**
700 short and the delivery timing sits unowned inside it — one question: "where does the route actually lose its time, and whose desk catches the retiming?" If the same squeeze shows next week it earns its own item.

**4. Thursday — Spark**
A curt word after two trays went out unsealed — one dated moment in a week he otherwise ran clean. One light question covers it: "have the trays gone out sealed since Thursday?" A single moment never earns a verdict.

**5. Production planner — Orbit**
- "The mislabel catch is the clearest thing on your record this quarter — which parts of the planner role does it already prove, and which does it leave open?"
- "You asked about the opening yourself — where would you want it to take you?"

## Off the sheet

His evening class — the timetable is private and HR already has what they need; the step is confirming dates so the rota can plan around it. Nothing on it is drafted here.

**Why it reads this way.** The recognition stays, anchored — the sales director named the save in public. The oven is a hold on an outside engineer, not a status, and his stagger is the remedy to back. "Blunt" is one dated instance after two unsealed trays, so a Spark rather than a Loop. The route gap is live but unowned, so it stays a question until the record says more. The planner ask is his and stands on the week's clearest signal. The class timetable belongs to HR, not this room.
