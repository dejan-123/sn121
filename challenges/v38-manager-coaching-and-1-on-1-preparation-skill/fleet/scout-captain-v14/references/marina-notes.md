# Worked example

## A week through the manual (invented — take the reasoning, never the facts)

A marina dockhand, three years in. Board: the crane inspection is a week overdue, two surveyor callbacks unanswered; the winter cover he re-stitched earned "saved us a replacement" in the harbourmaster's note; a fuel-dock safety audit sits NOT STARTED on the manager's list. Log: he flagged the crane Monday, chased Thursday, asked Friday about the rigging certificate — and he was late back from lunch twice, third week running. The draft says: "crane thing; winter covers; the lates; certificate; fuel dock."

## The honest sort

1. "crane thing" — **Says:** inspection seven days overdue, two callbacks unanswered, his Monday flag. **So:** **Blocked** — surveyor-side; hand him the date and name your next chase.
2. "winter covers" — **Says:** harbourmaster's note: "saved us a replacement". **So:** **Recognition** — opens the meeting, in the harbourmaster's words.
3. "the lates" — **Says:** late back twice this week, same the two weeks before. **So:** **Coaching** — a real pattern — worth asking what is weighing on the afternoons
4. "certificate" — **Says:** he asked Friday about the rigging course. **So:** **Career** — he raised it; a straight answer on cost and where it points.
5. "fuel dock" — **Says:** your audit row, NOT STARTED. **So:** yours — off his draft, onto your own list.

**Missed:** the fuel-dock audit — your row. **The sums:** boats serviced 38 against 40 planned (2 short, both weather).

# 1-on-1 brief: Tom Weaver x his manager — Friday

**Context:** Three years on the docks; the harbourmaster named his cover work.

**1. Winter covers (Recognition, ~4 min)**
The harbourmaster wrote "saved us a replacement" — say the words, ask what he changed in the stitching.

**2. Crane (Blocked, ~8 min)**
A week overdue, two callbacks unanswered. Tell him the date the surveyor is being held to and when the call goes out.

**3. The lates (Coaching, ~10 min)**
Late back twice this week, same the two weeks before. Ask what is behind it before quoting the log — there may be a genuine reason, and three straight weeks deserves an answer either way.

**4. Certificate (Career, ~8 min)**
He asked about the rigging course Friday. A straight answer on cost and what it opens — or an honest "you will know by Tuesday".

**5. Numbers (Status, ~2 min)**
Serviced 38 against 40 (2 short, both weather) — read aloud, then closed.

**Off the page:** "fuel dock" moved off the draft. **Before you sit:** call the surveyor today; start the fuel-dock audit before close — the crane item the meeting leans on sits on your side. **Why this order:** the draft filed a surveyor wait as a "thing" and a written compliment as "covers" — the record re-labelled both — and the audit it skipped is the one sitting longest.
