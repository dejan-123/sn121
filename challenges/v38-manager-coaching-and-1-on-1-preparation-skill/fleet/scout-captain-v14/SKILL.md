# Field manual: the weekly brief

A draft agenda is a memory document, written at the end of a long week. It keeps the headlines and loses the grounds: a held-up job lands as "status", a written compliment lands as "went well", one rough afternoon lands as "a pattern". The record knows better, so the manual's order is fixed — record first, draft second, and the weighing printed where the manager can see it. Working shown is working trusted.

## First chapter — the record

Read the material literally — tickets, messages, reviews, figures, praise. Rows sitting open, overdue or marked NOT STARTED are open rows; a resolved row stays closed unless a newer artifact contradicts it directly. A number living only in the manager's head does not exist; every computed figure is written from its operands, a point never a percent. While reading, gather two things: each open row the manager owns, and every piece of praise that landed by name.

## Second chapter — the weighing

Each draft item, in the draft's own order, takes one of six calls:

- **Recognition** — quotable praise exists. The meeting opens on it, in the giver's own words, the figure attached.
- **Blocked** — the hold sits at another desk: an approval withheld, a dependency gone quiet. The manager leaves carrying the move; and when the trail shows the hold wider than the draft admits — other teams queued, an exec already copied — say so, and it climbs whatever priority was written.
- **Coaching** — a behaviour on separate dated occasions, or a goal the report raised. Questions, never lectures; where the report already found the cause and tracks it, coach the step before — spot it sooner — on the real figures.
- **Career** — growth, training, scope or review timing raised by the report themselves. One sincere question, one keepable promise.
- **Status** — nothing to repair, praise or settle. Last, a line or two only.
- **Parked** — business for a different room: pay, HR, a dispute another team owns, next quarter. Sent on with a reason; personal matters stay "the personal matter", elaborated never.

The finer calls: frustration aimed at another desk's hold-up is the hold-up talking — Blocked, not Coaching. Several messages about one problem are one issue. An impression nothing supports is dropped, signals named. Coaching wants a pattern — one rough moment on a clean record, whoever spotted it, earns a passing word at most. A repair the report already owns shifts the item's weight to the manager's side — the safeguard, the gate — not a second telling-off. Recognition opens only on a quotable instance; without one, a line above the first item admits it.

## Third chapter — the reply

`## The honest sort` comes first — one numbered entry per draft item: its wording quoted; **Says:** what the record shows — name, place, date, row or figure; **So:** the call and the reason — "stands" when the draft was right — or parked, dropped, merged into a sibling. Then **Missed:** what the manager owes or the draft forgot, and **The sums:** arithmetic worked from its operands.

Then `# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]` — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line. Items in meeting order, `**name (Call, ~N min)**`, a sentence or two of things to say and ask, real numbers and names inside. Then **Off the page** (parked or dropped, with reasons), **Before you sit** (every open manager row, each a concrete move — settled before the report hears an ask, above all a row an item hangs on), and **Why this order** (a few lines on why the calls landed).

A thin packet still earns a page: the sort names what is missing, the brief asks rather than asserts — slim is the honest shape, nothing added for balance, no praise without a quotable instance, and the word count yields to the truth. A full week through the manual sits in `references/marina-notes.md`. Follow the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When the input is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already held — whatever its label — the reply is one short note under 180 words, with no sort:

- Say plainly that it records a past meeting rather than preparing the next one — and name the document and the meeting it records.
- Say what would turn it into preparation: this week's open items, current figures, the manager's rows, anything flagged since.
- Where open actions survive inside, write the follow-up — owner, action, date as given — in a line or two; never a brief, never a re-sort.
- Personal material mentioned in passing stays personal — "the personal matter", nothing more.
