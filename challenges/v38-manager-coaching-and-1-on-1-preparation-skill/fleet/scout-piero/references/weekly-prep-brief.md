# 1-on-1 brief: Sorin Vadasz, QA Lead, Release Gate

**This week:** The quality numbers are genuinely good — escape rate
2.1% → 1.3% across two releases (−0.8 points, ~38% relative), and 9 of
12 filed defects confirmed. The three rejections share one cause —
environment faults on aging lab hardware — which is also the root of his
own submitted topic and of the manager's overdue budget request.

**Order:** The earned recognition opens on the coverage push; the
release status follows; then the item that hands the manager the week's
real answer — his own question, which doubles as the justification for
the open budget request.

## Agenda

### 1. Test-coverage push · Recognition
Manager's label: "recognition for the coverage push", kept — the figures
carry it.
Escape rate down 0.8 points across two releases is a named, measurable
result and it is his. Open with it plainly — no ceremony needed beyond
the number itself.

### 2. Release readiness · Status
Manager's label: "release status", kept — with the environment caveat
named.
9 of 12 confirmed; the three rejections are environment-caused, not
quality-caused — ESC-118 reopened Wednesday on a clean env with a trace
attached. "The three rejections all trace to lab hardware — want to walk
through whether ESC-118-class failures would confirm on the new rigs?"

### 3. Defect write-ups · Feedback
Manager's label: "coaching — blunt write-ups" → Feedback: the accuracy
holds (dev lead, in the open channel, repro steps confirmed); the
friction is real but narrow.
"Ostrom's read is that the write-ups are right but land hard — is there
a channel or format change that keeps the content and lowers the
friction?" A venue question, never an accuracy concession — coaching
accuracy here would coach the one thing the record says is working.

### 4. QA lab budget · Status
Manager's label: not filed → Added: his own submission — three rejected
defects trace to the same hardware the budget request would replace.
"The three environment rejections point at the same hardware as your
budget question — want to use them as the concrete cost in the request?"
His filed item does double duty: it answers him and arms the manager's
own overdue ask.

## Parking lot

## A third shape — the input fails the first gate

Sometimes what arrives is last week's notes — "use this to prep the next
one." However the manager frames it, the input cannot enter the
sequence: it is the record of a session that already closed. The correct
output is the bounce, under ~180 words:

# Not a 1-on-1 prep input

This is a record of last Thursday's session — what was discussed and
the actions agreed — not this week's signals. A brief gated on it would
prep a meeting that already happened: settled items cleared again,
everything that moved since invisible.

What would make next week's brief pass: the open action items and their
states, the week's metrics or ticket figures, any threads worth knowing
about, and the manager's own topics for the session.

From the notes, the open items to carry: the manager's escalation draft
— due two days before the meeting; the report's coverage proposal —
due the day before. One item stays out of every brief: a family
situation that may need December flexibility — a dedicated support
conversation, not an agenda item.

## Branches — where a gate clears differently

Same first week, alternate records:

- **If Ostrom's post had instead read** "the write-ups keep reopening
  resolved defects without new evidence," item 3 re-types: the accuracy
  claim fails, the item stands as Coaching — anchored to the
  reopened-defect count, not to tone.
- **If the escape rate had drifted 1.3% → 2.1%**, recognition gates shut
  and the status item carries the deterioration — the session's deciding
  item, not its opener.
- **If Sorin had filed no submission**, item 4 does not exist — the
  budget request stays the manager's open item, surfaced but carrying
  no slot. A submitted topic is what earns the agenda real estate; its
  absence leaves the space empty.
- **If a signal had shown bluntness outside the work channel** — a sharp
  message in #general — the venue question hardens into a real coaching
  item: correct content, wrong room. Still narrow — venue, never
  accuracy.
- **If an item had filed low while carrying real weight** — a "quick
  question" whose dependency other teams are waiting on: filed priority
  is a claim like any other label. Re-weight it in the order, with the
  waiting parties named.
- **If the report had flagged the pattern himself** — a coaching item
  whose own submission names the failure and its cause becomes a
  process question, not a lecture — "what flags the next one
  early?" — anchored to his self-report.
- **If the manager's own overdue item caused the miss** — a deliverable
  on the manager's side that would have prevented the report's flag:
  it closes the brief as the manager's action, never charged to the
  report as a coaching item.

The sequence's invariant: an item clears the gate its own evidence
opens. The manager's label proposes the type; the signals decide which
one the item actually walks out with — and an input that is the record
of a meeting already held never enters the sequence at all.
