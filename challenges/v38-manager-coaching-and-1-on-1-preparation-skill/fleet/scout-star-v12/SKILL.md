Four kinds of material land in a weekly packet, and each kind can prove different things. The skill reads them in turn, builds the week's real shape from what they show, and only then looks at the manager's draft — drafts remember headlines and forget evidence. The testing stays on the reply, printed above the brief, so the manager can audit every call rather than take it on faith. Old minutes, transcripts and anything that is not this week's preparation get a short note back instead.

## What each source can tell you

- **Boards and trackers** show position: what is open, what is waiting, whose row it is. A row is open until something says otherwise — NOT STARTED, overdue and in-progress all count; resolved stays resolved unless a newer artifact contradicts it directly.
- **Messages** show movement: flags raised, chases unanswered, questions asked. Three ignored chases are a block, not a tone problem; a training question is the report raising their future themselves.
- **Figures** show pace: current versus plan, the difference written out — "41 of 50 (9 behind)". A number only in the manager's head does not exist.
- **Praise** shows what to open with: a client's note, a peer's thanks, work praised by name.

## From evidence to the six calls

Each draft item is tested against that picture and lands in one class:

- **Recognition** — praise exists in the record. Opens the meeting, quoted with the words and the number; never "good week".
- **Blocked** — somebody else holds it: missing approval, silent dependency, more than one person can carry. The manager leaves with a move of their own. If the signals show the hold reaching further than the draft's label suggests — teams queued behind it, a senior name already in the thread — the call says so and the item rises, regardless of the priority the draft assigned.
- **Coaching** — the same behaviour in two or more separate situations, or a goal the report set. Coached with questions, not lectures. An impression the evidence contradicts — "disengaged" while the log shows early flags and picked-up work — is dropped, the signals named. When the report already found the cause and tracks it, coach the step before it — flag changes sooner next time — anchored to the real figures, not the symptom they found.
- **Career** — the report raised training, scope, review timing themselves. One honest question and whatever can be truthfully promised.
- **Status** — nothing to fix, celebrate or decide. Closes the meeting in a line or two; opening on housekeeping spends the best minutes on the smallest items.
- **Parked** — pay, HR, another team's conflict, next quarter's plan: routed where it gets decided, a line saying why. Personal matters stay private — "the personal matter", elaborated never.

Calls the sources force: a complaint that traces to somebody else's hold-up is the hold talking — Blocked, not Coaching. Five messages on one problem make one signal, not five; Coaching needs separate dated occasions. An impression with no signal beneath it is dropped. A single self-caught slip is a quick word, never a coaching slot. And only quotable praise earns the opener; when none exists, a line above the first item admits it.

## The page, in order

**## Weighing the draft** — one numbered entry per draft item, in the draft's order: the label in quotes; **Signals:** who said or logged what, where and when, with the row or figure; **Call:** the class it earns and why — "holds" when the draft had it right — or parked, dropped, folded into another. Then **Missed by the draft:** — what the manager owes or skipped — and **Figures:** anything computed, worked from its operands — "9 of 14 is 64%"; a rise of two on four is up half, and points are not percent.

**# 1-on-1 brief: [Report] x [Manager] — [date]** — 350–700 words. **Context:** one line — report, tenure, the week in a phrase. Items in meeting order, **name — Call, ~N min** and a sentence or two written as things to say and ask, carrying the real numbers and names. Then **Set aside** (parked or dropped, with reasons), **Before the meeting** (every open row the manager owns, each with a concrete move — a NOT STARTED row is preparation, closed out before the report is asked for anything, particularly a row an agenda item needs), and **Thinking** (three or four lines on why the calls landed as they did).

A thin packet still gets a page — the weighing says what is missing and the brief asks instead of inventing: the gap in the manager's picture, the question that fills it, and what to hear in the reply.

One full worked week — sources read, draft weighed, page written — sits in `references/chair-notes.md`. Same calls apply; take the reasoning, never the facts.

# Not a 1-on-1 prep request

When what arrives is minutes, a transcript, or notes of a conversation already had — however labelled — the reply is one short note under 180 words, no weighing section:

- Explain that a past meeting's record cannot produce a forward brief — the week has moved.
- Where open actions survive inside it, write the follow-up — each open action with its owner and date, as recorded — in a line or two; no brief, no re-sorting.
- Ask for what preparation needs: the current open items, fresh figures, anything flagged since.
- Personal references stay "the personal matter", nothing more.
