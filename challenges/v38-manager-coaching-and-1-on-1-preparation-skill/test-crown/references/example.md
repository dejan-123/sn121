# Worked example — the form only, never the facts

This file shows the reply's shape on an invented input. In a real task the
facts come from the input; nothing here is copied into a real reply.

## The input (invented)

Draft plan for a weekly dispatch review, plus the week's shift log and
figures. Draft items: "1. Truck turnaround — status check. 2. Nina's
attitude on radios — coaching. 3. Dock crew did great — recognition.
4. Nina asked about shift lead — career." Log: two shipments waiting on a
vendor's missed pickup window (vendor acknowledged Tuesday); radio check
shows Nina called out one mislabelled pallet, once, Wednesday; shift
supervisor's note names dock crew's record 41 pallets cleared Thursday;
Nina submitted the shift-lead question herself. Figures: turnaround 52
min vs 44 last week.

## The reply (invented)

## The record says

Draft plan plus the week's shift log and figures — a review still to come.

1. "Truck turnaround — status check" — the log shows two shipments held on
   the vendor's missed pickup, acknowledged Tuesday. Not a status check:
   a routing fix the requester can make. Change.
2. "Nina's attitude on radios — coaching" — one callout, Wednesday, one
   dated instance. That is a question, not a coaching slot. Change.
3. "Dock crew did great" — the supervisor's note names Thursday's record.
   The praise is real when it carries the number. Keep.
4. "Nina asked about shift lead — career" — her own submitted topic; it
   keeps protected minutes. Keep.

Off the record: the vendor's pickup window is owed by the carrier, not by
anyone in the room.

Figures: turnaround (52 − 44) / 44 = +18% against last week.

# Weekly dispatch review: the week the vendor owed the window

**Context.** Turnaround slipped +18% and the log says why: two shipments
sat on the vendor's missed pickup. The crew's own numbers went the other
way — Thursday's 41 pallets was a record.

**1. Recognition: the dock record.** Thursday's 41 pallets, named in the
supervisor's note, is the earned opener.

**2. Fix to make: the pickup window.** Two shipments held on the vendor's
missed window, acknowledged Tuesday. The move is the requester's: get the
carrier to commit a recovery slot before Thursday's cut.

**3. One question: the Wednesday callout.** Nina flagged one mislabelled
pallet on the radio Wednesday — worth asking what the labelling step
missed, as a question, not a verdict.

**4. Her topic: the shift-lead question.** She raised it herself; it holds
its minutes. Anchor it on the week's own signal — the callout she made
was exactly the eyes-up work the role needs.

Why it reads this way: the status check came back a fix to make, the
coaching item came back a single Wednesday callout, and her topic kept
its minutes.
