# Two replies and a note (invented workplaces; form only)

All three cases are made up. A reply is written straight out, with nothing ahead of it: its first characters are `## What the week shows`, or, for the note in case three, `# ` and the report's name. Copy the form: the reading that takes the draft item by item in plain sentences, the title built from the week's signals, the header that combines and computes, the sentence budget, the parking lot that holds only what failed the venue reading, the closing paragraph that gives the manager the reasons. Copy no fact, name, phrase or figure; in a real reply each one comes from the inputs supplied.

## Case one: full inputs

What the manager supplied for Elif Karaca, Laboratory Operations Lead at Quillon Diagnostics:

- Draft agenda: "Kudos - good run from her lately" / "Status: calibration audit" / "Status: sample backlog" / "Quick feedback: weekly turnaround report went out late" / "Coaching: her locum briefings need work" / "Check in on the assessor course she started" / "Development: she asked what a laboratory manager role would take".
- Action log: file the calibration audit with the accreditation assessor (Elif, DONE Tuesday); renew the courier contract (Manager, DONE); send Elif the laboratory-manager role profile (Manager, OVERDUE).
- Figures: 240 samples were reported this week against a trailing average of 400; 600 samples on hold until the analyser vendor's support team restores the results interface.
- Messages: Tuesday, Tomasz Brandt (the accreditation assessor) in #lab-quality: "Elif's calibration audit matched every instrument log - first audit this year with no re-inspection." Monday, Elif in #vendor: "Results interface is down again; the vendor's support team gives no date. Six hundred samples can't be reported until it's back. I'd send results by the manual export in the meantime." The turnaround report went out after the Friday 15:00 cutoff on the 7th and again on the 14th. Saturday, the locum lead Rosa Whitlock in #lab-quality: "Briefing left out the two analysers out of service - found the tags when we got there. Can we sit down about how outages reach the locum rota?" No other message mentions a briefing. Wednesday, Elif in #lab-quality: "Module one of the assessor course done; the practical is on the 21st." Thursday, Elif to the manager: "Turnaround reports: both late ones were weeks when the reagent delivery moved to Friday afternoon. I should have said."
- Elif's own topic: "What would I need to show for the laboratory manager role?"

The reply:

## What the week shows

The manager supplied a draft agenda, an action log, two figures and this week's messages for a meeting still to come, so this is prep.

1. "Kudos - good run from her lately." The praise is real but the draft cannot point to it: on Tuesday Tomasz Brandt wrote in #lab-quality that Elif's calibration audit "matched every instrument log". It stays Recognition, on his words and not on "good run lately".
2. "Status: calibration audit." The log shows the audit filed and done on Tuesday, so there is no status left to ask about. It is dropped as an item and becomes the evidence for the opener.
3. "Status: sample backlog." That is not a status check: 240 samples were reported against a usual 400, and 600 wait on a results interface the vendor has not dated. A queue held by a vendor is not hers to clear, however many messages mention it, so it is an Unblock: the next move is the manager's, and Elif has already proposed a way round to back.
4. "Quick feedback: weekly turnaround report went out late." That is not a one-off: Elif's own report missed the Friday cutoff on the 7th and again on the 14th, the same lapse of hers on two dates. It becomes Coaching, asked as questions, and she has already named the cause.
5. "Coaching: her locum briefings need work." That is not coaching: Rosa Whitlock's Saturday message is the only dated instance, and no other message mentions a briefing. It becomes Quick feedback, with one light question on what is still owed.
6. "Check in on the assessor course she started." It holds as a light Career check-in: module one is done and the practical is dated the 21st.
7. "Development: she asked what a laboratory manager role would take." It holds as Career with protected time, because it is Elif's own question.

Outside the draft: the manager has two rows in the action log. The courier contract is done; sending Elif the laboratory-manager role profile is overdue, so it is owed, and it has to be cleared before her question about the role can be answered. Rosa Whitlock also asked for a talk about how outages reach the locum rota; that is her process to run, so it goes to the parking lot.

Figures: 240 against 400 is (240 - 400) / 400, down 40%. Action log: the audit and the manager's courier contract are closed; the manager's role profile is open and overdue.

# Elif Karaca, Laboratory Operations Lead: sample backlog stalled on the vendor's results interface

**Context.** The calibration audit is closed and praised, but reporting slowed to 240 samples this week against a trailing average of 400 (down 40%), with 600 waiting on a results interface the vendor's support team has not dated. In the action log the audit and the manager's courier contract are closed, and the manager's role profile for Elif is overdue. Rosa Whitlock's request for a talk about how outages reach the locum rota leaves this meeting.

**Manager owes.** The laboratory-manager role profile for Elif, overdue in the action log: bring it or name the day. Her question about the laboratory manager role cannot be answered properly without it.

## Agenda for Elif: the audit result, the stalled samples, the two observations, then her course and her question

**1. Recognition: calibration audit**
Tomasz Brandt wrote in #lab-quality that the audit "matched every instrument log - first audit this year with no re-inspection". Worth saying to Elif in those words, with what it spared: a second visit to every bench.

**2. Unblock: sample backlog**
Elif told #vendor on Monday that the support team gives no date, and proposed sending results by the manual export in the meantime. Manager's move: back her proposal by approving the manual export this week, and call the vendor's account manager for a dated fix.

**3. Quick feedback: locum briefing**
Rosa Whitlock wrote on Saturday that the briefing left out the two analysers out of service; it is one moment in a week that was otherwise praised, so it is asked about once and not coached, and the talk she asked for is routed below, not argued here.
- "Rosa found the two analysers out of service from their tags: is anything from that briefing still owed to her team?"

**4. Coaching: turnaround report timing**
- "You traced both late reports to the reagent delivery moving to Friday afternoon, which explains them: what happens to the report when the delivery moves?"
- "You wrote on Thursday that you should have said: who needed to hear it, and by which hour?"

**5. Career: assessor course**
- "Module one is done and the practical is on the 21st: what do you need between now and then?"

**6. Career: laboratory manager path**
- "The assessor needed no re-inspection after the calibration audit: which parts of the laboratory-manager role does that already show, and which does it leave untouched?"

## Parking lot for Elif
- How outages reach the locum rota (Rosa Whitlock asked for the talk): a separate session with Rosa Whitlock, who runs the locum rota; book it this week and bring Saturday's briefing.

**Why the brief reads this way.** Each label that moved did so on a dated signal: the audit went from status to the opener because Tomasz Brandt's message credits finished work, the backlog went from status to an Unblock because 600 samples wait on a vendor only the manager can press, and the report and the briefing swapped places because one has two dates and the other has one. The role profile comes before any ask of Elif because asking about her path while owing her the document gets the order backwards. Rosa Whitlock's request is left off the agenda on purpose: how outages reach the rota is hers to change, not something Elif can settle in this meeting.

## Case two: a draft with almost no signals

What the manager supplied for Lorcan Adeyinka, Cold Store Technician at Wexmoor Produce:

- A note: "Buried this week, no figures pulled. Draft: 1. Compressor replacement - where are we? 2. Coaching: he seems to have lost his spark lately. 3. Feedback: Thursday's closing temperature check wasn't recorded. 4. He asked about a night rate - should cover that. 5. Development chat."
- One message, Friday, Lorcan to the manager: "Thursday's check is missing because the door alarm faulted at closing and I stayed on it past handover. Recorded the readings this morning from the logger download. Won't happen again without a note to you."
- A second message, Monday, the quality lead Hannelore Voigt to the manager: "Thursday's missing check showed up in the weekly quality log. I'd like us to review how the closing check gets covered when an alarm faults."
- No action log, no figures, no other messages.

The reply:

## What the week shows

The manager supplied a draft agenda and two messages for a meeting still to come, so this is prep, but thin: no action log, no figures and no other messages came with it. Only one draft item can be tested against evidence.

1. "Compressor replacement - where are we?" Nothing supplied can answer that: there is no task list and no message about the work. It stays a Status question, and the contractor's task list is the record that would settle it.
2. "Coaching: he seems to have lost his spark lately." Nothing dated stands behind "seems" and "lately", and the one message from Lorcan shows him clearing a faulted alarm and repairing the record unasked. It is left off; an observed instance would bring it back as Quick feedback.
3. "Feedback: Thursday's closing temperature check wasn't recorded." This is the one item with evidence: Lorcan's Friday message explains it and says he recorded the readings from the logger download. One instance, already owned, so it is Quick feedback and not coaching.
4. "He asked about a night rate." Pay is not settled in a weekly slot, so it goes to the parking lot with nothing drafted on it.
5. "Development chat." Lorcan sent no topic, so it stays Career as one open question on the work the draft does name.

Outside the draft: on Monday Hannelore Voigt asked for a review of how the closing check gets covered when an alarm faults; that is her process to run, so it goes to the parking lot. No action log was supplied, so nothing is owed by the manager.

Figures: none can be worked, because no numbers came with the draft.

The draft asks "where are we?", and nothing supplied can answer it; the contractor's task list would, and with it in hand the question is about the one step that is late.

"Lost his spark" is left off because nothing dated supports it and the one message points the other way. ## Case three: a pasted record of the last meeting


The reply redirects the request with this note alone, with no reading and no brief:

# Catrin Bevan, Membership Coordinator: not a 1-on-1 prep request

**Redirect.** This reply redirects the request instead of building a brief. What was pasted is the record of the 1-on-1 already held on 6 February, with its "Discussed" list and action items; every status in it is a week old and the membership result was already discussed in that meeting, so a brief built from it would mislead. None is drafted: no agenda, no talking points, no opener.

**What it does support: a follow-up on its actions.**
- Manager: decide the number of instructors per session - by 12 February.
- Manager: ask the landlord for a second key to the gear store - by 9 February.
- Catrin: draft the safety briefing sheet - by 13 February.

**What a brief for the next meeting needs.** Where each of these three actions stands now, this week's signals (figures, messages, anything Catrin has raised), and a draft agenda.

**The personal matter.** The record mentions a family matter and possible days away this month. That stays a private conversation between the manager and Catrin, with HR for the leave options; nothing on it is drafted here.

## What would change

Had the role profile been sent, the line would read "Manager owes. Nothing open.": the courier contract, done, owes nothing, and chasing the vendor is an Unblock, never a debt.

Had the overdue row been one of Elif's own, it would be a Status question on the agenda, never a "Manager owes" line: that line holds only rows whose owner is the manager.

Had Rosa Whitlock asked for nothing, the parking lot would read "Empty this week: nothing in these inputs is pay, a level decision, a personal matter or someone else's process to fix."
