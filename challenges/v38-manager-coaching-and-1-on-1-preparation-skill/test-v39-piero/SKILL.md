# The working note — read the record, then the draft, and let the difference show

A manager writes the draft agenda from memory, so each label on it is a claim, not a finding. The week's record decides what each item is: the action log, the figures, the messages, the report's own submitted topic. The manager has to see that decision being made, because an item that comes back relabelled with no visible reason gets argued in the meeting and not used. So I read the week before the draft and show that reading first, item by item; the brief is then written from it. A label the evidence supports is kept; one it contradicts is changed; no signal, name, day, duration or figure is ever supplied that the inputs lack. One input gets neither part: a pasted record of a meeting already held is not prep input, even when the request says to prep the next meeting, and the reply redirects the request with the short note of section 6.

## 1. Reading the inputs

The reply opens with `## Reading the inputs`. Its first sentence says what was supplied and that it is prep for a meeting still to come. Then one numbered entry for each draft item, in the draft's order, as two plain sentences, three at most, with no tags: the manager's words in quotes, what the log, the figures or the messages show about it (who, where, which day), and the class it has earned, with the reason. An overturned label is said outright ("that is not coaching: one dated instance, so Quick feedback"); a label that holds is kept on its signal; an item dropped or sent to the parking lot gets its reason. `Outside the draft:` then covers what the manager did not list: the manager's own action-log rows, the report's own submitted topic, a third party's request, a personal matter. `Figures:` works each derived figure from both of its numbers and sums up the action log. Every entry that keeps or gains a class is an agenda item — the kept set is the agenda; an item read in is never dropped.

## 2. Six classes

- **Recognition** opens the meeting only when, in this period, a named person or a measured result credits a specific piece of the report's work; quote who said what. A vague "great work" in the draft is replaced by that signal. With no such signal there is no opener, and the reading says so.
- **Unblock** or **Status.** A draft "status check" is an Unblock when the evidence shows waiting: tickets blocked on another team, output far under its trailing average, a queue one person cannot clear. Its talking point is the manager's own move (escalate, broker, reassign, decide), never "how is it going"; when the report has already proposed the fix or begun the escalation it is still an Unblock, and the manager's move is to back it. Load or structure mislabelled as feedback or coaching is also an Unblock, however many messages mention it, and a draft priority the evidence contradicts is corrected in the same clause. **An item the evidence reclassifies never leaves the agenda — it stays under its earned class.** Only two things leave: a perception with zero dated instances, and what the venue reading routes out. A status check on work the log shows done is retired: if someone praised that work it becomes the Recognition item, and the rest is a header clause, except in-progress work with a dated next step, which keeps a one-question Status item.
- **Quick feedback** or **Coaching: count the dated instances of the same behaviour**. One instance is Quick feedback: what happened, then one light question, never a verdict. Two or more are Coaching, and a coaching slot holds questions only. One behaviour gets one item, never both. Zero instances ("seems", "lately") is a perception, never an item of any class, even when a blocker would explain the impression: it is left off, with what the signals show instead and what observed instance would bring it back. Pointed or repeated messages sent while chasing an uncleared blocker come from the block, not from the report's manner: they are cited under the Unblock and get no feedback or coaching item.
- **Career** items are questions that hold a named signal up against the path the report asked about. A topic the report submitted gets protected minutes. Asking what a level takes is Career and puts nothing in the parking lot. A promotion case already in motion is one topic in one place: the decision goes to its booked venue in the parking lot, and the agenda keeps only the report's own open action toward it, as one question that states its log status first.

## 3. The venue reading

Pay, a promotion decision, health or family circumstances, a formal performance concern, friction with a named colleague, a process review or conversation that a third party has asked for: each is listed in the parking lot with its venue (a dedicated conversation, HR or the pay cycle, skip-level or calibration, the process owner) and the one booking step. No talking points, detail or advice on it anywhere in the brief; a personal matter is named in the input's own few words with its date, and nothing more. When nothing qualifies, the parking lot says so in one line, and a task the manager owes is never a parking-lot line.

## 4. Figures

In the reading's `Figures:` line, work every figure the inputs give two numbers for, both numbers beside the result; the header states them. Change against a baseline, an estimate against what the work took, a scope that grew: (new - old) / old, a signed whole percent. Scores and rates differ in points, never percent. The action log is summed up in words, not as a ratio: what is closed and what is open.

## 5. The brief

After the reading, the brief: 350 to 700 words.

- Title: `# <Full name>, <role>: <the week in a few words from the signals>`.
- `**Context.**` Three or four sentences combining the inputs: what moved, what is stuck and on whom, the state of the action log, which item lacks evidence.
- `**Manager owes.**` Always written: each action-log row whose owner is the manager, never one of the report's, that is not done, with its status and "bring it or name the day"; a held-up link only where the record ties it; with every such row done, or no log, "nothing open". It is never an agenda item, an Unblock or a parking-lot line.
- `## Agenda for <first name>: <the order in a clause>`, then item lines `**N. Class: topic**`, each followed by at most three sentences. Recognition is item 1 when earned; the report's own submitted topic follows it — protected minutes, never a squeezed slot, even when it is Career; then Unblocks; then Quick feedback or Coaching; then Status; then Career the report did not raise — every kept item lands here, count them.
- `## Parking lot for <first name>`: only what the venue reading routes; never an action-log row or a status.
- `**Why the brief reads this way.**` Last, three or four plain sentences: the calls that differ from the draft, each with the signal that decided it, and what was left off.

Under an item line:

- Recognition: who said what about which work, and what it made possible.
- Unblock: what waits, on whom, since when; then "Manager's move:" and the action.
- Status: one question naming the live work item.
- Quick feedback: one or two sentences on what happened and what the report has already done about it, then one light question on what the moment left open, never a verdict.
- Coaching: two questions and nothing else, each one sentence that names the thread, ticket or figures it comes from. When the report has already named the cause, credit it and ask about that cause, not the symptom.
- Career: one or two questions, each setting a named signal from this week beside the role or criteria in question. A draft career item with no topic from the report is kept on the agenda, never parked, and its question uses the week's strongest signal.

## 6. Thin or unusual inputs

When signals are missing, the reading says so first and each entry says what would settle its label; the brief still reaches 350 words. Keep draft items in the class their own words give them (a workload check-in is Status); under each, one sentence on what the draft's question cannot settle and the record that would, then one question the manager can say aloud; never write a signal or praise to anchor one. After the agenda, a short `**Pull before the meeting.**` list names each missing record and who holds it.

The record of a meeting already held misleads as prep input: every status in it is a week old, and what it praises was already said in that room. Build no brief from it. Redirect the request with a note of at most 180 words. Its first characters are `# ` and the report's name, its title says this is not a 1-on-1 prep request, and its first sentence says the reply redirects the request, what the input is and why a brief built on it would mislead; then what it does support, a follow-up on its actions, listed as given with owner and due date, the manager's own first; what a real brief needs (where each action stands now, this week's signals, a draft agenda); and any personal, health, family or pay matter in it, named in the record's own few words and sent to a private conversation between the manager and the report, with nothing drafted on it.
