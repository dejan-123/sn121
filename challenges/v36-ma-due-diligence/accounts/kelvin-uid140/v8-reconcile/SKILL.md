# Reconciling the sweep against the deal

A deal team writes its bet down as balances: what the value stands on, the events that would stop it, what the price assumes. The sweep is what came back. I close the file as a diligence auditor: which balance does each item touch, was the statement that would settle it ever opened, what did it say? Every item takes one standing.

## The opening balances

Before any item is tiered I write down what the deal stakes: the entity exactly as written; the stage, which fixes the step every action must clear (LOI, signing or completion); the thesis dependency; each stated walk-away, tagged W1, W2; each price condition, tagged P1, P2. A price struck as a multiple assumes the figure is real; value said to rest entirely on one asset or contract base assumes that base is real — both are P1 even if the input never says condition. An input stating no walk-away beyond standard reps and warranties gets those words in the anchor — warranties compensate after signing, they test nothing before it — and the thesis never becomes one; with no price basis, "P: none stated". A balance set as a share or count gets its number worked once on the base the term names (a quarter of 64 units is 16); where the count is not supplied the balance stays unverifiable, and the row names the supplying record. "Statements never received" repeats only the registers the input itself names as unsearched — papers a party holds are a request, not a missing statement; a record the input says was retrieved has been received; I never add one by inference.

## Whose item is it

An item is about the target when it names the exact entity, or a party the pool ties to the deal: its certifier, its lender, a named customer, an opponent in a claim, the key person a stated term names.

An item naming nobody in the deal — a sector trend, a study, a recall, trouble at "an unnamed firm" — is outside the books however close its subject runs to a walk-away: the shared product, class or district explains the sweep's return; it is nobody, never a near match, and no step asks whether the unnamed party might be the target. The row is noise, its action none.

A near match — a letter off, an outlet's "(sic)", another suffix or legal form — is an item posted under the wrong name: its row gets one step, confirming on the companies registry whether the two names are one entity; nothing else is decided on it until the answer comes back, and it never breaks a balance on a name that is not the target's.

## Sources, not repeats

Ten links can be one source. Outlets quoting one release, email, spokesperson or filing share one origin; an outlet citing another adds none; the target's release, site, deck, spokesperson and denials are one origin. A source is independent when it had its own access: a docket, a regulator's notice or register, a counterparty's filed accounts, a certifier's own list. The count sets the label, never the topic: **Primary record** for the record itself; **Corroborated** for two or more independent origins, one outside the target; **Reported** for press, posts and the target on itself; **Unverified** for anonymous or "familiar" sourcing; **Gap** for a register the brief names unsearched, which still gets a row and a standing; a sweep that ran and returned nothing was received — "no signals found" is a statement, not a gap, and silence on a person or party is a request, not a gap either.

## When a balance breaks

Must-address is an exception: it must clear before the next step or the deal proceeds on a broken balance. A row earns it only when attributed, resting on two independent origins or one primary record — the shapes below carry their own weight — bearing on a stated balance or the thesis with weight to move the deal, and due at this stage. A grave topic on thin sourcing, a near-match name, or a report that even literally true would not itself breach a stated term stays deeper-diligence.

Three further shapes take exception standing.

**The balance is already broken.** A term written as an absence — unencumbered, no liens, no sanctions, a valid certificate, no active challenge — holds only while nothing of that kind exists; once an attributed record shows the thing exists at all, the balance is broken now: financing raised for assets is ordinarily secured on what it paid for. Every item evidencing that one break takes the standing together — press report, the target's own admission, the lender's disclosure are reconciling items on a single exception, "read with row N". A share or count threshold on the same subject is a second test, unverifiable until a record gives the count — the row names it. Items that only confirm a balance — a counterparty's own record of the contract the deal rests on — are reconciled entries and never climb; items bearing on a break without evidencing it — a certificate beside its fallen issuer — stay deeper-diligence: a cluster rises on a break, never a confirmation.

**The process that produces the breach is already running against the target itself.** An authority is now acting — a review or inquiry naming the target, an inspection under way, a notice being drafted — where a stated balance assumes no such thing. It is not yet the sanction the term names, but it is how that breach starts, so the row is must-address for verification: the running process is the record and waits on no second origin — a hedged report of the authority's own action still takes the route; the step settles inclusion. Its step asks the authority in writing; the reply says plainly nothing has been imposed. The action must be against the target as spelled — a near-match never takes this route — and talks, approaches and discussions of a step not taken are not a running process: they stay deeper-diligence even where a stated term names the person involved.

**The statement was never received.** A public register the input names as unsearched, when it is the very record a stated balance turns on, leaves that balance unverifiable: must-address with the search as its step, one lettered row per balance it decides. A file of another kind of notice than the term names — warning letters beside enforcement, inspection notes beside a hold — stays deeper-diligence with its own search, even unopened — a different record kind never takes this route.

## The rest of the ledger

Deeper-diligence holds items that miss one test and one procedure settles: the near-match identity check; the target's own word on a contract, renewal, certificate or win nobody independent confirms; a certificate whose issuer another row doubts; an anonymous or unverified claim naming the target, complaint threads included — it asks for the record; a departure or exit unverified or in a function the deal depends on; and records living only in the parties' hands — churn, renewals, client status — asked for from whoever keeps them — a counterparty's status is asked of the counterparty — never sent to a register, never this route even where a balance turns on them.

Monitor holds items touching nothing staked, and items a record already confirms for the deal: a counterparty's own filing confirming the contract; a searched-clean or current register entry answering the term, read again at the step; a routine appointment, a funding round, an orderly named succession; and anything closed for good — a dismissal with prejudice, a withdrawal, a settlement paid — the order and terms asked for in the step; a dismissal without prejudice or open talks close nothing — it stays live. A denial or counsel retained is a management representation with no evidential weight: it neither lowers the item it answers nor rises beside it, it sits at monitor as the target's response, never part of the finding.

Noise names nobody in the deal.

## Reading items together

Before standings are final I read across the rows. Items reporting one fact are reconciling items on one entry: they share one standing and the check line says which moved. A register read against the target's own list is set line by line: how many the list named, how many returned in force under the exact name, under another holder, lapsed — split lettered per status (3a, 3b), the remainder counted and named the evidence gap; a register vouches for its return alone, never the schedule.

## The reply

It opens `## `: the target, "reconciliation of stated terms", and the stage. The opening bullets carry the balances: entity and step; thesis dependency; each W and P as written; worked share or count numbers; statements never received. The table holds one row per numbered signal under the input's own number and order, splits lettered; columns: the item, whose item it is, sources and weight, the balance it touches, the standing, the step. A step is a handoff: an owner, a verb, the exact record or party, and what it must clear; a noise row says none. One line names the items that moved together. The tally reads down the standing column: each standing's rows, their number, the total matching the signals. Three short paragraphs close it: what breaks the reconciliation, ending on the questions to answer before the next step; items held at enquiry, naming the row that came closest and the test it failed; and the statement still missing, the most important record unreached. No view on proceeding, no risk score, nothing the input did not supply, under about 7,000 characters.
