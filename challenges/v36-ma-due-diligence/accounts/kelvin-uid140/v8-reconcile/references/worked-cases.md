# Worked cases — deal reconciliation

Three sweeps, each worked end to end in the reply's own form. The names, figures and events are invented; copy the form — which item earns which standing and why — never the facts. The situation index after the pools collects the calls that recur.

## Pool one — a loud break

The brief. Haldenbrook Marine Systems Ltd, signing in nine days. Priced at 3.2x its contracted service backlog. W1: borrowings above £5m not disclosed in the data room. W2: enforcement action against the company or a named founder. P1: the orderbook of 40 hulls — a 25% threshold means 10 hulls.

The sweep.

1. Trade press: Haldenbrook raised £18m through Nordvind Leasing for six production sheds and a build vessel.
2. Haldenbrook's own statement confirming an £18m facility with Nordvind, security taken over the financed assets.
3. Nordvind Leasing's quarterly filing discloses a registered security interest of £18m against Haldenbrook Marine Systems Ltd.
4. The sweep returned no search of the companies' charges register for Haldenbrook.
5. An anonymous industry forum post claims Haldenbrook "will default by spring", no sourcing.
6. A sector white paper on leasing trends in marine manufacturing, naming no company.
7. A trade item notes "Halwick Marine Ltd" of another district now sits on an export-restriction list.

The reconciliation.

```
## Haldenbrook Marine Systems Ltd — reconciliation of stated terms — signing in 9 days

- Entity and step: Haldenbrook Marine Systems Ltd; signing.
- Thesis dependency: contracted backlog priced at 3.2x.
- W1: borrowings above £5m not disclosed in the data room.
- W2: enforcement action against the company or a named founder.
- P1: orderbook of 40 hulls; 25% = 10 hulls.
- Statements never received: the companies' charges register for Haldenbrook.

| # | Item | Whose | Sources / weight | Balance | Standing | Step |
|---|------|-------|------------------|---------|----------|------|
| 1 | £18m Nordvind facility for sheds and a vessel | Target | Reported — one origin | W1 | must-address | Counsel: obtain the facility agreement and reconcile it against the data-room borrowing schedule; read with rows 2–3 |
| 2 | Target's release confirming the £18m secured facility | Target | Reported — target voice, same origin | W1 | must-address | Counsel: written schedule of the security package; read with rows 1, 3 |
| 3 | Nordvind filing: £18m registered security interest | Lender | Primary record | W1 | must-address | Counsel: pull the registered charge instrument; read with rows 1–2 |
| 4 | Charges register never opened | Target | Gap | W1 | must-address | Analyst: run the register today — the balance cannot be measured until it returns |
| 5 | Anonymous "default by spring" claim | Target | Unverified | W1-adjacent | deeper-diligence | Analyst: ask Nordvind for the facility's covenant terms and payment record |
| 6 | Sector leasing white paper | Nobody | Reported | None | noise | None — names no party to this deal |
| 7 | "Halwick Marine Ltd" on an export-restriction list | Near match | Reported | W2 | deeper-diligence | Analyst: one registry check on whether the two names are one entity; nothing enters the books on a name that is not the target's |

Moved together: rows 1–3 are reconciling items on one exception — a single £18m secured facility the data room may not have disclosed; row 4 is the statement that settles its measure.

Tally: must-address 4 (rows 1–4); deeper-diligence 2 (5, 7); monitor 0; noise 1 (6). Total 7.

What breaks the reconciliation: one exception runs the file. Three items evidence a single secured facility and the fourth is the statement never received. If the facility sits outside the data room, W1 fails at £18m against a £5m line; if it was disclosed, the exception clears on the document check alone. What needs answers before signing: the facility agreement, the charge instrument, the register return and the data-room reconciliation.

Items held at enquiry: the anonymous default claim names the target and touches W1's subject, yet carries no origin a ledger can post — it asks for the covenant record and gets it. The export-list entry came closest to the books; it fails on the name, and the registry check settles whether it is this company's item at all.

The statement still missing: the sweep never reached the charges register — the one record that states whether security exists on the company's own account. Everything else in this pool argues about a facility; only the register closes the balance.
```

## Pool two — a quiet ledger

The brief. Fenmore Compliance Software Inc, LOI signed, signing in three weeks. Priced at 4.1x recurring contracts; the deck rests the value on the Aldermere County engagement. W1: regulatory enforcement or fines above $250k. W2: loss of the SecureOps Trust Mark certification the company holds. P1: the recurring base implied by the 4.1x price. Every register the brief listed was searched.

The sweep.

1. The regulator's public register shows Fenmore in good standing, no enforcement entries, pulled this week.
2. An archive of warning letters under a consumer-labelling scheme includes one addressed to Fenmore.
3. Trade press: the CFO retires in March; a named internal successor begins a nine-month overlap.
4. A user-forum thread from an anonymous account: "Fenmore's onboarding broke our audit."
5. Aldermere County's published budget papers confirm the Fenmore engagement at the deck's value.
6. A report that "an unnamed Northeast compliance vendor" faces a class claim.
7. The Trust Mark body's current register lists Fenmore's certification in force.
8. Fenmore's counsel states publicly that the forum claims are unfounded.

The reconciliation.

```
## Fenmore Compliance Software Inc — reconciliation of stated terms — signing in 3 weeks

- Entity and step: Fenmore Compliance Software Inc; signing.
- Thesis dependency: recurring-contract base priced at 4.1x, resting on the Aldermere engagement.
- W1: regulatory enforcement or fines above $250k.
- W2: loss of the SecureOps Trust Mark certification.
- P1: the recurring base the 4.1x price assumes is real.
- Statements never received: none — every register the brief listed was searched.

| # | Item | Whose | Sources / weight | Balance | Standing | Step |
|---|------|-------|------------------|---------|----------|------|
| 1 | Regulator register: good standing, no enforcement | Target | Primary record | W1 | monitor | Analyst: re-pull the register at signing — it already answers the term |
| 2 | Warning letter under the labelling scheme | Target | Primary record, another kind | W1 | deeper-diligence | Analyst: run the enforcement register itself; warnings are not the record the term names |
| 3 | CFO retirement with named successor, nine-month overlap | Target | Reported | None | monitor | Counsel: confirm the succession terms appear in the employment schedule |
| 4 | Anonymous forum thread on a failed onboarding | Target | Unverified | W-adjacent | deeper-diligence | Analyst: ask Fenmore for the client ticket log covering that period |
| 5 | County budget papers confirm the Aldermere engagement | Counterparty | Primary record | P1 | monitor | Analyst: diarise the renewal date for the completion review — the balance reconciles |
| 6 | "Unnamed Northeast compliance vendor" class claim | Nobody | Reported | None | noise | None — the item names nobody in this deal |
| 7 | Trust Mark register lists the certification in force | Certifier | Primary record | W2 | monitor | Analyst: re-pull at signing; the term is already answered |
| 8 | Counsel calls the forum claims unfounded | Target | Reported — target voice | W-adjacent | monitor | The company's answer to row 4 — a response, not an origin; file it beside the ticket-log request |

Moved together: none — each row stands on its own record.

Tally: must-address 0; deeper-diligence 2 (rows 2, 4); monitor 5 (1, 3, 5, 7, 8); noise 1 (6). Total 8.

What breaks the reconciliation: nothing does. The two records that could break the stated terms were opened and answered clean, and the contract the price rests on is confirmed by the counterparty's own papers. The questions needing answers before signing are procedural — the enforcement-register pull and the ticket log.

Items held at enquiry: the labelling archive came closest — same authority, a different statement; it fails the record-kind test and keeps its own search rather than standing in for it. The forum thread carries the failure every unverified claim does: it names the target and no origin.

The statement still missing: none by the brief's own account — each named register was opened. The honest gap is party-held paper: the ticket log only Fenmore can produce, and the step already asks for it.
```

## Pool three — a mixed register

The brief. Corvid Analytics plc, LOI stage. The deck rests almost all value on the patent-protected analytics engine. W1: material IP litigation involving the company. P1: the portfolio the deck values — nine patents.

The sweep.

1. IP litigation search: Corvid v. Rennex Systems, a claim Corvid brought, dismissed with prejudice last quarter, costs settled.
2. An active claim against Corvid by Vantage IP Holdings, asserting a patent whose claims read on the analytics engine; docket retrieved.
3. Patent register pull against the deck's nine: four returned in force under the exact name; two recorded under "Corvid Research Ltd", a predecessor; one lapsed; two deck patents returned no register match at all.
4. A founder release: the flagship customer has "renewed for three years" — no counterparty confirmation in the pool.
5. Trade press: Corvid's CTO "in talks with a rival"; no appointment announced.
6. A sector M&A roundup naming neither company.

The reconciliation.

```
## Corvid Analytics plc — reconciliation of stated terms — LOI stage

- Entity and step: Corvid Analytics plc; LOI.
- Thesis dependency: value rests almost entirely on the patent-protected engine.
- W1: material IP litigation involving the company.
- P1: the portfolio the deck values — nine patents.
- Statements never received: none named as unsearched.

| # | Item | Whose | Sources / weight | Balance | Standing | Step |
|---|------|-------|------------------|---------|----------|------|
| 1 | Corvid v. Rennex dismissed with prejudice, costs settled | Target | Primary record | W1 | monitor | Counsel: obtain the order and its terms for the file — closed for good |
| 2 | Active Vantage claim against Corvid, docket retrieved | Target | Primary record | W1 | must-address | Counsel: pull the pleadings and asserted patent; instruct coverage analysis on the engine |
| 3a | Four deck patents in force under the exact name | Target | Primary record | P1 | monitor | Analyst: none beyond the completion re-pull — the register answered |
| 3b | Two deck patents held by "Corvid Research Ltd" | Target | Primary record | P1 | deeper-diligence | Analyst: obtain the assignment chain from the predecessor entity |
| 3c | One lapsed, two deck patents with no register match | Target | Gap | P1 | deeper-diligence | Analyst: pull renewal/fee records and the register history for all three — the unverified remainder is the evidence gap |
| 4 | Founder release: flagship renewal "signed for three years" | Target | Reported — target voice | P1-adjacent | deeper-diligence | Analyst: ask the counterparty for the executed renewal or its own confirmation |
| 5 | CTO "in talks with a rival", no appointment | Target | Reported | Thesis-adjacent | deeper-diligence | Analyst: ask the board directly whether a resignation or notice has been received |
| 6 | Sector M&A roundup | Nobody | Reported | None | noise | None — names no party to this deal |

Moved together: none — the register rows split by status and stand separately.

Tally: must-address 1 (row 2); deeper-diligence 4 (3b, 3c, 4, 5); monitor 2 (1, 3a); noise 1 (6). Total 8 rows across 6 signals.

What breaks the reconciliation: the Vantage claim. The asserted patent belongs to the claimant, but its claims read on the engine the price assumes is protected — a docketed challenge on staked ground is an exception until the claim resolves or materiality is set. What needs answers before the next step: the pleadings, the claim-construction read, and a coverage opinion on the engine.

Items held at enquiry: the register returned a mixed answer — four of nine reconcile, five do not. Row 3b came closest to the exception: two of the nine held under another name fails attribution until the assignment chain arrives. The founder's renewal is the company's own word and the CTO item is a step discussed rather than taken; neither climbs on what the pool shows.

The statement still missing: the register returned seven of nine. The two unmatched patents, the assignment filings and the renewal record are the papers this pool never produced — a register vouches for what came back alone, and the deck is not the record.
```

## Pool four — the process already running

The brief. Aldercroft Pensions Administration Ltd, pre-LOI. Priced at 1.6x its administered-asset base. W1: net outflows above 12% of assets in a year. P1: assumes no material sanctions or disciplinary action outstanding against the company.

The sweep.

1. Administered assets £9.4bn, outflows 4.1% for the year, from the filed report — two outlets cite it independently.
2. The pensions ombudsman's published determinations list Aldercroft in three resolved cases, all decided for the company.
3. A trade bulletin reports the conduct regulator is running a thematic review of fee disclosure across mid-tier administrators, and that Aldercroft "is understood to be among those under review". Sourcing described as "industry contacts"; no statement from the authority.
4. The conduct regulator's public enforcement register: not included in the retrieval scope.
5. An anonymous post alleges hidden charges at "a big pensions firm"; no company named.

The reconciliation.

```
## Aldercroft Pensions Administration Ltd — reconciliation of stated terms — pre-LOI

- Entity and step: Aldercroft Pensions Administration Ltd; pre-LOI.
- Thesis dependency: administered-asset base priced at 1.6x.
- W1: net outflows above 12% of assets in a year.
- P1: no material sanctions or disciplinary action outstanding.
- Statements never received: the conduct regulator's enforcement register.

| # | Item | Whose | Sources / weight | Balance | Standing | Step |
|---|------|-------|------------------|---------|----------|------|
| 1 | Assets £9.4bn, outflows 4.1%, filed report | Target | Corroborated — two outlets on one primary record | W1 | monitor | Analyst: re-pull the filing at LOI — the threshold is answered well inside |
| 2 | Three ombudsman determinations, all for the company | Target | Primary record | P1 | monitor | Counsel: file the determinations — resolved matters, answered |
| 3 | Trade bulletin: regulator's thematic review under way, Aldercroft "understood to be among those under review" | Target | Reported — hedged, one origin | P1 | must-address | Counsel: write to the regulator asking plainly whether the company is within the review's scope; the reply records that nothing has been imposed |
| 4 | Enforcement register not in retrieval scope | Target | Gap | P1 | must-address | Analyst: run the register today — the balance cannot be measured until it returns |
| 5 | Anonymous hidden-charges allegation, no company named | Nobody | Unverified | None | noise | None — names nobody in this deal |

Moved together: rows 3–4 both bear on the same price condition from different sides — the running process and the statement never received.

Tally: must-address 2 (rows 3, 4); deeper-diligence 0; monitor 2 (1, 2); noise 1 (5). Total 5.

What breaks the reconciliation: nothing is broken yet, and two exceptions stand open. The review named in row 3 is the process that produces the breach the price assumes away — a hedge in the sourcing does not send it back, because the written step is what settles inclusion. The register in row 4 is the other half of the same condition, never opened. What needs answers before LOI: the register return and the regulator's written confirmation.

Items held at enquiry: none — every other item reconciles cleanly or names nobody.

The statement still missing: the enforcement register, and alongside it the regulator's written answer on scope. Together they close the price condition in both directions.
```

The point of the fourth pool: a hedged report that an authority's process already covers the target takes exception standing on a single origin — the row's own step is the confirmation, and the reply says plainly nothing has been imposed. A complaint about the company, talks about an event, or an unnamed firm would each fail differently; a named place inside a running review does not.

## Situation index

The calls that recur, in the order they most often matter.

| In the sweep | Standing | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Dismissed with prejudice, withdrawn, or settled and paid — even on staked ground | monitor | Closed for good; the step asks for the order and its terms |
| Dismissed without prejudice, or licensing talks still open | the standing a live matter earns — must-address where a stated term turns on the challenge | Ended provisionally, not for good; it can be refiled at will |
| Denial, spokesperson statement, counsel retained | monitor | A response, not an origin — it never becomes part of the finding |
| Near-match name, "(sic)", another suffix, on a grave item | deeper-diligence | Identity check first; nothing breaks on a name that is not the target's |
| Register the brief names unsearched, when it decides a stated balance | must-address | The statement never received; the search itself is the step |
| File of another notice kind beside the balance | deeper-diligence | Warning letters are not enforcement; the right register keeps its own search |
| Register already returned and answering the term | monitor | The balance reconciles; re-pull it at the step |
| Independent items evidencing one breach of an absence-term | must-address, all of them | Reconciling items on a single exception — "read with row N" |
| Items confirming what the deal rests on | monitor | A cluster rises on a break, never on a confirmation |
| A party's own record evidencing the break itself | must-address with the cluster | Confirming the breach exists is still evidence of it — not a reconciliation |
| Share or count threshold the pool cannot measure | the row names the record | A second test, unverifiable until a record supplies the count |
| Authority already acting on the target — review, inquiry, inspection, notice being drafted | must-address | The process that produces the breach is running; say plainly nothing has been imposed. A hedged report — "understood to be included" — still takes this route; the step confirms inclusion, it does not wait for it |
| Talks, approaches, discussions of a step not taken | deeper-diligence | Not a running process, not a departure — even where a stated term names the person |
| A completed sweep returning nothing on a counterparty, client or named person | deeper-diligence | Not a register gap — the status lives in party-held paper; ask the holder |
| Anonymous or unverified claim naming the target | deeper-diligence | A complaint is not a sanction; the step asks for the record |
| Item naming nobody in the deal, however close the subject runs | noise | Outside the books; action none, no hedged step |
| Sector trend, study, M&A roundup | noise | Same reason — nobody's item |
| Target's own word on a contract, renewal or win | deeper-diligence | One origin and it is its own; ask the counterparty or the record |
| Certifier or accreditor loses its standing | must-address on the linked balance | The issuer falling puts the certificate itself in doubt |
| Certificate rows beside a fallen issuer | deeper-diligence | In doubt until the issuer's status resolves |
| Price struck as a multiple, or value resting "entirely" on one asset | P1 even if unstated | Write the balance down before tiering anything |
| Churn, renewal or client-status figures living only in the parties' own papers | deeper-diligence | Asked for directly from whoever keeps them; no register answers it |
| A pull measured against the company's own schedule | lettered split | It vouches for what came back alone; the unreturned balance gets counted and named |
| Report part record and part hearsay | lettered split | One label each |
| Grave claim on thin sourcing | deeper-diligence | The origin count sets the label, never the topic |
| Orderly succession named and dated | monitor | A routine entry until something suggests otherwise |
| Unverified exit or departure report | deeper-diligence | Ask the board; a report is not a resignation |

A ledger can close with zero exceptions — pool two did. A ledger can also carry one exception across several rows — pool one did. What it cannot do is let the topic, the tone, or the company's own voice set the standing; the balances do that.
