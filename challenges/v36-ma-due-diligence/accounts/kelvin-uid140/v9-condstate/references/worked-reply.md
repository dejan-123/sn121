# Worked replies — two pools answered in the target shape

Both are invented end to end: invented targets, terms, signals, and registers. They exist to show the reasoning pattern and the reply's skeleton, not the facts.

---

## Example 1 — elevated pool: the financing cluster against an "unencumbered" term

Input (abbreviated): Deal context: acquiring Brantwell Cold Storage Ltd (target). Stage: post-LOI, confirmatory diligence before signing. Thesis: Brantwell's value is its fleet of 41 owned refrigerated trucks running long-term grocery contracts. Walk-away threshold: any unresolved product-liability judgment over £250k against Brantwell. Price condition: deal price assumes the truck fleet is owned outright and unencumbered, free of recorded security interests. Retrieval scope: news and company-category coverage over the trailing 24 months. The company charges register NOT searched. Signals:

1. Brantwell announces a £3.1m asset-finance package with Kestrel Bank "to renew and expand the refrigerated fleet," per a trade press report quoting the CFO.
2. Companies filing shows Brantwell's 2024 annual accounts list 41 trucks as owned fixed assets.
3. 'Brantwell Cold Storage' cleared in a 2023 county-court slip-and-fall claim, dismissed "without prejudice" per a local paper.
4. Industry newsletter: "several regional cold-storage operators face HSE temperature-control inspections this quarter." No company named.
5. A single industry blog claims "Kestrel Bank filed a blanket charge over Brantwell assets last month," no filing cited.
6. Brantwell press release: "the company holds no outstanding security interests over its vehicle fleet."
7. LinkedIn post by an unverified account: "Brantwell's head of fleet maintenance just quit — third ops departure this year."
8. A regional outlet reports 'Brantwell Cold-Store Ltd' (note the spelling) lost a £400k food-safety judgment in May.
9. The grocery-contracts register shows all nine of Brantwell's anchor contracts current through 2027.
10. Trade report: diesel costs squeezing refrigerated-haulage margins across the region.

### The reply

## Brantwell Cold Storage Ltd, post-LOI confirmatory diligence — deal-risk classification

- Thesis dependency: the 41 owned refrigerated trucks running long-term grocery contracts.
- W1 (walk-away): any unresolved product-liability judgment over £250k against Brantwell.
- P1 (price): truck fleet owned outright and unencumbered, free of recorded security interests.
- Not searched: the company charges register.
- Conditions: W1 holding as reported; P1 **at risk** — a financing package raised against the fleet is ordinarily secured on the fleet, and an unsearched charges register cannot answer it.

| # | Signal | Attribution | Origins + label | Bears on | Tier | Required action |
|---|--------|-------------|-----------------|----------|------|-----------------|
| 1 | £3.1m Kestrel asset-finance package to renew the fleet | Target (named, exact; CFO quoted) | 1 origin — single-source, non-primary (trade press) | P1 — financing raised for vehicles is ordinarily secured on what it bought | must-address | Deal counsel obtain the Kestrel facility agreement and its security schedule from the data room before signing; an unencumbered price term is already contradicted by a facility of this kind. |
| 2 | 2024 accounts list 41 owned trucks | Target's own filing | 1 origin — single-source, primary-and-binding (statutory filing) | T0, P1 — ownership is stated; encumbrance is not answered | deeper-diligence | Corporate support pull the full filed accounts and note any security disclosures; ownership and freedom from charges are separate questions. |
| 3 | 2023 claim dismissed "without prejudice" | Target (named, exact) | 1 origin — single-source, non-primary (local paper) | W1 — a without-prejudice dismissal leaves the claim able to return | deeper-diligence | Litigation support pull the docket and confirm no refiling; verify the claim's quantum against the £250k threshold. |
| 4 | Sector-wide HSE inspections, no company named | Nobody in the deal | 1 origin — single-source, non-primary (newsletter) | None — no subject to bear on any term | noise | none — unattributed; topical overlap explains the retrieval. |

| 7 | Head of fleet maintenance quit — third ops departure | Target (own channel, unverified poster) | 1 origin — unverified | T0 — fleet maintenance is the function the thesis runs through | deeper-diligence | People lead ask the target for the ops org chart and any succession cover; an anonymous post still attributes when it names the target. |

| 9 | All nine anchor contracts current through 2027 | Independent register | 1 origin — single-source, primary-and-binding (register) | T0 — corroborates the contract base as of this date | monitor | Commercial lead note the register extract date and re-verify before signing; no other step owed. |
| 10 | Diesel costs squeezing regional haulage margins | Nobody in the deal | 1 origin — single-source, non-primary (trade report) | None — a market condition, not a target fact | noise | none — economics of the sector, not evidence about Brantwell. |

- Cross-signal check: rows 1+5 read together — one financing fact reported twice, one facility one charge claim; the same register extract answers both, and neither stands alone. Row 6 cannot lift P1 while 1 and 5 stand. Row 2 confirms ownership but does not reach encumbrance.
- Count: 10 rows from 10 signals. must-address: rows 1, 5 (2). deeper-diligence: rows 2, 3, 6, 7, 8 (5). monitor: row 9 (1). noise: rows 4, 10 (2). Total: 2+5+1+2 = 10.

**Must-address picture.** P1 is at risk on two feet: a £3.1m fleet-finance facility of the kind ordinarily secured on the assets it buys, and an unverified but exact-name claim of a blanket charge. The charges register — never searched — is the single record that answers both, and the deal cannot sign on an unencumbered price term against it. Before the next step: does a registered charge exist over Brantwell assets, and does the Kestrel facility agreement carry a security schedule over the trucks?

**Elevation rationale.** Row 5 is thin — one blog, no filing cited — but it names the exact entity and would by itself breach a stated price term if true; it elevates on the tripwire while keeping its honest unverified label. Row 8 is the closest near-miss: a £400k judgment would engage W1 directly, but 'Brantwell Cold-Store Ltd' is not 'Brantwell Cold Storage Ltd', and an unresolved near-match can never reach the top tier — identity resolves first.

**Evidence gap.** The company charges register is the decisive record and it was never searched. One extract answers rows 1, 5 and 6 at once — pull it before signing, dated and under the exact entity name.

## Example 2 — quieter pool: corroborated good news, thin noise

Input (abbreviated): Deal context: acquiring Velcourt Precision Optics Ltd (target). Stage: pre-LOI screening. Thesis: Velcourt's value is its proprietary lens-coating process under an exclusive supply agreement with two aerospace primes. No walk-away thresholds stated. Price condition: none stated. Retrieval scope: sweep across news plus company categories for the last 18 months. Patent-office registers searched; the litigation register NOT searched. Signals:

1. Trade press and the target's own site both report the exclusive supply agreement with 'Aerodyne Systems' renewed through 2030.
2. Aerospace supplier-awards listing names Velcourt as a top-ten certified optics vendor, per the certifying body's published list.
3. An anonymous forum post claims "Velcourt lost the Aerodyne contract months ago — internal source."
4. Regional news: planning permission granted for Velcourt's second coating line.
5. One blog speculates that "coating patents like Velcourt's are increasingly challenged sector-wide."
6. Job boards show Velcourt hiring three coating-process engineers.
7. 'Velcourt Optics Ltd' — a near-name — appears in a winding-up petition listing.
8. Patent register shows the core coating patent family in force, renewal fees current.
9. A recruiter's post says Velcourt's CTO departed for a competitor.
10. No signals returned on customer-renewal rates, churn, or contract pricing.

### The reply

## Velcourt Precision Optics Ltd, pre-LOI screening — deal-risk classification

- Thesis dependency: the proprietary coating process under exclusive supply agreement with two aerospace primes.
- W1: none stated.
- P1: none stated.
- Not searched: the litigation register.
- Conditions: none stated — T0 holding as reported (renewal corroborated and patents in force); the litigation register leaves challenge-exposure untested.

| 8 | Core coating patents in force, fees current | Independent register | 1 origin — single-source, primary-and-binding (patent register) | T0 — confirms the IP base as of this date | monitor | IP counsel record the register extract date and re-verify at signing; a register speaks only for its date. |

- Cross-signal check: row 3 is read against row 1 — a corroborated renewal outranks one unverified retraction claim, but the claim is cheap to extinguish in writing. Rows 1, 2, 8 independently support T0; row 9 is the only thin edge on the critical function.
- Count: 10 rows from 10 signals. must-address: none (0). deeper-diligence: rows 3, 7, 9, 10 (4). monitor: rows 1, 2, 4, 6, 8 (5). noise: row 5 (1). Total: 0+4+5+1 = 10.

**Must-address picture.** None — with no stated terms, only a live adverse claim on the thesis asset from an independent record could reach the top, and nothing here qualifies: the only adverse items are an unverified retraction claim already contradicted in the pool, an unresolved near-match, and an unverified personnel post. Before the LOI: written confirmation of the Aerodyne renewal, identity resolution on the winding-up petition, and a direct answer on the CTO.

**Elevation rationale.** Row 3 is the closest to the top — it names the target exactly and would negate the thesis if true — but the pool itself answers it: a corroborated renewal through 2030 reported across independent origins outranks one anonymous post, so it sits at deeper-diligence with a written confirmation as its action. Row 7's winding-up petition would be graver still, but the name is not the target's.

**Evidence gap.** Two: the litigation register — unsearched, and the one public record that would show any challenge to the coating patents or the company; and the commercial book — renewal rates, churn, pricing — which only the parties can produce. The register search is the cheap one; run it before LOI.
