# Verdict patterns and a worked pool

The recurring reads in a retrieved pool — each with the answer it dangles and the answer that survives — then one invented deal classified end to end. None of these companies exist; only the reasoning pattern does. Do not reuse these facts, names or sentences.

---

## Row shapes

| The row looks like | The bait | The surviving call |
|---|---|---|
| A grave accusation against a name one letter, one suffix, one word off — "(sic)" flags included | the story fits; elevate | only a register entry marries two names; deeper-diligence whose whole action is confirming the legal entity — every other judgment deferred |
| Several outlets repeating one release, quote or email, or one outlet citing another | more mastheads means corroborated | one origin; label single-source however often it is reprinted |
| A register inside scope returned nothing | cleared | cleared as of the search date only; re-verify before signing when a term depends on it |
| A decisive record never searched — said so, or outside scope | no signal means no row | the term it would decide is untested; must-address, the action IS the search; one gap deciding two terms splits lettered, one row per term |
| "No signals found" on internal facts — client status, churn, payroll, contract terms | log a gap, move on | deeper-diligence asking the parties for the papers; company silence proves nothing either way |
| A dispute dismissed "without prejudice", or settled beside live licence talks | ended | able to return — must-address where a stated term turns on it until the docket shows it ended for good; elsewhere deeper-diligence |
| A claim ended "with prejudice" on ground the deal stakes | a finished event needs nothing | deeper-diligence — obtain the order and terms; what was conceded or cross-licensed may still bind |
| Sector scare, unnamed company, unnamed regulator, class overlap with the target's assets | the topic is grave; elevate | nobody's row is noise, full stop — the target's exposure is tested through its own signals and unsearched records; no step asks whether the unnamed subject might be the target |
| The target's own channels confirming or denying | confirmation counts; denial disarms | one origin, never self-corroborating; a bare denial — "we dispute it", counsel retained — adds nothing to check and takes monitor; the target's affirmative word on what the value rests on stays deeper-diligence until confirmed |
| Financing raised for vehicles, plant or equipment while a term assumes them unencumbered | a facility is not a lien record | ordinarily secured on what it bought — the term is at risk; quantify the share, pull the filings index |
| Departure, hire or claim on the function a term or the thesis depends on | personnel gossip | deeper-diligence even anonymous; a hire in a function nothing depends on is monitor; where a term names an individual, a confirmed departure on a binding record is must-address |
| Individually thin signals jointly establishing one fact against one term | each stays thin | they elevate together — linked rows take must-address, "read with row N" |
| A register confirming the core while entries lapsed or sit under another name | entry equals clean bill | deeper-diligence — the lapse is a loss taken, the other name an identity question, the action an item-by-item schedule; entries differing in owner or status split lettered, one row per status class |
| A single thin report naming the target exactly which, if literally true, would by itself meet a stated walk-away or breach a stated price term | thin means deeper-diligence | must-address however thin — a near-match fails the test; a denial does not disarm it; the label keeps its honest origin count; the action names who verifies today |
| A report that even if true would not itself break a stated term | grave topic, top tier | can never reach must-address — a complaint is not a sanction, talks are not a departure; it is the pool's most urgent deeper-diligence at most |
| "Sources familiar" or an anonymous post on the staked topic | grave means must-address | unverified single source is never must-address — most urgent deeper-diligence, action naming who confirms it today |

## Worked pool — an invented deal

Input (abbreviated): Deal context: Acquiring Norrkeld Analytical Glassworks SA (target). Stage: post-LOI. Thesis: Norrkeld's lab-grade borosilicate output fills long-term supply contracts with pharmaceutical packagers; the value rests on the furnace line and the process patents behind it. Walk-away threshold: a confirmed environmental-violation finding against Norrkeld is a no-go. Price condition: the deal assumes the furnace line and the process-patent family are owned outright — unencumbered, free of recorded security interests and exclusive licences. Retrieval scope: sweep of news and company categories, 24 months. The environmental-enforcement register and the patent-office register were NOT searched.

Signals:

6. Materials Trade Digest (2026-10-24): borosilicate patent litigation rising across the sector. No company named.
7. Trade newsletter (2026-10-30): "sources familiar" say a regional water authority is reviewing discharge levels near Norrkeld's plant. No authority named; no filing cited.
8. Norrkeld spokesperson, quoted in the same newsletter item: "the complaint is completely without merit."

Cross-signal check: rows 1, 2 and 10 read as one encumbrance question — the facility report, the lender's own listing and the patent-family register all bear on "unencumbered"; row 1 carries the at-risk finding, row 10 splits lettered because one register returned entries in three different statuses, each a separate call. Rows 7 and 8 are the two sides of the discharge story — the rumour and the denial share one origin count, and only the enforcement register (row 9) can answer it. Row 3 stands alone until identity resolves.

**Count:** 13 rows from 11 signals (signal 10 split into 10a, 10b, 10c). Must-address: rows 1, 9 (2) · Deeper-diligence: rows 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10b, 10c (7) · Monitor: rows 8, 10a, 11 (3) · Noise: row 6 (1) · 2 + 7 + 3 + 1 = 13.

Must-address picture: two rows earn the top tier — the furnace financing that presumptively encumbers the line P1 assumes outright (row 1) and the enforcement register that was never opened on the only walk-away the deal states (row 9). The questions needing answers before signing: does the register show any violation finding against the exact name, and does the facility's security schedule confirm or widen what row 1 implies?

Elevation rationale: row 7 came closest and was held at deeper-diligence — the lazy call elevates the gravest rumour, but "sources familiar" with no authority and no filing cannot carry the top tier, and even if literally true a review is not a violation finding; the urgency lives in the action. Row 4 drew the opposite lazy call — an ended suit reads as closed — but the ground it ended on is the ground P1 assumes, so the terms are still owed. Row 8 is the easiest over-read in the pool: a flat denial sitting beside a hot claim — it adds nothing to check and takes monitor while the claim keeps its own tier.

Most important evidence gap: the environmental-enforcement register — one pull settles W1 outright and answers rows 7 and 8 in a single step; the patent register's open items (rows 10b, 10c) run a close second because P1 cannot close around an unexplained owner name and an unexplained lapse.

## How to read this

Name the probe, refuse the lazy call, file the verdict that survives. Attribution settled before any tier; origins counted not links; silences kept in their kinds; stated terms quoted as given and never invented; must-address rare and always justified by name; urgency carried in the action rather than borrowed into the tier; actions written so each could be handed to a named role. Match the shape and the restraint; do not reuse these facts, names or sentences.
