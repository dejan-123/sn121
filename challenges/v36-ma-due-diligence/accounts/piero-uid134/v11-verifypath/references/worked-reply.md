## Example — post-LOI pool: financing cluster, thin report, near-match, unsearched register

Input (abbreviated): Deal context: acquiring Marlowe Packaged Foods Ltd (target). Stage: post-LOI, confirmatory diligence before signing. Thesis: Marlowe's value is its three owned production lines and the exclusive recipe library behind them; the deal assumes the lines and recipes are owned outright. Walk-away threshold: any unresolved food-safety enforcement order against Marlowe. Price condition: deal price assumes the production lines are owned outright, unencumbered, free of recorded security interests. Retrieval scope: regional news and company filings over the trailing 24 months. The charges register and the litigation register NOT searched. Signals:

1. Regional business paper reports Marlowe signed a £4.4m equipment-finance agreement with Hartland Leasing "covering lines 2 and 3" per the CFO's quote.
2. A trade blog claims "Hartland registered a fixed charge over Marlowe plant in June," citing no filing.
3. Marlowe press statement: "no security interests exist over any company production assets."
4. Filed accounts show three production lines carried as owned fixed assets at £11.2m.
5. One regional outlet reports 'Marlowe Packaged Foods' faces an HSE improvement notice after a line-guarding inspection in March; no order text linked.
6. A second outlet reports the same inspection story, quoting the first.
7. 'Marlow Packaged Foods Ltd' — note the spelling — appears in a county-court judgment list with a £180k damages award.
8. Sector newsletter: two unnamed regional food producers received allergen-labeling improvement notices this quarter.
9. Independent certification body lists Marlowe's BRC food-safety grade AA, certificate current to next year.
10. An anonymous Glassdoor-style post claims Marlowe's head of food safety resigned in May.
11. The grocery-supply register shows Marlowe's two anchor supply agreements current through 2028.
12. No signals returned on the recipe library's registration, licensing, or assignment records.

**Must-address picture.** Two staked terms are live at once. P1 is at risk: a £4.4m equipment facility on the exact lines the price assumes unencumbered, plus an unverified charge claim pointing at the same fact — the unsearched charges register answers both with one pull. W1 is untested: an exact-name report of a live improvement-notice process stands on thin evidence but would breach the walk-away outright if it matures, and the litigation register was never searched. Before signing: does a charge exist over Marlowe's plant, and is the HSE process a notice, a draft, or already an order?

**Elevation rationale.** Rows 2 and 5 are the thinnest entries in the table — a blog with no filing and a single regional report — but each names the exact entity and each would by itself breach a stated term if true, so both elevate on the tripwire while keeping honest labels. Row 7 is the closest near-miss: a £180k judgment would graze W1, but 'Marlow Packaged Foods Ltd' is not the target's name, and an unresolved near-match can never reach the top — identity settles first via the company register.

**Evidence gap.** Two records were never touched: the charges register, which decides P1 in one extract, and the litigation register, which decides W1's standing across every claim at once. Both are public, cheap, and decisive — pull both before signing under the exact entity name, and record the search dates in the data room.
