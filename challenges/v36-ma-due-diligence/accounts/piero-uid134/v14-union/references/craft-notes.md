# Craft notes — the deal vocabulary and the reply voice

## Deal vocabulary

- **Signal** — one numbered item in the sweep output: a lead, not a fact. Each carries who reports it, where they saw it, and whether a record behind it exists.
- **Walk-away (W1, W2…)** — a stated condition the buyer exits over: quoted with its number and unit, and paired with the record that would breach it.
- **Price condition (P1, P2…)** — a stated or implied assumption inside the price: a multiple presumes the figure beneath it; value located in one asset presumes it owned.
- **Thesis** — what the price is paying for: an asset, a contract base, a licence, a person. Never a condition.
- **Register** — a record-settled source: a docket, list, filing, database. A press summary is never a register.
- **Origin** — first access to a fact. Outlets quoting one release share one origin; the target's channels are one voice that never corroborates itself.
- **Data room / Q&A log** — the deal channel through which party-held papers are requested; a request is an action, not a search.
- **Dated comfort** — a searched-clean register entry: it speaks for its search date and no further, so the pull repeats before the step it feeds.
- **Record kind** — the category of record a term turns on. A register of other kinds clears nothing however close its topic sits.
- **Party-held** — facts no register contains: contract renewals, term sheets, employment status. Asked for, never searched for.

## The reply voice

- Declarative, calm, owner-forward. Every row's action reads like a task handed to a named role: counsel, a verb, the record or party, and the step it must clear.
- The must-address picture states the top tier plainly — including when it is empty — and ends on what needs answering before the next step, never on a recommendation.
- The elevation rationale argues with the pool: the closest row is named with the test it failed, and every dramatic row that sat lower gets its reason in one breath.
- The evidence gap names the single record whose pull changes the most, then the next one. No numeric scores, no go/no-go language, nothing before the `## `.
- Short sentences beat nested ones; a row's action must survive being read alone.
