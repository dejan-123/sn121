# Worked replies

Two invented deals answered end to end in the required format. These companies do not exist; only the shape and the reasoning pattern matter.

---

## Example A — a pool with a genuine elevation

Input (abbreviated): Deal context: Acquiring Ferncliffe Automation Ltd (target). Stage: pre-LOI. Thesis: Ferncliffe's value is its installed base of warehouse robots under long-term service contracts; the deal cannot proceed if the fleet's core navigation patents are under active challenge. Walk-away threshold: any active third-party IP challenge to the navigation patents. Price condition: deal price assumes the FN-220 patent family is owned outright, free of recorded liens, exclusive licences or registered oppositions. Retrieval scope: sweep of news and company categories, 24 months. EUIPO and national patent-office registers NOT searched.

Signals:
1. Robotics Trade Daily (2026-08-02): 'Ferncliffe Automation Ltd confirms a five-year service agreement with Harwick Grocers covering 2,400 units.' No independent corroboration cited.
2. Ferncliffe corporate blog (2026-09-11): 'Ferncliffe announces record quarterly installations across three new regions.'
3. Patent Litigation Bulletin (2026-09-30) and IPWatch (2026-10-01): both report that Merrivale Robotics Inc has petitioned to invalidate Ferncliffe's FN-220 navigation patent; both pieces cite the same petition notice on Merrivale's investor site.
4. Automation Sector Wire (2026-10-05): 'Sources familiar with the matter say a major robotics vendor faces retrofit costs exceeding €30M under the new Machinery Regulation.' The target is not named.
5. Ferncliffe Automation Ltd press release (2026-10-12): 'The FN-220 platform remains certified; we are not aware of any challenge to our IP.'
6. Regional Business Chronicle (2026-10-14): 'Ferncliff Automation Ltd, a regional integrator, settles a minor contract dispute with a supplier.'
7. No signals retrieved on the EUIPO or national patent-office registers for Ferncliffe — those registers were outside the sweep's scope.

Cross-signal check: rows 3, 5 and 7 read as one finding — the reported petition, the target's denial, and the unsearched register are the same question. Row 7 splits lettered because the one register independently decides two stated conditions: 7a carries W1, 7b carries P1, each with its own action line. Rows 3 and 5 stay as the two sides of the story the register will answer. Row 6 stands alone until identity resolves.

**Count:** 8 rows from 7 signals (signal 7 split into 7a and 7b). Must-address: rows 7a, 7b (2) · Deeper-diligence: rows 1, 3, 5, 6 (4) · Monitor: row 2 (1) · Noise: row 4 (1) · 2 + 4 + 1 + 1 = 8.

Must-address picture: two rows earn the top tier — the split pair on the patent registers that were never searched. W1 turns on whether an active challenge to the navigation patents exists and P1 turns on whether the family is held free of recorded encumbrances; the same register decides both and it was outside the sweep, so each condition gets its own must-address row and its own action. The questions that need answers before the next step: does any register show a live challenge to FN-220, and does any entry record a lien, exclusive licence or opposition against it?

Elevation rationale: row 3 came closest and was held at deeper-diligence. It names the target exactly and would meet W1 if true, but its only origin is the petitioner's own notice amplified by two outlets — a single non-primary source stays where the evidence test puts it, and the urgency shows in its action (a same-day register pull) rather than in the tier. Rows 7a and 7b elevated as the never-searched decisive register: unlike row 3 they allege nothing — they are the missing measurement itself, one per condition. Row 4 was the loudest item in the pool and stayed noise — the vendor is unnamed and unattributed.

Most important evidence gap: the patent registers, same as the must-address pair — one direct pull of the FN-220 family closes both rows, settles W1 and P1, and answers rows 3 and 5 in a single step.

## Example B — a quiet pool where nothing earns the top tier

Input (abbreviated): Deal context: Acquiring Osterlund Marine Components AB (target). Stage: early diligence, pre-LOI. Thesis: Osterlund's proprietary anti-corrosion coating wins long contracts with offshore operators. No walk-away thresholds stated beyond standard reps and warranties. Price condition: deal assumes no outstanding environmental sanctions. Retrieval scope: sweep of news and company categories, 24 months.

Signals:
1. Marine Industry Review (2026-07-19): 'Osterlund Marine Components wins supplier-of-the-year award from the Offshore Contractors Association.'
2. Osterlund website (2026-08-30): 'Our coating line passed its annual ISO 12944 re-certification.'
3. Trade press digest (2026-09-22): 'European regulators are reviewing coating chemistries industry-wide; no company named.'
4. Local Business Journal (2026-10-08): 'Osterlund Marine Components appoints a new head of Nordic sales.'

## Osterlund Marine Components AB, early diligence pre-LOI: deal-risk classification

- Thesis dependency: the proprietary anti-corrosion coating that wins long contracts with offshore operators.
- W (walk-away): none stated.
- P1 (price): the deal assumes no outstanding environmental sanctions.
- Not searched: none stated.
- Conditions: P1 untested · thesis holding

| # | Signal | Attribution | Origins + label | Bears on | Tier | Required action |
|---|--------|-------------|-----------------|----------|------|-----------------|
| 1 | Supplier-of-the-year award | Target (named, exact) | 1 origin — single-source, non-primary (trade press) | Thesis dependency | monitor | Commercial lead note the awarding body's criteria in the data room for context on customer standing; no verification owed before the next step. |
| 2 | ISO 12944 re-certification announced by the target | Target (own channel) | 1 origin — single-source, non-primary (the target on itself) | Thesis dependency | deeper-diligence | Quality lead request the certificate from the issuing body (not the target's copy) and confirm scope and validity dates. |
| 3 | Industry-wide chemistry review, no company named | Nobody in the deal | 1 origin — single-source, non-primary (digest) | Topical only | noise | none — no company is named; P1 is tested through the sanctions registers, not through a sector review. |
| 4 | New head of Nordic sales | Target (named, exact) | 1 origin — single-source, non-primary (local press) | Nothing staked | monitor | none beyond noting the appointment — a routine hire adds nothing to check before the next step. |

Cross-signal check: no rows change each other — rows 1, 2 and 4 are independently about the target and row 3 names nobody.

**Count:** 4 rows from 4 signals. Must-address: none (0) · Deeper-diligence: row 2 (1) · Monitor: rows 1, 4 (2) · Noise: row 3 (1) · 0 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 4.

Must-address picture: nothing in this pool earns the top tier, and that is a finding worth saying plainly. The only sanction-relevant item (row 3) names no company and cannot bear on P1 as attributed evidence. The question that still needs an answer before the next step: do the environmental sanctions registers return anything on the target — the sweep never said they were searched.

Elevation rationale: row 3 came closest to elevation and failed the attribution test — a sector review with no named company is noise, not an untested condition, because P1 was never stated to depend on that review. Note the distinction drawn in the anchor: P1 is untested only if its register was unsearched; the input is silent on whether sanctions registers were run, so the open question above belongs to the next retrieval pass, not to this table.

Most important evidence gap: the input does not say whether any sanctions or environmental register was searched. A direct query of the relevant environmental enforcement registers on the target's exact registered name closes the only condition this deal states.

## How to read these

Both replies show the same discipline: attribution settled before any tier, origins counted not links, stated conditions quoted as given and never invented, must-address kept rare and always justified by name, urgency carried in the action rather than borrowed into the tier, actions written so each could be handed to a named role, and silence treated as "untested," never as "clear". Match this shape and this restraint; do not reuse these facts, names or sentences in a submission.
