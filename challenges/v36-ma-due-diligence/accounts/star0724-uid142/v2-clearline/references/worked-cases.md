# Worked cases — two pools cleared end to end

Two invented deals, answered in the reply's form. Every company, register, person and figure is invented; what transfers is the reasoning — whose paper, how many origins, which term, what settles it. Take the form, never the facts or the sentences.

---

## Case A — a loud pool

Input (abbreviated): acquiring Halloran Crane & Heavy Haul plc, post-LOI. Thesis: contracted heavy-lift capacity; the price rests on the 240-crane fleet and its order book. W1: more than 30% of the fleet under recorded security interests is a no-go. P1: the fleet delivered unencumbered — free of recorded security. Scope: news and company sweep, 24 months; the statutory charges register NOT searched.

Signals:
1. Plant Finance Weekly and Haulage Register both report Halloran closed a £55M refinancing of its crane fleet with Merrowdale Capital; the Register piece is built on the Weekly's.
2. Halloran press release: "a £55M facility accelerates our fleet renewal to 240 cranes."
3. Merrowdale Capital interim report: Halloran Crane & Heavy Haul plc listed among asset-finance borrowers. No terms.
4. Regional Safety Gazette: "Halloran Cranes Ltd (sic) named in an inspectorate notice over lift-plan documentation."
5. Trade bulletin: an unnamed East Midlands crane operator lost its operating licence over falsified maintenance records; several firms work the region, none named.
6. No entries from the statutory charges register — outside the sweep's scope.
7. Halloran spokesperson, asked about the facility: "the group carries no third-party charges over its cranes."
8. Kilnveil Construction plc filed accounts: "a four-year lifting services agreement with Halloran Crane & Heavy Haul plc, £9.1M total value."
9. Operators' forum, anonymous: "a third of Halloran's cranes failed their last LOLER inspection." No corroboration.

- Thesis dependency: contracted heavy-lift capacity on the 240-crane fleet.
- W1 (walk-away): over 30% of the fleet under recorded security — worked: over 72 of 240 cranes encumbered breaches it — settled by the statutory charges register.
- P1 (price): the fleet unencumbered — settled by the same register.
- Not searched: the statutory charges register.
- Conditions: W1 untested, not yet measurable · P1 at risk, not yet measurable · thesis holding

Cross-signal check: rows 1, 2 and 3 are one finding — the financing exists, admitted by the target and confirmed by its lender — so all three rows stand at must-address together, each with its own action, and the unanswered piece is the count, which only row 6's register supplies. Must-address picture: the fleet P1 assumes unencumbered carries an admitted £55M refinancing — established by three rows that share one fact — and both stated terms now hang on a register that was never opened. What is owed before signing: the charges pull itself, with every recorded interest itemised against unit count, because W1 fails above 72 cranes and P1 fails at one.

Elevation rationale: rows 2 and 3 might have been read as weak — the target's own voice and a listing with no terms — but a fact confirmed by the party holding the security is not thin; the cluster's weakness is only the missing count, and that is what the action cells chase. Row 9 came closest below the line: an anonymous inspection claim cannot climb because it is unverified and, if true, is still not the recorded security or enforcement event any term names. Row 7 is the trap: the flat denial feels like an answer, and it is none — it sits at monitor while the register decides.

Most important evidence gap: the statutory charges register — one pull settles W1 and P1 together and either confirms or dissolves the whole top tier. Behind it, Merrowdale's written confirmation of what the £55M stands secured against, which would let the deal size the exposure before the register arrives.

## Case B — a quiet pool

No walk-away stated. P1: a multiple of FY2026 filed revenue, reported at £31.2M. P2: the manufacturing licence valid at closing. Scope: news, company and litigation sweeps; the national pharmacovigilance notices archive NOT searched.

Signals:
1. Filed accounts FY2026: revenue £31.2M, including a £4.4M one-off licensing receipt booked inside operating revenue.
2. National medicines register: the Fenwick suite manufacturing licence live, expiry June 2028.
3. No entries from the pharmacovigilance notices archive — outside scope.
4. Pharma trade daily: an unnamed European fill-finish manufacturer received a deficiency letter after an inspection; its product class overlaps Sorrelhaugh's. No company named.
5. Court docket: a former distributor's contract claim against Sorrelhaugh was dismissed with prejudice in March.
6. Sorrelhaugh press release: "Kestrelle Pharma has renewed its fill-finish agreement for a further five years." Kestrelle has not confirmed.
7. Sorrelhaugh announcement: the quality director retires at year-end; the deputy quality director is named as successor.
8. Pharma Trade Journal: "sources familiar" say a second senior QA manager has also resigned. No corroboration.
9. Patent register: SP-114 live under the exact name; SP-209 lapsed 2024 for unpaid maintenance fees; SP-310 assigned to "Sorrelhaugh Biologics Holdings Ltd".
10. Litigation database sweep: no active proceedings returned against the exact name.
